## 2. Standard Practice — Key Extracts

**Introduction (verbatim, p.2):** "Preschool sector, plays a pivotal role in the foundational development of children between the ages of 4 to 6 years. It is widely recognised as a critical phase that shapes cognitive, emotional, physical, and social development. In Malaysia, the preschool sector is part of the broader education ecosystem and is governed by several ministries and agencies including the Ministry of Education (MOE), the Department of Social Welfare (JKM), Jabatan Kemajuan Masyarakat also known as KEMAS and Jabatan Perpaduan Negara dan Integrasi Nasional also known as PERPADUAN. Preschools provide structured learning environments that blend play-based activities with formal teaching to prepare children for primary education. The industry comprises both public and private providers, encompassing government-funded institutions, faith-based and NGO-run centres, as well as commercial kindergartens and internationally affiliated early childhood centres. With increasing emphasis on quality education and national policies such as the Malaysia Education Blueprint (2013–2025), the preschool industry has grown significantly in scope and demand. The need for qualified and competent educators, principals, and support staff has become more pronounced to ensure that preschool services meet national standards in curriculum, safety, and child development. Moreover, the preschool industry is a vital component in promoting inclusive education, social cohesion, and long-term human capital development. As such, continuous investment in training, professional certification, and regulatory compliance remains central to the sustainable advancement of the preschool sector in Malaysia."

**1.1 Occupation Overview (verbatim, p.2-3):** "Preschool teacher profession requires specialized training to ensure effective teaching and child development. Preschool teachers are expected to develop and deliver lesson plans based on the National Preschool Curriculum, incorporating play-based and interactive learning techniques. They must manage classrooms efficiently, ensuring a safe, inclusive, and engaging learning environment while encouraging positive behaviour among children. Additionally, they monitor and assess children's development, adapting teaching methods based on individual learning needs. Beyond academics, preschool teachers also play a key role in nurturing social and emotional development by teaching essential life skills such as sharing, cooperation, and empathy. Regular collaboration with parents is essential to discuss children's progress and provide guidance for continued learning at home. Moreover, ensuring child safety and well-being is a fundamental responsibility, requiring adherence to safety and hygiene regulations and readiness for emergency situations. The push to professionalize preschool education in Malaysia has been ongoing. The requirement for a diploma became official policy in the 2010, and the year 2020 was the initial deadline by which all practicing preschool teachers needed to be diploma-qualified. As 2020 approached, the government provided support such as in-service training programs and scholarships to help unqualified teachers upgrade their credentials."

**7. Work Conditions (verbatim, p.13-14):** "Preschool teacher typically works in preschool, which may be run by the government, private institutions, NGOs or organizations. Their work environment includes classrooms designed to be child-friendly, incorporating colourful decorations, learning materials, and play areas. Lessons are conducted across all pupils development domains includes both indoors and outdoors activities, hands-on activities, storytelling, singing, communicating, reading and writing. Hygiene and safety are major priorities, as preschoolers are more prone to accidents and illnesses, requiring teachers to maintain a clean and safe learning space. The working hours for preschool teachers generally, though this may vary depending on the type of institution. Government-run preschools, such as those under the Ministry of Education (MOE), Jabatan Kemajuan Masyarakat (KEMAS) and Jabatan Perpaduan Negara dan Integrasi Nasional (PERPADUAN), usually follow school hours and finish earlier, while private preschools may require teachers to stay until 5:00 PM to accommodate extended childcare services. Some international preschools also have longer teaching hours but offer better salaries and benefits. Despite their scheduled hours, many preschool teachers continue working at home, preparing lesson plans, reviewing pupils' progress, and creating teaching materials. The role of a preschool teacher is both dynamic and impactful, requiring a balance of creativity, patience, and strong pedagogical skills to foster early childhood development. Working in an engaging and stimulating environment, preschool educators create a structured yet playful atmosphere enriched with visual learning aids, interactive activities, and hands-on experiences. Their primary responsibility extends beyond academic instruction to include the cognitive, social, and motor skill development of young learners through storytelling, music, games, and creative arts. Effective classroom management is essential, as each child presents unique developmental needs and behavioural traits, necessitating adaptability and strong communication skills. Beyond educational instruction, preschool teachers hold a critical duty in ensuring the safety, well-being, and overall care of their pupil. This includes maintaining a secure learning environment, assisting with essential needs, and fostering positive social interactions. A collaborative relationship with parents is fundamental to the holistic development of the child, requiring consistent communication to provide developmental updates, address challenges, and reinforce learning strategies at home. While direct classroom engagement may constitute a portion of their responsibilities, preschool teachers also dedicate substantial time to lesson planning, curriculum development, pupil assessment and professional collaboration. Their work demands a high level of organization and commitment, yet it offers profound professional fulfilment. The ability to witness and contribute to a child's growth from foundational learning to increased confidence and social engagement is an invaluable reward. Preschool educators serve as the architects of early education, shaping not only academic skills but also essential life foundations, making their role indispensable in a child's lifelong learning journey."
