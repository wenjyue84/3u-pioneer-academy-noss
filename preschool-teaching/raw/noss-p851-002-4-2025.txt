Preface 
 
Standard Definition 
 
The National Occupational Skills Standard (NOSS) is a Standard document that outlines the 
minimum competencies required by a skilled worker working in Malaysia for a particular area 
and level of occupational, also the path to achieve the competencies. The competencies are 
based on the needs of employment, according to the career structure for the occupational area 
and developed by industry experts and skilled workers. 
 
The National Competency Standard (NCS) is a Standard document that outlines the 
competencies required by a skilled worker in Malaysia. 
 
Description of Standard Components 
 
The document is divided into three (3) components which includes: - 
 
Component I  Standard Practice 
This component is about the i nformation related to occupational area 
including introduction to the industry, Standard requirements, occupational 
structure, levelling of competency, authority and industry requirements as a 
whole. 
 
Component II  Standard Content 
This component is a reference to industry employers in assessing and 
improving the competencies that is required for a skilled worker. The 
competencies are sp ecific to the occupational area. The component is 
divided into two (2) section which are the chart (Competency Profile Chart, 
CPC) and details of the competencies (Competency Profile, CP). 
 
Component III  Curriculum of Competency Unit 
This component is a reference for the training personnel to identify training 
requirements, design the curriculum, and develop assessment. The training 
hours that included in this component is based on the recommendations by 
the Standard Development Committee (SDC). If there are modifications to 
the training hours, the Department provides the medium for discussion and 
consideration for the matter.

1 Daily Lesson 
Plan 
Also known as Rancangan Pengajaran Harian  (RPH) is a structured 
instructional document prepared by the teacher that outlines the learning 
objectives, teaching activities, materials, time allocation, and assessment 
for a specific day. It ensures that teaching is goal-oriented, organised, and 
aligned with the curriculum. 
2 Ergonomic 
gestures 
Refer to body movements and postures that are performed in a natural, 
safe, and efficient manner to reduce physical strain, prevent injury, and 
promote comfort during daily tasks. In a preschool or teaching context, 
this includes how teachers lift objects,  move furniture, interact with 
children at their eye level, or sit and stand during activities. 
3 National 
Preschool 
Curriculum 
A government-endorsed educational framework that outlines the learning 
standards, objectives, and outcomes for each subject and educational level. 
It provides uniform guidelines for curriculum design, teaching, learning, 
and assessment to ensure consistent educational quality nationwide. 
4 Pupil learning 
abilities 
The range of cognitive, emotional, social, physical, and language 
capabilities that determine how a pupil processes information, responds to 
instruction, and demonstrates learning across different contexts and 
domains. 
5 Regulatory 
bodies 
guideline 
A set of rules, policies, or standards issued by authorised government or 
statutory agencies to govern, monitor, and ensure compliance within 
specific sectors such as education, health, safety, and childcare. These 
guidelines provide the legal and procedur al framework for institutional 
operations. 
6 Stakeholder In the preschool context, stakeholder include parents, teachers, principals, 
government agencies, community members, and relevant NGOs. 
7 Structured 
learning 
A planned and organised approach to teaching and learning that follows a 
clear sequence of objectives, content, activities, and assessment, often 
based on a curriculum or syllabus. Structured learning ensures that pupils 
progress systematically through developmental stages. 
8 Traffic flow Refers to the planned and controlled physical movement of pupils and staff 
within a learning environment. It includes structured routines such as 
queueing in class, walking to the dining area, washing hands, sitting for 
meals, cleaning up, re -washing hands , and returning to class. Effective 
traffic flow ensures safety, discipline, and smooth transitions in daily 
preschool routines. 
9 Training 
provider 
A training provider refers to an institution, organisation, or company that 
offers structured skills development and training programmes aligned with 
national competency standards which includes providers conducting 
programs under the Sistem Latihan Dual Nasional (SLDN) and Sistem 
Latihan Program Bertauliah (SLaPB).

1. Introduction 
 
Preschool sector, plays a pivotal role in the foundational development of children between 
the ages of 4 to 6 years. It is widely recognised as a critical phase that shapes cognitive, 
emotional, physical, and social development. In Malaysia, the preschool sector is part of 
the broader education ecosystem and is governed by several ministries and agencies 
including the Ministry of Education (MOE), the Department of Social Welfare (JKM), 
Jabatan Kemajuan Masyarakat also known as KEMAS and Jabatan Perpaduan Negara dan 
Integrasi Nasional also known as PERPADUAN. 
 
Preschools provide structured learning environments that blend play-based activities with 
formal teaching to prepare children for primary education. The industry comprises both 
public and private providers, encompassing government -funded institutions, fait h-based 
and NGO-run centres, as well as commercial kindergartens and internationally affiliated 
early childhood centres. With increasing emphasis on quality education and national 
policies such as the Malaysia Education Blueprint (2013 –2025), the preschool  industry 
has grown significantly in scope and demand. 
 
The need for qualified and competent educators, principals, and support staff has become 
more pronounced to ensure that preschool services meet national standards in curriculum, 
safety, and child development. Moreover, the preschool industry is a vital com ponent in 
promoting inclusive education, social cohesion, and long -term human capital 
development. As such, continuous investment in training, professional certification, and 
regulatory compliance remains central to the sustainable advancement of the presc hool 
sector in Malaysia. 
 
1.1 Occupation Overview 
 
Preschool teacher profession requires specialized training to ensure effective 
teaching and child development. Preschool teachers are expected to develop and 
deliver lesson plans based on the National Preschool Curriculum, incorporating play-
based and interactive learning techniques. They must manage classrooms efficiently, 
ensuring a safe, inclusive, and engaging learning environment while encouraging 
positive behaviour among children. Additionally, they monitor and assess children's 
development, adapting teaching methods based on individual learning needs. 
 
Beyond academics, preschool teachers also play a key role in nurturing social and 
emotional development by teaching essential life skills such as sharing, cooperation, 
and empathy. Regular collaboration with parents is essential to discuss children's 
progress and provide guidance for continued learning at home. Moreover, ensuring 
child safety and well -being is a fundamental responsibility, requiring adherence to 
safety and hygiene regulations and readiness for emergency situations. 
 
The push to professionalize preschool education in Malaysia has been ongoing. The 
requirement for a diploma became official policy in the 2010, and the year 2020 was 
the initial deadline by which all practicing preschool teachers needed to be diploma-
qualified. As 2020 approached, the government provided support such as in -service

1.2 Rationale of NOSS Development 
 
As of 28 February 2025, there are 7,577 preschool operating across the country, 
employing a total of 31,826 teachers. However, only 6,439 of these teachers, which 
accounts for approximately 20.2%, meet the minimum academic qualification 
requirements2. This statistic highlights a significant gap in the qualifications of 
preschool educators in the current landscape. 
 
To address this gap and uplift the quality of education in the sector, the development 
of the National Occupational Skills Standard (NOSS) for Pre -primary Private 
Teaching has been identified as a strategic initiative. It is anticipated that this 
development will contribute to an increase in the number of teachers meeting the 
minimum academic standards, thereby strengthening the overall quality and 
professionalism in preschool education nationwide. 
 
This document serves as a strategic initiative to provide an alternative, industry -
recognised qualification pathway for experienced preschool teachers who possess 
significant practical expertise but lack formal certification due to financial 
constraints, time limitations, or limited access to conventional educational routes. 
 
In this context, the NOSS P854 -009-4:2020: Preschool Teaching and Education 
Administration was originally developed to define the core competencies required 
within the preschool sector. However, the framework primarily centres around 
managerial and adminis trative responsibilities such as organisational planning, 
institutional governance, and supervisory functions rather than encompassing direct 
teaching practices, pedagogical leadership, or child -centred instructional strategies. 
While the objective was to standardise qualifications for preschool professionals, its 
limited emphasis on teaching and learning competencies has resulted in a disconnect 
from the actual instructional needs encountered in classroom settings. 
 
Given the depth and breadth of leadership functions embedded in P854-009-4:2020, 
these competencies are more appropriately aligned with a Level 5 qualification, 
specifically for individuals serving in the role of a preschool principal, rather than 
early years teaching practitioners. 
 
Consequently, a comprehensive review of the existing standard was undertaken, 
leading to the development of NOSS P851 -002-4:2025. This revision was aimed at 
ensuring closer alignment with current industry demands, classroom realities, and 
contemporary pedagogical expectations. The review also involved redefining 
occupational titles to better reflect evolving professional rol es and restructuring 
selected competencies to enhance their relevance to technical, instructional, and 
developmental aspects of preschool education. The key refinements and updates are 
presented in Table 1 below.

1.3 Rationale of Occupational Structure and Occupational Area Structure 
 
In accordance with the Malaysia Standard Industrial Classification (MSIC), which 
standardizes the categorization of economic activities within the country, the NOSS 
development committee has determined that the role of Preschool Teaching falls 
under Section (P) Education. Given the nature of the work, the complexity of the 
process, and industry practices, the 3 -digit code that most closely aligns with this 
NOSS is Group (851), Pre-Primary and Primary Education. 
 
Based on input from industry experts, the revised NOSS is designated at level 4 
which in line with the Malaysian government's policy to elevate the quality and 
professional status of preschool education. NOSS development committee identified 
several key ar eas related to pre-primary and primary education, including Pre -
primary Teaching Assistance, Pre -primary Public Teaching, Pre -primary Private 
Teaching, Primary Public Teaching and Primary Private Teaching. However, for the 
development of this specific NOSS , the committee’s focus was exclusively on Pre -
primary Private Teaching ensuring a structured and competency -based framework 
that aligns with national education policies and industry best practices, fostering 
high-quality early childhood education and professional excellence in the sector.  
 
However, the competency standards for preschool teachers in both private and 
government institutions remain the same, ensuring uniformity in the quality of early 
childhood education across different preschool settings. Regardless of whether an 
educator works in a government -funded preschool or a privately owned institution, 
they are required to demonstrate the same level of knowledge, skills, and pedagogical 
competencies in areas such as curriculum implementation, child development, 
classroom management, as sessment, and child safety. This standardization aligns 
with national education policies and industry best practices, ensuring that all 
preschool educators are equipped with the necessary expertise to foster children's 
holistic development and create a nurturing learning environment. The occupational 
structure and occupational area structure are shown in Figure 1 and Figure 2.

4. Definition of Competency Levels 
 
The NOSS is developed for various occupational areas. Below is a guideline of each 
NOSS Level as defined by the Department of Skills Development, Ministry of Human 
Resources, Malaysia. 
 
Level 1:  Competent in performing a range of varied work activities, most of 
which are routine and predictable. 
  
Level 2: Competent in performing a significant range of varied work activities, 
performed in a variety of contexts. Some of the activities are non -
routine and required individual responsibility and autonomy. 
  
Level 3: Competent in performing a broad range of varied work activities, 
performed in a variety of contexts, most of which are complex and 
non-routine. There is considerable responsibility and autonomy and 
control or guidance of others is often required. 
  
Level 4: Competent in performing a broad range of complex technical or 
professional work activities performed in a wide variety of contexts 
and with a substantial degree of personal responsibility and 
autonomy. Responsibility for the work of others and allocation o f 
resources is often present. 
  
Level 5:   Competent in applying a significant range of fundamental principles 
and complex techniques across a wide and often unpredictable variety 
of contexts. Very substantial personal autonomy and often significant 
responsibility for the work of others and for the  allocation of 
substantial resources features strongly, as do personal accountabilities 
for analysis, diagnosis, planning, execution and evaluation.

The Preschool Teaching  Level 4 personnel is competent in performing the following core

a) Conduct daily routine activities; 
b) Perform preschool teaching and learning activities; 
c) Organise preschool classroom environment; 
d) Develop pupil’s assessment; and

7. Work Conditions 
 
Preschool teacher typically works in preschool, which may be run by the government, 
private institutions, NGOs or organizations. Their work environment includes classrooms 
designed to be child-friendly, incorporating colourful decorations, learning materials, and 
play areas. Lessons are conducted across all pupils development domains includes both 
indoors and outdoors activities, hands-on activities, storytelling, singing, communicating, 
reading and writing . Hygiene and safety are major priorities, as preschoolers are more 
prone to accidents and illnes ses, requiring teachers to maintain a clean and safe learning 
space. 
 
The working hours for preschool teachers general ly, though this may vary depending on 
the type of institution. Government -run preschools, such as those under the Ministry of 
Education (MOE), Jabatan Kemajuan Masyarakat  (KEMAS) and Jabatan Perpaduan 
Negara dan Integrasi Nasional (PERPADUAN), usually follow school hours and finish 
earlier, while private preschools may require teachers to stay until 5:00 PM to 
accommodate extended childcare services. Some international preschools also have 
longer teaching hours but offer better salaries and benefits. Despite their scheduled hours, 
many preschool teachers continue working at home, preparing lesson plans, reviewing 
pupils’ progress, and creating teaching materials. 
 
The role of a preschool teacher is both dynamic and impactful, requiring a balance of 
creativity, patience, and strong pedagogical skills to foster early childhood development. 
Working in an engaging and stimulating environment, preschool educators create a 
structured yet playful atmosphere enriched with visual learning aids, interactive activities, 
and hands -on experiences. Their primary responsibility extends beyond academic 
instruction to include the cognitive, social, and motor skill development of young learners 
through storytelling, music, games, and creative arts. Effective classroom management is 
essential, as each child presents unique developmental needs and behavioural traits, 
necessitating adaptability and strong communication skills.

Beyond educational instruction, preschool teachers hold a critical duty in ensuring the 
safety, well -being, and overall care of their pupil. This includes maintaining a secure

8. Employment Prospects 
 
As Malaysia moves toward professionalizing the preschool sector, employment prospects 
for preschool teachers are expected to become more competitive, with institutions 
preferring teachers who have at least a diploma or degree in early childhood education. 
This shift encourages educators to pursue further qualifications and professional 
development to remain competitive in the job market. Apart from traditional teaching 
roles, experienced preschool teachers can advance into positions such as head teachers,  
curriculum developers, preschool principals, or early childhood education trainers. 
Additionally, some educators choose to venture into entrepreneurship by establishing their 
own kindergartens or childcare centres, contributing to the growing early education 
industry. 
 
In accordance w ith the Malaysian government’s continuous efforts to improve early 
childhood education and the increasing number of working parents seeking quality 
preschool education for their children, the job outlook for preschool teachers remains 
stable and is expecte d to grow steadily. Those with higher qualifications, specialized 
training, and experience in innovative teaching methods will have better career 
opportunities and higher earning potential in the future. 
 
The profession offers structured career progression, with teachers able to advance into 
leadership roles such as preschool principal, curriculum developer, or education 
consultant through higher qualifications. Entrepreneurial opportunities also exist for 
experienced educators seeking to establish their own kindergartens or childcare centres, 
supported by government incentives for early education businesses. Additionally, 
educators specializing in special needs education and bilingual instruction (English, 
Mandarin, Tamil, or Arabic) are increasingly sought after, particularly in private and 
international preschools where multilingual and inclusive education is a priority.
