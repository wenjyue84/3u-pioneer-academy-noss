## 2. Standard Practice — Key Extracts

**Introduction (verbatim, p.1-2):** Video or film editing is part of the creative post-production process of film production. It involves the selection and combining of shots into sequences, and ultimately creating a finished story presentation. Video or film editing process is often referred to as the 'invisible art' because when it is well edited, the viewer becomes so engaged that he or she is not even aware of the editor's handiwork. ... The level 3 of this NOSS will describe capability in analysing visual editing project, visual editing preparation, online and offline visual editing, supervise visual editing, audio sweetening and shooting composition.

**Enrolment minimum requirement (p.2):** possesses SPM certificates, mentally fit, not colour blind. English proficiency and computer literature is an added advantage.

**Occupational Structure (p.3-4):** Sector ICT, Sub-Sector Digital Creative (Video/Film), post-production (Visual Editing) column. L3 = "Editor" (post-production/visual editing row); L2 = "Asst. Editor"; L4 = "Senior Editor"; L5 = "Supervising Editor". Occupational Area Analysis: L5/L4/L3 = Video/Film (Editing); L2/L1 = No Level.

**Malaysia Skills Certificate Level 3 definition (p.5, verbatim):** Competent in performing a broad range of varied work activities, performed in a variety of contexts, most of which are complex and non-routine. There is considerable responsibility and autonomy and control or guidance of others is often required.

**Malaysian Skill Certification (p.6):** Candidates after being assessed and verified and fulfilled the Malaysian Skill Certification requirements shall be awarded with Malaysia Skills Certificate (SKM) for Level 3.

**Job Competencies (p.6, verbatim list):** A Video / Film (Editing) Personnel (Level 3) is competent in performing:
- Visual Editing Project Analysis
- Visual Editing Preparation
- Offline Visual Editing
- Audio Sweetening
- Online Visual Editing
- Shooting Composition

**Working Conditions (p.6, verbatim):** Video or film Editor usually working in dimly light and in air-condition editing suites in TV stations and film studios and post-production houses. They are often do shift work, may have to work long and irregular hours to meet deadlines. They assemble footages from various genres such as feature film, television shows and documentaries, etc into a seamless end product... Video or film editors must have computer knowledge and skills to work in this industry and must ensure the editing equipments are in good condition at all time. Editors are advisable to visit the shooting location while it is in progress to avoid misunderstanding about the concept required by the Directors and should work closely with Sound and Musical Editors towards the end of editing process.

**Employment Prospects (p.7):** High demand locally and internationally; possible progression to Visual Effects (VFX) Directors and Sound Editors; also employable in production houses, advertising agencies, broadcasting agencies (TV Station), Multimedia department (large corporation), training centre, multi-national corporation, international airports, and as an entrepreneur.

**Training, Industrial Recognition, Advancement (p.8):** Experienced Editors usually begin as assistant editor and gradually learn new skills as they gain experience. Further certification may increase career advancement chances.

### 6.4 CU C04: Audio Sweetening (IT-072-3:2012-C04)

**CU Descriptor (verbatim):** Audio sweetening is a process of "juicing up" the video portion of a film, video or any other multimedia project. ### 6.6 CU E01: Shooting Composition (IT-072-3:2012-E01) — Elective

**CU Descriptor (verbatim):** Shooting composition is refers to the organisation pictorial elements in the frame and it is an important subject in producing video or film more interesting. ### CoCU: Shooting Composition (IT-072-3:2012-E01) — Learning Outcome (verbatim)

This shooting composition competency unit is to create a good composition result according to Director's requirement. Upon completion of this competency unit, trainees will be able to:
- Determine composition type
- Create shooting steps or guideline
- Determine props for scene
- Supply sufficient lighting
- Produce realistic scene made up of props
- Create excellent shot
- Produce smooth storyline

Training Duration: 260 Hours. Credit Hours: 26.

#### WA1: Plan required shot

**Related Knowledge:** i. Camera shot requirement (close up, wide angle); ii. Composition type.

**Related Skills:** i. Determine creativity on camera composition; ii. Determine camera angle before shooting.

**Attitude / Safety / Environmental:** i. Follow shooting board; ii. Follow Director's instruction; iii. Comply to safety procedure.

**Training Hours:** Related Knowledge 20 Hours (Lecture); Related Skills+Attitude 25 Hours (Demonstration).

**Assessment Criteria (verbatim):** Camera shot requirement confirmed. Composition type determined. Shooting steps or guideline created.

**Attitude / Safety / Environmental:** i. Follow shooting board; ii. Follow Director's instruction.

**Training Hours:** Related Knowledge 20 Hours (Lecture); Related Skills+Attitude 30 Hours (Demonstration).

**Assessment Criteria (verbatim):** Scene requirements determined. Suitable props for scene determined. Source of props supply determined.
