3. Describe rhythm, rate, pressure and continuity in massage.
4. Explain the massage flow sequence for a full-body massage.

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Always check the consultation card and confirm contraindications verbally before commencing treatment. Adjust pressure incrementally — never apply deep pressure without client consent. Stop immediately if the client reports pain, dizziness, or discomfort.

## 1.0 Massage Medium Selection

Select the appropriate oil or cream based on:
- Client's skin type
- Treatment goals (relaxation vs. deep tissue)
- Allergies (check consultation card)
- Duration of massage

Apply a small amount to hands; warm by rubbing palms together before applying to client.

## 2.0 The Five Classical Massage Movements (Lima Pergerakan Urutan Klasik)

### 2.1 Effleurage (Gelinciran)
- **Hand Position:** Whole palm flat on skin; fingers together; thumbs slightly apart. Fingertips lead on smaller areas (neck, forearm).
- **Technique:** Long, continuous gliding strokes following the contour of the body.
- **Direction:** Always **towards the heart** (centripetal) to aid venous return; return stroke is lighter and off-body or feather-light.
- **Pressure:** Light to medium (feathering to firm glide).
- **Effects:** Warms superficial tissue, spreads massage medium evenly, initiates relaxation response (parasympathetic activation), improves venous and lymphatic return.
- **Uses:** Opening and closing every body region; linking stroke between deeper techniques.

### 2.2 Petrissage (Uli-uli / Ramas)
- **Hand Position:** Both hands alternate; lift and compress muscle bulk between thumb and fingers (picking up); or use both palms to wring tissue in opposite directions.
- **Sub-types:** *Picking up* (lift and squeeze), *wringing* (alternate hand pull), *rolling* (lift and roll tissue between fingers), *squeezing* (compress and release).
- **Pressure:** Medium to firm; work within client's comfort range.
- **Effects:** Increases local arterial and venous blood flow, promotes lymphatic drainage, breaks down minor adhesions, releases muscle tension, improves tissue extensibility (keanjalan tisu).
- **Uses:** Major muscle groups — trapezius, latissimus dorsi, gluteals, quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius.

### 2.3 Tapotement / Percussion (Ketukan)
- **Hand Position:** Varies by sub-type — see table below.
- **Technique:** Rhythmic, bouncing, alternating hand strikes. Wrists stay loose; movement originates from the wrist, not the elbow.

| Sub-type | Hand Position | Suitable Area |
|----------|--------------|---------------|
| Hacking (*mencincang*) | Ulnar border of loosely held hands, alternating | Back, thighs, calves |
| Cupping (*cawan*) | Cupped palm creating air pocket | Back, buttocks |
| Beating (*pukul*) | Loose fist, ulnar side | Large fleshy areas (buttocks, thighs) |
| Pounding (*tumbuk lembut*) | Closed fist, knuckle side | Gluteals only |
| Tapping (*ketuk jari*) | Fingertips only, light | Scalp, face, delicate areas |

- **Pressure:** Light to medium; always rhythmic and even.
- **Effects:** Stimulates cutaneous nerve endings, increases local circulation, tones superficial muscles, invigorates and energises.
- **Avoid:** Bony prominences, spine, kidney area (posterior ribs), varicose veins, recent injuries.

### 2.4 Vibration (Getaran)
- **Hand Position:** Fingertips or flat palm in contact with skin; arm muscles create rapid micro-tremors transmitted to tissue.
- **Types:** *Static vibration* (hold position; vibrate continuously for 5-10 sec) or *running vibration* (move along nerve pathway while vibrating).
- **Pressure:** Light — the effect is neurological, not mechanical.
- **Effects:** Soothes peripheral nerve endings, relieves localised pain, stimulates sluggish circulation, aids visceral peristalsis (abdomen).
- **Uses:** Along nerve pathways (sciatic, ulnar), solar plexus (abdomen), tight paraspinal muscles.

### 2.5 Friction (Geseran)
- **Hand Position:** Thumb pad, fingertip pads, or knuckles. Tissue is *not* glided over — the skin and therapist contact point move together over the underlying structure.
- **Technique:** Deep, concentrated circular (around joints) or cross-fibre (across muscle belly) movements. No additional medium — or wipe medium away first.
- **Pressure:** Deep and focused; build gradually.
- **Effects:** Breaks down fibrous adhesions and scar tissue, increases localised hyperaemia (increased blood flow), promotes tissue remodelling, loosens tight joint capsules.
- **Uses:** Around knee/elbow/shoulder joints, along tendon insertions, on trigger points and tension nodules (*nodul ketegangan*).

## 3.0 Body Mechanics (Mekanik Badan Terapis)

Proper body mechanics prevent therapist injury and ensure effective massage delivery:

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Stance** | Feet shoulder-width apart; one foot forward (walk stance) or parallel (horse stance) |
| **Knees** | Slightly bent; never locked |
| **Back** | Straight; lean from the hips, not the waist |
| **Shoulders** | Relaxed; not raised or hunched |
| **Arms** | Elbows slightly bent; wrists neutral (not flexed or extended) |
| **Weight transfer** | Use body weight, not arm strength, to apply pressure |
| **Couch height** | Adjusted so therapist can work with straight back and relaxed shoulders |

## 4.0 Rhythm, Rate, Pressure and Continuity

| Element | Definition | Guidelines |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| **Rhythm** (*irama*) | Regularity and pattern of movements | Consistent, flowing; adjust to treatment goal (slow for relaxation, brisk for stimulation) |
| **Rate** (*kadar*) | Speed of movements | Slow for relaxation; faster for stimulation/tapotement |
| **Pressure** (*tekanan*) | Force applied to the tissue | Light for relaxation/effleurage; medium for petrissage; deep for friction. Always check with client. |
| **Continuity** (*kesinambungan*) | Maintaining unbroken contact | Hands should not lose contact with the client during the massage. Use one hand to maintain contact when re-applying medium. |

## 5.0 Massage Flow Sequence (Full Body)

A standard full-body massage flow:

**Prone (face down) — approximately 30 minutes:**
1. Back — effleurage, petrissage, tapotement, friction on tension areas
2. Gluteals (if appropriate) — petrissage, tapotement
3. Posterior legs (right then left) — effleurage, petrissage, tapotement
4. Feet (posterior)

**Turn client to supine (face up) — approximately 30 minutes:**
5. Anterior legs (right then left) — effleurage, petrissage
6. Feet (anterior/plantar)
7. Arms and hands (right then left) — effleurage, petrissage
8. Abdomen (if appropriate) — gentle clockwise effleurage
9. Chest/decollete (if appropriate) — light effleurage
10. Neck and shoulders — effleurage, petrissage, friction
11. Scalp (optional)

End with slow effleurage strokes to signal completion.

---

## 6.0 Monitoring Client Response

Throughout the massage:
- Check pressure: "Is the pressure comfortable?"
- Observe for: facial grimacing, muscle guarding, verbal cues of discomfort.
- Adjust technique based on client feedback.
- Monitor for adverse reactions (dizziness, nausea, emotional response).
- Respond to any discomfort immediately.

---

## 7.0 Post-Massage Procedures

7.1 Remove excess massage medium with warm towels.
7.2 Allow client to rest for 2-3 minutes.
7.3 Assist client to sit up slowly.
7.4 Offer water.
7.5 Usher client to change into own attire.
7.6 Return personal belongings.
7.7 Record massage results on consultation card.

## 8.0 Relevant Anatomy for Manual Body Massage (Anatomi Berkaitan)

### 8.1 Major Muscle Groups by Body Region

| Body Region | Key Muscles (*Otot Utama*) | Massage Technique Focus |
|-------------|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Back | Trapezius, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae, rhomboids | Effleurage, petrissage, friction (paraspinal) |
| Shoulders/Neck | Upper trapezius, levator scapulae, sternocleidomastoid | Petrissage, friction, vibration |
| Gluteals | Gluteus maximus, medius, minimus | Petrissage, tapotement |
| Posterior thigh | Hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) | Effleurage, petrissage |
| Anterior thigh | Quadriceps femoris (rectus femoris, vastus group) | Effleurage, petrissage |
| Lower leg | Gastrocnemius, soleus (*betis*) | Effleurage, petrissage, friction |
| Abdomen | Rectus abdominis, external/internal obliques | Gentle clockwise effleurage only |
| Arms | Biceps brachii, triceps brachii, forearm flexors/extensors | Effleurage, petrissage |

### 8.2 Physiological Effects of Massage

| System (*Sistem*) | Effect of Massage |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Circulatory (*peredaran darah*) | Dilates superficial capillaries (hyperaemia); increases venous return; reduces blood pressure |
| Lymphatic (*limfatik*) | Stimulates lymph flow; reduces oedema; supports immune function |
| Muscular (*otot*) | Reduces muscle tension and fatigue; improves extensibility; breaks down lactic acid accumulation |
| Nervous (*saraf*) | Activates parasympathetic response (rest-and-digest); reduces cortisol and adrenaline; releases endorphins |
| Integumentary (*integumen*) | Improves skin elasticity; increases sebaceous activity; removes dead epidermal cells |
| Skeletal (*rangka*) | Improves range of motion at joints; reduces periarticular stiffness |

### 8.3 Key Anatomical Landmarks to Avoid or Work Around

| Landmark | Location | Action |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Popliteal fossa (*lekuk lutut*) | Back of knee | Avoid direct compression — major vessels present |
| Femoral triangle | Upper inner thigh | Avoid deep pressure — femoral artery and nerve |
| Axilla (*ketiak*) | Armpit | Avoid deep pressure — brachial plexus, axillary vessels |
| Spine / vertebral column | Midline back | Never apply direct pressure on spinous processes |
| Kidney region | Posterior ribs, flank | Avoid tapotement directly over kidneys |
| Carotid triangle | Lateral neck | Avoid compression — carotid artery, jugular vein, vagus nerve |
