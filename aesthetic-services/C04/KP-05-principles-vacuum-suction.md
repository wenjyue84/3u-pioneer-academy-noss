## Objectives
1. Explain the principles of vacuum suction therapy.
2. Describe the procedure and precautions for body vacuum suction.

---

## 1.0 Principles of Vacuum Suction

**Vacuum suction** (*sedutan vakum*) uses negative pressure (suction) to lift tissue, stimulating lymphatic drainage and blood circulation.

### 1.1 Effects
| Effect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Lymphatic drainage | Moves lymph fluid; reduces oedema |
| Improved circulation | Increased blood flow to treatment area |
| Cellulite reduction | Breaks down fatty deposits |
| Skin stimulation | Brings nutrients to skin surface |
| Detoxification | Waste product removal via lymphatic system |

---

## 2.0 Equipment

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Vacuum suction machine | Adjustable suction pressure |
| Ventouses/cups (*ventous/cawan*) | Various sizes for different body areas |
| Suction tubing | Connects ventouse to machine |
| Massage medium (oil/cream) | Applied to skin for smooth gliding |

### 2.1 Treatment Parameters
| Parameter | Body Treatment |
|-----------|---------------|
| Suction pressure | 20-40% (body); start low, increase gradually |
| Ventouse size | Large (back, thighs), medium (arms, abdomen) |
| Direction | Towards nearest lymph node |
| Duration | 15-20 minutes per area |
| Technique | Gliding (continuous strokes) or pulsing (cup on/off) |

---

## 3.0 Lymphatic Drainage Direction Guide

Vacuum suction strokes must always be directed towards the nearest lymph node group:

| Body Area | Direction of Stroke | Nearest Lymph Node Group |
|-----------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Upper thigh / buttock | Upward and lateral | Inguinal nodes (groin) |
| Lower leg / calf | Upward toward knee | Popliteal nodes (back of knee) |
| Abdomen | Circular clockwise then outward | Inguinal nodes |
| Upper arm | Upward toward axilla | Axillary nodes (armpit) |
| Back | Toward axilla (upper) or inguinal (lower) | Axillary / inguinal |

---

## 4.0 Precautions

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Vacuum suction must NEVER be used over varicose veins (*vena varikoz*) — suction pressure risks rupturing the vein wall, causing internal haemorrhage. Similarly, never hold the cup stationary on skin — a stationary cup concentrates suction and causes deep bruising (petechiae) within seconds.

- Never use on bruised (*lebam*), broken, or fragile skin.
- Do not use over varicose veins, telangiectasia, moles, or bony prominences.
- Always test on forearm first to establish client's comfortable pressure level.
- Release suction before lifting cup (press vent hole or tilt cup edge — do not pull directly off skin).
- Do not hold cup stationary at any point during treatment.
- Monitor client for discomfort; adjust suction pressure if client reports pain.
- **Contraindications specific to vacuum suction:** Bruising tendency, anticoagulant medication, fragile/thin skin, varicose veins over treatment area, recent lymph node surgery.
