## 1. BEV Maintenance Safety (Keselamatan Penyelenggaraan BEV)

### 1.1 Safety Requirements for High Voltage Systems

BEV maintenance differs fundamentally from ICE vehicle maintenance due to the presence of high voltage (HV) systems. The HV system in a typical BEV operates between 200V DC and 800V DC — sufficient to cause fatal electrocution.

Under the **Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (Act 514)**, employers and technicians have a duty of care to ensure safe working practices at all times. The **Electricity Supply Act 1990 (Act 447)** further governs safe work on electrical systems above 60V DC / 30V AC.

**Required PPE for BEV HV Maintenance (Peralatan Perlindungan Diri):**

| PPE Item | Specification | Purpose |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| Insulated gloves (Sarung tangan bertebat) | Class 0, rated 1000V AC / 1500V DC (IEC 60900) | Electric shock protection during HV work |
| Leather over-gloves (Sarung tangan kulit pelindung) | Per manufacturer specification | Physical protection over insulated gloves |
| Safety glasses (Cermin mata keselamatan) | MS ISO 16321 / ANSI Z87.1 | Eye protection from arc flash and chemical splash |
| Dielectric footwear (Kasut keselamatan dielektrik) | DI rated | Electrical insulation from ground |
| Safety helmet (Topi keselamatan) | MS 340 or equivalent | Head protection when working under vehicle |

**Key safety rules for BEV maintenance:**

1. **Never assume HV is disconnected.** Always verify using a CAT III-1000V rated multimeter (complying with IEC 61010-1 / MS IEC 61010-1) before touching any orange-coloured cable or component.
2. **Follow the "Lock-Out / Tag-Out" / Kunci-Keluar/Tag-Keluar (LOTO) procedure** when performing any work that requires de-energising the HV system.
3. **Wait the prescribed discharge time** after HV disconnection (typically 5-10 minutes, per manufacturer's manual) before proceeding. HV capacitors in the inverter and motor controller retain lethal charge after system shutdown.
4. **Two-person rule:** HV work should always be performed with a second qualified person present who can provide emergency assistance.
5. **No metallic jewellery:** Remove all rings, watches, bracelets, and necklaces before working on or near HV components.

### 1.2 Condition of BEV High Voltage Components

| Component | What to Check | Danger |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| HV cables (orange) | Insulation integrity, routing, chafing | Electric shock, arc flash |
| HV battery pack | Housing integrity, leak signs, swelling | Thermal runaway, toxic gas release |
| HV connectors | Corrosion, moisture, proper seating | Short circuit, intermittent faults |
| Interlock/service plug | Physical condition, proper engagement | Unintended HV energisation |

## 2. Types of BEV and Specification (Jenis dan Spesifikasi BEV)

### 2.1 BEV Manufacturers and Models

The Malaysian market includes BEVs from multiple manufacturers. Common categories:

| Category | Examples | Typical Battery Voltage | Battery Capacity Range |
|----------|----------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Compact BEV | BYD Dolphin, Neta V, ORA Good Cat | 350-400V | 30-60 kWh |
| Mid-size BEV | Tesla Model 3, BYD Seal, Hyundai Ioniq 5 | 400-800V | 60-80 kWh |
| Premium BEV | Mercedes EQS, BMW iX, Volvo EX90 | 400-800V | 80-120 kWh |
| Commercial BEV | BYD e6, Maxus eDeliver | 350-400V | 50-80 kWh |

### 2.2 Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) — Nombor Pengenalan Kenderaan

The VIN is a unique 17-character code that identifies every vehicle. For BEV maintenance, the VIN is critical for:
- Confirming exact model and variant
- Identifying correct battery pack specification
- Accessing correct service manual procedures
- Ordering correct replacement parts

**VIN location:** Typically found on:
- Dashboard (visible through windscreen, driver side)
- Door jamb sticker (driver side B-pillar)
- Vehicle registration card (VOC / Geran)
- Diagnostic scan tool readout

### 2.3 Component Specifications

Key specifications a technician must identify before maintenance:

| Specification | Where to Find | Why Important |
|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| Battery type and capacity | VIN decode / door sticker | Determines service procedures |
| Battery voltage (nominal) | Service manual | Determines PPE and tool requirements |
| Coolant type | Service manual / reservoir cap | Using wrong coolant damages seals and HV insulation |
| Motor type (PSM/ASM) | Service manual | Affects diagnostic parameters |
| Charging capability (AC/DC) | Specifications plate | Determines charging system checks |

## 3. BEV Scheduled Maintenance Checklist (Senarai Semak Penyelenggaraan Berjadual BEV)

### 3.1 Types of Maintenance Items

BEV scheduled maintenance differs significantly from ICE vehicles. There is no engine oil, oil filter, spark plug, or transmission fluid to service. The key maintenance items for BEVs are:

| Maintenance Item | Interval (Typical) | Activity Type |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Cabin air filter | 12 months / 15,000 km | Replace |
| Brake fluid | 24 months | Replace |
| HV battery coolant | 48-72 months (varies) | Replace |
| Wiper blades | 12 months | Replace (if worn) |
| 12V auxiliary battery | 36-48 months | Replace (condition-based) |
| Brake pads | Condition-based (last longer on BEV due to regen braking) | Inspect / Replace |
| Tyre rotation | 10,000-15,000 km | Rotate |
| Tyre replacement | Condition-based | Replace |
| HV system insulation check | Every service | Test |
| Software update check | Every service | Update if available |
| Desiccant replacement (battery pack) | Per manufacturer (if applicable) | Replace |

### 3.2 Types of Maintenance Activities

Maintenance activities fall into three categories:

1. **Inspect (Periksa):** Visual and diagnostic checks without disassembly. Example: inspecting HV cable routing, checking coolant level, reviewing diagnostic fault codes.

2. **Top-up (Tambah):** Adding fluids to specified levels. Example: topping up windscreen washer fluid, brake fluid, HV coolant (only with manufacturer-specified fluid type).

3. **Replace (Ganti):** Removing worn/expired components and fitting new parts. Example: replacing cabin air filter, brake fluid flush and replace, 12V auxiliary battery replacement.

## 4. BEV Maintenance Diagnostic Tools and Equipment

### 4.1 Insulated Tools (Alatan Bertebat)

For any maintenance task that involves proximity to HV components (even if not directly working on them), insulated tools must be used. The insulated tool set should include:

- Insulated ratchet and socket set (6-24mm)
- Insulated combination spanners (8-19mm)
- Insulated screwdriver set (flat blade and Phillips)
- Insulated torque wrench
- Insulated pliers (combination, long-nose, side-cutting)
- Insulated cable knife

**Tool inspection:** Before each use, verify:
- No cuts, cracks, or damage to insulation
- Inner insulation layer not exposed
- Tool markings (IEC 60900, Class 0, 1000V) are legible

### 4.2 Common Tools (Alatan Biasa)

For non-HV components (e.g., brake pads, cabin filter, wiper blades):
- Standard socket set
- Screwdriver set
- Brake bleeding kit
- Jack and jack stands (rated for BEV weight — BEVs are typically 200-400 kg heavier than equivalent ICE vehicles)
- Torque wrench

**CAUTION:** When using a vehicle lift or jack, always position at manufacturer-specified lift points. BEV battery packs are located on the vehicle underbody — incorrect placement can damage the battery housing.

### 4.3 Equipment

| Equipment | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| Diagnostic scan tool | System health check, fault code reading, software updates |
| Battery tester (12V) | 12V auxiliary battery condition assessment |
| Brake fluid tester | Brake fluid moisture content measurement |
| Coolant refractometer | Coolant concentration verification |
| Tyre pressure gauge (digital) | Tyre pressure measurement |
| Vehicle lift (2-post or 4-post) | Underbody access, rated for BEV weight |

### 4.4 Dedicated BEV Work Bay Requirements

The BEV work bay for scheduled maintenance must include:
- Anti-static flooring with ESD-safe properties
- Vehicle lift rated for BEV weight (minimum 3,000 kg for passenger BEV)
- Class D fire extinguisher within 5 metres
- Electric rescue hook mounted within reach
- HV warning signage displayed prominently
- Barrier stanchions available for HV work isolation

## 5. BEV Maintenance Activities (Aktiviti Penyelenggaraan BEV)

### 5.1 Inspect Activities (Aktiviti Pemeriksaan)

**HV System Visual Inspection:**
- Check all orange HV cables for damage, chafing, or discoloration
- Inspect HV connector integrity and locking mechanisms
- Look for signs of coolant leaks around battery pack and cooling components
- Check battery pack underbody shield for damage

**Brake System Inspection:**
- Measure brake pad thickness (BEV brake pads last significantly longer due to regenerative braking — 80,000-100,000 km is common)
- Inspect brake discs for scoring, corrosion, or minimum thickness
- Check brake caliper operation and slide pin movement

**Suspension and Steering:**
- Inspect shock absorbers for leaks
- Check CV joint boots for damage
- Inspect tie rod ends and ball joints for play
- Check wheel bearings for noise or play

### 5.2 Top-Up Activities (Aktiviti Tambahan)

| Fluid | Specification | Checking Method | Top-Up Procedure |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Windscreen washer | Water + washer concentrate | Visual level check | Fill to MAX line |
| Brake fluid | DOT 4 (or per manufacturer) | Dipstick / visual | Fill to MAX, check for system leaks if low |
| HV coolant | Manufacturer-specific (e.g., Glysantin G40, BEV-specific coolant) | Visual level check at expansion tank | **CAUTION:** Only top-up with exact manufacturer specification. Do NOT mix coolant types. Some BEV coolants are non-conductive formulations critical for HV insulation. |

### 5.3 Replace Activities (Aktiviti Penggantian)

**Cabin Air Filter Replacement:**
1. Locate cabin air filter housing (typically behind glove box)
2. Remove housing cover
3. Extract old filter, noting airflow direction
4. Install new filter with correct airflow orientation
5. Refit housing cover

**12V Auxiliary Battery Replacement:**
1. Locate 12V battery (location varies: front trunk, rear trunk, under rear seat)
2. **IMPORTANT:** Do NOT disconnect 12V battery while vehicle is in "READY" mode
3. Ensure vehicle is powered off and key removed
4. Disconnect negative terminal first, then positive
5. Remove battery hold-down clamp
6. Remove old battery and install new battery (same type and specification)
7. Connect positive terminal first, then negative
8. Verify 12V system function — dashboard lights, central locking, etc.

**Note:** Disconnecting the 12V battery in a BEV may require a system reset or re-calibration through the diagnostic scan tool after reconnection. Always consult the manufacturer's service manual.
