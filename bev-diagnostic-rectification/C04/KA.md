### Bahagian B / Section B — Soalan Senario Bermarkah / Graded Scenario Questions (5 markah / marks)

*Jawab SEMUA soalan. Setiap soalan bernilai 1 markah. / Answer ALL questions. Each question carries 1 mark.*

**Q21.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A BEV arrives with fault code P0AC4 (Inverter Over-Temperature). Scan tool shows inverter inlet coolant temperature at 95°C (normal: ≤75°C) and coolant flow rate 20% below specification. Describe your diagnostic response and the required rectification steps. [CoCU C04.2.4 / C04.2.9 — Inverter Diagnosis and Coolant Refill]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Record fault code and freeze-frame data (inlet temp, flow rate). Inspect coolant level in the drive train cooling circuit — if low, identify leak source before adding coolant. Check coolant pump operation with scan tool actuator test; a pump delivering 20% below spec indicates partial blockage or pump wear. Inspect drive train cooling circuit for kinked hoses or blocked radiator fins. If pump is confirmed defective, initiate pump replacement. If coolant level was low, top up with manufacturer-specified coolant, bleed circuit, and road test to verify coolant temperature returns to normal operating range (≤75°C).

**Q22.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] During traction motor replacement, a technician observes that the motor resolver connector shows pin corrosion and the resolver signal was the root cause of the original fault. After installing the new motor, explain what electrical verification steps must be completed before the vehicle is road tested. [CoCU C04.3.4 / C04.3.10 — Motor Diagnostic and Functionality Test]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* After motor installation, connect the resolver harness and inspect the replacement connector for correct pin engagement. Using the scan tool, enter motor calibration mode and perform resolver offset calibration — the resolver offset value must be within ±10° of manufacturer specification. Read motor control live data: verify resolver signal is stable (no "implausible signal" flag), motor temperature sensor reads ambient temperature, and insulation resistance between motor windings and chassis exceeds 1 MΩ. Cycle READY and verify no motor fault codes before conducting a low-speed workshop function test (motor spins in forward and reverse) followed by a supervised road test.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A BEV complains of a grinding noise from the front axle area during low-speed manoeuvring that disappears above 40 km/h. Scan tool shows no active fault codes. Describe your diagnostic approach to determine if the motorised transaxle is the source. [CoCU C04.4.3 / C04.4.4 — Transaxle Inspection and Diagnostic Test]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Low-speed grinding that disappears at higher speeds suggests worn planetary or helical gear teeth damaged at the low-torque/low-speed mesh zone, or a worn CV joint. Raise the vehicle and manually rotate the front wheels — feel for roughness or pulsation that indicates bearing damage. Inspect the transaxle for lubricant leaks at shaft seals and check lubricant level and condition through the fill plug. Using the scan tool, review drive train vibration data and check for any previously cleared NVH-related fault codes. If manual rotation confirms rough bearing feel and lubricant is contaminated with metal particles, initiate motorised transaxle replacement per CoCU C04.4.8.

**Q24.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A BEV with 80,000 km arrives with a complaint that brake pedal feel is inconsistent — sometimes normal, sometimes requires more force. Fault codes show U0129 (Lost communication with brake control module). Describe the diagnostic approach for the integrated brake control system. [CoCU C04.5.3 / C04.5.4 — Brake Control Inspection and Diagnostic]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* U0129 indicates loss of CAN communication with the brake control module — the system may have fallen back to conventional friction braking only, explaining the inconsistent pedal feel. First, check 12V supply voltage and ground integrity at the brake control module connector; voltage below 11V will cause CAN dropouts. Inspect the CAN bus harness between the brake module and OBD gateway for damage or connector corrosion. Verify brake fluid level — low fluid can cause partial hydraulic circuit engagement. Measure brake pedal travel sensor voltage at rest and full travel; out-of-range values indicate sensor failure. If harness and supply are confirmed good, the brake control module is likely faulty and requires replacement per CoCU C04.5.6.

**Q25.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] During drive train workplace preparation, the Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) identifies "High-voltage shock from inverter capacitors" and "Musculoskeletal injury from heavy component handling" as Level 3 risks. Describe the control measures required for each hazard before work begins. [CoCU C04.1.4 / C04.1.2 — Hazard Identification and Safety Requirements]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* For HV shock: full HV de-energisation procedure including READY OFF, key to safety box, 12V disconnect, interlock removal, 5–10 minute capacitor discharge wait, and zero-energy verification with CAT III multimeter at minimum 3 HV points. Apply LOTO with tagged padlock. For heavy component handling: position vehicle on approved lift to correct working height; use transmission jack for inverter (15–25 kg) and hydraulic crane for traction motor (30–80 kg); ensure two-person team for components above 25 kg; clear floor of tripping hazards; confirm lifting equipment is within annual certification date per company SOP before commencing work.

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO — PENGIRAAN / CALCULATION] A BEV inverter has rated efficiency of 97.5% at full load. When the motor demands 120 kW output power at 80% of rated motor capacity, the inverter efficiency drops to 96.5%. Calculate: (a) The inverter input power from the battery at 80% load, and (b) The power loss (kW) in the inverter at 80% load. Show all working. [CoCU C04.2.3 — Inverter Component and Function]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:*
(a) Inverter output power at 80% load = 120 kW
Inverter efficiency at 80% load = 96.5% = 0.965
Inverter input power = Output power ÷ Efficiency = 120 ÷ 0.965 = **124.35 kW**

(b) Inverter power loss = Input power − Output power = 124.35 − 120 = **4.35 kW**

Note: This 4.35 kW of loss is dissipated as heat through the liquid cooling circuit. A coolant flow restriction or pump failure will cause rapid inverter temperature rise at sustained 80% load operation. [CoCU C04.2.7 / C04.2.9]

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO — PENGIRAAN / CALCULATION] A dual-motor AWD BEV has a front motor rated 120 Nm and a rear motor rated 260 Nm. At 80% total drive system capacity under dynamic torque vectoring, traction control requests a 60% rear / 40% front split to correct understeer. Calculate: (a) Total torque demand at 80% capacity, (b) Required rear and front motor torques, and (c) State whether each motor is within its rated limit. [CoCU C04.3.3 / C04.4.3 — Motor and Transaxle Functionality]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:*
(a) Combined rated torque = 120 + 260 = 380 Nm
80% capacity = 380 × 0.80 = **304 Nm total demand**

(b) Rear motor torque = 304 × 0.60 = **182.4 Nm**
Front motor torque = 304 × 0.40 = **121.6 Nm**

(c) Rear motor 182.4 Nm < rated 260 Nm → **within limit ✓**
Front motor 121.6 Nm > rated 120 Nm → **EXCEEDS rated limit ✗** — traction control must cap front motor at 120 Nm and distribute remaining 1.6 Nm to rear motor, giving rear = 184 Nm (still within 260 Nm limit ✓). A technician observing this calculation confirms why the rear motor bears greater torque load in AWD systems. [CoCU C04.4.3 / C04.4.10]

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] After completing a motorised transaxle replacement, the final QC inspection checklist requires you to verify lubricant type, fill level, and torque specifications. The vehicle is a 2023 BEV with an integrated single-speed transaxle. Describe the lubricant refill procedure and state what documentation must be completed. [CoCU C04.4.9 / C04.4.10 — Transaxle Lubricant Refill and Test]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Ensure vehicle is level on a lift at working height. Clean the fill plug area. Install the drain plug with a new seal washer; torque to manufacturer specification (typically 40–50 Nm). Fill through the fill plug hole using the specified lubricant (typically synthetic gear oil, e.g., API GL-5 or manufacturer-specific formulation) until lubricant just reaches the fill hole thread level. Install fill plug with new seal washer; torque to specification. Lower vehicle and inspect for leaks at drain and fill plugs. Record in the work order: lubricant type, part number, quantity added (litres), fill and drain plug torque readings, and disposal receipt for drained lubricant classified as SW305.

### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

*Soalan pengayaan untuk perbincangan berpandu. / Enrichment questions for guided discussion.*

**Q29.** Explain how Field-Oriented Control (FOC) in the inverter optimises motor torque and efficiency across the motor's operating speed range. [CoCU C04.2.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* FOC decouples motor current into torque-producing (Iq) and flux-producing (Id) components in a synchronous reference frame. At low speeds, maximum Id maintains full flux for maximum torque. As speed increases above base speed, flux weakening (reducing Id while increasing Iq) extends the operating range beyond rated speed while keeping inverter output voltage within limits. This allows the PSM to operate efficiently across the full 0–18,000 RPM range by continuously optimising the current vector angle — delivering maximum torque-per-ampere at all speeds and minimising copper losses.

**Q30.** A technician replaces a traction motor but does not perform resolver offset calibration. Explain the consequences and how they would manifest as customer complaints. [CoCU C04.3.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Without resolver calibration, the inverter miscalculates rotor position by a fixed angle offset. This causes the Field-Oriented Control algorithm to apply torque current at the wrong phase angle — the motor produces less than rated torque per ampere, increasing current draw and heating. Customer symptoms: reduced performance and acceleration; motor running hotter than normal (triggering OT warning); higher energy consumption; jerky low-speed torque delivery. In severe miscalibration (>20° offset), the motor may emit acoustic noise from torque ripple. The fault code "Motor Resolver Offset Out of Range" may appear after the first drive cycle.

**Q31.** Describe the complete procedure for verifying integrated brake control system function after replacement, including both the static workshop test and the dynamic road test requirements. [CoCU C04.5.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Static test: Connect scan tool; cycle READY; confirm no brake control fault codes; activate automated brake bleeding sequence via actuator test function; verify brake pedal travel sensor and master cylinder pressure sensor readings are within specification. Apply brake pedal with gradually increasing force and confirm master cylinder pressure responds linearly; verify iBooster assist engages correctly. Dynamic test: Drive at 20 km/h in a safe area; apply moderate braking and verify normal deceleration without pedal pulsation; verify regenerative braking contribution is visible on scan tool energy flow display; conduct emergency stop test at 40 km/h and verify ABS activation and straight-line stopping. Document all test results in the work order.

**Q32.** Explain why the same BEV inverter fault code (e.g., P0AC4 over-temperature) can be caused by three completely different root causes, and describe how to differentiate them using live data. [CoCU C04.2.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* P0AC4 threshold is exceeded whenever inverter junction temperature exceeds the safety limit — the code does not identify the cause. Three root causes: (1) Coolant flow restriction — live data shows low flow rate and high inlet temperature; (2) Coolant pump failure — scan tool actuator test fails to respond or shows zero flow; (3) Inverter internal IGBT degradation — flow rate and temperatures are normal but inverter draws higher current than expected at the same power output level, indicating increased conduction losses from aged IGBTs. Differentiation: compare inlet vs outlet temperature differential (should be 5–10°C; if outlet is not rising, pump is the issue), compare coolant flow rate against specification, and compare inverter current draw at known load against historical data.
