## Work Activity 5: BEV Integrated Brake Control System Replacement | **Reference:** C04/KP(5/5)

### S1 — Short Answer: a) Explain the two braking methods in a BEV and how they work together. b) How much range extension does regenerative braking typically provide?
### S2 — MCQ: The BEV electric brake booster operates on: A. Engine vacuum B. 12V electrical power C. HV electrical power D. Hydraulic pump
### S3 — Matching: Match component to function: 1. iBooster 2. Wheel speed sensors 3. Brake pedal sensor 4. ESC module (a. Measures driver brake input b. ABS/traction/stability control c. Electric brake booster d. Individual wheel speed for ABS)
### S4 — T/F: a) BEV brake pads wear faster than ICE vehicle brake pads. b) If the 12V system fails, the brake pedal will feel very hard. c) Brake system bleeding is required after hydraulic component replacement. d) Regenerative braking charges the 12V battery.
### S5 — Short Answer: Describe the brake system functionality test procedure after component replacement.
### S6 — Short Answer: List THREE situations where regenerative braking may be limited and explain why.

## Skema Jawapan
**S1:** a) Regenerative: motor acts as generator, converts kinetic to electrical energy. Friction: conventional hydraulic disc brakes. Integrated system blends both seamlessly. b) 15-30% range extension.
**S2:** B
**S3:** 1-c, 2-d, 3-a, 4-b
**S4:** a) FALSE — BEV pads last longer due to regen b) TRUE c) TRUE d) FALSE — charges the HV traction battery
**S5:** Connect scan tool, calibrate brake system, verify no fault codes, check pedal feel (firm, consistent), verify ABS/ESC lights off, road test at low speed (braking performance, regen response).
**S6:** Battery fully charged (no capacity to absorb energy), very cold temperatures (battery limits charging rate), motor fault or inverter fault (regen function disabled by BMS).
