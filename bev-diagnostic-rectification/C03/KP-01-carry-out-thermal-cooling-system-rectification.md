## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

1. State licensing, legislative, and regulatory requirements for thermal cooling system work

3. List tools and equipment for thermal cooling system diagnosis and rectification

5. Explain waste management and workplace preparation procedures

---

## 1. BEV Rules and Regulations
All regulatory requirements from C01/KP(1/3) and C02/KP(1/6) apply. Thermal cooling system work may involve both HV components (electric water pumps connected to HV circuits, electric compressors) and LV components.

---

## 2. Thermal Cooling System Safety, Health, and Environment

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Electric water pumps and electric compressors are powered by the HV system (up to 400V DC). Full HV de-energisation is required before servicing any cooling component connected to the HV circuit. Confirm zero-energy state before work commences. Refer to Manufacturer's Service Manual for HV isolation procedure.

### 2.1 Workplace Safety
- Anti-static flooring required (HV components in cooling circuit)
- Spill containment for coolant — BEV coolant may be non-conductive dielectric formulation
- Environmental control: coolant is scheduled waste (SW305); refrigerant handling requires certification

### 2.2 PPE
All standard BEV PPE applies. Additional for cooling system work:
- Chemical-resistant gloves for coolant handling (in addition to insulated gloves for HV work)
- Face shield when working with pressurised cooling systems
- Refrigerant handling certification for HVAC work (R1234yf or R134a)

### 2.3 Dielectric Coolant (Cecair Penyejuk Dielektrik) Awareness
BEV cooling circuits may use dielectric (non-conductive) coolant formulations. Key properties:
- **Non-conductive / Bukan-konduktif:** Prevents short circuits if coolant contacts HV components
- **Must not be mixed** with standard ethylene glycol coolant — mixing degrades dielectric properties
- Verify coolant specification from Manufacturer's Service Manual before topping up or replacing

## 3. Tools and Equipment

| Equipment | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Insulated tools (Class 0) | HV component access |
| Coolant refiller/suction tool | Coolant drainage and refill |
| Digital multimeter (CAT III) | Electrical measurements |
| Mega Ohm meter (CAT III) | Insulation resistance |
| Air conditioning refrigerant recycling machine (R1234yf/R134a) | Refrigerant recovery and recharge |
| Coolant pressure tester | Leak testing of cooling circuits |
| Thermal imaging camera (optional) | Hot spot detection |
| Common tools | General mechanical work |

---

## 4. Hazard Identification

| Hazard | Source | Control |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Electrical | Electric water pumps, electric compressor (HV-powered) | HV de-energisation |
| Chemical | Coolant (dielectric fluid), refrigerant (R1234yf — mildly flammable) | Ventilation, chemical PPE, spill kit |
| Pressure | Pressurised cooling circuits and refrigerant lines | Depressurise before opening |
| Thermal | Hot coolant, hot components after operation | Allow cooling period |
| Environmental | Refrigerant release (GWP), coolant disposal | Recovery machine, waste management |

---

## 5. Waste Management
- Spent coolant: SW305, sealed containers, licensed disposal
- Recovered refrigerant: must be recycled or disposed via certified facility
- Contaminated absorbent materials: SW410

---

## 6. Workplace Preparation Procedure
- Manpower arrangement: assign roles for cooling system work
- Equipment list layout: verify coolant tools, refrigerant machine, pressure tester
- Tool list and location verification
- Toolbox talk with emphasis on coolant handling and pressure hazards
- Assembly point and emergency escape route confirmation
