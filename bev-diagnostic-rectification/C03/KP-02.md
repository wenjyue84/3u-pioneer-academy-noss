## 1. BEV Cooling System Safety
- Safety precautions for liquid cooling: depressurise before opening any connection
- Coolant can be hot after vehicle operation — allow cooling period (minimum 30 minutes)
- BEV coolant may be dielectric (non-conductive) — verify type before mixing
- Electric water pumps are HV-powered in some BEVs — HV de-energisation may be required

---

## 2. BEV Cooling System Overview

BEVs typically have multiple cooling circuits (unlike ICE vehicles with one primary circuit):

| Circuit | Components Cooled | Temperature Range |
|---------|------------------|-------------------|
| Battery cooling | Traction battery pack | 15-35 deg C (precisely controlled) |
| Powertrain cooling | Motor, inverter, DC-DC converter | Up to 65 deg C |
| Cabin cooling/heating | HVAC system for occupants | Per climate demand |
| Charger cooling | OBC during charging | Up to 55 deg C |

Some circuits may share a common coolant loop; others are fully independent. Refer to manufacturer's cooling system diagram.

## 3. Electric Water Pumps (Pam Air Elektrik)

### 3.1 Function
Electric water pumps replace the mechanical (belt-driven) water pump found in ICE vehicles. They circulate coolant through the cooling circuits. Advantages:
- Operate independently of motor speed (variable flow rate)
- Can operate when vehicle is stationary (during charging, thermal pre-conditioning)
- Controlled by the Thermal Management ECU for optimal temperature regulation

### 3.2 Types
- **LV water pumps (12V):** Lower power, used for cabin circuits and auxiliary cooling
- **HV water pumps:** Higher power, used for battery and powertrain circuits (HV de-energisation required for service)

### 3.3 Checking Procedure
1. Connect diagnostic scan tool
2. Run active test: activate each water pump
3. Listen for pump operation — should be smooth, no grinding or cavitation noise
4. Check flow rate if diagnostic tool provides this data
5. Measure pump current draw — compare to specification
6. Visual inspection for leaks at pump housing and connections

## 4. Cooling System Inspection

### 4.1 Leakage Inspection
- Visual: check all hose connections, fittings, heat exchangers, pumps for wet spots or staining
- Pressure test: connect coolant pressure tester to expansion tank, pressurise to specification (typically 1.0-1.5 bar), observe pressure decay over 10 minutes. Any pressure drop indicates a leak.
- UV dye: some manufacturers recommend adding UV-reactive dye to coolant and using a UV lamp to locate leaks

### 4.2 Corrosion Inspection
- Check coolant condition: colour, clarity, pH (using test strips)
- Degraded coolant appears brown/dark and may have particulate matter
- Check metal components (heat exchangers, fittings) for green/white corrosion deposits
- Corrosion indicates coolant has degraded and requires replacement

### 4.3 Clogging Inspection
- Restricted flow indicated by: higher-than-normal temperatures, reduced flow rate reading on diagnostic tool, noisy pump (cavitation)
- May require cooling circuit flush to remove blockages
- Check coolant filter (if equipped) for contamination

## 5. Faulty Cooling System Component Replacement

Common replaceable components:
- Electric water pump (LV or HV)
- Coolant hoses and fittings
- Expansion tank and cap
- Heat exchanger / radiator
- Thermostat valve (electronic)
- Coolant temperature sensor

### Replacement Procedure (General)
1. De-energise HV system if the component is HV-connected
2. Drain coolant from the relevant circuit
3. Disconnect electrical connectors
4. Remove hose connections
5. Remove mounting hardware
6. Install new component
7. Reconnect hoses, electrical connectors, mounting hardware
8. Refill coolant and bleed air
9. Test system functionality

---

## 6. Cooling System Functionality Test

1. Refill coolant to specified level
2. Bleed air from all circuits per manufacturer procedure
3. Run each water pump via diagnostic tool active test — verify operation
4. Start vehicle in "READY" mode
5. Monitor coolant temperatures across all circuits using diagnostic tool
6. Verify temperatures stabilise within specification under various conditions:
   - Idle: battery temperature should maintain 20-30 deg C
   - Simulated load (if available): temperatures should not exceed limits
7. Pressure test after filling — verify no leaks
8. Verify no diagnostic fault codes related to cooling system
9. Record all results
