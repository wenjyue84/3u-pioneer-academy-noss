## Question 4 (10 marks)

**System State:** A BEV is being DC fast-charged at a public charger. The Thermal Management ECU activates the battery cooling circuit. Battery temperature rises to 45 °C (manufacturer limit: 40 °C). Fault code **P0A93 — High Voltage Battery Pack Temperature** is stored. The charger automatically reduces charging rate to 20% of maximum.

**Symptoms:** Battery over-temperature MIL active; charging slower than expected; electric water pump runs continuously.

**Required:** (a) List FOUR (4) possible causes of the battery cooling failure in the liquid cooling circuit. *(4 marks)* (b) Describe the FIRST FOUR (4) diagnostic steps a technician should perform, in correct sequence. *(6 marks)*

## Question 5 (10 marks)

**System State:** Ambient temperature 35 °C. Driver operates HVAC on maximum cooling (set: 18 °C). Refrigerant high-side pressure reads 28 bar; low-side reads 1.2 bar. Normal high-side should be 16–22 bar at this ambient temperature.

**Symptoms:** Cabin vent temperature 24 °C (should be 5–10 °C); compressor makes clicking/cycling noise every 30 seconds; no refrigerant leaks visible.

**Required:** (a) Interpret the abnormal refrigerant pressures and identify the MOST LIKELY cause. *(4 marks)* (b) Describe the diagnostic procedure to confirm your diagnosis and state the corrective action. *(6 marks)*

## SKEMA PEMARKAHAN / MARKING SCHEME

### Question 1 (10 marks — 3–4 marks per circuit; deduct for incomplete descriptions)

| Circuit | Components | Temperature Range |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| Battery cooling | Traction battery pack | 15–35 °C (precisely controlled) |
| Powertrain cooling | Motor, inverter, DC-DC converter | Up to 65 °C |
| HVAC (cabin) | Condenser, evaporator, PTC heater | Per climate demand |

**Rubric per circuit:** 4 = name + components + temp range correct; 3 = two elements correct; 2 = one element correct; 1 = vague answer.

### Question 2 (10 marks — 2 marks each)

(a) Electric water pump: circulates coolant independently of motor speed; can run stationary; **HV-powered** (battery/powertrain circuit) or **LV** (auxiliary).
(b) PTC heater: electric resistance cabin heater, self-regulating, **HV-powered**, 3–8 kW.
(c) 3-way valve: directs coolant between circuits; **LV** controlled by Thermal ECU.
(d) Electric compressor: compresses refrigerant for HVAC cooling; **HV-powered**.
(e) EXV: regulates refrigerant flow/pressure at evaporator inlet; **LV** (electronically controlled).

### Question 3 (10 marks)

**(a) 6 marks:** Dielectric coolant prevents electrical short-circuit if coolant contacts HV components (2m). Mixing consequences: degraded dielectric properties leading to electrical conductivity risk (2m); possible corrosion/precipitation/gel from incompatible inhibitor packages (2m).
**(b) 4 marks:** R1234yf = A2L (mildly flammable, low toxicity) (2m). Handling requirement: store away from ignition sources / use approved refrigerant recovery machine rated for A2L / no open flames in work area (2m).

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a) 4 marks — 1 mark each:** Water pump failure; clogged coolant passage/heat exchanger; low coolant level (leak); thermostat/3-way valve stuck; degraded coolant (reduced thermal transfer).
**(b) 6 marks — 1.5 marks each step:** (1) Read and record all fault codes; (2) Check coolant level in expansion tank; (3) Perform active test on battery cooling pump — verify current and flow; (4) Pressure test cooling circuit for leaks.

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a) 4 marks:** High-side 28 bar = significantly above normal (16–22 bar) → over-charged system or blocked condenser (2m). Low-side 1.2 bar = normal/slightly low. Most likely: refrigerant overcharge OR condenser airflow blockage causing excessive head pressure (2m).
**(b) 6 marks:** Confirm condenser fan operation (1m); inspect condenser for blockage (leaves, debris) (1m); if condenser clear, measure refrigerant charge vs. manufacturer specification by weight (2m); if overcharged, recover excess refrigerant using recovery machine until correct charge weight achieved (2m).

### Question 6 (10 marks)

**(a) 3 marks — 1 each):** Coolant hose connection/fitting; expansion tank cap seal; heat exchanger (radiator) pinhole; water pump housing seal; coolant temperature sensor O-ring.
**(b) 7 marks:** Connect pressure tester to expansion tank neck (1m); pressurise to manufacturer specification — typically 1.0–1.5 bar (2m); observe pressure gauge for 10 minutes (1m); pressure drop indicates leak present (1m); inspect all hoses, fittings, pump, heat exchangers systematically while pressurised (2m); UV dye can supplement if leak not visible.

### Question 7 (8 marks — 2–3 marks each)

(a) **Pump itself:** Disconnect pump connector → measure resistance across pump motor terminals; out-of-spec = pump failed internally (3m).
(b) **Drive circuit:** With connector disconnected, measure voltage at supply terminal with pump commanded ON; 0V = blown fuse or open circuit wiring (3m).
(c) **Thermal ECU:** If supply voltage present and pump resistance in spec but no rotation → pump seized; if no command signal output → ECU fault; verify ECU output with oscilloscope or scan tool live data (2m).

### Question 8 (8 marks)

Refill coolant to MAX level (1m) → bleed air per manufacturer procedure (gravity fill or vacuum fill tool) (2m) → connect scan tool and run active test on water pump; verify current 4–8A and flow audible (2m) → start vehicle in READY mode; monitor coolant temperatures via scan tool (1m) → battery circuit temperature should stabilise at 20–30 °C at idle (1m) → verify no pressure decay on pressure test and no fault codes (1m).

### Question 9 (4 marks)

(a) Equipment: refrigerant recycling/recovery machine rated for R1234yf (1m).
(b) Reason: R1234yf is a greenhouse gas; venting is prohibited under environmental regulations; must be recovered for reuse or certified disposal (1m).
(c) HV safety: perform full HV de-energisation and confirm zero-energy state before disconnecting any compressor electrical connector or refrigerant lines — compressor drive operates at HV (up to 400V DC) (2m).

### Question 10 (10 marks)

**(a) 4 marks:** Prioritise P0AAD (pump circuit low) first — it is a root cause that directly leads to P0A93 (over-temp) (2m). B1678 (pressure sensor) is separate HVAC issue (1m). Correct prioritisation order: P0AAD → P0A93 (related) → B1678 (independent) (1m).
**(b) 6 marks:** Diagnose P0AAD first: active test battery pump, check fuse and wiring (2m); if pump fault confirmed and repaired, re-scan — if P0A93 clears, faults were related (2m); diagnose B1678 independently: check refrigerant pressure sensor wiring, connector, and sensor output voltage (2m).

### Question 11 (10 marks)

**(a) 3 marks — 1 each:** Degraded coolant (reduced thermal conductivity after years of service); internal clogging of battery heat exchanger (scale/particulate buildup); reduced electric water pump performance (wear, reduced flow rate).
**(b) 7 marks:** Coolant condition: check colour (should be clean, specified colour), pH with test strips (2m); clogging inspection: compare water pump flow rate (active test current draw vs. spec); check for particulate in coolant (2m); heat exchanger: inspect for external debris blockage; perform cooling circuit flush test; compare inlet vs. outlet temperature differential across heat exchanger — reduced differential indicates reduced heat transfer (3m).
