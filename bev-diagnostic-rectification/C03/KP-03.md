> **AMARAN / WARNING:** The electric compressor operates at HV (up to 400V DC). HV de-energisation and discharge verification are mandatory before compressor removal. Residual HV charge in the compressor drive circuit can persist for up to 10 minutes after isolation. Refer to Manufacturer's Service Manual for compressor-specific discharge procedure.

## 1. BEV HVAC System Safety
- HVAC refrigerant (R1234yf) is mildly flammable (A2L classification) — avoid open flames
- R134a is non-flammable but high GWP (Global Warming Potential) — must be recovered, never vented
- Electric compressor is HV-powered — HV de-energisation required before compressor service
- Pressurised refrigerant can cause frostbite on skin contact — wear protective gloves and glasses

## 2. Fundamentals of BEV HVAC System

### 2.1 Key Differences from ICE HVAC
| Feature | ICE Vehicle | BEV |
|---------|------------|-----|
| Compressor drive | Belt-driven from engine | Electric motor (HV-powered) |
| Cabin heating | Engine waste heat | PTC heater or heat pump |
| Cooling performance | Linked to engine RPM | Independent — operates at any speed |
| Idle heating | Engine heat available | Must consume battery energy |

### 2.2 Heat Pump System (Sistem Pam Haba) — Advanced BEV Heating
Modern BEVs increasingly use a **heat pump** instead of or supplementing PTC heaters for cabin heating. A heat pump extracts heat from ambient air (or waste heat from motor/inverter) and transfers it to the cabin coolant loop — achieving 2–3x greater efficiency than a PTC heater at moderate temperatures (>5 deg C). Below 0 deg C ambient, heat pump efficiency decreases; PTC heaters activate as supplemental heat source.

### 2.3 PTC Heater (Positive Temperature Coefficient Heater)
- Electric resistance heater used for cabin heating
- Self-regulating: resistance increases with temperature, limiting current draw automatically
- Typically operates at HV (connected to HV bus)
- Power consumption: 3-8 kW (significant impact on battery range)
- Modern BEVs increasingly use heat pumps (more efficient) supplemented by PTC heaters

### 2.4 Electric Water Pump (for HVAC)
- Circulates coolant through the cabin heater core
- Controlled by the HVAC ECU
- Typically 12V (LV) for cabin heating circuit
- Variable speed for temperature regulation

### 2.5 3-Way Valve (Injap 3-Hala)
- Directs coolant flow between different circuits
- Controlled electronically by the Thermal Management ECU
- Functions: directs waste heat from motor/inverter to cabin (heat recovery), or bypasses cabin heater when cooling only is needed
- Multiple 3-way valves may be present in complex thermal management systems

### 2.6 HVAC System Diagram
A typical BEV HVAC system includes:
- Electric compressor (HV) — compresses refrigerant for cooling
- Condenser — rejects heat to ambient air
- Expansion valve — reduces refrigerant pressure/temperature
- Evaporator — absorbs heat from cabin air
- PTC heater — provides cabin heating
- Heater core — heat exchanger for cabin heating via coolant
- 3-way valves — direct coolant flow paths
- Electric water pumps — circulate coolant
- Blower motor — circulates cabin air through evaporator/heater core

## 3. HVAC System Checking Procedure

### 3.1 Diagnostic Check
1. Connect diagnostic scan tool
2. Read HVAC-related fault codes
3. Check refrigerant pressure readings (high side and low side)
4. Check compressor operation status
5. Check PTC heater activation and power draw
6. Monitor cabin temperature sensor readings
7. Check 3-way valve positions
8. Run active tests: compressor, blower, PTC heater, water pumps, valves

### 3.2 Leakage and Functionality Test
- **Refrigerant leakage:** Use electronic refrigerant leak detector around all connections, condenser, evaporator, and compressor
- **Coolant leakage:** Visual inspection and pressure test of heating circuit
- **Performance test:** Measure cabin vent temperature during cooling and heating modes; compare to specification (typically 5-10 deg C for cooling, >40 deg C for heating)

## 4. HVAC Component Replacement

### 4.1 General Procedure
1. Recover refrigerant using recycling machine (R1234yf or R134a — do NOT mix)
2. De-energise HV system if replacing HV component (compressor, PTC heater)
3. Drain coolant if replacing heating circuit component
4. Remove faulty component
5. Install new component
6. Reconnect all connections
7. Evacuate system (vacuum pump) to remove moisture
8. Recharge with correct refrigerant type and volume
9. Refill coolant if drained
10. Test functionality

### 4.2 Important Notes
- Always replace O-rings and seals when reconnecting refrigerant lines
- Apply refrigerant-compatible oil to new O-rings
- Evacuate system to minimum 29 inHg vacuum for at least 30 minutes before recharging
- Never mix R1234yf and R134a refrigerants

---

## 5. HVAC System Functionality Test

1. Start vehicle in "READY" mode
2. Set HVAC to maximum cooling:
   - Verify compressor engages (listen for operation, check via diagnostic tool)
   - Measure vent temperature (should reach 5-10 deg C within 5 minutes)
   - Check refrigerant pressures (high and low side within specification)
3. Set HVAC to maximum heating:
   - Verify PTC heater activates (check power draw via diagnostic tool)
   - Measure vent temperature (should reach >40 deg C within 5 minutes)
   - Verify water pump and 3-way valve operation
4. Verify no fault codes
5. Check for refrigerant leaks with detector
6. Record all results

## 6. HVAC System Failure Modes (Mod Kegagalan Sistem HVAC)

| Component | Failure Mode | Symptom | Diagnosis |
|-----------|-------------|---------|-----------|
| Electric compressor | Motor winding failure | No cooling; compressor fault code | Active test; check HV supply and control signal |
| PTC heater | Open circuit | No heating; PTC fault code | Check heater current draw via scan tool |
| 3-way valve | Stuck / actuator failure | Incorrect coolant routing; temperature erratic | Active test valve positions via scan tool |
| Refrigerant circuit | Low refrigerant (leak) | Reduced cooling performance; low-side pressure below spec | Leak test; refrigerant pressure check |
| Blower motor | Brush / bearing wear | Noisy or no airflow | Active test; measure current draw |
| HVAC ECU | Communication loss | Multiple HVAC faults; no climate control response | Check CAN bus communication; inspect connectors |
