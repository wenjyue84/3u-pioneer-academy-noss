**Q21.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] During BMS diagnostics, Module 4 shows all cell voltages at 3.78–3.80V except Cell #11, which reads 3.52V — a deviation of 0.27V. Interpret this finding and state the required action. [CoCU C02.5.8 — Battery Module System Inspection]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A deviation of 0.27V far exceeds the acceptable ±0.05V tolerance, indicating severe capacity loss in Cell #11 likely due to lithium plating, internal micro-short circuit, or advanced electrolyte decomposition. The BMS is forced to terminate discharge when Cell #11 reaches cut-off voltage, significantly reducing pack range and performance. Escalate immediately to a traction battery pack replacement work order. Document all individual cell voltages in the diagnostic report. Do NOT clear the fault code; preserve freeze-frame data for root cause analysis.

**Q22.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A BEV arrives with fault code indicating "Pre-charge Circuit Timeout — capacitor voltage did not reach threshold within 2.0 seconds." Describe your diagnostic response. [CoCU C02.4.3 / C02.4.6 — Pre-charge Diagnosis]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Record the fault code and freeze-frame data before clearing. Pre-charge timeout means DC-link capacitors failed to reach approximately 95% of battery voltage within the allowed window, indicating high resistance in the pre-charge path or a defective relay. Measure the pre-charge resistor value against manufacturer specification. Check pre-charge relay coil resistance and contact resistance. Inspect all wiring and connectors in the pre-charge loop for corrosion, pin damage, or breaks. If the relay is confirmed faulty, initiate a pre-charge relay pack replacement work order per CoCU C02.4.7.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] During underbody inspection, you observe an orange HV cable with abrasion damage — the outer conduit is worn down to the inner insulation layer but no conductor is visible. Describe your required action. [CoCU C02.2.5 / C02.2.7 — HV Cable Inspection and Replacement]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Even though no copper conductor is exposed, damage to the outer conduit compromises the double-insulation protection required for HV circuits and creates risk of further abrasion against chassis metal. Do NOT clear the vehicle for service. Photograph the damage and document exact location and cable run in the work order. Measure cable insulation resistance at the damaged point using a CAT III insulation tester — if below 1 MΩ, the cable must be replaced immediately. Initiate an HV cable replacement work order referencing CoCU C02.2.7.

**Q24.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A service plug is missing from a BEV brought in for OBC replacement. Another technician suggests proceeding with HV de-energisation using only the 12V disconnect. Explain why this is unacceptable and state the correct action. [CoCU C02.1.4 / C02.2.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A missing service plug means the main HV battery circuit is not physically broken — the contactors are the only remaining protection, and they can close unexpectedly if a control fault occurs or the 12V battery is briefly reconnected. Proceeding without a service plug violates manufacturer safety protocol and OSHA 1994 requirements. The correct action is to STOP all HV work immediately, notify the supervisor, document the missing plug in the work order, and obtain a replacement service plug from the parts department before any HV work begins.

**Q25.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A customer reports Level 2 AC charging stopped after 20 minutes, with an amber warning displayed. Fault codes P0AA9 (Battery Voltage Sensor) and U0100 (OBC CAN lost communication) are stored together. Describe your diagnostic approach. [CoCU C02.3.3 / C02.3.8 — OBC Diagnosis]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Record both fault codes with freeze-frame data before clearing. P0AA9 and U0100 together suggest OBC CAN communication failure or a shared supply fault causing simultaneous ECU loss of synchronisation. Check the OBC CAN bus connector for corrosion, pin push-out, and harness damage near the OBC unit. Test OBC 12V supply voltage and ground integrity. Inspect the AC inlet socket connector for burn marks or deformation indicating connection overheating. If OBC is confirmed faulty through substitution testing, initiate OBC replacement per CoCU C02.3.9.

### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

*Soalan senario lanjutan untuk penilaian kefahaman mendalam tahap 3. Jawapan contoh menunjukkan kedalaman teknikal yang diperlukan. / Extended scenario questions for in-depth Level 3 competency assessment. Sample answers demonstrate required technical depth.*

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the difference between State of Charge (SOC) and State of Health (SOH). Why must both be monitored in a BEV? [CoCU C02.1.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* SOC is the current charge level as a percentage of the pack's present usable capacity — it indicates available driving energy. SOH is the pack's current capacity relative to its original new capacity — it indicates long-term degradation. Both must be monitored because a 100% SOC in a pack with 70% SOH delivers far less range than the same SOC in a healthy pack. The BMS uses SOH to progressively adjust SOC charge and discharge limits, protecting degraded cells from over-charge and over-discharge stress that accelerates thermal runaway risk.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe active vs passive cell balancing. Explain which is used in most production BEVs and why. [CoCU C02.1.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Passive balancing bleeds excess energy from higher-charged cells as heat through shunt resistors, equalising all cells to the lowest cell's voltage. Active balancing transfers charge from stronger to weaker cells using inductors or DC-DC converters, losing very little energy. Production BEVs overwhelmingly use passive balancing because it is simpler, less expensive, and sufficiently effective during normal charge cycles when voltage deviations are small. Active balancing adds cost and complexity only justified in ultra-high-performance applications where even small energy losses are unacceptable.

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain thermal runaway propagation in a battery module and why BEV battery fires require a different approach than standard vehicle fires. [CoCU C02.5.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* When one cell enters thermal runaway, it generates intense heat and vents toxic gases including hydrogen fluoride and carbon monoxide, causing adjacent cells to enter runaway in a chain reaction that can propagate through an entire module in seconds. The sealed battery housing means standard fire suppression cannot reach internal cells. Large volumes of water are required to cool the battery externally and slow propagation — CO₂ or dry powder only suppresses surface flames temporarily. JBPM guidelines require submerging or continuously flooding the battery pack until thermal runaway ceases completely.

**Q29.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the role of the BMS contactor driver circuit in preventing battery damage during a collision. [CoCU C02.1.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The BMS receives crash signals from the airbag control module via CAN bus within milliseconds of impact detection. Upon receiving the crash signal, the BMS immediately opens both main contactors and disables the pre-charge circuit, disconnecting the battery pack from all vehicle HV circuits. This prevents high energy stored in the pack from flowing through damaged HV cables or components short-circuited by the collision. Post-crash, the contactors remain open until a technician manually resets the system, preventing unexpected HV re-energisation during rescue operations.

**Q30.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Cell voltage deviations increase significantly during high-rate discharge but appear normal at low rate. Explain what this indicates and how to confirm the diagnosis. [CoCU C02.5.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Voltage deviations appearing only under high current load but normalising at low load are the classic signature of increased internal resistance in specific cells. Higher internal resistance causes greater voltage sag under load (V = EMF − I×R), making affected cells appear weaker at high discharge rates. At low rates, the small IR drop is negligible and cells appear equal. The root cause is typically advanced electrolyte decomposition or lithium plating. Confirm by measuring individual cell internal resistance using the BMS diagnostic tool; cells significantly above the manufacturer baseline require module replacement.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the six-step Lockout-Tagout (LOTO) procedure for BEV HV work. State what documentation must be completed after LOTO is applied. [CoCU C02.1.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Six steps: (1) Notify all affected personnel that energy isolation is being performed. (2) Turn READY off and remove key to the safety lockbox. (3) Disconnect 12V auxiliary battery negative terminal. (4) Remove the manual service plug and secure in the safety lockbox. (5) Apply LOTO padlock and "DANGER — DO NOT OPERATE" tag to the service plug port and battery disconnect points. (6) Verify zero energy with a CAT III multimeter at minimum three HV points. Documentation: complete the LOTO record form with technician name, work order number, date, time, zero-energy readings, and supervisor sign-off before any HV contact.

**Q32.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain HV cable routing rules that must be followed during replacement. Why must HV cables not share conduit with LV harnesses? [CoCU C02.2.1 / C02.2.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Routing rules: maintain minimum bend radius (typically 3× cable outer diameter) to prevent insulation stress; secure in manufacturer-specified clamps at required intervals; protect from heat sources with heat shields; route away from sharp chassis edges. HV cables must NOT share conduit with LV harnesses because any insulation failure or pinching of the HV cable inside shared conduit could induce dangerous HV voltage onto LV signal wires, damaging ECUs or creating shock hazard through LV connectors that technicians handle without HV PPE. Segregation is a mandatory safety design principle in all BEV platforms.

**Q33.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the complete inspection and replacement procedure for a damaged HV cable section. Include pre-work and post-work tests. [CoCU C02.2.5 / C02.2.7 / C02.2.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Pre-work: full HV de-energisation, LOTO application, zero-energy verification at three points. Inspection: photograph damage, measure insulation resistance of damaged cable — must exceed 1 MΩ to chassis. Replacement: disconnect both ends using insulated tools; record cable routing path, segregation points, and clamp positions; install new cable following manufacturer's routing specification; reconnect all interlock connectors. Post-work: remove LOTO in reverse order; reconnect service plug and 12V battery; cycle to READY; verify no HV fault codes; perform HV insulation resistance test; confirm interlock continuity; complete functional test.

**Q34.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the symptoms and diagnosis of a welded main positive contactor. What risk does this create for workshop personnel? [CoCU C02.4.3 / C02.2.6]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Symptoms: HV system remains energised even after READY OFF and service plug removal; current sensing shows HV bus voltage present; BMS logs contactor commanded open but voltage does not drop. Diagnosis: measure HV bus voltage after full de-energisation — if voltage remains, contact faces have welded shut from inrush current arc damage. Risk: the welded contactor creates a permanently live HV bus that cannot be isolated by normal procedures, removing the primary safety isolation mechanism. The vehicle must be treated as permanently energised, requiring specialist insulation tools and advanced disassembly procedures before any rectification work proceeds.

**Q35.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A technician omits the post-replacement HV insulation resistance test and returns the vehicle to the customer. Explain what could happen and the legal implications. [CoCU C02.2.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Without post-replacement insulation resistance testing, an improperly installed cable with insufficient insulation or a damaged grommet could create a leakage path between the HV circuit and the vehicle chassis. Moisture ingress over time could reduce insulation resistance below 500 Ω/V, causing the IMD to trigger isolation faults, unexpected HV system shutdown while driving, or in severe cases HV shock if occupants contact conducting body panels. Legally, the workshop bears liability under OSHA 1994 Section 17 (duty of care to non-employees) and the Road Transport Act 1987 for returning an unsafe vehicle to public roads.

**Q36.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain how an OBC power factor correction (PFC) circuit improves charging efficiency and reduces impact on the AC supply grid. [CoCU C02.3.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Without PFC, the OBC draws pulsed current from the AC grid, creating harmonic distortion that degrades power quality for all connected equipment. The PFC circuit uses an active boost converter to shape input current to closely match the AC voltage sinusoid, maintaining near-unity power factor. This means apparent power consumed equals real power delivered to the battery, minimising reactive current draw from the grid. PFC reduces harmonic injection, prevents voltage distortion on shared circuits, and maximises charging efficiency — allowing the OBC to deliver more energy to the battery per unit of AC energy drawn.

**Q37.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the typical OBC diagnostic fault categories and what each indicates about the component or circuit at fault. [CoCU C02.3.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Five main categories: (1) AC input fault — incorrect voltage/frequency, damaged inlet socket, or AC cable issue. (2) OBC over-temperature — blocked coolant circuit, failed coolant pump, or ambient overload. (3) Output voltage/current deviation — internal OBC electronics failure, battery contactor fault, or CMU data conflict. (4) Isolation fault — moisture ingress to OBC housing or damaged HV-LV isolation barrier. (5) CAN communication fault — OBC unable to receive charge commands from BMS/VCU, indicating harness damage or ECU supply issue. Each category directs different diagnostic focus — thermal faults lead to cooling circuit inspection; communication faults lead to harness and ECU supply checks.

**Q38.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A BEV is equipped with DC fast charging (CCS2). Explain how the HV circuit topology differs during DC fast charging compared to Level 2 AC charging. [CoCU C02.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* During Level 2 AC charging, current flows through the OBC: AC inlet → OBC AC input → OBC power conversion → HV bus → battery pack; the OBC controls charge rate. During DC fast charging via CCS2, the external fast charger directly supplies DC at the required battery voltage — the OBC AC conversion stage is bypassed entirely. The DC inlet connects directly to the HV bus through a dedicated inlet contactor, allowing far higher power delivery up to 350 kW. The BMS communicates with the external charger via ISO 15118 protocol to dynamically adjust charge voltage and current throughout the session.

**Q39.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the dual supply paths for the DC-DC converter and why the converter must operate correctly for the vehicle to start. [CoCU C02.6.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The DC-DC converter steps down HV battery voltage to 13.5–14.4V DC to charge the 12V auxiliary battery and supply all 12V ECUs, lights, and accessories. Unlike ICE vehicles where the alternator only operates when the engine runs, the BEV DC-DC converter runs continuously when READY is on. If the converter fails, the 12V system depletes rapidly. Below a critical 12V threshold, the BMS master controller, contactors, and interlock circuits lose power — the main contactors fail open, disconnecting HV from the drive system. The vehicle cannot enter READY state without 12V power, making DC-DC converter operation essential for every start.

**Q40.** After DC-DC converter replacement, what functional tests must be conducted before returning the vehicle to service? [CoCU C02.6.10 — DC-DC Converter Functionality Test]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Mandatory tests: (1) HV insulation resistance test — confirm no HV-to-chassis leakage from new converter installation. (2) 12V output voltage measurement — must read 13.5–14.4V DC under load with READY on. (3) 12V auxiliary battery charge current — confirm converter is actively charging the auxiliary battery. (4) BMS fault code scan — confirm no HV isolation or DC-DC communication faults. (5) Coolant circuit pressure check — confirm no leaks at converter connections after coolant refill. (6) Full load functional test — operate headlights, climate, and multimedia simultaneously and verify output voltage remains stable under maximum 12V load.
