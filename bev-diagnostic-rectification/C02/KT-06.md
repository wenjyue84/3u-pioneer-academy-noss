## Skema Jawapan / Answer Scheme

### S1
**Answer: B** — The DC-DC converter replaces the alternator found in ICE vehicles. In a BEV, there is no engine-driven alternator, so the DC-DC converter steps down the HV battery voltage (200–800V DC) to 13.5–14.4V DC to charge the 12V auxiliary battery and power 12V accessories.

### S2
a) Typical DC-DC converter voltage ranges:
- **HV Input:** 200–800V DC (from traction battery, varies by vehicle platform)
- **LV Output:** 13.5–14.4V DC (to charge 12V auxiliary battery and power 12V system)

b) Four symptoms of a failing DC-DC converter:
1. 12V auxiliary battery constantly draining (not being recharged)
2. Dashboard 12V system or charging warning lights illuminate
3. Vehicle unable to enter "READY" mode (12V system too low to close HV contactors)
4. Intermittent loss of 12V accessories (lights, radio, power windows) while driving

### S3
a) **TRUE** — The DC-DC converter output should measure 13.5–14.4V DC when the vehicle is in "READY" mode, confirming it is actively charging the 12V auxiliary battery.

b) **FALSE** — Most modern BEV DC-DC converters are liquid-cooled and integrated into the HV cooling loop, not air-cooled. Air cooling is insufficient for the power levels involved (typically 1.5–3 kW output).

c) **TRUE** — A failing DC-DC converter that allows the 12V auxiliary battery to drain below ~10V will prevent the BMS and VCU from powering up, which means the HV contactor coils cannot energise, and the vehicle cannot enter "READY" mode.

### S4
DC-DC converter diagnostic inspection procedure:
1. Connect diagnostic scan tool; read all fault codes related to DC-DC converter and 12V system
2. With vehicle in "READY" mode, measure DC-DC converter output voltage at 12V battery terminals (should be 13.5–14.4V DC)
3. Monitor output voltage stability over 5 minutes under varying 12V load (turn on accessories)
4. Check DC-DC converter communication via scan tool: verify it reports operational status to BMS/VCU via CAN bus
5. After HV de-energisation and zero-energy verification: visually inspect unit for coolant leaks at hose connections; inspect connectors for corrosion, damage, or moisture
6. Inspect mounting brackets and fasteners for damage
7. Check for any signs of overheating (discolouration, burned smell)
8. Compare all readings to manufacturer service manual specifications

### S5
**Answer: C** — BEV DC-DC converters achieve >93% efficiency, making them far more efficient than ICE alternators (~60%). Despite high efficiency, liquid cooling is still required due to the high power throughput.
**S6:** Correct sequence (write 1–7):
1. Complete HV disconnection procedure → bracket = **2**
2. Drain coolant from DC-DC converter circuit → bracket = **4**
3. Disconnect HV input cables → bracket = **5**
4. Remove mounting bolts → bracket = **6**
5. Install new DC-DC converter → bracket = **3**
6. Reconnect coolant hoses → bracket = **1**
7. Perform functionality test → bracket = **7**
