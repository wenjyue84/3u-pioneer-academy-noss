## Soalan / Questions

### Soalan 1 — Multiple Choice
What is the primary function of the On-Board Charger (OBC)?
A. Convert DC from the battery to AC for the motor
B. Convert AC from an external source to DC to charge the HV battery
C. Convert 12V to HV for the motor
D. Monitor battery temperature

### Soalan 2 — Short Answer
a) List FOUR (4) common OBC power ratings and their corresponding charging types (single-phase/three-phase).
b) Explain why the OBC requires liquid cooling and what happens if the cooling system fails.

### Soalan 3 — Multiple Choice
What is the typical power conversion efficiency of an OBC?
A. 50-60%
B. 70-80%
C. 90-95%
D. 99-100%

### Soalan 4 — Matching
Match each OBC fault category with its possible cause:

| Fault Category | Possible Cause |
|---------------|---------------|
| 1. Communication fault | a. Moisture intrusion |
| 2. Over-temperature | b. Internal power electronics failure |
| 3. DC output fault | c. Blocked coolant flow |
| 4. Isolation fault | d. Wiring fault or ECU failure |

### Soalan 5 — Short Answer
Describe the coolant drainage procedure for OBC replacement. Include waste disposal requirements.

### Soalan 6 — True/False
a) The OBC can be removed without draining the coolant circuit.
b) The OBC provides galvanic isolation between the AC grid and the HV DC battery.
c) After OBC replacement, charging should be tested with an AC cable to verify functionality.

## Skema Jawapan / Answer Scheme

### S1
**Answer: B** — The OBC converts AC power from an external charging source (wall outlet or EVSE) into regulated DC voltage to charge the HV traction battery.

### S2
a) Common OBC power ratings:
- 3.3 kW — single-phase slow AC (home charger, 13–16A outlet)
- 7.4 kW — single-phase standard AC (dedicated EVSE, 32A)
- 11 kW — three-phase AC (commercial EVSE)
- 22 kW — three-phase fast AC (commercial EVSE, 32A per phase)

b) The OBC dissipates 5–10% of charging power as heat (e.g., a 7.4 kW OBC generates ~370–740W of waste heat). Without liquid cooling, internal component temperatures would exceed safe limits for power electronics (typically >150°C junction limit). Consequences of cooling failure: thermal shutdown (charging stops), reduced charging efficiency, and permanent damage to power electronics (IGBTs, diodes, gate drivers).

### S3
**Answer: C** — Modern OBCs achieve 90–95% conversion efficiency. This is why liquid cooling is required despite the high efficiency, as even 5–10% losses at kilowatt power levels generate significant heat.

### S4
**Answer: 1-d, 2-c, 3-b, 4-a**

Explanation:
- Communication fault (1-d): Wiring fault or ECU failure breaks CAN/LIN bus communication between OBC and BMS/VCU
- Over-temperature (2-c): Blocked coolant flow prevents heat dissipation
- DC output fault (3-b): Internal power electronics failure corrupts voltage/current regulation
- Isolation fault (4-a): Moisture intrusion degrades insulation between AC grid and HV DC circuits

### S5
Coolant drainage procedure:
1. Ensure vehicle is powered OFF and HV de-energised
2. Position drain pan under OBC coolant connections (inlet and outlet)
3. Identify inlet hose (marked IN or by flow direction) and outlet hose
4. Loosen hose clamps using insulated tools; gently pull hoses to break seal
5. Allow complete natural drainage — do not blow compressed air
6. Cap the vehicle-side coolant ports to prevent contamination
7. Transfer drained coolant to sealed, clearly labelled containers
8. Store in secondary containment area (spill tray)
9. Arrange disposal via DOE-licensed scheduled waste contractor
10. Record waste quantity; classify as SW305 (spent process liquid)

### S6
a) **FALSE** — Coolant hoses must be disconnected and coolant drained before the OBC can be removed. Leaving coolant lines connected will cause spillage and contamination of HV components.

b) **TRUE** — The OBC incorporates a galvanic isolation transformer that electrically separates the AC grid side from the HV DC battery side. This is required for safety and meets IEC 61851 / IEC 61000 standards.

c) **TRUE** — After OBC replacement, a full charging test using an AC supply cable should be performed to verify: charging initiates correctly, BMS receives charging data via CAN, and no new fault codes appear.
