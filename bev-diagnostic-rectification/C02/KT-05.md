## Skema Jawapan / Answer Scheme

### S1
**Answer: C** — The traction battery pack typically weighs 200–700 kg depending on capacity and vehicle type, making specialist lifting equipment (battery lifting table) mandatory.

### S2
a) Any five from: battery modules (containing cells in series/parallel), battery cells (individual electrochemical cells), BMS master controller, Cell Management Units (CMUs), HV contactors (main positive, main negative, pre-charge), current sensor (hall-effect or shunt), cooling plates/channels, housing/enclosure, service plug/interlock, HV junction box/fuse assembly.

b) Cell chemistry comparison (other valid advantages accepted):
- **NMC** — high energy density (150–220 Wh/kg); good balance of cost and performance
- **LFP** — excellent thermal stability and long cycle life (2000–4000 cycles); lowest fire risk
- **NCA** — high specific energy (200–260 Wh/kg); used in Tesla vehicles for maximum range

### S3
a) **TRUE** — Battery pack removal requires minimum three qualified persons: one to operate lifting table, two to guide and control the heavy pack (200–700 kg).

b) **TRUE** — Individual battery modules typically weigh 10–40 kg depending on cell count and chemistry.

c) **FALSE** — Cell voltage deviation in a healthy pack must be within ±0.05V. A 5.0V deviation would indicate a severely failed cell requiring immediate module replacement.

d) **TRUE** — Coolant must be fully drained before battery pack removal to prevent spills and contamination of HV components during lowering.

e) **FALSE** — Damaged or decommissioned battery modules must be stored at <30% SOC (low state of charge) to reduce the chemical energy available for thermal runaway during storage and transport.

### S4
Complete battery pack removal procedure (minimum 10 steps in order):
1. Connect diagnostic scan tool; record all active fault codes
2. Complete HV disconnection procedure and verify zero-energy state with CAT III multimeter
3. Drain coolant from battery pack cooling circuit into labelled containers (SW305)
4. Remove underbody shields and aerodynamic panels
5. Disconnect HV cables from battery pack (orange, insulated connections)
6. Disconnect LV control cables (BMS communication harness)
7. Disconnect coolant inlet and outlet hoses; cap all openings
8. Position battery lifting table under vehicle; align with pack mounting points
9. Raise lifting table until it contacts and supports the battery pack
10. Remove all mounting bolts (note: may be 12–20 bolts depending on model)
11. Slowly lower lifting table while monitoring pack for catching points
12. Roll lifting table out from under vehicle
13. Transfer battery pack to approved transport hanger; secure with straps
14. Store in designated HV battery storage area (fireproof, temperature-monitored)

### S5
**Answer: B** — An HV battery module balancer (also called a cell equaliser or battery module tester/charger) is used to equalise cell voltages within and across modules after replacement, ensuring uniform SOC across the entire pack before the BMS is re-activated.

### S6
Coolant refill procedure:
1. Close all coolant drain points and confirm all hose connections are secure
2. Use only manufacturer-specified coolant type and volume (non-conductive formulation)
3. Fill slowly through the designated fill/expansion point to avoid air entrapment
4. Run the electric cooling pump via the diagnostic scan tool (forced activation mode) to circulate coolant and purge air
5. Bleed air from the circuit using the highest bleed point on the cooling system
6. Recheck coolant level in expansion tank and top up to maximum mark
7. Run a 10-minute thermal cycle if possible; recheck for leaks at all connection points
8. Record coolant type, volume added, and date in the service record

**Why air bleeding is critical:** Air pockets in the cooling circuit act as insulating pockets that block heat transfer. Consequences include: localised hot spots on battery modules (risk of thermal runaway), pump cavitation (noise, reduced flow, pump damage), and inaccurate temperature sensor readings, which can cause false over-temperature shutdowns.
