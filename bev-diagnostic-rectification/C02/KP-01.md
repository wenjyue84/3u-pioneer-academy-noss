## Competency Unit: Perform Battery Management System (BMS) Rectification
## Work Activity 1: Carry Out BMS Rectification Workplace Preparation

---

## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees will be able to:
1. State the licensing, legislative, and regulatory requirements for BMS rectification work
2. Explain BMS rectification safety and health requirements, including HV-specific PPE
3. List HV-rated tools and equipment required for BMS rectification
4. Describe hazard identification specific to BMS rectification work
5. Explain scheduled waste management procedures for BMS-related waste

---

## 0. BMS Component Overview (Gambaran Keseluruhan Komponen BMS)

The Battery Management System (BMS) — *Sistem Pengurusan Bateri* — is an electronic control unit that monitors, protects, and manages the HV traction battery. BMS rectification work covers the following components:

| Component | BM Term | Function | Failure Mode |
|-----------|---------|----------|--------------|
| BMS master controller | Pengawal Induk BMS | Monitors overall pack, communicates with VCU | Communication fault, software error |
| Main positive contactor | Penyentuh Positif Utama | Connects battery positive to HV bus | Weld failure (stuck closed), open circuit |
| Main negative contactor | Penyentuh Negatif Utama | Connects battery negative to HV bus | Weld failure, coil open circuit |
| Pre-charge relay | Geganti Pra-cas | Limits inrush current on start-up | Open circuit, resistor degradation |
| Current sensor | Penderia Arus | Measures charge/discharge current | Drift, offset error, signal failure |
| HV cables | Kabel Voltan Tinggi | Transmit HV power between components | Insulation breakdown, connector corrosion |
| Service plug / interlock | Plag Servis / Interlock | Manual HV isolation point | Broken locking mechanism, connector damage |

## 1. BEV Rules and Regulations for BMS Rectification

BMS rectification work involves direct interaction with HV components operating at 200-800V DC. This carries significantly higher risk than scheduled maintenance (C01). All regulatory requirements from C01/KP(1/3) apply, plus additional requirements:

### 1.1 Additional Certification Requirements
- Technicians performing BMS rectification must hold specific HV safety certification
- The Energy Commission may require additional electrical worker licensing for HV battery work
- Workshop must have documented HV work procedures reviewed and approved by a competent person

### 1.2 Regulatory Compliance for HV Work

| Requirement | Standard | Details |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| HV work permit | Company SOP | Written permit required before commencing any HV disconnection |
| Two-person rule | OSHA 1994 best practice | Minimum two qualified persons present during HV work |
| Emergency preparedness | OSHA 1994 | CPR/AED-trained personnel must be on-site |
| HV area demarcation | IEC 61010 | Physical barriers with HV warning signage |

## 2. BMS Rectification Safety and Health Requirements

### 2.1 Workplace Safety Guidelines

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** BMS rectification involves HV circuits operating at 200–800V DC. Contact is FATAL. Full HV disconnection and zero-energy verification are MANDATORY before any physical work. Never bypass this requirement.

BMS rectification requires a workspace meeting ALL C01 requirements PLUS:
- **Anti-static flooring:** Mandatory throughout the entire work bay, not just the immediate work area
- **HV isolation zone:** A clearly demarcated zone using barrier stanchions and HV warning signage. Only authorised personnel may enter.
- **Insulated mat (Class 0, 1000V):** Must be placed on the floor at all work positions around the vehicle
- **Emergency equipment:** AED (Automated External Defibrillator) available within 30 seconds reach

### 2.2 BMS-Specific PPE Requirements

All PPE from C01 applies, with emphasis on:

| PPE Item | Specification | Inspection Frequency |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|
| Insulated gloves (Class 0) | 1000V AC / 1500V DC per IEC 60903 | Before EVERY use — air test mandatory |
| Leather over-gloves | Physical protection for insulated gloves | Before every use |
| Safety glasses (MS ISO 16321 / ANSI Z87.1) | Arc flash protection | Before every use |
| Dielectric safety footwear | DI rated per IEC 61340 | Daily |
| Safety helmet | MS 340 with face shield option | Before every use |
| Arc-rated clothing (optional) | ATPV rated, if arc flash risk assessed | Per risk assessment |

## 3. BMS Rectification Tools and Equipment

### 3.1 Insulated Tools
Same as C01/KP(1/3) Section 4.1, rated to Class 0 (1000V). For BMS rectification, additional insulated tools may include:
- Insulated torque wrench with appropriate sockets for HV terminal bolts
- Insulated pry bar for connector release
- Insulated cable cutters (for emergency use only)

## 4. Hazard Identification in BMS Rectification

BMS rectification carries the highest risk level of all BEV maintenance activities. Specific hazards include:

| Hazard | Source | Consequence | Control Measure |
|--------|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| Electrocution | HV battery (200-800V DC) | Fatal | Full HV disconnection procedure, PPE, LOTO |
| Arc flash | HV terminal short circuit | Severe burns, blindness | Insulated tools, arc-rated PPE, proper technique |
| Chemical burn | Battery electrolyte (LiPF6) | Skin/eye burns, respiratory damage | Chemical-resistant PPE, ventilation, spill kit |
| Thermal runaway | Damaged battery cell | Fire (>600 deg C), toxic gas (HF) | Class D extinguisher, evacuation plan, thermal monitoring |
| Crush injury | Battery pack (200-700 kg) | Fracture, death | Lifting equipment rated for load, proper rigging |
| Toxic gas exposure | Damaged cells releasing HF, CO | Respiratory damage, death | Ventilation, gas detector, respiratory PPE |

### 4.1 BMS-Specific JHA Requirements
The JHA for BMS rectification must include:
- Assessment of each HV disconnection step
- Verification of zero-energy state before commencing work
- Emergency response plan specifically for battery thermal event
- Spill containment plan for coolant and electrolyte

## 5. Scheduled Waste Management for BMS Rectification

### 5.1 Waste Classification

| Waste Type | SW Code | Handling | Storage |
|-----------|---------|---------|---------|
| Spent battery coolant | SW305 | Collect in sealed containers | Secondary containment area |
| Damaged/defective battery modules | SW110 | Handle as hazardous; do NOT crush or puncture | Fireproof container, temperature-monitored, outdoor or ventilated area |
| HV cable off-cuts | SW409 | Collect separately | Designated e-waste bin |
| Contaminated absorbent material | SW410 | Treat as hazardous | Sealed bag in hazardous waste bin |
| Used OBC/converter units | SW110 | Label as HV component waste | Secure storage pending licensed disposal |

### 5.2 Battery Module Disposal
Defective battery modules require special handling:
- Must be stored at <30% SOC before disposal
- Must be in fireproof, ventilated container
- Transport by licensed hazardous waste carrier only
- Disposal records must be maintained for DOE compliance

## 6. BMS Rectification Workplace Preparation Procedure

### 6.1 Pre-Work Checklist
- [ ] HV work permit signed by supervisor
- [ ] Two qualified persons confirmed present
- [ ] All PPE inspected and donned
- [ ] Insulated tools inspected — all serviceable
- [ ] Insulated mat placed at work positions
- [ ] Barriers and HV signage deployed
- [ ] Safety box positioned for key/interlock storage
- [ ] Fire extinguisher (Class D) within reach
- [ ] Electric rescue hook accessible
- [ ] AED location confirmed
- [ ] Spill kit available
- [ ] JHA completed and briefed to team
- [ ] Toolbox talk conducted with attendance recorded
