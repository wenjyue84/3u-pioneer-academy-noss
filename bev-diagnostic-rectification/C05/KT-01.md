## Work Activity 1: Conduct BEV Daily Briefing | **Reference:** C05/KP(1/4)

### S1 — Short Answer: List SIX (6) items that should be covered in a BEV workshop daily briefing.
### S2 — MCQ: What is the recommended maximum duration for a daily briefing? A. 5 minutes B. 15-20 minutes C. 45 minutes D. 1 hour
### S3 — T/F: a) Attendance list is optional for daily briefings. b) The job control board should be updated after the briefing. c) Safety concerns raised during briefing should be documented.
### S4 — Short Answer: Explain THREE (3) effective communication techniques for conducting a daily briefing.
### S5 — Short Answer: Describe the role of the job control board in workshop management. What information should it display?

### S6 — Case Study: Ali is a newly promoted BEV workshop supervisor. During his first daily briefing, two senior technicians dominate the discussion while junior technicians remain silent. After the briefing, a junior technician privately tells Ali that she has a concern about a vehicle's HV warning light but was afraid to speak up. (a) Which leadership style should Ali apply in his next briefing to encourage participation from all team members? (b) State TWO specific actions Ali can take to ensure all team members can raise safety concerns during briefings.

## Skema Jawapan
**S1:** Safety reminder, work progress update, new assignments, service information updates, housekeeping/5S, questions and feedback.
**S2:** B
**S3:** a) FALSE — mandatory, auditable document b) TRUE c) TRUE
**S4:** Clarity (simple/direct language), brevity (concise), structure (consistent format), engagement (questions/input), visual aids (whiteboard/job board). Any three.
**S5:** Displays: vehicle registration and job status, technician assignments, expected completion times, parts status. Updated daily to track workshop workflow.
**S6:** a) Democratic or coaching style — consult team members, invite input, create a safe environment for questions. b) Any two: create structured Q&A round at end of briefing; use anonymous written feedback cards; acknowledge all concerns without judgement; establish "no such thing as a stupid safety question" policy; speak to quieter members individually.
