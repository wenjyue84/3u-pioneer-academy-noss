## BAHAGIAN A: SOALAN PELBAGAI PILIHAN / SECTION A: MULTIPLE CHOICE (30 marks)

Answer all 15 questions. Each question carries 2 marks. Circle the correct answer.

**1.** In ITIL Capacity Management, "Business Capacity Management" refers to:

- (a) Managing individual server CPU and RAM utilisation
- (b) Translating business plans and growth forecasts into future IT capacity requirements
- (c) Managing the capacity of the IT Service Desk to handle tickets
- (d) Negotiating bandwidth capacity with the ISP

**2.** An organisation's server CPU utilisation has been measured at 84% for three consecutive months. According to standard capacity management thresholds, the IT Manager should:

- (a) Do nothing — this is within the normal range
- (b) Power off non-critical virtual machines to reduce load
- (c) Initiate a procurement or capacity expansion process immediately
- (d) Reduce the number of users authorised to use the server

**3.** The correct formula for calculating IT asset Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) over a 3-year period is:

- (a) TCO = Purchase Price only
- (b) TCO = Purchase Price + Annual Maintenance Cost × 3
- (c) TCO = Acquisition Cost + Installation Cost + (Annual Operating Cost × Asset Life) + Disposal Cost
- (d) TCO = CAPEX + OPEX for Year 1 only

**4.** An email system has a monthly service window of 730 hours. It experienced 2.19 hours of downtime in the month. The availability percentage is:

- (a) 99.7%
- (b) 99.5%
- (c) 99.9%
- (d) 98.5%

**5.** Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) is used to measure:

- (a) How quickly IT staff respond to an incident
- (b) The average time taken to restore service after a failure
- (c) The average time a system operates between successive failures — a measure of reliability
- (d) The frequency of planned maintenance windows

**6.** A Service Level Agreement (SLA) differs from an Operational Level Agreement (OLA) in that an SLA is:

- (a) An internal agreement between IT sub-teams
- (b) A formal agreement between the IT department and the user/customer defining the expected service level
- (c) Only applicable to outsourced IT services
- (d) An agreement with an external vendor

**7.** The ITIL process responsible for permanently eliminating the root cause of recurring incidents is:

- (a) Incident Management
- (b) Change Management
- (c) Problem Management
- (d) Event Management

**8.** A "Standard Change" in ITIL Change Management is:

- (a) A change that requires full CAB approval and risk assessment
- (b) A change requiring Emergency CAB (ECAB) approval due to urgency
- (c) A pre-approved, low-risk, frequently repeated change following a defined procedure
- (d) Any change affecting more than 10 users

**9.** In IT performance monitoring, "alert fatigue" occurs when:

- (a) Network latency causes monitoring dashboards to load slowly
- (b) Too many low-priority alerts are generated, causing engineers to ignore or miss critical alerts
- (c) The IT monitoring budget is insufficient to cover all systems
- (d) Monitoring agents consume too much server CPU

**10.** A server experiences 4 failures in a quarter (2,160 operating hours), with total downtime of 8 hours. The MTTR is:

- (a) 540 hours
- (b) 2 hours
- (c) 4 hours
- (d) 270 hours

**11.** In the IT Risk Assessment Matrix, a risk with Likelihood = 4 (Likely) and Impact = 4 (Major) has a risk rating of:

- (a) 8 (Medium)
- (b) 12 (High)
- (c) 16 (Extreme)
- (d) 20 (Extreme)

**12.** An IT budget variance of +RM 18,000 on a YTD budget of RM 120,000 represents a variance percentage of:

- (a) −15% (favourable)
- (b) +15% (adverse — overspend)
- (c) +18% (adverse — overspend)
- (d) −18% (favourable — underspend)

**13.** The "Reduce" risk treatment strategy involves:

- (a) Stopping the activity that causes the risk entirely
- (b) Transferring the financial consequence to a vendor or insurer
- (c) Implementing controls to lower the likelihood or impact of the risk
- (d) Accepting the risk without any active treatment

**14.** In an IT management report, the RAG status "AMBER" for a service means:

- (a) The service is performing above its SLA target
- (b) The service has completely failed
- (c) The service is below target or at risk, requiring attention but not yet a breach
- (d) The service is not covered by an SLA

**15.** The CMDB (Configuration Management Database) supports IT lifecycle management by:

- (a) Processing user password reset requests
- (b) Recording all IT assets with purchase date, warranty expiry, and support end date — enabling proactive lifecycle alerts
- (c) Storing all incident tickets and their resolutions
- (d) Approving change requests from the IT team

Answer all 6 questions.

**16.** Define "IT capacity planning" at Level 5 and explain TWO (2) differences between Business Capacity Management and Component Capacity Management. (5 marks)

**17.** An ERP application server has a vendor hardware support contract that expires in 9 months. The organisation has 200 users dependent on the ERP system. Describe THREE (3) risk-based actions the IT Manager should take immediately. (6 marks)

**18.** A cloud service subscription has been running 22% over budget for three consecutive months. As IT Manager, describe: (a) how you would investigate the root cause; (b) what corrective actions are available; (c) how you would report this to management. (6 marks)

**19.** Explain the relationship between an SLA, an OLA, and a vendor Underpinning Contract (UC) in IT service delivery. Use a specific example to illustrate how all three work together. (6 marks)

**20.** List FOUR (4) monitoring layers in an IT monitoring framework, give one example of what is monitored at each layer, and name one monitoring tool suitable for each. (8 marks)

## BAHAGIAN C: SOALAN ESEI / SECTION C: ESSAY (40 marks)

Answer all 2 questions.

**22.** You are the IT Manager of a logistics company with 350 staff. An audit has revealed the following:

- 3 production servers are operating past their vendor end-of-support date (expired 6 months ago)
- Storage utilisation across the 2 NAS arrays is at 87% (combined)
- The IT budget has been overspent by RM 35,000 (14%) at the 6-month mark
- The incident SLA breach rate has risen from 4% to 12% over 4 months
- The primary internet link has had 3 outages in the past 2 months (combined downtime: 11 hours; SLA target: 99.0%)

(a) For EACH of the five issues identified above: state the risk category (Infrastructure, Financial, Performance, or Availability); assess the severity; and recommend a specific management action with a realistic timeline. (20 marks)

(b) Prepare a one-page Executive Summary that you would include in the quarterly management report presenting these findings to the CEO and CFO. Apply all management report writing principles. Every issue must have a recommended action. The summary must be written in non-technical language. (20 marks)

**23.** You are presenting the annual IT performance review to the Board of Directors. The year's data shows:
- Annual IT availability (average across 5 services): 99.3% (target: 99.5%)
- Annual incident volume: increased 28% year-on-year
- Annual IT budget outturn: RM 720,000 actual vs. RM 680,000 budget (+5.9% adverse)
- 4 risks remain in the "High" or "Extreme" category at year-end
- A major ransomware incident in Q2 was contained within 6 hours; no data was permanently lost

(a) For each of the five data points above, provide: the RAG status you would assign; your interpretation of what it means for the organisation; and the management narrative you would deliver to the Board (non-technical language, 3–4 sentences per item). (20 marks)

(b) Propose a Service Improvement Plan (SIP) for the coming year that addresses the three most critical issues from the year-end data. For each action in the SIP, state: the issue it addresses; the specific action; the expected outcome; the owner; and the target completion date. (20 marks)
