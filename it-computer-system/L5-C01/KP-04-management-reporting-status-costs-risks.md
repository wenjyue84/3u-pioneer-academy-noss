## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees shall be able to:

1. Identify the types and audiences of IT management reports produced at Level 5.
2. Structure and write an IT operations status report that communicates service performance clearly to non-technical management.
3. Analyse and report IT cost variance against budget with appropriate commentary and recommendations.
4. Apply an IT risk management framework to identify, assess, treat, and report on IT risks.
5. Produce a complete IT management report package suitable for senior management review.

---

## 1.0 Introduction to IT Management Reporting

IT management reporting (Pelaporan Pengurusan IT) is the practice of communicating IT operational status, financial performance, and risk exposure to organisational leadership in a structured, accurate, and decision-relevant format. At Level 5, the IT Manager is the author and owner of management reports — accountable for the accuracy of data, clarity of narrative, and relevance of recommendations.

Effective management reporting bridges the gap between technical operations and business leadership. Senior management need to understand:
- Is IT delivering the services it has committed to?
- Is IT spending within approved budget?
- What risks is the organisation exposed to, and are they being managed?
- What decisions or approvals are needed from management?

Management reports are not merely summaries of data — they must present analysis, context, and recommended actions.

## 2.0 Types of IT Management Reports

| Report Type | Frequency | Primary Audience | Key Content |
|------------|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| IT Operations Monthly Report | Monthly | IT Manager, Department Heads, Senior Management | Service availability, incident summary, SLA compliance, change summary |
| IT Budget Variance Report | Monthly / Quarterly | IT Manager, Finance, CFO | Actual vs. budget expenditure by line item; variance commentary; forecast |
| IT Risk Register Report | Quarterly | IT Manager, Senior Management, Audit Committee | Current risks; risk ratings; treatment status; new risks |
| Service Level Report | Monthly | Service owners, user departments, Senior Management | SLA performance by service; breach summary; improvement actions |
| IT Project Status Report | Weekly / Monthly (per project) | Project sponsor, IT Manager, PMO | Milestone status, issues, budget, risks per project |
| Annual IT Performance Review | Annually | Board / Senior Management | Year-in-review: achievements, key metrics, budget outturn, priorities for next year |

## 3.0 IT Operations Status Report

### 3.1 Structure of the Monthly Operations Status Report

A well-structured monthly IT operations status report contains the following sections:

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Executive Summary | 3–5 bullet points summarising key achievements, major incidents, and items requiring management attention |
| Service Availability | Table of availability % per service vs. SLA target; RAG status |
| Incident Summary | Total incidents by priority; top 3 incident categories; SLA compliance rate; notable Major Incidents |
| Change Summary | Approved changes implemented; change success rate; emergency changes |
| Capacity Status | Key utilisation indicators (CPU, storage, bandwidth) vs. threshold |
| Project Updates | One-line status per active IT project (On Track / At Risk / Delayed) |
| Achievements | Positive highlights: projects completed, improvements delivered, certifications obtained |
| Issues and Escalations | Items requiring management decision or awareness |
| Actions and Next Steps | List of planned actions for next period with owner and due date |

### 3.2 Writing Principles for Management Reports

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| Executive language | Avoid technical jargon; explain acronyms on first use; focus on business impact |
| Data before narrative | Present the metric/data first; then explain what it means and why it matters |
| RAG (Red/Amber/Green) status | Use consistent colour coding: Green = on target; Amber = at risk; Red = breached or failed |
| Recommendations, not just problems | Every Red or Amber item should be accompanied by a recommended action |
| Conciseness | The Executive Summary must be readable in 2 minutes; supporting data in appendices |
| Accuracy | All figures must be sourced from the monitoring system or ITSM platform — not estimated |

## 4.0 IT Budget Variance Reporting

### 4.1 Budget vs. Actuals Framework

Budget variance reporting compares approved budget allocations against actual expenditure to identify:
- **Favourable variance** — Actual spending is less than budget (underspend). May indicate delayed projects or procurement delays.
- **Adverse variance** — Actual spending exceeds budget (overspend). Requires explanation and corrective action.

**Variance Formula:**

> Variance = Actual Expenditure − Budgeted Expenditure

> Variance % = (Variance ÷ Budgeted Expenditure) × 100

A positive variance % = overspend (adverse); a negative variance % = underspend (favourable).

### 4.2 IT Budget Variance Report Template

| Budget Line | Annual Budget (RM) | YTD Budget (RM) | YTD Actual (RM) | Variance (RM) | Variance % | Commentary |
|------------|-------------------|----------------|----------------|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| Hardware acquisition | 150,000 | 75,000 | 82,500 | +7,500 | +10% | Additional switches procured due to network expansion |
| Software licences | 80,000 | 40,000 | 38,200 | −1,800 | −4.5% | Licence renewal deferred to Q3 |
| Cloud services | 60,000 | 30,000 | 34,500 | +4,500 | +15% | Data transfer costs higher than projected; review in progress |
| Maintenance contracts | 50,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 | 0 | 0% | On track |
| Staff and training | 120,000 | 60,000 | 55,000 | −5,000 | −8.3% | Training programme deferred to Q3 |
| Contingency | 20,000 | 0 | 0 | 0 | — | Reserved |
| **TOTAL** | **480,000** | **230,000** | **235,200** | **+5,200** | **+2.3%** | Slightly above YTD budget; within acceptable range |

### 4.3 Budget Commentary Guidelines

| Situation | Required Commentary |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Variance > ±10% on any line | Explain root cause; state corrective action or budget re-allocation request |
| Recurring overspend (2+ months) | Flag as a budget risk; propose revised full-year forecast |
| Significant underspend | Confirm whether deferred spend will be incurred later in the year; advise on risk of year-end underspend |
| Unbudgeted expenditure incurred | Identify source of funding; confirm management approval was obtained |

### 4.4 IT Cost Allocation

At Level 5, the IT Manager may be required to allocate IT costs to business units (cost allocation / pengagihan kos). Methods include:

| Method | Description | Suitable When |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| Equal allocation | Total IT cost ÷ number of departments | All departments use IT services equally |
| Headcount-based | Allocate proportionally to number of users per department | Usage scales with number of staff |
| Usage-based | Allocate based on actual consumption (storage, bandwidth, tickets logged) | Accurate metering data is available |
| Fixed fee per service | Department charged a fixed rate for each service subscribed | Service catalogue is well-defined and stable |

## 5.0 IT Risk Management and Reporting

### 5.1 IT Risk Management Framework

IT risk management follows a four-stage cycle:

| Stage | Activity |
|-------|---------|
| 1. Identify | Identify potential IT risks through risk workshops, audit findings, incident post-mortems, and PESTLE analysis |
| 2. Assess | Evaluate each risk using Likelihood × Impact = Risk Rating |
| 3. Treat | Select a treatment strategy: Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, or Accept |
| 4. Monitor and Report | Track risk treatment progress; report risk status to management quarterly |

### 5.2 Risk Assessment Matrix

| Likelihood \ Impact | Insignificant (1) | Minor (2) | Moderate (3) | Major (4) | Critical (5) |
|--------------------|-------------------|-----------|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Almost Certain (5) | 5 (Med) | 10 (High) | 15 (High) | 20 (Extreme) | 25 (Extreme) |
| Likely (4) | 4 (Low) | 8 (Med) | 12 (High) | 16 (Extreme) | 20 (Extreme) |
| Possible (3) | 3 (Low) | 6 (Med) | 9 (Med) | 12 (High) | 15 (High) |
| Unlikely (2) | 2 (Low) | 4 (Low) | 6 (Med) | 8 (Med) | 10 (High) |
| Rare (1) | 1 (Low) | 2 (Low) | 3 (Low) | 4 (Low) | 5 (Med) |

**Risk Level:** Low (1–5) | Medium (6–9) | High (10–15) | Extreme (16–25)

### 5.3 IT Risk Register (Sample)

| Risk ID | Risk Description | Category | Likelihood | Impact | Rating | Treatment | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|--------|-----------------|----------|-----------|--------|--------|-----------|-------|----------|--------|
| R-01 | Primary internet link failure causing loss of cloud services | Infrastructure | 3 | 4 | 12 (High) | Reduce — implement secondary ISP failover link | Network Engineer | Q3 2026 | In progress |
| R-02 | Ransomware attack encrypting file server | Security | 3 | 5 | 15 (High) | Reduce — deploy EDR solution; immutable backups | Security Officer | Q2 2026 | Completed |
| R-03 | ERP vendor end-of-support in 18 months | Lifecycle | 4 | 4 | 16 (Extreme) | Avoid — initiate ERP upgrade project | IT Manager | Q1 2027 | Planning |
| R-04 | Key IT staff resignation (bus factor) | People | 2 | 4 | 8 (Medium) | Reduce — document runbooks; cross-train backup staff | IT Manager | Q4 2026 | In progress |
| R-05 | Storage capacity exhaustion before next procurement cycle | Capacity | 3 | 3 | 9 (Medium) | Reduce — procure additional storage in current budget cycle | System Admin | Q2 2026 | Completed |

### 5.4 Risk Treatment Strategies

| Strategy | Definition | When to Apply |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Avoid | Eliminate the activity or condition that causes the risk | Risk is Extreme and the activity can be discontinued |
| Reduce | Implement controls to lower likelihood or impact | Risk is High or Extreme and elimination is not feasible |
| Transfer | Shift financial consequence to a third party (insurance, vendor SLA) | Risk is High but can be covered by contractual or insurance mechanisms |
| Accept | Acknowledge the risk and monitor; no active treatment | Risk is Low or Medium; treatment cost exceeds expected loss |

## 6.0 Structuring the Management Report Package

A complete quarterly IT management report package contains:

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Cover letter / Transmittal memo | Introduces the report; highlights items requiring management decision |
| IT Operations Status Report | Service performance, incidents, changes (current quarter) |
| Budget Variance Report | Financial performance vs. approved budget (YTD) |
| IT Risk Register Report | Current risk landscape; treatment progress |
| KPI Dashboard | One-page visual summary of key metrics vs. targets |
| Appendices | Raw data, detailed incident log, vendor scorecard results |

## 7.0 Common Errors in Management Reporting

| Error | Consequence | Prevention |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| Data dumps without analysis | Management receives tables of numbers with no context or recommended action | Always include a written commentary section explaining what the data means and what action is proposed |
| Reporting only positive results | Management has incomplete picture; risks and issues are concealed | Report all RAG statuses honestly; present problems with proposed solutions |
| Inconsistent reporting formats | Management cannot track trends over time; credibility is reduced | Use standardised report templates; maintain format consistency month to month |
| Late submission of reports | Management decisions are delayed; perception that IT is not in control | Set a fixed reporting calendar; automate data extraction where possible |
| Risk register not updated | Management approves budgets and decisions based on stale risk information | Assign a Risk Register review as a standing monthly agenda item |
