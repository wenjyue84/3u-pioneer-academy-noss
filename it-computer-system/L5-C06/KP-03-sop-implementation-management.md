## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees shall be able to:

1. Plan and coordinate an SOP rollout programme including communication, training, and go-live scheduling
2. Manage the change management process for SOP introduction in an operational ICT environment
3. Design and deliver SOP training programmes appropriate to each staff category
4. Monitor SOP compliance through audits, metrics, and non-conformance management
5. Manage SOP change control after implementation including emergency revisions

---

## 1.0 SOP Implementation Planning

SOP implementation is not simply distributing a document. It is a managed change programme that requires planning, communication, training, and sustained monitoring to embed the new procedures into daily operations.

### 1.1 Implementation Plan Components

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| Rollout schedule | Phased or simultaneous go-live dates for different sites or departments |
| Communication plan | Who is informed, when, through what channel, and what the message contains |
| Training plan | Who is trained, what training method is used, and by when |
| Resource plan | Budget, personnel, tools, and facilities needed for implementation |
| Risk register | Identified implementation risks and mitigation measures |
| Success criteria | Measurable indicators that confirm successful implementation |
| Escalation path | Who to contact if implementation encounters a blocking issue |

### 1.2 Phased vs Simultaneous Rollout

| Approach | When to Use | Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phased rollout** | Large organisation; multiple sites; high operational risk | Lower risk; lessons from early phases can improve later phases; easier to manage | Longer overall timeline; interim period where different sites operate under different procedures |
| **Simultaneous rollout** | Small organisation; simple SOP; low operational risk | Faster; all staff align immediately | Higher risk if SOP has errors; less opportunity to correct before full deployment |
| **Pilot rollout** | New or complex SOP; significant process change | Real-world validation before full deployment; identifies issues with minimal impact | May create inconsistency during pilot period; requires careful delineation of pilot boundaries |

## 2.0 Change Management for SOP Introduction

Introducing a new or revised SOP is an organisational change. Staff may resist changing familiar work habits, especially if the new procedure is more demanding than previous practice.

### 2.1 Change Management Principles (ADKAR Model Applied to SOP Rollout)

| ADKAR Element | Application to SOP Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Communicate why the new SOP is needed: the risk, audit finding, or regulatory requirement that drives it |
| **Desire** | Engage staff early; show how the SOP benefits them (clarity, protection, reduced errors) |
| **Knowledge** | Deliver training on the new procedure before the go-live date |
| **Ability** | Provide supervised practice; allow questions; supply job aids (quick reference cards) |
| **Reinforcement** | Monitor compliance; recognise good practice; address non-compliance constructively |

### 2.2 Communication Plan Structure

| Communication | Audience | Channel | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive briefing | CIO, Department Heads | Meeting / memo | 4 weeks before go-live | Purpose, scope, go-live date, management expectations |
| Staff announcement | All affected staff | Email, intranet, briefing session | 3 weeks before go-live | What is changing, why, when, and where to get training |
| Training reminder | All affected staff | Email, calendar invite | 1 week before training | Training schedule, venue/platform, attendance requirement |
| Go-live notice | All affected staff | Email, notice board | Go-live date | SOP is now in effect; where to access the document; who to contact for queries |
| Post-implementation update | Management | Report | 30 days after go-live | Compliance status, issues encountered, corrective actions |

## 3.0 SOP Training Programme Design and Delivery

### 3.1 Training Needs Analysis

Before designing training, identify:
- **Who** needs training (by role and level of involvement with the SOP)
- **What** each group needs to know (process overview vs detailed step-by-step)
- **Prior knowledge** (existing familiarity with related procedures)
- **Learning constraints** (shift workers, remote sites, language preferences)

### 3.2 Training Methods

| Method | Best For | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Instructor-led classroom | Complex SOPs with decision logic; Q&A intensive | Scheduling; cost; not scalable for large organisations |
| Hands-on lab / simulation | Technical SOPs (e.g. network configuration, patch deployment) | Requires lab environment; higher setup cost |
| E-learning module (LMS) | Awareness-level training; large, dispersed staff | Lower engagement; unsuitable for complex procedural training without practical component |
| Job aid / quick reference card | Supplementary; supports on-the-job use | Not a substitute for formal training |
| Competency assessment | All staff post-training | Verifies training effectiveness |

### 3.3 Training Record Requirements

For each training session, the following must be documented:
- Training programme title and SOP reference
- Date, time, and venue
- Trainer name and qualifications
- Attendance register with signatures
- Assessment results (pass/fail per trainee)
- Certification issued (where applicable)

All training records must be retained for a minimum period as specified in the organisation's records retention schedule (typically 3–7 years for compliance purposes).

## 4.0 SOP Compliance Monitoring

### 4.1 Monitoring Methods

| Method | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal audit** | Structured review against SOP; interviews, document checks, system log review | Annually (or per audit plan) |
| **Spot check** | Unannounced verification that a specific SOP step is being followed | Monthly or ad hoc |
| **Self-assessment** | Process owner reports on SOP compliance using a standard questionnaire | Quarterly |
| **System-generated metrics** | Automated data from ITSM tool, log management system, or SIEM | Continuous / daily |
| **Non-conformance review** | Analysis of reported incidents, deviations, and complaints to identify SOP failures | As incidents occur; monthly aggregate review |

### 4.2 Key Performance Indicators (KPI) for SOP Compliance

| KPI | Formula / Measure | Target |
|---|---|---|
| SOP compliance rate | (Number of audited activities compliant with SOP ÷ Total audited activities) × 100% | ≥ 95% |
| Training completion rate | (Staff trained ÷ Staff required to be trained) × 100% | 100% before go-live |
| Non-conformance closure rate | (Non-conformances closed within target period ÷ Total non-conformances) × 100% | ≥ 90% within 30 days |
| SOP document currency | Percentage of SOPs reviewed within their scheduled review date | 100% |
| Incident rate attributable to SOP non-compliance | Number of incidents per quarter where root cause is SOP non-compliance | Target: decreasing trend |

### 4.3 Non-Conformance Management

When a deviation from an SOP is detected:

1. **Record** the non-conformance in the Non-Conformance Register (Form ICT-SOP-NC)
2. **Classify** severity: Minor (isolated, low risk), Major (systemic, high risk), Critical (safety or security breach)
3. **Investigate** root cause: Was the SOP unclear? Was training inadequate? Was the SOP impractical?
4. **Corrective action**: assign responsibility, set target date, and verify closure
5. **Preventive action**: where root cause indicates a systemic issue, revise the SOP or training programme
6. **Close and report**: close the non-conformance record; include in next compliance report

## 5.0 Post-Implementation SOP Change Control

After an SOP is implemented, changes must go through a formal change control process to maintain document integrity.

### 5.1 Triggers for SOP Revision

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| Technology change | Upgrade from on-premises Active Directory to Azure AD requires revision of user account SOP |
| Regulatory change | New PDPA amendment requires updated data handling procedures |
| Audit finding | Internal audit identifies a control gap requiring a new or amended SOP step |
| Incident / near-miss | Post-incident review identifies SOP weakness |
| Planned review | Scheduled review date reached; SOP assessed for continued relevance |
| Process improvement | Automation tool introduced that changes the workflow |

### 5.2 SOP Change Control Workflow

```
Trigger Identified
      ↓
Change Request submitted (Form ICT-SOP-CR)
      ↓
Technical review by process owner and SME
      ↓
Compliance review (Internal Audit / Security)
      ↓
Management approval
      ↓
Document updated (new version number assigned)
      ↓
Controlled distribution (superseded copies recalled/invalidated)
      ↓
Training (if procedural change affects staff actions)
      ↓
Effective date communicated
      ↓
Document register updated
```

### 5.3 Emergency SOP Revision

In critical situations (e.g. active security incident, major system failure), an emergency SOP revision may be needed:
- Emergency revision may be authorised verbally by the appropriate manager and documented retrospectively
- An **Emergency Change Record** must be completed within 24 hours
- The revised SOP must be submitted for formal review and approval within 5 working days
- Emergency revisions must be highlighted in the next management review meeting

## 6.0 SOP Lifecycle Management

An SOP has a full lifecycle from initiation to retirement:

| Phase | Activities |
|---|---|
| **Initiation** | Requirements analysis; gap identification; scope definition; management approval to proceed |
| **Development** | Drafting; review; validation testing; approval |
| **Implementation** | Communication; training; controlled distribution; go-live |
| **Operation** | Compliance monitoring; non-conformance management; periodic review |
| **Revision** | Change control; update; re-approval; re-distribution |
| **Retirement** | SOP withdrawn from use; document marked OBSOLETE; archived per retention schedule |
