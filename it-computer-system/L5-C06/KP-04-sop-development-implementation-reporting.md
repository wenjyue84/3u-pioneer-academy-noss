## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees shall be able to:

1. Identify the types, audiences, and reporting frequencies for SOP programme reports
2. Compile and present quantitative compliance metrics and qualitative findings in a management report
3. Apply executive summary writing conventions for senior management and audit committee audiences
4. Prepare a final SOP Development and Implementation Completion Report
5. Archive SOP programme records in accordance with records management requirements

---

## 1.0 Purpose and Types of SOP Programme Reports

Reporting serves three functions in the SOP development and implementation programme:
1. **Governance**: provides management with evidence that the programme is on track and achieving its objectives
2. **Accountability**: documents what was done, by whom, and with what result — creating an auditable record
3. **Continual Improvement**: identifies what worked, what failed, and what should be done differently in future SOP programmes

### 1.1 Report Types and Audience

| Report Type | Audience | Frequency | Content Summary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Progress Report (Laporan Kemajuan) | Project sponsor, IT management | Weekly / fortnightly during development | Milestones achieved, issues, risks, upcoming activities |
| Training Completion Report (Laporan Latihan) | HR, IT management, Compliance | On completion of each training batch | Attendance rates, assessment pass rates, outstanding training needs |
| Compliance Monitoring Report (Laporan Pemantauan Pematuhan) | IT management, Internal Audit | Monthly / quarterly | KPI results, non-conformances raised and closed, trend analysis |
| Audit Report (Laporan Audit) | IT steering committee, Audit Committee | Annually (or per audit cycle) | Detailed compliance findings, non-conformances, recommendations |
| Completion / Closure Report (Laporan Penutupan) | Senior management, CIO | Once, at programme closure | Full programme summary; outcomes vs objectives; lessons learned |

## 2.0 Compliance Metrics and Data Collection

### 2.1 Data Sources for Compliance Reporting

| Data Source | Information Provided |
|---|---|
| ITSM (IT Service Management) tool | Ticket volume, SLA adherence, change request records |
| SIEM / log management system | Audit trails; access control logs; anomaly detection events |
| Document management system | SOP access logs; controlled copy distribution records |
| Learning Management System (LMS) | Training completion rates; assessment scores |
| Internal audit records | Findings, non-conformances, corrective action status |
| Non-conformance register | Number, type, severity, and closure status of non-conformances |

### 2.2 Metrics Compilation Table

For each KPI (as defined in KP-03, Section 4.2), compile data into a reporting table:

| KPI | Target | Current Period | Previous Period | Trend | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOP compliance rate | ≥ 95% | 93% | 89% | ↑ Improving | Below target |
| Training completion rate | 100% | 100% | 95% | ↑ Met | On target |
| NC closure rate (30 days) | ≥ 90% | 87% | 82% | ↑ Improving | Below target |
| SOP document currency | 100% | 96% | 92% | ↑ Improving | Below target |

### 2.3 Qualitative Findings

In addition to quantitative metrics, management reports must include qualitative findings:
- **Root cause analysis** of significant non-conformances
- **Trend analysis**: is the compliance situation improving, stable, or deteriorating?
- **Barriers and enablers**: what factors are helping or hindering SOP compliance?
- **Stakeholder feedback**: comments from process owners and end users on SOP usability

## 3.0 Executive Summary Writing

### 3.1 Executive Summary Principles

An executive summary is written for a senior management audience who may not read the full report. It must:
- Be no longer than one page (approximately 300–500 words)
- State the purpose of the report in the first sentence
- Summarise the key findings and their significance
- Clearly state whether the programme is on track, ahead, or behind objectives
- List the top 3–5 recommendations with the action owner and target date
- Use plain language; avoid technical jargon

### 3.2 Executive Summary Structure

```
[1] Programme Status (1–2 sentences)
    → e.g. "The Computer System & Network SOP Development and Implementation
       Programme completed its development phase on [date] with all 12 SOPs
       approved. The implementation phase is currently 80% complete."

[2] Key Achievements (3–5 bullet points)
    → Milestones met, SOPs approved, training completed, audits passed

[3] Key Issues and Risks (3–5 bullet points)
    → Current challenges, non-conformances above target, risks to programme completion

[4] Compliance Summary (1–3 sentences with headline figures)
    → e.g. "Overall SOP compliance rate stands at 93%, against a target of 95%.
       Non-conformance closure rate is 87% within the 30-day target."

[5] Recommendations (3–5 items)
    → Each recommendation: What action | Who is responsible | By when

[6] Next Steps (2–3 sentences)
    → What will be reported in the next period
```

## 4.0 SOP Development and Implementation Completion Report

At the conclusion of the SOP development and implementation programme, a formal **Completion Report** is produced. This document serves as the programme's permanent record.

### 4.1 Completion Report Structure

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Executive Summary | Programme status, outcomes vs objectives, key metrics |
| 2. Programme Background | Original mandate, trigger, scope, and objectives |
| 3. Programme Governance | Project team, roles, sponsorship, and oversight structure |
| 4. SOP Inventory | Complete list of all SOPs developed, revised, or retired |
| 5. Development Phase Summary | Timeline, milestones, issues encountered, and resolution |
| 6. Implementation Phase Summary | Rollout dates, training completion, go-live status per SOP |
| 7. Compliance Monitoring Results | KPI summary, audit findings, non-conformance summary |
| 8. Lessons Learned | What went well, what did not go well, and recommendations for future programmes |
| 9. Outstanding Actions | Unresolved issues, incomplete training, deferred SOPs — with owner and target date |
| 10. Programme Closure Approval | Sign-off by programme sponsor and CIO |

### 4.3 Lessons Learned Register

| Ref | Area | Observation | Impact | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LL-001 | Requirements analysis | Stakeholder interviews took 40% longer than planned due to scheduling conflicts | 2-week delay to development start | Plan 6 weeks for stakeholder engagement in future programmes; secure management mandate for compulsory participation |
| LL-002 | Training | E-learning module completion rate was 72% — lower than the 90% target | Training gap going into go-live | For Level 5 SOPs, instructor-led training should be mandated; e-learning should be supplementary only |
| LL-003 | Compliance monitoring | ITSM tool did not generate the metrics needed for SOP compliance reports | Manual data collection required | Define reporting requirements at the start of the programme and configure ITSM tools accordingly |

## 5.0 Records Management and Archiving

### 5.1 Records to be Retained

The following records from the SOP development and implementation programme must be retained as controlled records:

| Record | Retention Period | Custodian |
|---|---|---|
| Approved SOP documents (all versions) | Minimum 7 years after retirement | Document Controller |
| Requirements Report | Minimum 5 years | Document Controller |
| Review comment registers | Minimum 5 years | Document Controller |
| Training attendance records | Minimum 5 years | HR / Document Controller |
| Training assessment results | Minimum 5 years | HR |
| Compliance monitoring reports | Minimum 5 years | Document Controller |
| Non-conformance records | Minimum 5 years | Quality / Audit team |
| Completion Report | Minimum 7 years | Document Controller |

### 5.2 Archiving Requirements

- All physical records must be stored in a secure, climate-controlled environment
- All electronic records must be stored in a document management system with access controls, version history, and backup
- Superseded SOP versions must be marked OBSOLETE and archived — not deleted
- Access to archived records must be restricted to authorised personnel

### 5.3 Records Disposal

Records may only be disposed of after the retention period has expired, subject to:
- Written approval from the department head and records manager
- Compliance with the National Archives Act 2003 (Akta Arkib Negara 2003)
- Secure destruction (shredding for physical; certified deletion for electronic)
- Entry in the Disposal Record confirming date, description, and authorising officer
