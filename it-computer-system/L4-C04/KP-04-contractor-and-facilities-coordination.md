## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees shall be able to:

1. Describe the roles and responsibilities of all stakeholders in a cabling project (owner, facilities manager, IT administrator, cabling contractor)
2. Prepare and evaluate scope of work (SOW) and technical specifications for cabling contractors
3. Conduct site coordination meetings and manage contractor activities during installation
4. Apply permit-to-work and site safety requirements for cabling work
5. Inspect and accept completed contractor work against agreed specifications and standards

---

## 1.0 Stakeholder Roles in a Cabling Project

A network cabling project typically involves multiple parties. Understanding each party's role is essential for effective coordination:

| Stakeholder | Role and Responsibility |
|-------------|------------------------|
| Building owner / Client | Approves budget; signs off on completed work; receives as-built documentation |
| Facilities manager (Pengurus Kemudahan) | Controls building access; coordinates with M&E (mechanical and electrical) services; issues permits to work |
| IT/Network administrator (Level 4) | Defines technical requirements; produces SOW; supervises contractor; accepts completed work |
| Cabling contractor | Executes physical installation as per SOW and drawings; provides test reports and as-built drawings |
| M&E contractor | Installs conduits, cable trays, and power to telecommunications rooms — must be coordinated with cabling work |
| Safety officer | Ensures all on-site personnel comply with Occupational Safety and Health Act 1994 (OSHA 1994) requirements |

---

## 2.0 Scope of Work (SOW) and Technical Specifications

### 2.1 Scope of Work Document

The SOW defines precisely what the contractor is required to supply and install. It must include:

1. **Project description** — building, floors, number of outlets, cable type, standards compliance required
2. **Materials specification** — cable category, connector type, patch panel brand, fibre type — specified by manufacturer brand or "approved equal"
3. **Installation requirements** — conduit type, tray type, bend radius, fill ratio, bonding/grounding requirements
4. **Labelling requirements** — TIA-606-B compliant; machine-printed; label specification
5. **Testing requirements** — 100% channel certification using a Category-rated tester (e.g. Fluke DSX-8000 for Cat6A); fibre OTDR traces for all backbone fibres; test reports in electronic and hard copy
6. **Documentation deliverables** — as-built drawings (AutoCAD .dwg format), cable schedule, test reports, within 30 days of completion
7. **Warranty** — minimum 20-year system warranty (manufacturer's channel warranty program) for Cat6A installations; 25-year preferred

### 2.2 Contractor Qualification Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Certification | Manufacturer authorised installer (e.g. Panduit, CommScope, Belden authorised) |
| CIDB registration | Registration with the Construction Industry Development Board Malaysia |
| Public liability insurance | Minimum RM 1 million; copy required before work begins |
| Relevant experience | At least 3 completed projects of similar or greater scale; references available |
| OSHA compliance | Signed declaration of OSHA 1994 compliance; workers trained in on-site safety |

## 3.0 Site Coordination Meetings

### 3.1 Pre-Installation Meeting (Mesyuarat Pra-Pemasangan)

Held before any physical work begins. Attendees: IT administrator, facilities manager, cabling contractor, M&E contractor (if relevant), safety officer.

Agenda:
1. Review and confirm approved drawings and SOW
2. Confirm material delivery schedule
3. Identify access restrictions (secure areas, server rooms, fire compartments)
4. Agree on working hours (normal hours or after-hours to avoid disruption)
5. Review permit-to-work procedure
6. Establish communication channels and escalation contacts
7. Agree on inspection and sign-off milestones

### 3.2 Progress Meetings (Mesyuarat Kemajuan)

Held weekly or at agreed milestones during installation:
- Contractor reports progress against schedule (cables pulled, terminated, tested)
- Issues and variations logged in the meeting minutes
- Any design changes agreed and issued as formal variation orders (not verbal instructions)

### 3.3 Handover Meeting (Mesyuarat Serah Terima)

At practical completion:
- Contractor presents test results and documentation
- IT administrator and facilities manager inspect completed installation
- Punch list (senarai kecacatan) prepared for outstanding items
- Sign-off withheld until punch list is cleared

## 4.0 Permit-to-Work System

A permit-to-work (PTW) system (sistem permit bekerja) is a formal safety procedure required for cabling work in occupied buildings, especially:
- Working above suspended ceilings or false floors
- Working in electrical rooms or near live electrical equipment
- Hot work (drilling through fire-rated walls)
- Working at height (ladders, scaffold, MEWP/cherry picker)

### 4.1 Permit-to-Work Process

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Contractor submits PTW request to facilities manager at least 24 hours in advance |
| 2 | Facilities manager reviews scope, identifies hazards, and specifies control measures |
| 3 | PTW issued with specific validity period, work area, and conditions |
| 4 | Contractor signs PTW; safety briefing conducted on site before work begins |
| 5 | IT administrator or facilities representative supervises or spot-checks during work |
| 6 | Upon completion, PTW is closed out by the authorised person and filed |

### 4.2 Common PTW Conditions for Cabling Work

- No drilling into structural elements without structural engineer approval
- All ceiling tiles to be replaced at end of each work session
- Firestop materials must be applied immediately after each wall/floor penetration
- No cabling run exposed in occupied areas (must be in conduit or trunking)
- All waste and offcuts removed from site daily

## 5.0 Contractor Work Inspection and Acceptance

### 5.1 Staged Inspections

Inspections must be carried out at each stage:

| Stage | Inspection Items |
|-------|-----------------|
| Material delivery | Verify brand/model matches SOW; check for shipping damage; reject non-conforming materials |
| Post-pulling (before termination) | Cable paths follow approved routes; bend radii observed; labels at both ends; no damage |
| Post-termination | Pair untwist within spec; no split pairs; jacket intact at termination; labels on panel ports |
| Post-testing | 100% pass rate on certification tester; test report format correct; all cable IDs match records |
| Final (as-built) | Drawings updated and submitted; all labels present and legible; TR tidy; excess materials removed |

### 5.2 Non-Conformance Reporting (NCR)

When a contractor's work does not meet the SOW or standard:
1. Issue a written Non-Conformance Report (NCR) — not a verbal instruction
2. State the specific deficiency and reference the violated specification clause
3. Set a deadline for corrective action
4. Re-inspect and sign off only after correction is verified
5. File the NCR and correction record; retain for the project file

---

## 6.0 Managing Variations and Change Requests

During installation, design changes may be required (user moves, new outlets, changed routing). All variations must be managed formally:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Identify the change; assess impact on cost, time, and other trades |
| 2 | IT administrator raises a variation request with written justification |
| 3 | Contractor provides a variation quotation within agreed time (e.g. 3 working days) |
| 4 | Client/building owner approves and signs the variation order |
| 5 | Work proceeds per variation order; design drawings updated |
| 6 | As-built drawings and cable records updated to reflect variation |

---

## 7.0 Common Errors in Contractor Coordination

| Error | Consequence | Prevention |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| Approving materials verbally without checking specification | Non-compliant materials installed; manufacturer warranty void | Always check delivery docket against SOW; reject before installation |
| Accepting verbal variation instructions | Scope creep; no cost control; disputes at project close | All variations in writing; signed by both parties |
| Skipping staged inspections | Defects discovered only at final acceptance; expensive rework | Make inspections mandatory at each stage; sign-off before next stage |
| No PTW issued | Safety incident; regulatory penalty under OSHA 1994 | Facilities manager enforces PTW before any work; contractor not permitted to start without valid PTW |
| Incomplete as-built documents at handover | Infrastructure undocumented; records gap from day one | Include as-built documents as a contractual deliverable with payment tied to submission |
