| Type / Framework | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| SOP development lifecycle | A structured process for creating standard operating procedures: requirements analysis, information gathering, work process confirmation, activities mapping, drafting, committee/management review, approval, implementation, and periodic review for improvement. Each stage has defined inputs, outputs, and approval gates. Following the lifecycle ensures SOPs are complete, accurate, and aligned with organisational policy. |
| Business process mapping | Visual representation of work processes showing sequence, workflow, starting and ending points, decision points, approval points, and systems used. Techniques include flowcharts, swimlane diagrams, and SIPOC (Supplier-Input-Process-Output-Customer). Process maps serve as the foundation for writing clear, step-by-step SOP instructions. |
| Document control and versioning | The practice of assigning unique codes (naming convention), version numbers, effective dates, review dates, and approval signatures to each SOP. Ensures that only the current approved version is in use and that superseded versions are archived. A document register tracks all SOPs, their status, and review schedule. |
| Regulatory and policy framework (MAMPU, MCMC) | Government authorities that issue directives, guidelines, and standards applicable to IT operations. MAMPU (Malaysian Administrative Modernisation and Management Planning Unit) governs public sector ICT policies. MCMC (Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission) regulates communications and content. SOPs must comply with applicable directives and be updated when regulations change. |
| Business process improvement (BPA, BPI, BPR) | Strategies for enhancing SOP effectiveness: Business Process Automation (BPA) eliminates manual steps through automation; Business Process Improvement (BPI) makes incremental enhancements based on feedback and metrics; Business Process Re-engineering (BPR) fundamentally redesigns processes for significant gains. Applied when SOP effectiveness evaluation reveals inefficiency or non-compliance. |
| SOP effectiveness evaluation | Measurement of how well SOPs achieve their intended purpose. Indicators include user feedback and complaints, number of incidents (accidents, damages, equipment loss), productivity metrics, regulatory compliance status, and reduction of duplication activities. Results are reported to management and used to trigger SOP revision cycles. |

| Management Framework / Tool | Description |
|------------------------------|-------------|
| SOP scope categories | The areas covered by network SOPs: internet usage, network security, network hardware/software management, digital storage, network maintenance, network management, and disaster recovery. Each scope area may have one or more SOPs depending on complexity. Scope definition ensures SOPs are focused and maintainable. |
| SOP dissemination strategy | Methods for distributing approved SOPs to staff and users: printed document, notice board posting, company website or intranet, email distribution, and document management system. The chosen medium must ensure accessibility, version control, and acknowledgement of receipt. Multiple channels may be used depending on audience. |
| SOP awareness programme | Activities to ensure staff understand and can follow SOPs: briefings, classroom training, hands-on demonstrations, and quick-reference guides. Attendance is tracked and competency is verified through observation or quiz. New employees receive SOP orientation during onboarding. Refresher training is conducted when SOPs are revised. |
| SOP implementation monitoring | Ongoing oversight to verify that SOPs are being followed in daily operations. Monitoring methods include spot checks, audit checklists, incident tracking, and user feedback collection. Non-compliance is documented, root causes are analysed, and corrective actions are taken. Monitoring results feed into the effectiveness evaluation. |
| SOP coding and documentation standards | A system for uniquely identifying each SOP: naming convention (department code, process code, version number), document register, and archival rules. Ensures traceability, auditability, and easy retrieval. Coding standards are defined once and applied consistently across all departments. |
| Technical writing and presentation | Skills required to produce clear, accurate, and usable SOP documents. Includes plain language writing, consistent formatting, effective use of flowcharts and diagrams, and presentation skills for committee review. SOPs must be understandable by the target audience (technical staff, end users, or management). |


| SOP Infrastructure Scope | Description |
|--------------------------|-------------|
| Network infrastructure requirements | Physical media (cabling, fibre), active network components (switches, routers, firewalls), IP protocol and addressing, network interface configuration, and monitoring/management tools. SOPs define standard configurations, naming conventions, and change procedures for each element. |
| Network services requirements | Gateway services, remote access services, name services (DNS, WINS), DHCP/RARP/BOOTP, and authentication services. SOPs specify how each service is provisioned, configured, monitored, and restored. Service-level targets and escalation paths are included. |
| Operating system and application services | Standard and non-standard operating systems, application software, security software, and update/patch procedures. SOPs define approved software lists, installation procedures, configuration baselines, and decommissioning steps. |
| User access and identity management | SOPs covering user account lifecycle (creation, modification, suspension, deletion), role-based access control, password policy enforcement, and multi-factor authentication procedures. These SOPs ensure consistent access provisioning across the organisation, reduce security risks from orphaned accounts, and support audit compliance. Procedures must reference the organisation's security policy and be updated when authentication methods or identity platforms change. |


| Common Issue | Root Cause | Management Action |
|--------------|------------|-------------------|
| SOPs not followed by staff | SOPs are too complex, inaccessible, or staff are unaware of them; no enforcement mechanism | Simplify SOP language; improve accessibility (intranet, quick-reference cards); conduct awareness programme; enforce through spot checks and performance review |
| SOPs become outdated quickly | No scheduled review cycle; no trigger mechanism for regulatory or process changes; owner not assigned | Assign SOP owner for each document; establish mandatory review schedule (e.g. annually); trigger review on any regulatory, system, or process change |
| Inconsistent SOP format across departments | No organisation-wide template or coding standard; each department creates SOPs independently | Define and distribute a standard SOP template, coding convention, and style guide; centralise SOP review through a document control function |
| SOP effectiveness not measured | No defined indicators; no feedback mechanism; evaluation not part of management reporting | Define KPIs (incident count, compliance rate, user feedback score); include SOP effectiveness in periodic management reports; use results to drive SOP revision |

## Employability Skills

This CoCu develops the following employability skills through the work activities:

### 1. Communication Skills
- **Mapping:** Present SOP development proposals and effectiveness reports to senior management; prepare SOP documents with clear technical writing for diverse audiences; communicate SOP changes, awareness programmes, and compliance requirements across the organisation

### 2. Teamwork & Collaboration
- **Mapping:** Lead SOP development committees and review panels; collaborate with department heads and subject-matter experts during requirements analysis; coordinate SOP dissemination and training with HR, operations, and IT teams

### 3. Problem-solving
- **Mapping:** Identify gaps between current operations and documented procedures; resolve conflicting requirements from different stakeholders during SOP development; address SOP non-compliance through root cause analysis and process improvement

### 4. Initiative & Self-reliance
- **Mapping:** Drive SOP development lifecycle from requirements to implementation without waiting for audit findings; initiate SOP revisions when regulatory changes or process improvements are identified; take responsibility for SOP currency and organisational compliance

### 5. Planning & Organizing
- **Mapping:** Plan SOP development timelines including information gathering, drafting, review, and approval stages; organize SOP awareness programmes, training schedules, and effectiveness evaluations; manage document control, versioning, and distribution across the organisation

### 6. Self-management & Safety Awareness
- **Mapping:** Ensure SOPs comply with regulatory directives (MAMPU, MCMC) and organisational policies; manage the risk of outdated or non-compliant procedures; maintain document control governance and audit readiness

### 7. Technology Use & Technical Proficiency
- **Mapping:** Use business process mapping tools and flowchart software for SOP development; manage document control and distribution systems; apply process improvement techniques (BPA, BPI, BPR) to enhance SOP effectiveness

### 8. Learning Skills & Continuous Improvement
- **Mapping:** Stay informed on regulatory changes, SOP best practices, and process improvement methodologies; analyze SOP effectiveness metrics to drive revision cycles; learn from audit findings and user feedback to improve SOP quality and usability



## Attitude, Safety and Environmental

### Workplace Safety
- **Document Control Security:** Restrict access to SOP documents that contain sensitive procedures (security configurations, access credentials, disaster recovery steps) to authorised personnel only. Use access-controlled document management systems with version tracking, and ensure superseded SOPs are archived securely rather than left in shared folders where outdated procedures could be followed.
- **Records Management Compliance:** Maintain SOP documentation in compliance with organisational records management policies and applicable regulations (e.g. MAMPU directives, PDPA). Implement appropriate retention schedules, secure storage, and controlled disposal for SOPs that contain personal data, security procedures, or regulatory-sensitive content.
- **Ergonomic Considerations for Training Delivery:** Ensure that rooms used for SOP awareness programmes and training sessions meet ergonomic standards: adequate lighting, appropriate seating, screen height at eye level, and regular breaks during extended sessions. Trainers and participants should adjust workstations to prevent repetitive strain and fatigue during prolonged document review and hands-on practice.
- **Safe Handling of Sensitive Information During SOP Development:** During the information-gathering phase (interviews, brainstorming sessions, questionnaires), protect notes and drafts that capture sensitive operational procedures. Store working documents securely, limit distribution to committee members, and shred or securely delete draft materials that are no longer needed to prevent information leakage.

### Environmental Considerations
- **Sustainable IT Strategy:** Develop long-term IT strategies that consider environmental impact; promote energy-efficient infrastructure, virtualisation, and cloud solutions that reduce organisational carbon footprint.
- **Responsible Technology Governance:** Establish policies for responsible technology use, data privacy, and ethical IT practices that align with organisational values and community expectations.

### Professional Attitudes
- **Strategic Integrity:** Make decisions based on long-term organisational benefit; resist short-term pressures that could compromise system stability, security, or sustainability.
- **Visionary Leadership:** Champion innovation and emerging technologies while balancing risk; inspire teams to think strategically about technology's role in organisational success.
- **Stakeholder Trust:** Maintain credibility with executive leadership, boards, and external partners through transparent communication, ethical conduct, and delivery of promised results.
- **Continuous Improvement Culture:** Foster an environment where teams continuously learn from experiences, share knowledge, and improve processes to enhance organisational capability and competitiveness.
