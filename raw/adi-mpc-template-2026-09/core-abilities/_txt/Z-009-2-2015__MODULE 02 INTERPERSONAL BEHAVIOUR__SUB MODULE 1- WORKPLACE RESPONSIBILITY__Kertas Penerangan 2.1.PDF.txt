1. 
Introduction 
 
A workplace is a location at which an employer provides works for employees. The 
workplace is located in a variety of settings including offices, manufacturing facilities or 
factories, stores, farms, out-of-doors, and in any location where work is performed. 
 
Home offices, telecommunicating work arrangements, and world-wide employment 
relationships mean that almost any location, including the employee's home, may serve as 
and accurately be called, a workplace and provides guidance and regulations for the 
workplace in such areas as workers compensation, breaks and lunch requirements, leave 
requirements, equal employment opportunity, and unemployment compensation.  
 
2. 
How To Demonstrate Responsibility 
Responsibility is an admirable trait and leads to further gains. Responsibility is not just 
doing your homework or feeding your pets; it is making proper choices and doing the right 
things you were asked for.  You cannot show responsibility without having it.  "When life 
gives you lemons..." what will you do? The answer differs from one to another, but 
showing and having responsibility can and will , pay off. 
 
2.1 Identify your daily routine 
Everyone has their own set of tasks to complete every day, from small things like 
brushing your teeth to the big things like paying overdue taxes. Think of your daily 
life, and make a list of your responsibilities. Then, put a note next to each item such 
as "crucial" and "important" for tasks that must be completed, or "minor" for tasks that 
could be ignored or defer to later date. Something may be important or not based on 
your personal views, but do not leave events you cannot get away with.  Making this 
list will help you become more organized and help you manage your time wisely and 
get what needs to be done right away. Hence, your routine. 
 
2.2 Plan 
After generating your ‘things to do’ list, go through all the events marked "important" 
and think about where they will fit into your schedule. Need to fax some important 
papers? Bring them to the office if you have a fax machine! Need to take your pet to

3. 
Factors That Demonstrate A Strong Work Ethic 
 
3.1 Integrity 
Integrity stretches to all aspects of an employee's job. An employee with integrity 
fosters trusting relationships with clients, peers and supervisors. Coworkers value the 
employee's ability to come out with honest feedbacks. Clients trust the employee's 
advice. Supervisors rely on subordinates’ high moral standards, trusting them not to 
pinch the company material goods or create problems. 
 
3.2 Sense of Responsibility 
A strong sense of responsibility affects performance of employee at work place and 
the number of work done. When the employees feel personally responsible for their 
job performance, they will show up on time, put in their best efforts and complete 
work tasks to the best of their ability. 
 
3.3 Emphasis on Quality 
Some employees perform only to the bare minimum of their capability, just enough to 
keep their job intact. Employees with strong work ethic care about the quality of their 
works. They do their best to produce great result in works, not merely churn out what 
is needed. The employee's commitment to achieve good quality will improve the 
company's overall value. 
 
3.4 Discipline 
It takes a certain level of commitments to finish your tasks every day. An employee 
with good discipline stays focused on his/her goals and is determined to complete 
his/her assignments. This employee shows a high level of commitment and 
dedicated to his/her job and always ensure he/she accomplishes his/her works. 
 
3.5 Sense of Teamwork 
Most employees have to work together to meet a company's objectives. An 
employee with a high sense of teamwork helps the team to meet its goals and deliver 
quality work. These employees appreciate their team mates’ works and be of 
assistance where and when possible.

4. 
Benefits Of Punctuality At Work 
It is very important to earn and maintain respect at workplace.  The followings are some 
reasons why you should be punctual at work. 
 
4.1 Respect 
Punctuality speaks of a person’s respect of others. In giving respect to others we can 
earn it back in return. 
 
4.2 Deadlines 
An employee who isn’t stressed about reaching late or has come in early enough to 
give him minutes to settle in before the day’s work begins, has a better chance to get 
more accomplishments than a late comer who will be more under pressure. Some 
professionals, such as writers, journalists, producers and accountants, have daily, 
weekly and monthly deadlines. Meeting deadlines and timelines reassure clients that 
they can count on you to get the job done. Without professional punctuality, 
customers might look for another alternative to supply their needs. Striving to meet 
deadlines helps establish your strong work ethic and your desire to be productive. 
 
4.3 Team-Centered Goals 
When you are on time for meetings, conference calls and shift changes, you show 
others that you have team-centered work goals. Punctuality demonstrates your 
respect to co-workers and clients and hence reinforces your time-management skills. 
Team-focused employees avoid extended lunch breaks, respond to emails in a 
timely fashion and avoid tardiness, making sure to carry their weight on the job. If 
you want your boss and co-workers to see you as a responsible team-centered 
worker, then be punctual. 
 
4.4 Credibility 
It might not seem like your job security is based on punctuality, but it certainly doesn't 
hurt, especially in a struggling economy. The number of employees arriving late to 
work at least once a week has slid from 20 percent to 15 percent (Channel 2 news 
report in Reno Nevada). In a tough job market, you don't want to risk losing your job 
because your boss sees you as lazy or irresponsible. Punctuality demonstrates your 
willingness to get up early, plan and make every effort to complete your work on 
time.

5. 
Professionalism 
Punctuality is a sign of professionalism and helps you stand out as a reliable and 
trustworthy employee. If you don't get your part of a project completed on time, you keep 
others from being able to finish theirs. Being punctual helps you establish your reputation 
as a dependable and consistent worker. In an interdependent working environment, 
everything runs like a well-oiled piece of machinery. Being on time helps ensure that 
you're doing your best to keep things running smoothly. 
 
5.1 Tardy 
 An employee is deemed to be tardy when he/she: 
i)  
Fails to report for work at the assigned/scheduled work time. In these 
instances, managers may replace the tardy employee for the full shift. 
ii)  
Leaves work prior to the end of assigned/scheduled work time without 
prior supervisory approval. 
iii) 
Takes an extended meal or break period without approval 
iv)  Arrives to work past his/her scheduled start time may be replaced for the 
full shift at the discretion of his/her supervisor. 
 
5.2 Time Clocks and Failure to Clock in/out 
Employees are required to follow established guidelines for recording their actual 
hours worked. A missed clock in/out is a violation of this policy and includes: 
i) Failure to clock in/out on their allocated time clock (i.e. Kronos station) at 
the beginning and/or end of their assigned work shift; 
ii) Failure to clock in/out on their allotted time clock for the meal break; 
iii) Failure to exactly and timely report for work. 
iv) Clocking in/out early (or late) of assigned shift without prior approval. 
 
5.3 Departmental Notification Procedure 
Employees are expected to follow departmental notification procedures if they are 
late for work, absence from work, or requesting time off.  Punishment for late 
reporting for work is normally by pay cut.  Appropriate amount of worker’s salary will 
be deducted according to standard guidelines stipulated by the employer.  
Employees must request in advance through their superior and in accordance with 
departmental procedure if they wish to change or revise their assigned work shift.

7. 
Sense Of Ownership 
In the modern corporate environment, where time spent in one company has shortened 
drastically, it is becoming increasingly important to inculcate a sense of ownership 
amongst the employees to retain talent. Shreya Roy finds out how employees can be 
transformed into having sense of attachment to what they do. 
Compromising on mistakes is a very important part of building a sense of ownership 
amongst the employees. "In your own business, you are bound to go through trial and 
error, and you learn from them. Do that with your employees as well. Employees should 
feel safe and innovate by taking chances," says Maclean Raphael, executive director — 
human resources, 3M, India and Sri Lanka.

7.1 Let employees speak out 
Employees feel like part of a greater whole when they can contribute ideas and 
solutions to problems. Intel, for instance, used crowd-sourcing for inputs on how it 
could be a better place to work in. "The tool we used to crowd-source allowed 
employees to rate each other's inputs, and build on each other's ideas to make Intel 
a better place to work," says Preethi Madappa, HR director, Intel India. 
 
7.2 Clarify where things are headed 
Show employees the 'big picture' of the organization. Share the good news as well 
as the bad news. "Create visibility, use plain speak, talk about the challenges and 
explain what you are still trying to figure out in an open and transparent manner." 
adds Madappa. 
 
7.3 Illustrate benefits beyond money 
Employees need to see long-term benefits, and not just the show of money. "Provide 
them opportunities for learning and development, ask for contributions through 
action, thoughts, projects; implement the ones which can be taken up," says 
Sangeetalala, senior vice president and co-founder, Team Lease Services. 
 
7.4 
Show respect 
Nothing drives people as much as being treated well, with respect and in a level 
playing field. "It is important to practice equality and diversity across the organization.  
Any job you do is going to "have your fingerprints all over it." That is why it is so 
important to take ownership of your job, anything you do, and really own it. Do it the 
best you can; do it the best it can be done. That is how you succeed.

Additional Competencies 
A supervisor may add competencies that he or she believes are unique to the 
specific department or job.  Additional competencies are optional and may not 
be needed in many departments; however, competencies are not to be 
deleted.   
i. 
Performance Indicators 
Following each competency is a brief definition of the competency followed by a 
bulleted list of performance indicators.  Achievement in these areas will directly 
relate to successful performance.  The bulleted indicators are examples; they 
are intended to be illustrative only.  It is the supervisor’s responsibility to identify 
for the employee which of the indicators apply to the job and modify them, or 
create new indicators, as appropriate. It is not required to address all of the 
indicators.   
 
8.2 Class of competencies.   
There are classes of competency involve in management of hierarchy. One grade y 
be applicable to all staff positions.  Another is restricted to staff members who are in 
managerial/supervisory positions and/or who hold leadership roles.  The supervisor 
may determine whether this section applies to the employee based on his/her
