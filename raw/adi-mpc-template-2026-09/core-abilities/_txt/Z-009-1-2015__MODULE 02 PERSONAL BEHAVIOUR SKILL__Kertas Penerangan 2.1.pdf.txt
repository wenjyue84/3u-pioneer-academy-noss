1. Concept Of Honesty And Integrity 
 
  
1.1 Introduction 
 
i) 
The majority of humans prefer peace over war and conflict, appreciate 
stability and value happiness, enjoyment and comfort. Generally, chaos and 
conflict, killings, instability and anarchy are viewed as negative factors and 
do not resonate well with the reasonable man.    
ii) 
Observing thousands of years of documented human history, it seems clear 
that mankind will always strive towards betterment and improvement of the 
quality of life. They built ships to sail to explore new lands and acquire 
wealth and riches. They created technologically and mechanically advanced 
equipment like compasses, telephones, computers and machinery to elevate 
their quality of life which constantly push the boundaries to higher levels. 
Every effort of mankind seems to suggest that when given a choice, 
mankind will always work towards making life easier.     
iii) 
Poverty is usually due to circumstances of life and very rarely out of choice. 
People generally do not choose to be poor but are in that situation due to 
circumstances beyond their control. When given a chance to improve their 
lives and acquire wealth, they rarely would choose to return to the state of 
being poor and under privileged. This proves that mankind generally shuns 
hardship and will pick wealth over poverty when given a choice. An ideal 
situation would be individuals who work towards wealth, comfort and 
happiness without compromising or abusing the rights and boundaries of 
others. 
iv) 
Therefore it is safe to say that our integrity will always be tested especially 
when we have to make decisions on what is fair, just and correct.

1.2 Definition 
 
i) 
Definition of Integrity 
ii) 
The Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary defines “integrity” as “the quality of 
being honest with strong moral principles”. A person may possess personal, 
professional or artistic integrity. 
iii) The Macmillan Dictionary defines “integrity” as “The quality of always 
behaving according to the moral principles that you believe in, so that people 
respect and trust you” and  “the quality of behaving according to the rules and 
standards of your job or profession”. 
iv) From the above definitions, we may summarise that integrity denotes qualities 
of virtue, righteousness, goodness and morally good and correct behaviour. It 
also means obedience, cooperation, adherence and compliance. 
 
  
1.2 Definition of Honesty 
 
 
i) 
The Oxford Advanced Leaner’s Dictionary defines “honesty” as “always telling 
the truth, never stealing or cheating”. 
ii) 
The Macmillan Dictionary defines “honesty” as “a person who does not tell lies 
or cheat and obeys the law”. 
 
1.3 Integrity At The Workplace 
 
i) 
Integrity at the workplace is imperative for a healthy working environment. It 
does not matter what position one holds, whether it is the chief executive 
officer or the cleaner or the most junior staff, workplace integrity starts with 
honesty, dependability, trustworthiness and decency. Keeping to your word 
and being ethical and accountable in your actions creates respect and 
professionalism.  
ii) 
Examples of workplace integrity  
iii) 
Work when you are on the clock 
Working diligently when you're on the clock is a clear example of workplace 
integrity. Socialising, surfing the Internet, making personal phone calls, texting 
and frequent snacking are activities that detract from work time. Saving those 
activities for break time will show your boss, co-workers and customers that

Follow company policies 
Abiding by company policies is a powerful way to demonstrate integrity. 
Cutting corners and neglecting to follow workplace regulations can lead to 
mistakes, problems and even dangerous situations. Your willingness to 
properly record financial transactions, safely dispense of hazardous or toxic 
materials, follow company protocol for dealing with clients, perform clean-up or 
set-up procedures and properly maintain equipment shows others that you're 
not just looking for the easy way out. Establishing yourself as a trustworthy 
worker who submits to company policies shows your boss and co-workers that 
you'll faithfully carry out your duties. 
v) 
Respect co-workers and build trust 
Respecting those you work with reveals your desire to create a healthy work 
environment. Polite communication, appropriate interactions and respect for 
co-workers' thoughts and ideas demonstrate your ability to look beyond your 
own interests to pursue team-centered work goals. As you deal with co-
workers honestly and respectfully, you establish a level of trust with them. 
According to Amy Rees Anderson, a contributor to Forbes magazine, those 
who trust you will spread the word of that trust to their associates, and word of 
your character will spread like wildfire. 
vi) 
Exhibit responsible behaviour 
Integrity in the workplace often stems from moral and ethical behavior. Making 
sure there is no reason to question your conduct is one of the best ways to 
prove that you are an honest and dependable employee. Avoid using 
company products or equipment for personal use and submit exact receipts 
for travel or meal reimbursements. Don't over-promise what you can't provide 
and strive to meet deadlines. Work productively and cooperate during 
company meetings so you don't appear lazy or apathetic, and don't call in sick 
if you aren't. By exhibiting responsible behavior, you don't give co-workers or 
clients the opportunity to question your integrity. 
vii) 
The importance of integrity at the workplace 
Integrity is the foundation of character. A person who has integrity has 
developed core virtues such as compassion, dependability, generosity, 
honesty, kindness, loyalty, maturity, objectivity and trust that will ultimately 
bring value and success to any organisation.

2. 
Principles Of Confidentiality 
 
2.1 Introduction 
 
i) In any organisation, there is often a lot of data or information being received, 
recorded, kept and processed in both hardcopy and digital formats. Handling 
such information and data with confidentiality is part of ethical behaviour and one 
of the principles of integrity.    
 
2.2 Definition Of Confidentiality 
 
i) The Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary defines ”confidentiality” as 
”Information or data which is to be kept secret or private and not to be told or 
shared with other people”. 
 
2.3 Categories Of Confidential Information 
 
i) Confidential workplace information can be broken down into 3 main categories: 
 
a. 
Employee information 
Some organisations have rules that govern confidentiality and disposal of 
’personal indentifying information’. Employee information includes information 
such as social security numbers, home addresses, contact information, personal 
information, work history, medical history, family names, etc. 
b. 
Management information 
Confidential management information covers employee relations, disciplinary 
actions, investigation of misconduct, salary, remuneration, pay, overtime claims, 
impending lay-offs, etc. Disclosure of management information to unauthorised 
parties is unethical, counter-productive and creates damage and disharmony in 
the workplace. 
 
c. 
Business information

Confidential business information is sometimes known as ”proprietary 
information” or ”trade secrets” and refers to information not generally known to 
the public and would not ordinarily be available to competitors except through 
illegal or improper means. Examples of trade secrets include manufacturing 
process and methods, business plans, financial data, budgets and forecasts, 
computer programmes, clients’ lists, ingredient formulas or recipes, supplier lists, 
etc. 
 
2.4  The Importance Of Confidentiality In The Workplace 
 
i) Failure to properly secure and protect confidential business information can 
lead to loss of business and clients 
ii) Confidential information can be misused to commit illegal activity which may 
compromise parties eg. Fraud, cheating, forgery. 
iii) Disclosure of sensitive employee and management information can lead to a 
loss of employee trust, confidence and loyalty therefore resulting in a loss of 
productivity. 
 
 
3.  
Workplace Confidentiality  
 
3.1 Methods By Employers To Ensure Confidentiality In The Workplace 
i) Implement Confidentiality Policies and Procedures 
ii) Training of management and employees on confidentiality issues, policies and 
procedures 
iii) Enforcement of policies, procedures and training including disciplinary action 
iv) Non-Disclosure Agreement signed by employees to protect Business 
Information.  
 
3.2 Methods By Employees To Practice Confidentiality In The Workplace 
i) 
Ensure you have undergone training on company policies and procedures 
ii) 
Ensure you understand the training  
iii) Refer to the policies and procedures on a regular basis 
iv) Adhere to the policies and procedures 
v) 
Suggest continuous improvements or offer constructive feedback to the 
management regarding the policies and procedures

4. 
Ethical Guidelines 
 
4.1 Introduction 
i) Ethics refers to an external system of rules and law. There are rewards when the 
ethical guidelines are followed and adhered to and there is punishment when the 
rules are broken. An example of ethical guidelines are those drawn up by 
professional bodies for lawyers and doctors that monitor compliance and mete out 
the appropriate punishment or disciplinary action when the guidelines are 
breached.  
ii) Organisations often have a code of ethics which their employees are expected to  
obey.  
 
4.2 How To Apply The Code Of Ethics 
i) 
Understand the Code of Ethics 
ii) 
Refer to the Code of Ethics on a regular basis 
iii) Adhere to the Code of Ethics 
iv) Suggest continuous improvement or constructive feedback.
