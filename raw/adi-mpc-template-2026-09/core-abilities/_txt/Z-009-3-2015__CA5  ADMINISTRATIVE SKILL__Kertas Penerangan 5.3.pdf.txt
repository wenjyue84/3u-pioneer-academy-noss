1.  
HUMAN RESOURCE PLANNING 
 
The purpose of human resource planning is to ensure that a certain desired number of 
persons with the correct skills are available at the right time. Recruitment done with no 
proper planning may end up with excess or shortage of manning levels. 
 
1.1  Forecasting Demand for Human Resource 
 
It is the duty of human resource department to plan and manage sufficient man power for 
the job.  There are two aspects of planning, namely long term and short term of manning 
level.  
In Malaysia, it is very common to short term forecasting to meeting the company’s 
objectives, even though they fully understand the necessity of having long term planning 
of their human resources.  Very few actually do it. 
 
i)  
Long Term Forecasting of Human Resource Requirements 
 
Long term human resource planning is part of the organization strategic plans. 
Though forecasts of human resource requirements over five years are mere 
projections which may not be accurate, long term forecasting of two to five years is 
necessary to ensure that the company’s objectives are achieved. 
Knowledge of the organization’s long term corporate plans is essential so that it can 
be managed for the future needs of human resources 
Human resource planners should also be aware that advances in technology have 
definite effects on the demand for jobs in terms of nature and mixture of jobs 
availability. Advances in technology has resulted a decrease in the number of 
workers.  New machines lead to higher productivity. At the same time it decreases 
in demand for manpower. For example, the use of robots may result in the loss of 
certain manual jobs. 
ii)  
Short Term Forecasting of Human Resource Requirements 
Even though many organizations have started the practice of long term planning, 
there is still a need for short term planning as the human resource department

1.2 Human Resource Actions  
Human resource actions are vital whenever demand for human resources does not 
match the supply. The human resource department should manage the situation 
efficiently.  
When there are vacancies, replacement can be done either through new recruitments or 
a priority be given to qualified serving staff. Or alternatively the management may opt for 
overtime works to existing staff or put a strict control on absenteeism. 
i)  
Actions To Take When A Vacancy Occurs  
When a vacancy occurs, especially in a management job, firstly examine the 
reasons for the vacancy. Do not haste in filling it up.  An objective evaluation may 
save time and effort and help avoiding further expenses, disturbances or 
disruptions due to recruitment and selection. The most common reasons for a 
vacancy to occur are resignations, dismissals or new positions.

If the vacancy is due to a resignation, an exit interview should be conducted to find

organization or was it a case of ‘good riddance to bad rubbish’?  Some big

If the vacancy was due to an undesirable person being dismissed or forced to 
leave, again there is a need for analysis. It is obvious that a mistake was made in 
the first place and this analysis should be aimed at finding out the reason for the 
mistake. It could be due to the appointment of a square peg in a round hole due to 
educational background, personality, incompatibility with co-workers or bosses, or 
other reasons. The important point here is not to go on a witch-hunt but to 
objectively conduct an analysis so as not to repeat the mistake. 
A thorough analysis of human resource requirements should be conducted before a 
new vacancy is approved. Getting a new person in is easy compared to getting a 
person out of the organization. It would be a shame to hire a new person and find 
that there is not enough work for him or her to do, or to find that the work was only 
for a temporary period. 
 
If the result is that hiring of new staff is required than the next step is to conduct the 
recruitment and selection process. However, before conducting the recruitment and 
selection process we must obtain information as to what the job involves and what 
type of person is suitable for the job. This can be obtained from a job analysis.

A job description is a document that provides information regarding the tasks,

iii) 
Job Analysis Process 
The process of analyzing a job is essentially one of data collection. A substantial 
amount of information is required for successful accomplishment of job analysis. 
The job is first studied to determine the tasks to be performed, the duties, and the 
responsibilities. This information is then used to decide the level of knowledge, 
skills and abilities required for a person to perform the job efficiently. 
Other aspects of the job, such as tools, equipment or materials used, the products 
made, or the services performed are then looked into. Since jobs are often 
performed in conjunction with others in an organization, the relationships of work 
flow and procedures, as well as lines of promotion are also studied.  
In some organizations, the job analysis process also determines job standards. It 
involves the measurement of time taken to perform a task, or the output quantity 
per time period. 
Various approaches can be used in job analysis depending on the organizational 
needs and resources for conducting the task. Selection of a method depends on the 
information to be used and its practicality.

and selection of management jobs, the most popular approaches are

THE IMPORTANCE OF RECRUITMENT AND SELECTION 
One of the most important functions in human resource management is the recruitment 
and selection of people. Proper and effective recruitment and selection techniques will 
ensure that the company hires only competent, motivated and productive employees. 
There are cases whereby this process is left in the hands of unfamiliar personnel to make 
appropriate decisions. As a result, many hiring process were wrongly decided.  When 
recruitment and selection is not properly managed, it may lead to hiring of non-conformist 
employee who will eventually end up with resignation or be terminated within short period 
of service. Or else they are kept back as non performer workers. 
There is no fast and hard rule to prevent mistakes during recruitment and selection 
process.  To minimize such erroneous decision and progress with hiring the most

ALTERNATIVES TO RECRUITMENT   
Recruitment of employees is a tough work.  Recruitment and selection costs are high and 
the costs of maintaining the employees are also high.  Employees’ cost keep escalating 
as they will get their annual increment and other adjustments to salaries as well as 
increases in fringe benefits. Furthermore, once employees are hired and placed on the 
payroll, they become difficult and costly to remove. The employment act is enacted to 
protect the rights of employees and prevent them from being exploited or victimized by 
the employers. 
Due to the above hectic reasons, the organization should have the right consideration 
before adding up personnel to the payroll. There are various alternatives to substitute 
recruitment. 
4.1  Overtime  
Many organizations use overtime to compensate short term fluctuations in work volume. 
The overtime is considered as a common practice nowadays and is advantageous to 
both parties on win-win situation. 
The disadvantages on overtime are that it may create loopholes for employees to go on 
slow work so as to earn extra overtime pay.  Also when overtime work stops, the 
employees will face stress due to income reduction. As a result they become de-
motivated and their productivity drops. 
Too much overtime work may also cause fatigue and exhaustion to the employees and 
becoming less productive.  The fact is, some managers estimate that the productivity rate 
from an employee who works on overtime after his regular shift-work may produce only 
about two-thirds of his normal output. The overtime rates normally disburse no less than 
one and one half (1½) times the ordinary pay rate, but in return the employer gets only 
two-thirds of the normal work output. 
4.2  Subcontracting  
Some organizations farm-out their works as an alternative to recruitment of staff, 
especially in situations of temporary demand for human resource. This is because 
subcontracting is generally advantageous for both parties. 
Two opposing situations exist when subcontracting the organizations’ work.

STAFF TRAINING 
 
Employers should have clear goals for the training function in their organization. These 
goals include: 
i) 
Assisting employees in developing the skills and knowledge needed to 
perform effectively in their current jobs. 
ii) 
Preparing employees to take on a variety of jobs as and when required. 
iii) 
Helping employees develop and achieve their potential. 
Many organizations survive without offering any training to their employees. They believe 
that training is a waste of time and money. They might have gone through negative 
experiences with training in the past. Alternatively, they are willing to buy in employees

6.3  Determining Who Is To Facilitate the Training 
 
Training can be conducted by: 
 
i) 
Full-time trainers employed by the company who do nothing but organize and 
conduct training. 
ii) 
Senior and experienced staff who, on occasions, are required to share their 
knowledge and skill with their colleagues. 
iii) 
Free-lance training consultants and vendors who either conduct training 
themselves or source it to someone who will do it for them. 
 
Generally, large companies with thousands of workers find it economical to employ full-
time trainers. Even that it may still be necessary occasionally hire external trainers or 
consultants for the programs rarely offered. 
Where a very specialized or specific training is considered necessary, the best trainers 
will be selected from serving employees who are amongst top performers. In that case, it 
is vital that these ‘part-time’ internal trainers to undergo a thorough ‘Train the Trainer’ 
course to ensure they are proficient enough in conducting trainings. 
In various circumstances, internal workforce makes the best trainers. No one else could 
compete their comprehensive understanding of the company’s problems, procedures, 
requirements and restrictions. 
Selecting an external training provider is not an easy task.  Again you have to pick up 
preferences. Do you want to enter into a long-term relationship with the vendor who will 
provide most, if not all, your training needs?  If the expert cannot facilitate a particular 
program you ask for, they will look for necessary replacement for it.

8.1  Duration of programs  
The duration of the training program is determined by the scope of coverage.  However 
do not take too lightly on matters related to endurance and physical and mental stamina 
of the participants.  The most common practice and to have an effective session, a three 
day course would be pleasant. 
Two-way participative programs, where inputs from trainees are called for, will extend the 
interval fairly longer period. 
8.2  Timing of Program  
If programs are conducted outside normal office hours some other problems may pop-up.  
There will be refusal due to other personnel commitments.  Giving sufficient notice may 
help. Trainees may reschedule their daily routines.  On personal interest, trainees feel 
that they should be rewarded for extending extra hours for the sessions.
