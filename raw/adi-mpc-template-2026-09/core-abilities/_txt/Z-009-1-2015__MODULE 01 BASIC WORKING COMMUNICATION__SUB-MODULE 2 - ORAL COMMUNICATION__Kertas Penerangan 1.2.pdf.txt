1  Introduction To Communication 
1.1  Have you ever felt the messages you convey are not communicated properly or 
have you ever felt guilty of not conveying the message as it wants to be conveyed? If so 
it is because of your weakness towards communication skills. Apart from the basic  
necessities, you need to be equipped with habits for good communication skills, as  
this is what will make you a happy and successful social being. In order to develop  
these habits, you need to first acknowledge the fact that communication skills need  
an improvement from time to time. The only constant in life is change, and the more  
you accept your strengths and work towards dealing with shortcomings, especially in  
the area of communication skills, the better will be your interactions and the more  
your social popularity. Thus the present unit enables you to get a detailed picture of  
the need and importance of developing communication skills and feel confident and  
empowering to face any type of situation in life. 
   
1.2   We use ‘communication’ usually to speak, write or sending a message to another  
person. It involves a number of choices and decisions but being natural and  
unnoticed in informal situations. In formal situations, our communication needs to be  
more effective and carefully chosen, that is, we need to plan our communication.  
 
1.3   Communication may be broadly defined as the process of interaction among 
human beings. It is the process by which meanings are perceived and understandings 
are reached among human beings. It involve the cooperation between two parties, one  
active or at the giving end and the other passive or at the receiving end. 
 
1.4  The sender selects appropriate symbols to suit the situation and realizes the 
meaning through speech or writing depending upon the socially regulated requirements 
or self-perceived needs. At the receiving end the symbols are identified and 
identification obviously implies recognition and realization of meaning through the 
interpretive process.

from one individual or group to another by word of mouth.

b. Telephone talk.

4.  Advantages and Disadvantages of Oral Communication 
 
4.1 Advantages of Oral Communication 
 
i. There is high level of understanding and transparency in oral communication  
as it is interpersonal. 
 
ii. There is no element of rigidity in oral communication. There is flexibility for  
allowing changes in the decisions previously taken. 
 
iii. The feedback is spontaneous in case of oral communication. Thus decisions 
can be made quickly without any delay. 
 
iv. Oral communication is not only time saving, but it also saves upon money  
and efforts. 
 
v. Oral communication is best in case of problem resolution. The conflicts can be  
put to an end by talking them over. 
 
vi. Oral communication is an essential for teamwork and group energy. 
 
vii. Oral communication promotes a receptive and encouraging morale among  
organizational employees. 
 
viii. Oral communication can be best used to transfer private and confidential  
information / matter.

4.2:  Disadvantages/Limitations of Oral Communication 
 
i.  Relying only on oral communication may not be sufficient as business  
communication is formal and very organized. 
 
ii.  Oral communication is less authentic than written communication as they are  
informal and not as organized as written communication. 
 
iii. Oral communication is time-saving as far as daily interactions are concerned,  
but in case of meetings, long speeches consume lot of time and are  
unproductive at times.  
 
iv. Oral communications are not easy to maintain and thus they are unsteady. 
 
v. There may be misunderstandings as the information is not complete and may  
lack essentials.  
 
vi. It requires attentiveness and great receptivity on part of the receivers or  
audience. 
 
vii. Oral communication (such as speeches) is not frequently used as legal  
records except in investigation work 
 
   5.  Types of oral communication 
 
5.1 There are seven (7) forms that oral communication often takes: 
 
i. Intrapersonal communication is self-talk or a conversation you hold with 
yourself  under certain circumstances. For example, when you need to make an 
important decision or learn something about yourself. It is a form of thinking that 
goes on inside us which relies on language to express itself. Often increases self-
awareness and mindfulness, and hones critical thinking skills.   
 
ii. Interpersonal communication is communication between several people. This

6. Sign language and gestures 
6.1  A sign language and gesture can give clues and additional information and 
meaning over verbal communication. Its allow:- 
i. Reinforce or modify what is said in words. For example, people may nod their 
heads when saying "Yes" to emphasise that they agree with the other 
person.  
ii. Convey information about their emotional state. 
iii. Define or reinforce the relationship between people. 
iv. Provide feedback to the other person. 
v. Regulate the flow of communication, for example by signaling to others that  
 they have finished speaking or wish to say something. 
 
6.2 A smile, a gestures and several body movements send out a message to the  
people around you.

   8. Cultural awareness 
 
   8.1 Cultural sensitivity begins with the understanding that there are differences among 
     cultures. Knowing that cultural differences as well as similarities exist, without  
     assigning values (i.e. better or worse, right or wrong) to those cultural differences. 
 
 8.2 Developing sensitivity and understanding of another ethnic group usually involves  
     internal changes in attitudes and values. Awareness and sensitivity also refer to  
     the qualities of openness and flexibility that people develop in relation to others. 
 
 8.3  Familiarization with selected cultural characteristics, history, values, belief   
systems, and behaviours of the members of another ethic group. We can never learn  
     everything about another culture but we can take to develop our cultural awareness.  
            Here are some suggestions: 
i. Develop awareness. Take a look at your own biases and prejudices, become  
aware of cultural norms, attitudes, and beliefs, and value diversity; 
ii. Don’t allow cultural differences (preferences) to become the basis for criticism  
and judgements.  
iii. Build friendships of mutual respect and a desire for understanding. 
iv. Be yourself and show that you care about the person and that you honestly 
want  to help. 
v. Take time to listen. If you don’t understand, or you are not being understood,  
take time to find out why or ask questions.  
vi. Acquire knowledge about other cultures by attending classes or seminars,  
reading books or watching movies about other cultures, and attending cultural  
events / festivals.
