1. Organisation 
1.1 An organisation is an entity comprising multiple people such as an institution 
or an association that has a collective goal. 
1.2 It is widely recognized that different organisations have distinctive cultures. 
1.3 A commonly used definition of organi sational culture is “the way w e see and 
do things around here”. 
1.4 Through tradition,  history and structure, organisation s build up their own 
culture. 
1.5 Culture therefore gives an organi sation a sense of identity – “who we are”, 
“what we stand for” and “what we do”.  It determines through t he organisation ’s 
legends, rituals, beliefs, meanings, values, norms and language, the way in which 
“things are done around here”.

2. Branding 
2.1 Branding is a marketing strategy that involves creating a  differentiated name 
and image, often using a logo and / or tag line in order to establish a presence in 
the consumer’s mind and attract and keep customers. 
2.2 Branding should both precede and underlie any marketing effort.  Branding 
is not push but pull.  Br anding is the expression of the essential truth or value of an 
organization, product or service. 
2.3 It is communication of characteristics, values and attributes that clarify what 
this particular brand is and is not. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Figure 2.1 : Example meaning of branding 
 
2.4 A brand will help encourage someone to buy a product and it directly 
supports whatever sales or marketing activities are in play, but the brand does not 
explicit say “buy me”. 
2.5 Instead it says “This is what I am. This is why I exist. If you agree, if you like 
me, you can buy me, support me and recommend me to your friends” 
2.6 The brand is what remains after the marketing has swept through the room. 
2.7 It is what sticks in your mind associated with a product, service or 
organization – whether or not, at that particular moments you bought or did not buy. 
2.8 The brand is ultimately what determines if you will become a loyal customer 
or not.  The marketing may convince you to buy a particular Toyota, and maybe it is 
the first foreign car you ever owned, but it is the brand that will determine if you will 
only buy Toyotas for the rest of your life.

3. Culture 
3.1 Over time the organization will develop “norms” ie. establish ed expected 
behavior patterns within the organization.  A norm is an established behavior 
pattern that is part of a culture. 
3.2 At the base of the identity of an organization is its organizational culture.  A 
culture is comprised of the shared values, customs, traditions, rituals, behaviors 
and beliefs shared by a social group. 
3.3 Cultures also share languages or ways of speaking. 
3.4 From a communic ation perspective, cultures are made and remade through 
the words we use to describe our world. 
3.5 Culture change with the times but the speed at which the culture of different 
institutions changes varies widely. 
3.6 Organisational culture plays several important roles ie. 
i) culture unites employees by providing a sense of identity with the 
organization. 
ii) culture enables organisations to differentiate themselves from one 
another. 
iii) Culture often generates commitment, superceding personal interests. 
iv) Culture sets organization norms, rules and standards.  Thereby, culture 
enables employees to function in an organization by teaching them how 
to behave. 
v) Culture becomes especially important in a program / project based 
organisations.  In such an organization, the hiera rchy is flat and decision-
making moved to the project / programme purpose units and 
departments.  In this context, culture provides the guiding light towards 
achievement of goals and objectives.

5.  Ideas of Branding 
5.1 Branding is one of the most important aspects of any business.  An effective 
brand strategy gives you a major edge in increasingly competitive markets. 
5.2 Your brand is your promise to your customer.  It  tells them what they can 
expect from your products and services, and it differentiates your offering from 
your competitions’.

5.3 Your brand is derived from who you are, who you want to be and who people 
perceive you to be. 
5.4 Normally consumers have more than one need, and analyzing the 
multiplicity of needs provides insight for marketers. 
5.5 The concept of attitudes will lead us to several interesting and useful insights 
about consumers because the concept deals with both internal aspects of 
consumer mindset and the intention to buy products and brands. 
5.6 Attitudes serve as quick reference points to consumer behavior.  In the case 
of a new -concept products, consumers develop an attitude towards the product 
category. 
5.7 When they accept the product category, they develop an attitude towards the 
specific brands that can be measured by appropriate marketing research 
techniques. 
5.8 There is a strong link between beliefs, attitudes and values of consumers. 
5.9 Time tested tips on branding are 
i. get a great logo 
ii. write down our brand messaging 
iii. integrate our brand 
iv. create a “voice” for our company that reflects our brand 
v. develop a tagline 
vi. design templates and create br and standards for your marketing 
materials 
vii. be true to your brand 
viii. be consistent 
5.10 Do your research.  Learn the needs, habits and desires of your current and 
prospective customers.  Don’t rely on what you think they think. Know what 
they think.
