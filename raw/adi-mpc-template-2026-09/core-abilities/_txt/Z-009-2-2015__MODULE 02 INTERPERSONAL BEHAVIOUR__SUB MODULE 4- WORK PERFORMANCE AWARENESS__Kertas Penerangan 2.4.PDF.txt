1. 
Overview 
Many corporations are focused on growth with the least cost.  Achieving high revenue with 
profit through great level of work force efficiency.  Leaders are looking for a break through 
performance and productivity gains from employees. To achieve these gains, organizations 
have to be ready with dramatic shifts underway in the work environment and refocus on 
enabling higher levels of workforce performance. 
 
2. 
Setting Performance Standards / Expectations 
In view of setting up performance standards and expectations, a manager needs to obliged by 
the followings; 
i) 
Review job description for the position  
ii) 
Determine desirable result for most of the employees duties  
iii) 
Describe the quality performance required such as communication, customer 
service, interpersonal skills, etc. 
iv) 
Describe how much work must be accomplished within prescribed period with 
standard of accuracy, document style/format or effectiveness  
v) 
Ensure results are achievable and realistic. Indicate needed source of reference 
vi) 
Determine how performance of duty will be measured 
 
2.1  Advantages to setting performance standard / expectations 
Setting performance targets for your organisation does not warrant an increase in your bottom 
line.  Fixing goals setting will help the company and staff working for a common result.  
Meeting important targets will initiate the company's current success and future growth. Your 
goals should be measurable so that you can achieve a quantitative analysis of your progress. 
Identifying the vital indicators of your company's success will help you establish accurate 
performance targets. For example, the number of customers you have may not be as 
important as the total revenue your sales generate.

by the management of an organisation.

Interact professionally, courteously, efficiently and friendly when dealing with 
customers, the general public and co-workers  
ii) 
Achieve high level of productivity and accuracy  
iii) 
Punctuality in attendance and work presentations

3. 
Provide feedback  
The return of information about the results of processes or activities. It is used for evaluating 
the response or finding for betterment.  Feedback should fulfill these criteria. 
i) 
Should be specific  
ii) 
Should be timely and/or immediate  
iii) 
Focused on behaviour or performance and not the person  
iv) 
Intended to help and not for control purpose 
v) 
Sincere and faithful in giving information 
vi) 
Frequent and informal  
vii) 
Well documented 
 
3.1 Forms of feedback 
Feedback can be tracked intentionally or unintentionally. Feedback should help an employee 
feel motivated to improve their performance.  Amongst the most common feedbacks are 
conveyed through; 
i) 
Verbal comments - praise/criticism 
ii) 
Non-verbal gestures and cues or silence responds 
iii) 
Memorandums or e-mails 
iv) 
Strike-through and corrections on written work

4.1 Change and Agility 
Effectively deals with change and shifts gears when needed.  It is productive and can move 
forward on work efforts without having all the information by maintaining positive outlook when 
plans are challenged. Change should be able to manage risk and uncertainty by 
communicating to all concerns about the change in order to avoid negative behaviors or 
resistance to change. 
4.2 Creativity  
It developes innovative ideas and methods of doing things.  Searches for new and more 
effective methods, to connect the past unrelated idea s which contribute in brainstorming 
settings. Creativity is very resourceful when challenged by obstacles. 
 
4.3 Leadership 
Serves as a role model for others.  Inspires respect and trust; fosters a culture with high 
standards of ethics.  Good leadership reacts well under pressure; maintains focus and 
intensity, remains optimistic and persistent when facing adversity. High quality leaders recover 
quickly from setbacks. 
Among other things, a leader functions to influence others to transfer vision into action.  He 
assumes a long-term view and acts as a catalyst for organizational change.  Competent in 
building consensus to tolerate cooperation in order to accomplish goals. 
 
4.4 Meetings 
Accountable to put everything in order, participates actively and follows up on actions as 
appropriate.  Minimizes meetings when possible by using alternative communication 
mechanisms. 
 
4.5 Problem Solving 
Problem solving is accomplished after a combination of logic, analysis, and knowledge are 
considered. Probe appropriate sources to identify the benefits, drawbacks, and probable 
outcomes for each proposed solution. It is also equally important to be aware of levels of 
inclusion necessary for ownership and effective action.

5.  
Quality of Work on Performance Improvement 
Keep the discussion positive and informal.  Nothing produces anxiety in managers and 
employees quite like a performance review.  Unless everything is perfect and there is no room 
for improvement, you have to provide constructive criticism and your employee has to listen.  
Quality of work is especially difficult because you want to help your employees improve without 
causing them to become defensive. Planning and organizing on your part in developing and 
presenting the review can lead to improved performance and a stronger working relationship. 
i) 
Step 1 
Approach the interview with a focused plan. Prepare a list of strengths and weaknesses, 
including examples, if needed, and strategies for growth to discuss with your employees. 
Prioritize your concerns so that the most important issues can be resolved first. 
ii) 
Step 2 
Discuss examples of especially strong performance. Starting with your employees 
strengths helps them relax and be more open to feedback concerning weaker areas. In 
addition, it helps them focus on what you like, enabling them to continue developing 
those skills. 
iii) 
Step 3 
Organize areas of weakness into themes. For example, your employees may struggle 
with customer service. Instead of discussing phone calls, returns and follow-up 
separately, group them together into "customer service." A few themed areas of 
improvement are easier to absorb than a laundry list of specific problems. 
iv) 
Step 4 
Ask your employees to comment on areas of concern. Prompt them to discuss both what 
they enjoy most about those tasks and what they struggle with. This approach sets up a 
dialogue that you can use to work together to help them improve their performance. For

Emphasize your employees’ job performance in relation to their personal goals. Helping

motivate them to address areas of weakness. For example, an employee who wants to 
go into marketing, but has weak organizational skills, might not realize the effect his 
disorganized presentation has. Show hem that customers often perceive disorganization 
as amateurish, resulting in a poor image presentation and a negative marketing effect. 
vii) Step 7 
Focus on a poor fit, or your inability to provide sufficient training, if you have to terminate 
some employees. Emphasize the positive aspects of their performance, and suggest jobs 
that might be a better match for their skills or additional training from which they might 
benefit. Keeping the conversation proactive when the review becomes an exit interview 
reduces defensiveness and might help your employees find a more satisfying position.
