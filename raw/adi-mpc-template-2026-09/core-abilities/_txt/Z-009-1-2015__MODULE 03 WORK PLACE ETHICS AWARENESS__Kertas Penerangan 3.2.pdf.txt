What is employee discipline? 
 
According to the business dictionary, employee discipline is defined as the regulations or 
conditions that are imposed on employees by management in order to either correct or 
prevent behaviors that are detrimental to an organization.

The purpose of employee discipline is not to embarrass or degrade an employee.  The 
purpose is to ensure that an employee performs in a manner that is deemed acceptable 
by the organization. 
Over the years, the concept of employee discipline has grown.  Management realized that 
discipline is a part of the job.  They also realize that there must be disciplinary action 
within an organization to maintain some sense of control. 
In today’s workplace, discipline and documentation go hand in hand.  Most disciplinary 
action policies are progressive.  This means that rule violations increase, so do the 
consequences.  To protect the organization, adequate documentation of the rule violation 
and consequences must exist. 
Human resources experts advise that workplace discipline is best administered in a 
private, one-on-one conference that focuses on behavior not personalities.  It is important 
not to ignore the issue related to discipline as it may disturb organization’s progress. 
The employee has to know what is expected from them at the time they are performing 
their task, what they receive upon successfully completing that task and what they will 
receive if the path to the task is deviated.  Once this has been established, the means of 
discipline employee can be successful.

1. Discipline guideline 
 
1.1 
Discipline is training to act in accordance with rules.  It is a behavior in 
accord with rules of conduct and order maintained by training and control. 
1.2 
Self-discipline can be considered a type of selective training, creating new 
habits of thought, action and speech toward improving employee and reaching 
goals. 
1.3 
These guidelines are intended to review the most effective approach in 
managing performance and/or discipline issues. 
1.4 
Discipline issues generally result in disruption to the work environment; for 
example workplace misconduct and/or rules violations as well as performance 
issues, result in a failure to meet goals and/or properly perform tasks such as when 
an individual lacks the knowledge, skills or ability to perform the job, or where the 
work is consistently unacceptable in terms of quality of productivity. 
1.5 
It is employer responsibility to explain to the employee those areas in which 
he or she is expected to improve, to make suggestions about how to improve and 
to allow time for the employee to make improvements.

2. Principle of confidentiality 
 
2.1 
In 
today’s 
increasingly 
litigious 
anf 
highly 
competitive 
workplace, 
confidentiality is important for a host of reasons.  Failure to properly secure and 
protect confidential business information can lead to the loss of business or clients. 
2.2 
Confidentiality is a set of rules or a promise that limits access or places 
restrictions on certain types of information. 
2.3 
In the wrong hands, confidential information can be misused to commit 
illegal activity e.g. fraud or discrimination which can in turn result in costly lawsuit 
for the employer. 
2.4 
Company has laws protecting the confidentiality of certain information in 
workplace.  The disclosure of sensitive employee and management information can 
lead to a loss of employee trust, confidence and loyalty.  This will almost always 
result in a loss of productivity.

3. Standard and codes of practice 
 
3.1 
Whether written of communicated informally, a code of conduct defines 
behavior expectations for both management and other employees. 
3.2 
Practice is perform or exercise repeatedly or regularly in order to improve or 
maintain one’s proficiency. 
3.3

4. Working guideline 
 
4.1 
Many rules are already in place in the form of policies and laws, but some 
rules and procedures need to be department specific such as : 
i) 
How your area works together to meet customer expectations. 
ii) 
Procedures for employees reporting absences 
iii) Procedures for requesting time off for illness, vacation, emergency leave, 
bereavement leave and jury duty. 
iv) Workweek departmental hours and whether or not flexible work 
arrangements are permitted. 
4.2 
It is important that they are in compliance with existing policies and laws, and 
that they are practical, clear and understandable.  They must also be enforceable 
and applied to all employees. 
4.3 
Working guidelines can be very helpful by providing continuity and equity in 
the workplace.  Having a document in writing that has been given to employees can 
help ensure that everyone understands the procedures that are in place for 
employee area.

5. Social interactions procedures 
 
5.1 
A social interaction is an exchange between two or more individuals and is a 
building block of society. 
5.2 
This procedure defines the core principles that govern employee behavior 
and how the company conducts its business, as well as provides tools and 
resources to help employees to comply. 
5.3 
Social interaction at the workplace promotes enhanced collaboration, higher 
metacognition, richer sensory experience by way of emotion, better planning where 
each member feels included and better understanding of common values and 
purpose. 
5.4 
Employer which promote social interaction within organization are able to 
engage wider range of human intelligence which includes physiological, social, 
emotional, constructive, reflective and dispositional. 
5.5 
In this manner, a wider range of human intelligence is integrated in a natural 
manner to achieve the individual and organization goal.
