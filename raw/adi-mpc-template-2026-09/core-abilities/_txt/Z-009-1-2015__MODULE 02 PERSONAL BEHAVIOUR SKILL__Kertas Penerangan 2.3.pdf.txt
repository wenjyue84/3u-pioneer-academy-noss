1 Teams In The Workplace 
 
1.1 Most of  us are already familiar with the idea of a team especially if we’ve ever 
participated in or watched any type of organized sports activity. Work teams 
are formal groups, made up of interdependent individuals responsible for 
attaining a goal. Thus all work teams are groups but only formal groups can 
be work teams. 
 
1.2 Teams can vary in their purpose or goal. The duration of a team can be either 
permanent or temporary. Functional department teams and others that are 
part of the organization’s formal structure is a type of permanent team. 
Temporary teams include task forces, project teams, problem-solving teams 
and any other types of  short term teams created to develop, analyze or study 
a business or work-related issue. 
 
1.3 Team membership can either be functional or cross-functional. Functional 
teams are composed of a manager and his or her  subordinates from a 
particular functional area. Functional team are often involved in efforts to 
improve work activities or solve specific problems within that particular area 
only. Within this functional team, issues such as authority, decision-making, 
leadership and interaction are relatively simple and clear.  
 
1.4 The cross-functional team has members from various functional areas, with 
different areas of expertise and from different organizational levels, and often 
formed to tackle issues which affect the whole organization. For example, the 
team could involve members from Production, Marketing, Accounts, Research 
& Development, CEO’s office etc. The team is often led by a facilitator rather 
than a manager, with leaders appointed for different workgroups within the 
team. Decisions are much more consensus-based. 
 
1.5 Teams can either be supervised or self-managed.  A supervised team will be 
under the direction of a manager who’s responsible for guiding the team in

2 Concept Of Teamwork 
 
2.1 Good teams enable each member to be more than they would be as just 
individuals. This is because Team Spirit means members demand a lot from 
each other, go extra lengths to support each other, have high motivation to 
achieve their joint vision, and enjoy high morale. 
 
2.2 Work teams are critical for companies because they allow for better and faster 
decisions to be made by those who are close to the issue, and free up 
managers to focus on strategic and longer term matters. 
 
2.3 The use of diverse and cross-functional teams should result in more 
innovative ideas and better decisions than might arise if individuals operate 
alone. This is due to the sharing of different experiences and knowledge and 
the use of different perspectives to consider the facts.  
 
2.4 Some of the traits of Effective Teams are 
i) 
A shared sense of purpose. All team members are clear on what their 
team’s vision and goals are, from the big picture, down to the 
milestones which need to be achieved along the way. 
ii) 
Clear roles. Team members know what is expected of each member, 
there’s accountablity and no unnecessary duplication or ”dead wood”. 
iii) 
Defined team process. Team members not only know why and what 
they will achieve, they also know how they will go about achieving it.

iv) 
Strong relationships. Members of effective teams  have good rapport 
with one another, and and kind and caring towards one another. 
v) 
Effective interactions between team members and with the manager. 
This means communication is open and honest, feedback is frequent 
and constructive and there is timely follow-through action.   
 
2.5 Although it is mainly the role of the team leader or manager to ensure that 
their team embodies these traits, he/she cannot succeed unless all team 
members also appreciate the importance of embracing these team ideals.  
 
2.6 Some of the typical obstacles which detract from the effectiveness of a team 
are:  
i) 
The team is too big. The most effective teams are smaller rather than 
bigger, avoiding ”dead wood” and duplications in their structure. 
ii) 
The team members are not aligned on vision, goals or strategy, either 
because they are poorly informed, or they have not bought into these 
elements.   
iii) 
Poor, unhealthy interaction between members. This may happen 
because team members do not really feel they belong to the team, 
and are functioning like a collection of strangers, or worse still, 
competitors, rather than partners. 
iv) 
The team leader micro-manages and fails to delegate to, and 
empower, team members. 
v) 
Team members are not disciplined, accountable and committed. 
When one or two members consistently under-deliver, and these 
”weak links” are not reprimanded for it, this affects the morale of the 
whole team.

4 Information Sharing 
 
4.1 Not surprisingly, effective teams are characterized by good communication. 
Members are able to convey messages between each other in a form that is 
readily and clearly understood. This includes nonverbal as well as spoken 
messages. 
 
4.2 Members also take responsiblity for the dissemination of relevant information 
to the parties that need it. They double check that their colleagues are in the 
know. Miscommunication or non-communication rarely happens in effective 
teams.

4.3 The communication culture in high-performing teams is open. Members on 
are able to quickly and efficiently share ideas and feelings directly and be 
assured that their points will be taken into consideration, without 
prejudgement. Due to this open atmosphere, they feel confident to suggest 
something creative or different. 
 
4.4 Communication in high-performing teams is also characterized by frequent, 
kind and healthy feedback from team members and management. This helps 
to guide fellow team members and even the team leader, to correct any 
weaknesses, and ensure consistent high performance. 
 
5 Basic Problem-Solving Steps And Process 
 
5.1 Very often, team are formed to solve a problem. As such, one of the most 
important skills all teams must have is problem-solving. The most basic, and 
perhaps most widely used, technique for problem-solving is brainstorming.  
 
5.2 Brainstorming uses a face-to-face, interactive group to spontaneously suggest 
ideas for problem solution.’’ Brainstorming is perhaps the best- known 
decision aid; its primary role is to supply additional creative solutions. The 
brainstorming technique encourages group members to suggest alternatives 
regardless of their likelihood of being implemented. No critical comments of 
any kind are allowed until all suggestions have been listed. Members are 
encouraged to brainstorm possible solutions out loud, and freewheeling is 
welcomed. The more novel and unusual the idea, the better. The object of 
brainstorming is to promote freer, more flexible thinking and to enable group 
members to build on one another’s creativity.

6 Socio-Cultural Communication 
 
5.1 The more diverse teams are, the more risk there is about miscommunication 
and misunderstandings around verbal and non verbal communication. This is 
because people from different backgrounds have different norms for verbal 
and non verbal communication. 
 
5.2 For example, traditionally in Malaysia, it can be deemed disrespectful to 
criticize or disagree openly, and it may be deemed acceptable to give vague 
commitments. The more subtle communication used by persons from such a 
background may not be well-understood by those from more modern 
backgrounds. Conversely, the former may also feel upset by the directness 
and assertiveness of the communication by the latter. 
 
5.3 In diverse teams, it becomes very important for the facilitator or team manager 
to set clear ground rules about communication upfront, specifically about: 
 
i) 
Taking care of clarity of what is being said 
ii) 
Directness (going straight to the point)  
iii) 
Specificity of commitments 
 
5.4 Team members should also make effort to be aware of their default styles, 
and adapt to the style required for the group.
