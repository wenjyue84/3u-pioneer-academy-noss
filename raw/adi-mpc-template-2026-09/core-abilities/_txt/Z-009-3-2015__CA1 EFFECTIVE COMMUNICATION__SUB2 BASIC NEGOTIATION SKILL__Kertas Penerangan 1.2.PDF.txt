1.  Definition Of Negotiation 
1.1 Negotiation can be defined as bargaining process between two or more parties to   
discover common ground and reach an agreement to settle differences of mutual 
concern or resolve a conflict. It is a process by which a compromise or agreement is 
achieved while avoiding non-productive argument and dispute. In a simpler explanation, 
negotiation is process of bargaining your agreeable wants while avoiding conflicts..  
 
1.2 Understanding the negotiation process increases the chance of positive outcomes 
for each parties while still maintaining effective working relationships. Negotiation 
occurs in business, organizations, government, legal proceedings, among nations and 
in personal situations. 
 
1.3 In any disagreement, any individuals is understandably aiming to achieve the best 
possible outcome for their position or an organization. However, in the principles of 
fairness, seeking mutual benefit and maintaining a relationship are the keys to a 
successful outcome. 
 
2.  Importance of Negotiation 
2.1 Negotiation is an important skill in life. Difference in interests, views and opinions 
makes conflicts bound to happen. Negotiation skill is a tool to achieve results while 
resolve lingering issues.  
 
 2.2 It is inevitable that from time-to-time, conflict and disagreement will arise as the 
differing needs, aims and beliefs of people are brought together and collide.  Without 
negotiation, such conflicts may lead to argument and resentment resulting in one or all 
of the parties feeling dissatisfied. The objective of negotiation is to reach a mutual 
agreement without causing future barriers to further development or works.  Any 
misunderstanding that arises between parties will propagate their prejudices and trigger 
unwanted emotion.

3.  Characteristic Of Good Negotiator 
3.1 There are seven (7) basic characteristic of a good negotiator : 
i) 
Good listener.  
ii) 
Good communicator 
iii) 
Posses appropriate knowledge  
iv) 
Decisive  
v) 
Self-confident 
vi) 
Understand and recognizes the value of relationships 
 
4.  Negotiation styles 
     There are five styles of negotiation. These styles can be used based on individuals’  
     preferences depending on their pro-self or pro-social goals.  
     4.1  Accommodating.  
            For individuals who enjoy solving problems while preserving personal 
relationships. Accommodators are sensitive to the emotional states, body  
            language, and verbal signals of the other parties. They can, however, feel as if  
            being taken advantage in situations when the other party places little emphasis  
            on the relationship.  
  
    4.2  Avoiding.  
           For individuals who do not like to negotiate and avoid it unless warranted. When  
           negotiating, avoiders tend to defer and dodge the confrontational aspects of 
           negotiating. However, they may be perceived as tactful and diplomatic.  
 
   4.3  Collaborating. 
          For individuals who enjoy negotiations that involve solving tough problems in  
          creative ways. Collaborators are good at using negotiations to understand the  
          concerns and interests of the other parties. They can, however, create problems by  
          transforming simple situations into more complex ones.  
 
  4.4  Competing.  
         Some individuals enjoy negotiations activity because they present an opportunity to  
         win something. Competitive negotiators have strong instincts for all aspects of

5.  Basic principles for negotiation  
     5.1 The five (5) basic principles in a good  negotiation process : 
            i)  Preparation (Gather the information).  
                Information is the foundation of negotiation. Without knowing who our  
                counterpart is, it is almost impossible to establish any good negotiation points.  
                We must know the background of the parties’ and what are the parties’ goals  
                and issues. A good strategy for gathering information would be to answer the  
                following questions: 
a) What kind of information do we want to know? 
b) Where can we get this information? 
c) Can we learn anything from past negotiations with this party? 
d) How much business are we doing with this party? 
e) What is the company’s performance? 
f) 
What do you want the other company to know about you? 
 
          ii)  Establish relationships.  
               Trust is essential for any conflict handling. It must be established through  
               relationships or even friendship but building relationships takes time and  
               dedication.

6.  Types of Negotiation  
      6.1 There are 7 types of negotiations. It's important to consider which type you're  
      facing because each demands a different strategy.  
 
            i)  Win - Lose Negotiations 
                 In game theory they call a win-lose negotiation a zero-sum game. A common  
                 analogy for a zero-sum game is dividing a pie. The pie doesn't get smaller or  
                 bigger — the players play a game to decide who gets the bigger slice. If you're  
                 facing a win-lose negotiation focus your strategy on determining the other  
                 party's minimum requirements.   
 
     ii)  Win-Win Negotiations 
      Win-win negotiations involve expanding the pie. For example, if two people                
      decide to go into business together: their partnership negotiations are win-win.  
 
                However, a win-win outcome is possible because they hope to make money on 
                their investment (expand the pie). Salary negotiations and business-to-business  
                sales can usually be considered win-win.  
 
               Win-win negotiations may be just as focused on building a bigger pie as  
     dividing the pie fairly. Every effort should be made to keep negotiations friendly  
     and constructive.  
 
     iii)  Lose-Lose 
               Lose-Lose negotiations involve a situation in which everyone is going to lose.                    
               Lose-lose negotiations can quickly turn bitter and adversarial. Despite the fact  
               that both parties will lose it's important to try to maintain a collaborative  
               approach.  
 
    Let's say you leave your jacket at a restaurant coat-check and they lose it.  
              Your negotiations for compensation with the restaurant's manager are lose-lose. 
              You not likely to get more money than the coat was worth. The restaurant also

        iv)  Adversarial Negotiations 
             Adversarial negotiations are highly competitive in nature. This type of  
             negotiations require battle strategies.  
 
             Win-lose and lose-lose negotiations are most likely to be adversarial. Nobody  
             wants to lose, this tends to drive intense competition. In some cases, win-win  
             negotiations are also adversarial. For example, high stakes business-to- 
             business sales negotiations often become adversarial (customer vs seller).  
 
             In extreme cases, negotiations are adversarial because the parties involved  
             intensely dislike each other. In such cases, negotiators may not be interested  
             in winning. Instead, they may seek to maximize the losses of the other party.  
             Negotiations between political rivals may turn destructive in this way.  
 
        v. Collaborative Negotiations 
            Collaborative negotiations are creative and friendly. For example, business  
            partnership negotiations are often collaborative. Win-win negotiations that are  
            expected to yield big wins tend to be collaborative. Collaborative negotiations rely  
            on persuasive techniques, optimism and creativity     
 
      vi)  Multi-Party Negotiations 
            Multi-Party negotiations are complex negotiations between two or more  
            parties. They can be extremely challenging and may take years to complete.  
            International treaties between nations are often multi-party. This type of   
            negotiations require advanced diplomatic techniques.  
 
      vii) Bad Faith Negotiation 
            Bad faith negotiation occurs when a party pretend to negotiate but secretly has 
no intention of compromising. Bad faith negotiation is often used as a delay or      
diversionary tactic. For example, a country may sign an environmental treaty with   
no intention of implementing it just to relieve political pressure from its citizens.

7.   Negotiation Technique 
Negotiating techniques are important in getting the best, possible outcome. A good 
knowledge of negotiating techniques will enable you to maximize your confidence, 
increased harmony among the team, leading to the desired outcome while still 
maintaining effective working relationships toward future success. 
 
     There are several basic negotiation techniques to various situations: 
i) 
Assessing your needs and wants and what you are really after. 
ii) 
Knowing the other parties’ needs and wants.  
iii) 
Staging the discussion.  
iv) 
Identifying areas for compromise.  
v) 
Following-up with an action plan. 
 
      Before you go into any negotiation, make sure you have considered your needs and  
      what you want to achieve. Identify each of the needs and wants from various angles  
      such as the root cause, implications, and some possible solutions.  
 
      Document the ideas/points during formal negotiation meetings. This will help you              
      focus on the discussion and give credibility to your argument. Don’t leave               
      unaddressed points because it can cause distress and obstacles that are not easily  
      overcome.  
 
      Focus on the discussion points. If additional discussion points arise, do not add  
      them to the current meeting. Be polite, calm and move for a short break if the 
negotiation becomes into an argumentative situation and tensions increased. 
 
      Never begin with your final offer. Give yourself a sufficient “cushion” between what  
      you are asking for and what you actually want. Some of the negotiation items will               
      be used to barter and trade for priority needs on achieving at least some of their               
      goals. This will enable you to make compromises without giving up anything

8.  Negotiation Process 
 8.1  Negotiation Stage 
In order to achieve a desirable outcome, it may be useful to follow a structured                   
approach to negotiation. There are five (5) process stages of negotiation:- 
i) 
Preparation and planning 
ii) 
Clarification and justification 
iii) 
Bargaining     
iv) 
Agreement 
v) 
Implementation of a course of action 
  
                 i) Preparation and planning 
                    Before any negotiation takes place, a decision needs to be taken as when 
and where will the meeting held and who will participate. Setting a limit time-
scale can prevent the disagreement continuing.  
 
                    Undertaking preparation before discussing the disagreement will help to  
                    avoid further conflict and unnecessarily wasting time during the meeting. 
 
                ii) Clarifying and justification 
                    Clarification and justification is an essential part of the negotiation process.  
                    Both parties will be discussing at length. Each side will get a chance to  
                    explain, justify and support their original request. Key skills during this stage  
                    include  questioning, listening and clarifying. Without it, misunderstandings  
                    are likely to occur  which may cause problems and barriers to reaching a  
                    beneficial outcome. 
                  
                    From the discussion, goals, interests and viewpoints of both sides of         
                    the negotiation need to be clarified. It is helpful to list the factors in order of  
                    priority. Through this clarification it is often possible to identify or establish  
                    some common ground.

              iii)  Bargaining 
                    The bargaining stage is the most important stage where both sides feel they  
                    have gained something positive through the process of negotiation and both  
                    sides feel their point of view has been taken into consideration. This mean  
                    that each party would have to give up something to gain another. It is

                    This is where the actual deal will begin to take place with terms and

                     agreement needs to be made perfectly clear so that both sides know what  
                     has been decided. 
 
                v)  Implementing a Course of Action

10.  Negotiation Outcome 
 10.1  Negotiating outcomes are the types of results that can happen at the end of a 
 negotiation. Obviously, the goal in a negotiation is for both parties to walk away with        
 their needs being satisfied.  
 
     10.2  All negotiations end up with one out of four possible outcomes:  
 
          i)  One party wins and the other loses.  
               This outcome is known as win-lose scenario. Both sides attempt to win, without  
              much regard for the outcome of the other party. In this scenario, both parties may  
              enter the negotiation with a best case scenario and a "walk away" point. In a Win  
              - Lose scenario, one party falls within their target range (or better) and the other  
              party falls outside their target range.  
      
              In this type of outcome, one side wins and the other side loses. There is no  
              compromise with a win-lose outcome. It's a one-side takes all battle with one side  
              getting all their needs satisfied and the other side getting nothing. While the side  
              that wins may be very happy about the outcome; the losing side has a high level  
              of resentment over the deal because they did not have any of their needs met.  
              This usually results in the end to any future negotiations and a termination of the  
              relationship.

        ii)  Both parties lose. 
            This outcome is known as lose-lose scenario. One of two things can happen either  
             both parties concede bargaining positions outside their target ranges, or no deal is  
             struck. Both parties may end up in worse positions than when they started the  
             negotiations. In this type of outcome, ego's come into play which thwart the  
             negotiating process. Both sides dig into their positions and are unwilling to  
             compromise with each other. In the end, both parties end up losing in the deal.  
             Resentment exists between both parties as a result of the outcome and it is  
             unlikely that they will ever negotiate with each other again. 
 
      iii)  They get stuck in a stalemate. 
            In this type of outcome, neither side wins or loses and after a long negotiating  
            session, both sides are at the exact same place that they started off at. This is a  
            result of not being able to deal with interests and only positions. Stalemates  
            happen when both sides aggressively defend their positions and neither side is  
            able to make the other side budge. 
 
      iv). Both end up winning.  
            This outcome known as win-win scenario. Both parties end up at minimum within  
            their target ranges. In some cases, a win-win can mean that a new solution is  
            found that improves the position of both parties.  
 
            In this type of outcome, both sides walk away with their interests and needs being  
            met. Both sides leave the negotiating table satisfied because they came out of the  
            negotiation with more than they had started with. Relationships are preserved  
            because both parties cooperated with each other in determining a fair solution to  
            the problem. This outcome also bolsters trust for future negotiations between the  
            two parties because they have established a positive relationship.

11.   Setting Up For Success Negotiation 
         Since negotiations are normally a one-time affairs, it is usually towards a win-  
         win  outcome. When both parties parts from with a positive feeling about achieving  
         their goals, they will probably be willing to negotiate again. Using all skills to ensure  
          that all parties feel like winners. 
 
12.  Strategy For Successful Negotiations  
        i)  Know what you want. Set clearly your interest and goals. 
       ii)  Know the other parties. Learn as much as you can about the people with whom  
            you are going to be negotiating. Know their negotiating style, negotiation skills,  
            backgrounds, hopes, fears, aspirations, and interests.
