1. Definition 
1.1    Information technology (IT) is the application of computers and 
telecommunications equipment to store, retrieve, transmit and manipulate data, often in 
the context of a business or other enterprise. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Picture  1.1 . Information Technology Relationship 
 
1.2    An Information System (IS) is a system composed of people and computers that 
processes or interprets information. The term is also sometimes used in more restricted 
senses to refer to only the software used to run a computerized database or to refer to 
only a computer system.

2. Components of Information System Technologies  
2.1    Generally there are 6 components of Information System Technologies.They are : 
i)    Hardware 
The term hardware refers to machinery.This category includes the computer 
itself, which is often referred to as the central processing unit (CPU), and all of 
its support equipments. Among the support equipments are input and output 
devices, storage devices and communications devices.  
ii)    Software 
The term software refers to computer programs and the manuals (if any) that 
support them. Computer programs are machine-readable instructions that 
direct the circuitry within the hardware parts of the CBIS to function in ways 
that produce useful information from data. Programs are generally stored on 
some input / output medium,often a disk or tape.  
iii)    Data 
Data are facts that are used by programs to produce useful information.Like 
programs,data are generally stored in machine-readable form on disk or tape 
until the computer needs them.  
iv)    Procedures 
Procedures are the policies that govern the operation of a computer system. 
"Procedures are to people what software is to hardware" is a common analogy 
that is used to illustrate the role of procedures in a CBIS.  
v)    Networks

3.  Data entry 
3.1    Data entry refers to user actions involving input of data to a computer, and 
computer responses to such inputs. The simplest kind of data entry consists merely 
of pointing at something -- selecting an item or designating a position on a computer-
generated display. In more complicated modes of data entry, a user may have to 
control the format of data inputs as well as their contents. Thus questions of format 
control in text entry/editing and graphic interaction may properly be considered 
questions of data entry.

4.  What are the basic considerations in selecting the right software? 
4.1    There are 9 considerations in selecting the right software. They are: 
i)    Features against requirements – most software offers more than what is  
      required thus future-proofing is a good strategy if the cost is right 
ii)    Cost/benefit analysis : 
a) Initial setup cost 
b) (Main) ongoing charge–monthly or annual (might depend on specific 
modules)  
c) Per-member surcharge 
d) Per-transaction charges (% or $) 
e) Technical support fees 
f) Update charges  
iii) 
Future upgradability – All software normally have their update cycle and     
sometimes offer upgrading. One of the things need to be confirm is the 
planning of the software maker for the future; either they will only upgrade the 
software or totally replace it with a new programming. This will impact the cost 
versus functionality. 
iv) 
Hardware compatibility – current specification vs. compatible specification 
v) 
System compatibility – Does the software works in the Operating System 
currently used?If it works,how well does it operate in the Operating System?

5.  Why do we need Technology Update Awareness? 
5.1    Interaction between users and technology/systems is unavoidable. One needs to 
be aware of the connection and advantages/disadvantages directly or indirectly 
affecting his work. He/she needs to be aware of the current IT trending and any 
updates related to his work. Technology improves production/communication and 
database handling.  Obsolete technology can affect efficiency and production. As one 
moves up in the career ladder, sophistication in work and interaction with its 
surrounding increases thus justifying the needs to be aware and updated with 
technology. 
5.2    Previously, all paperworks, reports, charts, presentations, minutes of meetings 
etc…etc.. are done manually and fully utilizing the usage of paper. When the transition 
from paper to paperless occurs, i.e. using computer/software, one needs to know how 
to transform the format and information into a softcopy version. For example, if a 
report previously prepared using typewriter, when converting it into a softcopy version, 
one needs to know how to use the software and how to format the report. Using 
software, there are endless selections of options to present the report. These 
softwares are usually updated and continuously evolving based on continuous 
changes thus user needs to know the updates and how they affect them. This 
technology update awareness applies to all type/level of technology.
