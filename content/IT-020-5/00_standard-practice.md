# Standard Practice

## National Occupational Skills Standard (NOSS) for Computer System Management Level 5

**Code:** IT-020-5:2013

---

## 1. Introduction

The ever-changing world fuelled largely by the continuing advancement in computer technology requires organizations to be well-equipped to remain current and relevant. Businesses from multi-national corporations to home-based enterprises have to adopt current technologies to be competitive. Government agencies and institutions too, need to stay "at the top of the game" in order to provide fast, reliable and accurate services to the people.

This climate creates job opportunities in computer systems management personnel to support the industry growth. Computer system management job area personnel normally known by **IT Manager** plays a vital role in the implementation of technology within organisation to plan, execute, coordinate, and facilitate the computer-related activities. They help determine both technical and business goals in consultation with top management, and develop guidelines and procedures to achieve business goals. This requires a strong understanding of both technology and business practices.

The IT Managers are also required to analyse project requirements, estimate budgets, and develop schedules of the projects. They coordinate such projects from development through implementation, working with internal and external clients, vendors, consultants, and computer specialists. These managers are increasingly involved in projects that upgrade the computer system infrastructure and information security of organisations.

As computer systems become more closely connected with day-to-day operations of businesses, IT Managers are also expected to be aware of business practices. They must possess strong interpersonal, communication, and leadership skills as they are required to interact with staff members and other people outside their organisations. Further, they should possess teamwork skills to work on group projects and other collaborative efforts. In addition, they must also have an understanding of how a business functions, how it earns revenue, and how technology relates to the core competencies of the business.

Employment of IT Managers is expected to grow in this decade. Through Economic Transformation Program (ETP) initiated by the government and rapid development of information technology in the workplace will continue to drive demand for more managers. To remain competitive, firms will continue to install sophisticated computer system and networks, making the outlook for IT Managers remains strong.

IT Managers may advance to progressively higher leadership positions in the information technology department. Some may become managers in non-technical areas such as marketing, human resources, or sales because in high technology firms, an understanding of technical issues is helpful in those areas.

### Pre-requisite

The candidate must complete any of the following as a pre-requisite to pursue this course:

- Computer System Administration Level 4 diploma, or
- Computer Network Administration Level 4 diploma, or
- IT-020-4: Computer System Assistant Executive Level 4 diploma, or
- IT-051-4: Assistant Network Engineer Level 4 diploma, or
- Any Diploma in Computer Science, Information Technology, Information System, Engineering, IT Security or equivalent

## 3. Description of Competency Level

The NOSS is developed for various occupational areas. Candidates for certification must be assessed and trained at certain levels to substantiate competencies. Below is a guideline of each NOSS Level as defined by the Department of Skills Development, Ministry of Human Resources, Malaysia.

| Level | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | SKM (Operation/Production) | Routine and predictable work activities. |
| **L2** | SKM (Operation/Production) | Varied work in multiple contexts; some non-routine, requiring individual responsibility. |
| **L3** | SKM (Supervisory) | Complex, non-routine work; considerable autonomy; control/guidance of others often required. |
| **L4** | DKM (Executive) | Complex technical/professional work; substantial personal responsibility; resource allocation and supervision of others. |
| **L5** | DLKM (Managerial) | Fundamental principles and complex techniques across unpredictable contexts; significant responsibility for others and substantial resources; accountable for analysis, diagnosis, planning, execution, and evaluation. |

## 4. Malaysian Skill Certification

Candidates who have attended and successfully completed each competency shall be awarded Certificate of Attendance on that competency. Those who have attended and successfully completed all the core competencies and fulfilled Malaysian Skill certification requirements shall receive **Diploma Lanjutan Kemahiran Malaysia (Malaysia Skills Advanced Diploma)** in Computer System Management at Level 5.

## 5. Job Competencies

IT Manager is competent in performing the following **core competencies**:

1. Computer Systems Planning and Operations Management
2. Computer System Asset Management
3. Computer System Security Management
4. Disaster Recovery Management
5. Computer System & Network Project Management
6. Computer System & Network SOP Development and Implementation Management

**Elective competency:**

7. Server Scripting

---

## 6. Working Condition

IT Managers generally spend most of their time in offices. Most of them work at least 40 hours a week and some may have to work evenings and weekends to meet deadlines or solve unexpected problems. Some of them may experience pressure in meeting technical goals with short deadlines or tight budgets. As networks continue to expand and more work is done remotely, IT Managers have to communicate with and oversee offsite employees using Internet.

IT Managers need to keep up to date with the ever-changing specific software and operating systems and hardware related technology. They must also work well with people and should be able to communicate effectively.

---

## 7. Employment Prospect

The increasing use of technology in the workplace is projected to lead faster than average growth in this occupation. As so much business is carried out over computer networks, security will continue to be an important issue for businesses and other organisations. The emergence of security as a key concern for businesses should lead to strong growth for IT Managers.

IT Managers are employed by a range of organisations including:

- Government departments and agencies
- Institutions of higher learning
- Private corporations
- Banks
- Private companies that provide computer, database and network services to clients
- Telecommunication companies
- Many other private organisations

A growing number of computer system management advanced diploma holders are self-employed, working independently as contractors or consultants.

## 8. Training, Industrial/Professional Recognition, Other Qualifications and Advancement

### 8.1 Industrial Recognition / Professional Qualification

Computer system management advanced diploma holders have strong foundation in both areas, computer system and network. There are a few professional certifications that recognise IT personnel as professional such as:

- The Computing Technology Industry Association (CompTIA)
- Cisco Networking Academy
- Linux Professional Institute
- SANS Institute
- EC-Council

### 8.2 Other Prominent Qualifications Recognised (Malaysia or International)

Technologies change rapidly and it is an essential part of the IT Managers to remain up to date with any development in hardware and software, as well as being aware of previous versions that may still be in operation. Among professional technical training that can enhance the IT Managers skill in performing their daily tasks are listed below.

| Certification | Description |
|---|---|
| **MCTS** (Microsoft Certified Technology Specialist) | Proficiency with specific Microsoft technologies. |
| **VCP** (VMware Certified Professional) | VMware virtual environment implementation and maintenance. |
| **CSSA** (Certified SonicWALL Security Administrator) | Security administration. |
| **CISSP** (Certified Information Systems Security Professional) | Information security. |
| **ACSP** (Apple Certified Support Professional) | Apple platform support. |
| Computer System and Network Audit | Audit skills for IT infrastructure. |
| Ethical Hackers | Ethical hacking and penetration testing. |

### 8.3 Types of Occupation for Career Advancement

Career advancement for computer system management level 5 personnel locally and internationally is enormous. Among them are:

- Computer Specialist
- Chief Operation Officer (COO)
- Chief Information Technology Officer (CIO)
- IT Consultant
- Trainer

### 8.4 Related Industries

Computer system personnel are employed in every sector of the economy, private and public sector.
