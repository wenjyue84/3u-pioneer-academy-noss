| Type / Framework | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| ISO/IEC 19770 (IT asset management) | An international standard that provides a framework for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an IT asset management (ITAM) system. It defines processes for managing the lifecycle of IT assets from acquisition through disposal, including licence management, hardware inventory, and software compliance. The standard helps organisations demonstrate control over their IT estate for audit and regulatory purposes. Alignment with ISO 19770 supports integration with broader IT governance frameworks such as COBIT and ISO 27001. |
| Asset lifecycle management | The practice of managing IT assets through defined stages: planning, procurement, deployment, operation, maintenance, and disposal. Each stage has documented procedures, approval gates, and responsible roles. Lifecycle management ensures that assets deliver value throughout their useful life and are replaced or retired before they become a risk or cost burden. Lifecycle policies are reviewed annually and aligned to technology refresh cycles and business requirements. |
| Total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis | A financial estimation model that captures the full cost of an IT asset over its lifecycle, including purchase price, deployment, configuration, training, maintenance, support contracts, energy consumption, floor space, and disposal. TCO enables comparison between alternatives (e.g. lease vs buy, on-premise vs cloud) and supports evidence-based budget planning. Assumptions and calculation methods must be documented and validated against actual expenditure. TCO reports are presented to management as part of budget justification and investment appraisal. |
| Configuration Management Database (CMDB) | A centralised repository that stores information about IT assets (configuration items) and the relationships between them. The CMDB supports incident management, change management, and impact analysis by providing a single source of truth for the IT estate. Data quality depends on automated discovery, regular reconciliation, and defined ownership. The CMDB is a core component of ITIL service management and underpins effective asset and configuration management. |
| Asset classification and categorisation | The practice of grouping IT assets by type (hardware, software, network, cloud), criticality (mission-critical, business-important, standard), ownership (department, project, shared), and financial category (CAPEX, OPEX, leased). Classification drives decisions on security controls, maintenance priority, lifecycle policy, and reporting granularity. A classification scheme must be defined, communicated, and consistently applied across the organisation. |
| Regulatory compliance (data disposal) | Legal and regulatory requirements governing the handling and disposal of IT assets, particularly those containing personal or sensitive data. In Malaysia, relevant regulations include the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) 2010 and sector-specific guidelines. International standards such as NIST SP 800-88 (Guidelines for Media Sanitization) define methods for data clearing, purging, and destruction. Non-compliance may result in fines, reputational damage, and legal liability. Disposal processes must be documented with certificates of destruction retained for audit. |

| Management Framework / Tool | Description |
|------------------------------|-------------|
| Asset inventory methodology | A structured approach to collecting, validating, and maintaining an accurate record of all IT assets. Methods include physical stocktake (walk-through verification), network discovery (automated scanning), purchase order reconciliation, and user self-declaration. Data captured includes asset type, make, model, serial number, location, assigned user, purchase date, warranty, and configuration. Inventory accuracy is measured through periodic reconciliation and reported as a KPI. Discrepancies trigger investigation and corrective action. |
| Operational status workflow | A defined set of status categories (e.g. in service, standby/spare, under repair, pending disposal, disposed) with transition rules governing movement between states. Each transition requires authorisation, documentation, and update in the ITAM system. Status data supports capacity planning, replacement scheduling, and financial reporting. Regular status reviews ensure that the ITAM system reflects the actual state of the IT estate and that no assets are unaccounted for. |
| Cost estimation and space planning | Models and tools for estimating the total cost of ownership and physical space requirements for IT assets. Cost models include acquisition cost, deployment cost, annual maintenance, energy and cooling, floor space rental, and end-of-life disposal. Space planning considers server room capacity, rack units, cable management, power distribution, and cooling requirements. Estimates are documented with assumptions and presented to management for budget approval. Actuals are tracked against estimates for variance analysis. |
| ITAM system selection and implementation | The process of evaluating, selecting, and deploying an IT asset management system that meets organisational requirements. Evaluation criteria include asset discovery capability, integration with ITSM and procurement systems, reporting and analytics, barcode/RFID support, licence management, scalability, and total cost. Implementation involves data migration, user training, process alignment, and ongoing data quality management. The selected system must support audit requirements and produce management reports. |
| Tagging and labelling standards (barcode/RFID) | Defined standards for physically marking IT assets with unique identifiers (barcode labels, QR codes, or RFID tags) and linking them to records in the ITAM system. Standards specify tag format, placement, durability, and replacement criteria. RFID enables automated tracking and bulk scanning, while barcodes offer a lower-cost alternative. Periodic tag verification audits ensure that all assets are tagged, tags are readable, and records match physical assets. Non-compliance is reported and corrected. |
| Secure disposal (NIST SP 800-88) | The process of securely removing data from IT assets before disposal, recycling, or redeployment, following NIST SP 800-88 guidelines. Methods include Clear (logical overwrite), Purge (degaussing or cryptographic erase), and Destroy (physical destruction such as shredding or incineration). The method selected depends on data sensitivity and the asset's next use. A certificate of destruction is issued for each asset, and disposal records are retained for audit and regulatory compliance. Environmental regulations for e-waste disposal must also be followed. |

| Software / Tool Type | Description |
|----------------------|-------------|
| ITAM platforms (ServiceNow ITAM, Lansweeper, Snipe-IT) | Dedicated IT asset management software that provides centralised inventory, lifecycle tracking, licence management, and reporting. Enterprise solutions (ServiceNow ITAM) integrate with ITSM, CMDB, and procurement modules. Mid-market tools (Lansweeper) offer network discovery and hardware/software inventory. Open-source options (Snipe-IT) provide cost-effective asset tracking for smaller organisations. All platforms support barcode/RFID integration and customisable reporting. |
| Barcode and RFID systems | Hardware and software for generating, printing, and scanning asset tags. Barcode systems include label printers, handheld scanners, and mobile scanning apps. RFID systems include tags (passive or active), readers (fixed or handheld), and middleware for integration with the ITAM platform. RFID enables faster bulk scanning and automated tracking through doorway readers. Selection depends on asset volume, environment, and budget. |
| Data wiping and destruction tools (DBAN, Blancco) | Software and services for securely erasing data from storage media before disposal or redeployment. DBAN (Darik's Boot and Nuke) is a free, open-source tool for overwriting hard drives. Blancco is a commercial solution that provides certified erasure with tamper-proof audit reports accepted by auditors and regulators. Physical destruction services (shredding, degaussing) are used for assets that cannot be wiped. Selection depends on data sensitivity, volume, and compliance requirements. |
| Reporting and analytics tools | Business intelligence and reporting software (Power BI, Excel pivot tables, ITAM built-in reporting modules) used to analyse asset data, produce management dashboards, and generate audit-ready reports. Automated scheduled reports reduce manual effort and ensure timely delivery to stakeholders. Custom queries and visualisations support TCO analysis, lifecycle trending, and compliance tracking. |


| Common Issue | Root Cause | Management Action |
|--------------|------------|-------------------|
| Inaccurate asset inventory | Manual data entry errors; no automated discovery; infrequent stocktakes; assets moved without updating records | Implement automated network discovery integrated with ITAM system; schedule quarterly reconciliation audits; enforce policy requiring ITAM update for all asset movements and changes |
| Ghost assets (recorded but not physically present) | Assets disposed, lost, or transferred without updating inventory; no periodic physical verification; weak custody controls | Conduct annual physical stocktake and reconcile with ITAM records; implement check-out/check-in procedures; assign asset custodians with accountability for updates |
| Non-compliant asset disposal | No formal disposal policy; data not securely wiped; disposal not documented; e-waste regulations not followed | Establish formal disposal policy aligned to NIST SP 800-88 and local e-waste regulations; use certified data destruction tools; retain certificates of destruction; conduct periodic disposal audits |
| TCO not tracked or used for decisions | Cost data scattered across departments; no standard TCO model; lifecycle costs not captured; management unaware of true asset costs | Define standard TCO model and calculation methodology; integrate cost data from procurement, finance, and ITAM systems; present TCO analysis in budget proposals and lifecycle reviews; train managers on TCO-based decision-making |

## Practical Exercises

The following management-level exercise scenarios develop the strategic and governance competencies required for Computer System Asset Management. Each exercise is designed for L5 managers and includes case studies, policy scenarios, governance frameworks, strategic planning simulations, and assessment criteria aligned with NOSS standards.

### Lab 2.1: IT Asset Inventory Analysis and Audit Planning

**Objective:** Conduct comprehensive analysis of IT asset inventory and plan audit

**Duration:** 120 minutes

**Resources Required:**
- Asset inventory report
- ITAM system access
- Audit checklist
- Analysis template

**Procedures:**
1. Export current ITAM system inventory across all locations
2. Analyse inventory completeness: compare ITAM records to purchase orders, HR records, network discovery
3. Identify discrepancies: ghost assets, missing tags, incorrect locations
4. Classify assets by type, criticality, age, and condition
5. Calculate inventory accuracy percentage (matching vs total)
6. Identify high-risk assets: end-of-life, unsupported versions, security vulnerabilities
7. Create remediation plan for discrepancies and high-risk assets
8. Present audit findings and remediation roadmap to management

**Expected Outcome:**
Comprehensive asset inventory audit report with remediation plan

**Assessment Checklist:**
[ ] Inventory exported and validated
[ ] Data sources cross-checked
[ ] Discrepancies identified and categorised
[ ] Asset classification completed
[ ] Inventory accuracy calculated
[ ] High-risk assets flagged
[ ] Remediation plan created with timeline
[ ] Management sign-off obtained

### Lab 2.2: Asset Lifecycle Status Management and Workflow

**Objective:** Design and implement asset status tracking and workflow

**Duration:** 90 minutes

**Resources Required:**
- Asset status framework
- Workflow diagram tool
- Approval matrix
- Configuration management tool

**Procedures:**
1. Define asset status categories: planning, procurement, deployment, in service, maintenance, pending disposal, disposed
2. Create workflow diagram showing status transitions and required approvals
3. Define responsibilities: who updates status, who approves transitions
4. Create business rules: conditions triggering status changes, approval requirements
5. Design notifications: who gets notified when status changes
6. Plan audit procedures: periodic status reconciliation, data quality checks
7. Configure status fields in ITAM system with workflow automation
8. Train administrators on status update procedures and approvals

**Expected Outcome:**
Documented asset status workflow integrated into ITAM system

**Assessment Checklist:**

[ ] Workflow diagram created
[ ] Approval rules documented
[ ] Responsibilities assigned

[ ] Audit procedures established
[ ] System configuration completed
[ ] Training delivered

### Lab 2.3: TCO Analysis and Capacity Planning

**Objective:** Develop total cost of ownership model and space forecasting

**Duration:** 120 minutes

**Resources Required:**
- TCO template
- Capacity calculator
- Historical cost data
- Vendor pricing

**Procedures:**

3. Create TCO calculator for hardware refresh decisions (lease vs buy)
4. Analyse space requirements: current utilisation, growth forecast, cooling/power capacity
5. Forecast 3-year space and infrastructure needs
6. Evaluate alternative solutions: on-premise vs cloud, internal vs outsourced
7. Create cost-benefit comparison models for decision support

## Employability Skills

This CoCu develops the following employability skills through the work activities:

### 1. Communication Skills
- **Mapping:** Present asset inventory reports and lifecycle analysis to senior management; prepare asset disposal and procurement justifications for board approval; communicate asset tagging and tracking policies to all departments

### 2. Teamwork & Collaboration
- **Mapping:** Lead cross-departmental asset stocktake and reconciliation activities; collaborate with finance on TCO analysis and budget forecasting; coordinate with procurement and facilities on asset deployment and disposal

### 3. Problem-solving
- **Mapping:** Resolve discrepancies between physical inventory and asset records; evaluate repair-versus-replace decisions using lifecycle cost data; address ghost assets and untracked equipment through audit and reconciliation

### 4. Initiative & Self-reliance
- **Mapping:** Proactively identify assets approaching end-of-life and plan replacements; drive adoption of automated ITAM tools to replace manual tracking; take responsibility for asset register accuracy and compliance

### 5. Planning & Organizing
- **Mapping:** Develop asset lifecycle plans aligned with organisational growth and budget cycles; organize periodic physical stocktakes and reconciliation schedules; manage asset disposal workflows in compliance with regulations

### 6. Self-management & Safety Awareness
- **Mapping:** Ensure asset disposal complies with data protection regulations and e-waste standards; manage risk of data exposure on decommissioned equipment; maintain audit trails for all asset movements and status changes

### 7. Technology Use & Technical Proficiency
- **Mapping:** Use ITAM software for asset tracking, tagging, and reporting; manage barcode/RFID labelling systems; leverage asset data for capacity planning and TCO analysis

### 8. Learning Skills & Continuous Improvement
- **Mapping:** Stay current on ITAM best practices and asset management standards; analyze asset failure and cost trends to improve lifecycle planning; learn from disposal audits to refine decommissioning procedures



## Attitude, Safety and Environmental

### Workplace Safety
- **Enterprise Risk Governance:** Establish and maintain governance frameworks that identify, assess, and mitigate IT-related risks at the organisational level. Ensure compliance with regulatory requirements and industry standards (ISO 27001, COBIT, ITIL).
- **Change Management at Scale:** Implement formal change management processes that balance innovation with stability; ensure all significant infrastructure changes are reviewed, approved, and documented for organisational accountability.
- **Cybersecurity Leadership:** Foster a culture of security awareness throughout the organisation; establish policies and procedures that protect critical systems, data, and intellectual property from threats.
- **Crisis Preparedness:** Develop and maintain disaster recovery and business continuity plans that protect the organisation's ability to operate during emergencies.

### Environmental Considerations
- **Sustainable IT Strategy:** Develop long-term IT strategies that consider environmental impact; promote energy-efficient infrastructure, virtualisation, and cloud solutions that reduce organisational carbon footprint.
- **Responsible Technology Governance:** Establish policies for responsible technology use, data privacy, and ethical IT practices that align with organisational values and community expectations.

### Professional Attitudes
- **Strategic Integrity:** Make decisions based on long-term organisational benefit; resist short-term pressures that could compromise system stability, security, or sustainability.
- **Visionary Leadership:** Champion innovation and emerging technologies while balancing risk; inspire teams to think strategically about technology's role in organisational success.
- **Stakeholder Trust:** Maintain credibility with executive leadership, boards, and external partners through transparent communication, ethical conduct, and delivery of promised results.
- **Continuous Improvement Culture:** Foster an environment where teams continuously learn from experiences, share knowledge, and improve processes to enhance organisational capability and competitiveness.
