## 1. Key Performance Indicators (KPI) for Malaysian SMEs

### 1.1 What Is a KPI?

A **Key Performance Indicator (KPI)** is a measurable value that demonstrates how effectively a business is achieving its key objectives. KPIs are selected based on what matters most to the business — profitability, efficiency, customer growth, or operational capacity.

Good KPIs share four characteristics:

| Characteristic | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific (Khusus)** | Measures one clear thing | "Gross Profit Margin" — not "how well we're doing" |
| **Measurable (Boleh Diukur)** | Can be expressed as a number | 34.6% (not "pretty good") |
| **Actionable (Boleh Diambil Tindakan)** | Management can act on the result | If margin drops, review pricing or costs |
| **Time-bound (Terikat Masa)** | Tracked at regular intervals | Monthly, quarterly, annually |

### 1.2 Five Core KPIs for Malaysian SMEs

The following five KPIs are used in every worked example in this Information Sheet. All calculations use data from the fictional **Syarikat Mentari Raya Sdn Bhd** dataset established in KP-01 and KP-02.

*Note: Syarikat Mentari Raya Sdn Bhd is a fictional company created solely for teaching purposes.*

---

#### KPI 1: Revenue Growth Rate (Kadar Pertumbuhan Hasil)

**Definition:** The percentage increase (or decrease) in revenue from one period to the next.

**Formula:**
```
Revenue Growth Rate = (Current Period Revenue - Previous Period Revenue) / Previous Period Revenue × 100%
```

**Worked Example:**

Using the Syarikat Mentari Raya 2025 monthly data (from KP-01):

| Period | Revenue (RM) | Growth Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 2025 | 98,000 | — (baseline) |
| Feb 2025 | 105,000 | (105,000 − 98,000) ÷ 98,000 × 100 = **7.1%** |
| Mar 2025 | 112,000 | (112,000 − 105,000) ÷ 105,000 × 100 = **6.7%** |
| Jul 2025 | 122,000 | (122,000 − 130,000) ÷ 130,000 × 100 = **−6.2%** |
| Dec 2025 | 150,000 | (150,000 − 142,500) ÷ 142,500 × 100 = **5.3%** |

**Verification (February):** (105,000 − 98,000) = 7,000 ÷ 98,000 = 0.07143 × 100 = **7.1%** ✓
**Verification (July — negative growth):** (122,000 − 130,000) = −8,000 ÷ 130,000 = −0.0615 × 100 = **−6.2%** ✓

**Annual Revenue Growth Rate (comparing total 2025 to a fictional 2024 total):**

For this worked example, assume Syarikat Mentari Raya's total 2024 revenue was **RM 1,320,000** (fictional baseline for 2024).

Annual Growth Rate = (1,480,500 − 1,320,000) ÷ 1,320,000 × 100 = **12.2%**

**Verification:** 1,480,500 − 1,320,000 = 160,500 ÷ 1,320,000 = 0.1216 × 100 = **12.2%** ✓

---

#### KPI 2: Gross Profit Margin (Margin Untung Kasar)

**Definition:** The percentage of revenue remaining after deducting the cost of goods sold or direct operating expenses.

**Formula:**
```
Gross Profit Margin = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue × 100%
                    = (Revenue − Expenses) ÷ Revenue × 100%
```

**Worked Example — Annual 2025:**

Annual Gross Profit Margin = 481,500 ÷ 1,480,500 × 100 = **32.5%**

**Verification:** 481,500 ÷ 1,480,500 = 0.3252 × 100 = **32.5%** ✓

**Interpretation:** A gross profit margin of 32.5% means that for every RM 1 of revenue, Syarikat Mentari Raya retains RM 0.325 after covering operating expenses. The margin improved from 26.5% in January to 38.7% in December, indicating growing operational efficiency as revenue scaled.

---

#### KPI 3: Employee Productivity Index (Indeks Produktiviti Pekerja)

**Definition:** Revenue generated per employee per month, indicating how efficiently the workforce contributes to business output.

**Formula:**
```
Employee Productivity Index = Monthly Revenue ÷ Number of Sales Staff
```

**Worked Example:**

| Month | Revenue (RM) | Sales Staff | Productivity (RM/person) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 98,000 | 5 | 19,600 |
| Jun | 130,000 | 7 | 18,571 |
| Dec | 150,000 | 8 | 18,750 |

**Verification (January):** 98,000 ÷ 5 = **19,600** ✓
**Verification (June):** 130,000 ÷ 7 = **18,571** ✓

**Observation:** Despite revenue growth, revenue per staff member slightly declined from January (RM 19,600) to December (RM 18,750), suggesting that the 3 additional staff hired during the year contributed to revenue growth but at a slightly lower per-person rate. Management should evaluate whether this reflects onboarding lag or whether the new hires are assigned to lower-revenue activities.

---

#### KPI 4: Customer Acquisition Cost / CAC (Kos Pemerolehan Pelanggan)

**Definition:** The average cost incurred to acquire one new paying customer.

**Formula:**
```
Customer Acquisition Cost = Total Marketing Spend ÷ Number of New Customers Acquired
```

**Worked Example:**

From the KP-02 expense data, annual Marketing spend = **RM 97,000**.

Assume Syarikat Mentari Raya acquired **85 new trade customers** in 2025 (fictional figure).

CAC = 97,000 ÷ 85 = **RM 1,141 per new customer**

**Verification:** 97,000 ÷ 85 = **1,141.18 ≈ RM 1,141** ✓

**Interpretation:** If the average new customer generates RM 5,000 in annual gross profit (fictional), then acquiring them at RM 1,141 yields a **Customer Lifetime Ratio of 4.4:1** — generally healthy for a B2B wholesaler. However, if CAC exceeds the first year's gross profit from that customer, the acquisition is unprofitable.

---

#### KPI 5: Accounts Receivable Days / AR Days (Hari Belum Terima)

**Definition:** The average number of days it takes to collect payment after issuing an invoice. A lower number means faster collection and better cash flow.

**Formula:**
```
AR Days = (Accounts Receivable Balance ÷ Annual Revenue) × 365
```

**Worked Example:**

Assume Syarikat Mentari Raya's accounts receivable balance at 31 December 2025 = **RM 185,000** (fictional).

AR Days = (185,000 ÷ 1,480,500) × 365 = **45.6 days**

**Verification:** 185,000 ÷ 1,480,500 = 0.12496 × 365 = **45.6 days** ✓

**Interpretation:** On average, customers take 45.6 days to pay. Standard trade terms in Malaysia are typically 30–60 days. If AR Days rises above 60, it signals that customers are paying late — which strains the company's cash flow and may require follow-up action.

## 2. Writing the Executive Performance Narrative with AI

### 2.1 What Is an Executive Narrative?

An **executive narrative (naratif eksekutif)** is the written interpretation that accompanies a dashboard or KPI report. It translates numbers into management-readable language — explaining not just what happened but what it means and what action to consider.

A well-written executive narrative answers three questions:

1. **What happened?** — State the key metric and its movement
2. **Why did it happen?** — Identify the likely driver (supported by data)
3. **What should we do?** — Recommend a specific, actionable response

### 2.2 AI Prompt for Executive Narrative Generation

```
Act as a business analyst writing a management performance summary.

Using the following data for a fictional Malaysian electrical goods wholesaler:
- Full-year revenue: RM 1,480,500 (vs. RM 1,320,000 in the prior year)
- Revenue growth rate: 12.2%
- Annual gross profit margin: 32.5%
- Lowest margin month: August at 25.2%
- Highest margin month: December at 38.7%
- Staff costs as % of expenses: 52.5%
- AR Days: 45.6 days

Write a 3-paragraph executive performance narrative for the Operations Director.
Paragraph 1: Revenue performance and growth trend.
Paragraph 2: Profitability analysis — margin improvement and the August anomaly.
Paragraph 3: Two improvement recommendations based solely on the data provided.

Use plain, professional English. No jargon. No figures not provided above.
```

### 2.3 Sample AI-Generated Narrative (Based on Syarikat Mentari Raya Data)

The following is a sample narrative that ChatGPT would be expected to produce from the prompt above. Trainees must verify that every figure in the AI output matches the data provided in the prompt — and by extension, the source data in KP-01 and KP-02.

> **Revenue Performance (January–December 2025)**
>
> Syarikat Mentari Raya Sdn Bhd achieved full-year revenue of RM 1,480,500 in 2025, representing a 12.2% increase over the prior year (RM 1,320,000). Revenue grew consistently through the first half of the year, peaked in June at RM 130,000, and experienced a mid-year dip in July (RM 122,000) before recovering strongly in the fourth quarter. December recorded the highest monthly revenue of RM 150,000, confirming a positive year-end trajectory.
>
> **Profitability Analysis**
>
> The annual gross profit margin of 32.5% reflects sound cost management relative to revenue growth. Margin improved steadily from 26.5% in January to 38.7% in December, indicating that the business benefited from economies of scale as revenue increased. August was the weakest month, with a margin of 25.2%, coinciding with elevated staff costs (RM 46,000) and sustained logistics spending (RM 21,000) at a period of lower revenue (RM 115,000). This pattern warrants investigation into whether August's cost base can be partially deferred or reduced.
>
> **Improvement Recommendations**
>
> First, the Operations Director should review logistics cost management during the July–August period, when logistics spending remained at RM 21,000–22,000 despite a revenue dip — a proactive renegotiation with logistics providers for seasonal volume-adjusted rates could protect margins in this window. Second, with accounts receivable at 45.6 days, introducing an early-payment incentive (e.g., 1% discount for payment within 15 days) could reduce AR Days to below 30, materially improving monthly cash flow.

> **IMPORTANT:** The narrative above must be cross-checked word by word against the data. Every number cited (RM 1,480,500; 12.2%; 32.5%; 25.2%; 38.7%; RM 130,000; RM 150,000; 45.6 days) must appear in KP-01 or KP-02. Do not let AI add figures that are not in the source data.

## 3. Anomaly Detection and Early Warning Indicators

### 3.1 What Is an Anomaly in Business Data?

An **anomaly (anomali)** is a data point that deviates significantly from the expected pattern. In BI reporting, anomalies can indicate:

- A genuine business event (e.g., a large one-off order inflating revenue)
- A data entry error (e.g., an invoice recorded as RM 50,000 instead of RM 5,000)
- An emerging operational problem that needs management attention

### 3.2 Types of Anomalies

| Anomaly Type | Description | Example from Mentari Raya Data |
|---|---|---|
| **Spike** | Value jumps sharply above normal range | Revenue spike to RM 150,000 in December |
| **Dip** | Value drops sharply below normal range | July revenue dip to RM 122,000 after June's RM 130,000 |
| **Trend break** | Consistent trend suddenly changes direction | Gross Profit Margin fell 6.7% from June to July despite similar revenue |
| **Outlier in category** | One category disproportionately large | Staff Costs = 52.5% of all expenses — unusually high vs. logistics at 25.4% |

### 3.3 Early Warning Indicators for Malaysian SMEs

| Indicator | Warning Signal | Threshold to Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Growth Rate | Negative for 2 consecutive months | Below 0% |
| Gross Profit Margin | Sustained decline | Below 25% for 2+ months |
| AR Days | Rising trend | Above 60 days |
| Staff Cost % of Expenses | Increasing trend | Above 55% |
| Marketing Spend Efficiency (Revenue ÷ Marketing Spend) | Falling ratio | Below 10× |

**Marketing Spend Efficiency — Syarikat Mentari Raya 2025:**
= RM 1,480,500 ÷ RM 97,000 = **15.3×**

**Verification:** 1,480,500 ÷ 97,000 = **15.26 ≈ 15.3×** ✓

A ratio of 15.3× means every RM 1 spent on marketing generated RM 15.30 in revenue — well above the 10× warning threshold.

## 4. Quarterly Report Structure for Management Presentation

### 4.1 Standard Sections of a Quarterly Performance Report

A quarterly business performance report for a Malaysian SME should contain the following sections:

| Section | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Executive Summary (Ringkasan Eksekutif)** | 3 key highlights: what went well, what needs attention, one action item | 1 paragraph |
| **2. Revenue Performance (Prestasi Hasil)** | Revenue vs. target, month-by-month trend, growth rate | 1 chart + 1 paragraph |
| **3. Expense Analysis (Analisis Perbelanjaan)** | Expense breakdown by category, month-on-month change | 1 pie chart + 1 table |
| **4. KPI Scorecard (Kad Skor KPI)** | All 5 KPIs in a summary table with status (green/yellow/red) | 1 table |
| **5. Anomalies and Early Warnings (Anomali dan Amaran Awal)** | Any data points outside normal range with commentary | 1 paragraph |
| **6. Recommendations (Cadangan)** | 2–3 specific, actionable recommendations with rationale | Bullet list |
| **7. Data Sources and AI Disclosure** | List of data sources and confirmation of AI tool use with human review | Footer or appendix |

### 4.2 The KPI Scorecard Table

Every quarterly report should include a KPI scorecard showing current performance vs. target:

**Example KPI Scorecard — Syarikat Mentari Raya Q4 2025:**

| KPI | Target | Q4 Actual | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue Growth Rate (vs. Q4 2024) | ≥ 10% | 12.2% (annual) | GREEN |
| Gross Profit Margin | ≥ 30% | 38.7% (Dec); avg Q4 = 37.0% | GREEN |
| Employee Productivity (RM/person) | ≥ RM 18,000/month | 18,750 (Dec) | GREEN |
| Customer Acquisition Cost | ≤ RM 1,500 | RM 1,141 | GREEN |
| AR Days | ≤ 45 days | 45.6 days | YELLOW |

**Q4 Average Gross Profit Margin verification:**
Q4 months: Oct (35.6%) + Nov (36.8%) + Dec (38.7%) ÷ 3 = 111.1% ÷ 3 = **37.0%** ✓

### 4.3 Data Source Declaration and AI Disclosure

Every BI report must include a statement at the end disclosing:

1. **Data sources used** (e.g., "Monthly sales data extracted from AutoCount Accounting, version [X], exported on [date]")
2. **AI tools used** (e.g., "ChatGPT Advanced Data Analysis used for trend summarisation; all figures cross-checked against source data by [name of staff]")
3. **Review declaration** (e.g., "This report has been reviewed by [name/designation] and all figures confirmed accurate as at [date]")

> **Principle:** AI assists the analysis — but a human must be accountable for every number in a management report. The AI disclosure ensures transparency and supports PDPA 2010 obligations regarding data processing.

## 5. Visualising KPI Trends for Non-Technical Audiences

### 5.1 Matching KPI Type to Chart Type

| KPI Type | Best Chart | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Trend over time (monthly revenue) | Line chart | Shows continuous movement; direction is clear |
| Category comparison (expenses by type) | Bar chart (sorted) | Easy to rank and compare |
| Proportion (expense category % of total) | Donut chart (≤5 segments) | Proportion at a glance |
| Single number vs. target | Scorecard with colour coding | Immediate visual status |
| KPI performance across multiple metrics | Table with RAG (Red/Amber/Green) | Multi-metric overview |

### 5.2 Writing Chart Titles for Non-Technical Readers

A chart title should state the finding, not just the variable:

| Weak Title (avoid) | Strong Title (use) |
|---|---|
| "Revenue" | "Monthly Revenue Grew 12.2% in 2025, Peaking at RM 150,000 in December" |
| "Expenses by Category" | "Staff Costs Account for Over Half of All Operating Expenses (52.5%)" |
| "Gross Profit Margin %" | "Profit Margin Improved from 26.5% in January to 38.7% in December 2025" |

### 5.3 The 5-Second Rule

A well-designed BI visual should be understood in 5 seconds by a first-time viewer. Test this by showing a draft to a colleague who has not seen the data and asking:

1. What is this chart showing?
2. What is the main message?
3. What should management do based on this?

If the colleague cannot answer in 5 seconds, the chart needs redesigning — typically by simplifying the title, reducing the number of data series, or adding data labels.
