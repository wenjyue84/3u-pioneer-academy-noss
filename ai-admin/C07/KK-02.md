## Work Activity Title

Conduct a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) for an AI Tool and Draft a PDPA Data Breach Notification Letter

**Primary Work Activities Covered:** Work Activities 2 and 4

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this activity, trainees will have:

- Completed a structured Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) for an AI tool across five risk dimensions
- Made a documented risk decision (approve / approve with conditions / reject) for the AI tool
- Drafted a PDPA breach notification letter to the Personal Data Protection Commissioner for a simulated incident
- Completed a post-incident review form

## Part B: Simulated Data Breach Response

### Breach Scenario

**Date and Time of Incident:** Monday, [leave blank — trainee does not fill in a real date]

**What Happened:** A staff member at Syarikat Cemerlang Sdn Bhd, while working from home, used the personal (free) ChatGPT account to summarise a client onboarding form. The form contained the following client data: full name, IC number, home address, monthly income figure, and tax identification number. The staff member did not realise this constituted a disclosure of personal data to a third-party processor without consent.

When the IT coordinator reviewed the company's AI usage logs (flagged by a browser monitoring tool), the incident was detected. The staff member acknowledged what had happened. The incident was assessed as a **potential breach of the Disclosure Principle and Security Principle of PDPA 2010**, affecting one (1) data subject.

## Evaluation Criteria

| Criterion | Excellent (4) | Good (3) | Adequate (2) | Needs Work (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PIA Completeness** | All 5 dimensions fully completed with specific observations, risk levels, justifications referencing PDPA principles by name, and practical controls | 4–5 dimensions complete; PDPA linkage present but 1–2 principles missing by name | 3–4 dimensions complete; risk levels stated but PDPA linkage weak | Fewer than 3 dimensions complete; PIA decision unsupported |
| **PIA Decision Quality** | Decision clearly justified with specific PDPA principles; conditions (if any) are specific and enforceable | Decision justified; conditions present but somewhat generic | Decision present; justification weak | Decision with no justification, or decision contradicts findings |
| **Containment Checklist** | All 6 actions ticked with clear, accurate reasons | 5 actions ticked; reasons mostly accurate | 4 actions ticked; reasons partially accurate | 3 or fewer actions; reasons missing or incorrect |
| **Notification Letter** | All 8 required elements present; letter is professional, accurate, and appropriately formal; no real personal data used | 6–7 elements present; professional tone | 5 elements present; mostly appropriate | Fewer than 5 elements; significant errors or inappropriate content |
| **Post-Incident Review** | All fields completed; root cause accurately identified; corrective actions are specific and realistic | 7–8 fields complete; root cause identified | 5–6 fields complete; some accurate | Fewer than 5 fields; root cause not identified |

## Reflection Questions (Soalan Refleksi)

1. If the same incident involved 500 data subjects instead of one, how would your breach response differ?
2. The Act requires notification to the Commissioner "as soon as practicable" after the data controller becomes aware of the breach. What systems or processes should an organisation have in place to ensure early detection so that this obligation can be met promptly?
3. In this scenario, what action should be taken regarding the staff member who caused the breach? Consider both disciplinary and training responses.
