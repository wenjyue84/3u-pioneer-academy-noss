## 1. Overview of PDPA 2010 (Akta Perlindungan Data Peribadi 2010)

### 1.1 What Is PDPA 2010?

The **Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (PDPA)**, also known as **Akta Perlindungan Data Peribadi 2010 — Act 709**, is the primary Malaysian legislation governing the collection, recording, holding, storage, use, disclosure, and processing of **personal data (data peribadi)** in commercial transactions.

"Personal data" under the Act means any information that directly or indirectly identifies a living individual. Examples relevant to office AI use include: employee names, IC numbers, salary details, customer email addresses, phone numbers, health records, and photographs.

**Regulatory authority:** The Act is enforced by the **Personal Data Protection Commissioner (Pesuruhjaya Perlindungan Data Peribadi)**, housed under the **Department of Personal Data Protection (Jabatan Perlindungan Data Peribadi — JPDP)**, which operates under the **Ministry of Communications and Digital (Kementerian Komunikasi dan Digital — KKD)**.

> **NOTA PENTING:** The PDPA 2010 was amended by **Act A1727** (Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024, royal assent 9 October 2024). Key new obligations — including mandatory breach notification and the formal Data Protection Officer (DPO) role — were phased in from 2025 onwards (Phase 1: 1 Jan 2025; Phase 2 — increased penalties and terminology changes: 1 Apr 2025; Phase 3 — mandatory DPO, mandatory breach notification, data portability: 1 Jun 2025). Trainees should check the **JPDP official website (pdp.gov.my)** for the operative subsidiary regulations.

### 1.2 Scope of PDPA 2010

| Aspect | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **Who must comply** | Any person or organisation that processes personal data in Malaysia **in the course of a commercial transaction** |
| **What data is protected** | Any information that identifies or can identify a living individual |
| **Sensitive personal data** | A subset requiring additional protection: health/medical data, political opinions, religious beliefs, criminal offences, biometric data |
| **Exclusions** | Personal data processed for personal or household affairs; data processed for credit reference purposes (governed separately); government data (though government is encouraged to apply equivalent standards) |

### 1.3 Penalties Under PDPA 2010

The Act provides for criminal penalties for breaches. For **general contraventions** of the Act (including breach of the data principles), the law provides for fines and/or imprisonment. Under **Act A1727 (in force 1 April 2025)**, the maximum penalty for offences such as processing data without consent or failing to comply with a notice from the Commissioner is **a fine not exceeding RM1,000,000 or imprisonment not exceeding three (3) years, or both**. The original 2010 Act carried a maximum of RM300,000 / 2 years (now superseded).

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Do not state specific section numbers or penalty amounts for individual offences beyond what is written here without verifying the current text of the Act from the Attorney General's Chambers (AGC) of Malaysia or the JPDP. Always refer trainees to the official consolidated text.

## 2. The Seven Principles of PDPA 2010 (Tujuh Prinsip PDPA 2010)

Every organisation processing personal data must observe all seven principles. Breach of a principle is a criminal offence.

### Principle 1 — General Principle (Prinsip Am)

Personal data must not be processed unless **at least one of the following conditions** is met:

- The **data subject has given consent** to the processing
- Processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party
- Processing is required to comply with a legal obligation of the data controller
- Processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject
- Processing is necessary for the administration of justice or in the performance of a function under any written law
- Processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests of the data controller or a third party, except where such interests are overridden by the interests of the data subject

**Application to AI in the office:** When a data controller feeds employee performance data into an AI analytics tool, the General Principle requires that either employee consent is obtained, or processing is justifiable under one of the listed conditions. Uploading customer PII into a public AI tool without consent violates this principle.

### Principle 2 — Notice and Choice Principle (Prinsip Notis dan Pilihan)

Before collecting personal data, the data controller **must inform the data subject** (in writing, in two languages if practicable) of:

- The description of data being collected
- The purpose for which data is being collected
- The right to request access and correction
- Whether the data is to be disclosed to any third party
- Whether the provision of data is obligatory or voluntary, and the consequences of failing to provide it
- The contact information of the data controller

**Application to AI in the office:** A Privacy Notice (Notis Privasi) on every customer intake form, online registration page, or HR onboarding document is mandatory. The data controller bears this obligation regardless of whether forms are generated by AI.

### Principle 3 — Disclosure Principle (Prinsip Pendedahan)

Personal data must not be disclosed to **any third party** without the consent of the data subject, unless:

- Disclosure is required or authorised by law
- Disclosure is necessary for the prevention, detection, investigation, or prosecution of an offence
- Disclosure is in the public interest as determined by a competent authority

**Application to AI in the office:** Using a third-party AI tool (such as a public chatbot) to process client data constitutes disclosure to a third party. Organisations must either obtain consent or ensure the AI provider is bound by a data processing agreement that provides equivalent protection.

### Principle 4 — Security Principle (Prinsip Keselamatan)

Data controllers must take **practical steps to protect personal data** from any loss, misuse, modification, unauthorised or accidental access or disclosure, alteration, or destruction.

Security measures must be proportionate to the sensitivity of the data and the harm that could result from a breach.

**Application to AI in the office:** Accessing AI tools on unsecured public Wi-Fi while handling personal data violates this principle. Organisations must implement access controls, encryption for stored data, and clear policies on which devices may access personal data systems.

### Principle 5 — Retention Principle (Prinsip Pengekalan)

Personal data must not be kept **longer than is necessary** for the fulfilment of the purpose for which it was collected.

Organisations should establish a **data retention schedule (jadual pengekalan data)** defining the maximum retention period for each category of data, and a secure disposal procedure for data that has exceeded its retention period.

**Application to AI in the office:** Chat logs, transcripts, and data exports from AI tools that contain personal data must be deleted or anonymised once the business purpose is served. Storing old customer inquiry logs indefinitely in AI training datasets is a potential violation.

### Principle 6 — Data Integrity Principle (Prinsip Integriti Data)

A data controller must take reasonable steps to ensure that personal data is **accurate, complete, not misleading, and kept up-to-date** where necessary, having regard to the purpose for which the data is to be used, including any disclosure.

**Application to AI in the office:** AI tools that automatically fill in or update employee or customer records must be monitored. AI-generated summaries or profiles must be verified before being stored as official records, as AI may introduce inaccuracies (hallucinations).

### Principle 7 — Access Principle (Prinsip Akses)

A data subject has the right to:

- **Access** their personal data held by the data controller
- **Correct** inaccurate, incomplete, misleading, or outdated personal data
- Withdraw consent (subject to the terms of any contract)

Data controllers must provide a mechanism for data subjects to submit access and correction requests, and must respond within a reasonable time. The data controller may charge a prescribed fee for providing access.

**Application to AI in the office:** If an employee requests to see what personal data is held about them in an AI HR system, the organisation must be able to retrieve and provide that data. AI systems that make it technically impossible to identify and extract individual records may create compliance risks.

## 3. Roles: Data Controller vs. Data Processor (Pengawal Data vs. Pemproses Data)

Under PDPA 2010:

| Term | Act A1727 Term (since 1 Apr 2025) | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data Controller (Pengawal Data)** *(formerly "Data User / Pengguna Data" under the original 2010 Act)* | Primary regulated party | A person who either alone, or jointly, or in common with other persons processes personal data, or has control over or authorises the processing of any personal data | Your employer; a hospital; an SME that collects customer orders |
| **Data Processor (Pemproses Data)** | Secondary regulated party | Any person other than an employee of the data controller who processes personal data on behalf of the data controller | An AI vendor; a cloud storage provider; a payroll outsourcing firm |

> **Practical note on AI tools:** When an organisation subscribes to an AI platform (e.g., Microsoft Copilot for Business, Google Workspace AI features), the organisation is the **data controller** and the AI platform provider is the **data processor**. The organisation remains responsible for ensuring the data processor provides sufficient guarantees about data security. A written **Data Processing Agreement (Perjanjian Pemprosesan Data)** between the data controller and the data processor is a critical governance document.
>
> **Terminology note:** Act A1727 (Phase 2, 1 Apr 2025) replaced "data user (pengguna data)" with "data controller (pengawal data)" throughout the Act. Both terms may appear in older reference materials.

## 4. Rights of Data Subjects (Hak Subjek Data)

Under PDPA 2010, data subjects (individuals whose personal data is being processed) have the following rights:

| Right | Description | How to Exercise |
|---|---|---|
| **Right to Access (Hak Akses)** | Request a copy of personal data held about them | Submit written request to the data controller |
| **Right to Correct (Hak Pembetulan)** | Request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data | Submit written correction request |
| **Right to Withdraw Consent (Hak Menarik Balik Persetujuan)** | Withdraw consent for processing at any time (subject to any contractual obligations) | Written notice to data controller |
| **Right to Prevent Processing for Direct Marketing (Hak Menghentikan Pemasaran Langsung)** | Opt out of receiving direct marketing communications | Written notice to data controller; must be honoured promptly |
| **Right to Prevent Processing Likely to Cause Damage or Distress** | Object to processing where it is causing or likely to cause substantial unwarranted damage or distress | Written notice; data controller may contest |

## 5. Amendments Under Act A1727 — Key New Obligations

The PDPA 2010 was amended by Act A1727 (Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024). The amendments introduced significant new obligations phased in from 2025. All three phases are in force as of the preparation of this material:

- **Phase 1 (1 January 2025):** Initial commencement provisions
- **Phase 2 (1 April 2025):** Terminology changes (data user → data controller; see Section 3), processor obligations, increased penalties
- **Phase 3 (1 June 2025):** Mandatory DPO appointment, mandatory data breach notification, data portability rights

Key new obligations trainees should be aware of:

### 5.1 Mandatory Data Breach Notification

Under Act A1727 (Phase 3, in force 1 June 2025), data controllers are required to **notify the Personal Data Protection Commissioner** when a personal data breach occurs that is likely to affect the rights and freedoms of data subjects. The notification must be made **"as soon as practicable"**. Where the breach is likely to cause significant harm to affected individuals, the data controller must also notify those data subjects **"without unnecessary delay"**.

> **NOTA PENTING — TIMEFRAME:** The 72-hour deadline appeared only in the public consultation paper and **was NOT enacted** in the final legislation. The statutory standard is "as soon as practicable" for the Commissioner, and "without unnecessary delay" for affected data subjects. The penalty for failing to notify is a fine **not exceeding RM250,000** and/or imprisonment **not exceeding 2 years**. Trainees must refer to **pdp.gov.my** for the operative guideline and any subsidiary regulations setting out detailed requirements.

### 5.2 Data Protection Officer (DPO / Pegawai Perlindungan Data)

Act A1727 (Phase 3, in force 1 June 2025) requires **every** data controller and data processor to appoint at least one **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**. There is no turnover threshold or headcount threshold — the obligation is universal. The DPO is responsible for:

- Ensuring the organisation's compliance with PDPA 2010 and its amendments
- Serving as the point of contact for the Personal Data Protection Commissioner
- Advising on data protection impact assessments
- Training staff on data privacy obligations

> **NOTA PENTING:** The Act does not prescribe a specific penalty for failing to appoint a DPO. Do not state a penalty figure for this obligation. For specific advice, refer trainees to pdp.gov.my or a qualified legal practitioner.

## 6. MCMC AI Usage Guidelines (Garis Panduan Penggunaan AI MCMC)

The **Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC / Suruhanjaya Komunikasi dan Multimedia Malaysia)** has issued guidance on responsible and ethical AI use in Malaysia. These guidelines complement the legal obligations under PDPA 2010 by establishing a principles-based framework for AI governance.

> **NOTA PENTING:** MCMC guidance documents are periodically updated. Trainees should refer to the MCMC official website (mcmc.gov.my) for the current version of any AI governance guidelines. The themes below reflect the general direction of Malaysian AI policy as of the preparation of this material.

### 6.1 Core Themes in Malaysian AI Governance

| Theme | Description | Workplace Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Responsible AI Use** | AI must be used in a manner that is lawful, ethical, and does not harm individuals or society | Do not use AI to generate false or misleading content; do not use AI to discriminate against employees or customers |
| **Transparency (Ketelusan)** | Users and affected parties should be informed when AI is making or influencing decisions | Disclose to job applicants if AI was used to screen their resume; label AI-generated content |
| **Accountability (Akauntabiliti)** | Organisations must be able to explain and take responsibility for AI-generated outputs | Maintain audit logs of AI decisions; assign human reviewers for high-stakes outputs |
| **Human Oversight (Pengawasan Manusia)** | Critical decisions must involve human review; AI should not operate autonomously on high-risk matters | HR termination letters, loan approvals, and medical decisions must be reviewed by qualified humans |
| **Data Privacy by Design** | Privacy protections must be embedded in AI system design from the start, not added as an afterthought | When selecting an AI tool, evaluate its data handling policy before deployment |
| **Inclusivity and Fairness** | AI systems must not perpetuate or amplify bias based on race, religion, gender, or disability | Audit AI recruitment tools for potential bias in screening |

## 7. Data Storage Policies — ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, and Claude

When using AI tools in the workplace, understanding where data goes is essential for PDPA compliance.

| AI Tool | Data Storage Policy (General — verify with provider's current policy) | PDPA Risk Level | Recommended Workplace Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT (Free / Plus — OpenAI)** | Conversations may be used to improve models by default; data is processed on OpenAI servers (USA); users can opt out of training data use | **HIGH** for personal data — do not input client PII, employee data, or confidential business data | Personal productivity tasks only; no personal data |
| **ChatGPT (Enterprise / API)** | Data is not used for model training; enterprise-grade data processing agreement available; data remains within agreed boundaries | **LOWER** with DPA in place | Approved for business use with proper DPA and access controls |
| **Microsoft Copilot for Microsoft 365** | Data stays within the organisation's Microsoft 365 tenant; Microsoft acts as a data processor under Microsoft's DPA; data is not used to train foundation models | **LOWER** with proper M365 licensing and configuration | Approved for business use; preferred option for PDPA-compliant AI in Malaysian offices |
| **Claude (Anthropic — Consumer)** | Similar to ChatGPT consumer — conversations may be used to improve models; processed on Anthropic servers | **HIGH** for personal data without enterprise agreement | Personal productivity only; no personal data |
| **Claude (Anthropic — API / Enterprise)** | Data is not used for training; enterprise DPA available | **LOWER** with DPA in place | Approved with proper DPA in place |

> **PENTING:** Provider policies change. Always check the current Privacy Policy and Data Processing Agreement of any AI tool before approving it for workplace use involving personal data. Consult your organisation's legal counsel or DPO if uncertain.

## 8. Sector-Specific Data Obligations

Beyond PDPA 2010, certain sectors in Malaysia have additional data protection requirements:

| Sector | Additional Obligation | Governing Body |
|---|---|---|
| **Health / Medical** | Patient records are highly sensitive; special consent rules apply; medical data is "sensitive personal data" under PDPA requiring explicit consent | Ministry of Health Malaysia (KKM) |
| **Banking / Finance** | Customer financial data is regulated under Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) guidelines; Financial Institutions Act obligations | Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) |
| **HR / Employment** | Employee payroll and statutory data (EPF, SOCSO) governed by PDPA; Employment Act 1955 creates data obligations for record-keeping | JPDP; Department of Labour (JTKSM) |
| **Communications / Telco** | Personal data in subscriber records governed by CMA 1998 and PDPA | MCMC |
