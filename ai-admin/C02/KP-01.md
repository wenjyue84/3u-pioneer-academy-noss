1. Explain the capabilities and limitations of AI writing tools — ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Word Copilot, and Google Docs AI — for business correspondence.
2. Apply prompt engineering principles (CRAFT framework) to generate formal BM surat rasmi, English business letters, memos, and quotations.
3. Identify the structural requirements of Malaysian government-standard business correspondence formats.
4. Review and quality-check AI-generated text for accuracy, tone, and cultural appropriateness.
5. Produce multilingual business documents (BM / English / Chinese) using AI assistance with appropriate human editing.

## 1. AI Writing Tools for Business Correspondence

### 1.1 Overview of Available AI Writing Tools

AI writing tools use large language models (LLM / Model Bahasa Besar) to generate, rewrite, summarise, and translate text based on user instructions (prompts). For administrative professionals in Malaysia, these tools significantly reduce drafting time for routine correspondence.

| Tool | Developer | Key Features | Access Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ChatGPT** (GPT-4o) | OpenAI | Multilingual drafting, tone adjustment, long documents | chat.openai.com (free/paid) |
| **Claude** (claude.ai) | Anthropic | Accurate tone, excellent at formal writing, context window large | claude.ai (free/paid) |
| **Microsoft Word Copilot** | Microsoft | Embedded in Word; drafts from brief, rewrites, tone checker | Microsoft 365 subscription |
| **Google Docs AI** (Help me write) | Google | Embedded in Google Docs; drafting and rewriting | Google Workspace account |
| **Notion AI** | Notion | Summarisation, knowledge base writing, action items | Notion subscription |

> **NOTA PENTING:** AI writing tools are drafting assistants, not replacements for human judgment. All AI-generated correspondence must be reviewed, edited, and verified before sending. AI tools may produce factual errors, outdated information, or culturally inappropriate phrases — especially in formal Malay government correspondence.

### 1.2 How AI Writing Tools Work

AI writing tools predict the most likely continuation of text based on patterns learned from billions of documents. Understanding this helps users write better prompts and evaluate outputs critically:

- **Strengths:** Generating structure, varying tone, producing first drafts quickly, multilingual translation, grammar checking
- **Limitations:** May hallucinate facts (invent names, dates, reference numbers), may use outdated language conventions, cannot access your internal systems or real-time information
- **Best practice:** Always provide context in your prompt (who is writing, to whom, what purpose, what tone)

### 1.3 The CRAFT Prompt Engineering Framework

Effective AI output requires structured instructions. The **CRAFT** framework provides a reliable structure for business writing prompts:

| CRAFT Element | Meaning | Example for a Business Letter |
|---|---|---|
| **C** — Context | Background situation | "I am an admin officer at a training centre in Kuala Lumpur." |
| **R** — Role | Who the AI should act as | "Act as a professional business correspondence writer." |
| **A** — Action | What to produce | "Write a formal apology letter in English." |
| **F** — Format | Structure and length | "Use standard business letter format, 3 paragraphs, under 200 words." |
| **T** — Tone | Emotional register | "Formal, respectful, and apologetic — not defensive." |

**Example CRAFT Prompt:**
```
Context: Our training company (3U Pioneer Academy) missed a scheduled meeting with a 
         government agency (HRDC) due to a system error.
Role: Act as a professional business correspondence writer fluent in formal Malaysian business English.
Action: Write an apology letter from the Academy Principal to the HRDC Director.
Format: Standard Malaysian business letter format — date, reference, salutation, 3 paragraphs 
        (apology, explanation, remediation), close.
Tone: Formal, sincerely apologetic, professional, not defensive.
```

## 2. Malaysian Business Correspondence Formats

### 2.1 Surat Rasmi (Formal Letter) — Government Standard (BM)

Malaysian government-standard formal letters (Surat Rasmi) follow a prescribed format set by the Public Service Department (Jabatan Perkhidmatan Awam). All correspondence with government bodies must comply.

**Structure of a BM Surat Rasmi:**

```
[Nama Pengirim / Jawatan]
[Nama Organisasi]
[Alamat]
[Poskod, Negeri]
                                    [Tarikh: DD Bulan YYYY]

[Rujukan Surat]

[Nama Penerima / Jawatan]
[Nama Agensi / Organisasi]
[Alamat Penerima]

Tuan/Puan,

TAJUK SURAT DALAM HURUF BESAR DAN DITEBALKAN

    Dengan hormatnya saya merujuk kepada perkara di atas dan ingin memaklumkan bahawa...

    [Perenggan isi utama]

    [Perenggan penutup — tindakan yang dikehendaki]

2. Kerjasama dan pertimbangan pihak tuan/puan didahulukan dengan ucapan terima kasih.

Sekian, terima kasih.

"BERKHIDMAT UNTUK NEGARA"

Yang benar,


.....................................................
[NAMA PENUH DALAM HURUF BESAR]
[Jawatan]
[Nama Organisasi]
```

**Key Rules for BM Surat Rasmi:**

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Salutation** | "Tuan" (male) or "Puan" (female); "Tuan/Puan" if gender unknown |
| **Subject line** | UPPERCASE, bold, underlined — briefly describes the matter |
| **Paragraph numbering** | First paragraph uses "Dengan hormatnya..." formula; subsequent paragraphs numbered 2, 3, 4... |
| **Closing** | Always includes "BERKHIDMAT UNTUK NEGARA" motto for government bodies |
| **Signature block** | Name in UPPERCASE, title below name, no personal details |
| **Reference number** | Format: `[Organisation Code]/[Series]/[Year]` e.g. `3UPA/ADM/001/2026` |

### 2.2 English Business Letter

Standard Malaysian English business letters follow British business letter conventions (reflecting Malaysia's Commonwealth heritage):

**Structure of an English Business Letter:**

```
[Sender Name]
[Designation]
[Organisation]
[Address]
[Date: DD Month YYYY]

[Reference Number]

[Recipient Name]
[Designation]
[Organisation]
[Address]

Dear [Title Surname / Sir / Madam],

[SUBJECT: UPPERCASE BOLD]

    [Opening paragraph — state purpose]

    [Body paragraph(s) — provide details, context]

    [Closing paragraph — state required action or next steps]

Yours sincerely / Yours faithfully,


.............................
[FULL NAME IN CAPITALS]
[Designation]
[Organisation]
```

> **Yours sincerely** vs. **Yours faithfully:** Use "Yours sincerely" when you have addressed the person by name (Dear Mr Tan). Use "Yours faithfully" when the salutation is "Dear Sir/Madam."

### 2.3 Memo (Memorandum Dalaman)

Memos are internal documents for communication within an organisation. They are shorter and less formal than letters.

**Standard Memo Format:**

```
MEMORANDUM

KEPADA    :  [Name / Department]
DARIPADA  :  [Name / Designation]
TARIKH    :  [DD Month YYYY]
PERKARA   :  [SUBJECT IN UPPERCASE]
RUJUKAN   :  [Reference number if applicable]

[Body — direct, concise. No salutation, no formal close.]

[Name]
[Designation]
[Date]
```

**Key differences from a formal letter:**
- No recipient address block
- No salutation ("Dear...") or formal close ("Yours sincerely...")
- Subject line replaces a lengthy opening
- Can be distributed to multiple recipients (CC list at bottom)

### 2.4 Quotation Letter (Surat Sebut Harga)

A quotation letter is a formal business document that states the price, terms, and specifications of goods or services offered. In Malaysia, procurement quotations for government projects above RM200 follow specific formats under Treasury Instructions (Arahan Perbendaharaan).

**Essential elements of a Quotation Letter:**

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Header** | Company name, SSM registration, address, contact |
| **Reference** | Tender/quotation reference number |
| **Date** | Issue date |
| **Validity period** | e.g., "This quotation is valid for 30 days" |
| **Itemised table** | Item no., description, quantity, unit price (MYR), total |
| **Tax line** | SST (if applicable) — 6% service tax or 10% sales tax |
| **Grand total** | Total in figures AND in words (Ringgit Malaysia...) |
| **Terms** | Payment terms (e.g., "30 days from invoice date") |
| **Authorised signatory** | Name, designation, company stamp |

## 3. Multilingual Drafting with AI

### 3.1 Language Context in Malaysian Office Administration

Malaysian administrative offices routinely produce documents in multiple languages:

- **Bahasa Malaysia (BM):** Required for all government correspondence; official language of Malaysian legislation and public administration
- **English:** Widely used in private sector, multinational companies, and international correspondence
- **Mandarin Chinese (普通话):** Common in Chinese-owned SMEs, trading companies, and correspondence with Chinese-market clients

AI tools handle all three languages, but the quality and formality of BM output requires particularly careful human review, as AI may use informal register or non-standard government terminology.

### 3.2 Drafting a BM Surat Rasmi with AI — Step-by-Step

**Step 1 — Prepare your context brief:**
Before opening any AI tool, note down: who is writing, to whom, what is the purpose, what tone is needed, what specific facts must be included.

**Step 2 — Enter a structured prompt (CRAFT):**
```
Konteks: Saya seorang Penolong Tadbir di sebuah akademi latihan swasta di Kuala Lumpur 
         bernama 3U Pioneer Academy.
Peranan: Berlakon sebagai penulis surat rasmi BM yang mahir dalam format Jabatan 
         Perkhidmatan Awam Malaysia.
Tindakan: Tulis surat rasmi dalam Bahasa Malaysia memohon kelulusan penggunaan dewan 
          majlis bandaraya untuk majlis graduasi kami pada 15 November 2026.
Format: Format surat rasmi standard JPK — alamat, rujukan, nama penerima, tajuk surat 
        huruf besar tebal, perenggan bernombor, penutup formal dengan "BERKHIDMAT UNTUK NEGARA".
Nada: Formal, sopan, hormat — mengikut protokol surat rasmi kerajaan Malaysia.
```

**Step 3 — Review the AI output against this checklist:**

| Checklist Item | Pass / Fail |
|---|---|
| Subject line in UPPERCASE and bold | |
| First paragraph begins with "Dengan hormatnya..." formula | |
| Paragraphs after first are numbered (2., 3., ...) | |
| No fabricated reference numbers or names | |
| Closing motto "BERKHIDMAT UNTUK NEGARA" present | |
| Language is formal Malay, not informal/colloquial | |
| Signature block has name in UPPERCASE | |

**Step 4 — Edit and personalise:**
- Replace any placeholder names with actual names
- Add the real reference number from your records system
- Check spelling of department names and titles
- Confirm all facts (dates, amounts, event details) are accurate

**Step 5 — Final review by a senior or supervisor** before sending to a government body.

### 3.3 Complaint-Response Memo with AI

When drafting an internal response to a complaint, the tone must balance professionalism with empathy. Prompt example:

```
Write an internal memo in English from the Operations Manager to all training staff 
addressing complaints about late issuance of certificates. 
Include: acknowledgement of the delay, brief explanation (system migration), 
remediation steps (batch processing by 15 August 2026), and apology. 
Tone: professional, accountable, solution-focused. 
Format: standard Malaysian business memo — KEPADA / DARIPADA / TARIKH / PERKARA fields.
```

### 3.4 AI Quality-Check Protocol for Generated Text

Every AI-generated document must pass a **4-point quality check** before use:

| Check | What to Verify |
|---|---|
| **1. Factual accuracy** | Are all names, dates, reference numbers, and amounts correct? |
| **2. Tone appropriateness** | Is the register suitable for the recipient and relationship? |
| **3. Legal safety** | Does the document make any claims that could create legal liability? |
| **4. Cultural sensitivity** | Are salutations, titles, and any cultural references appropriate? |

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Never send AI-generated correspondence to a government body, client, or external party without a complete human review. Incorrect titles (e.g., addressing a "Datuk" as "Encik"), wrong reference numbers, or factual errors in official letters can damage institutional relationships and, in the case of procurement letters, create legal disputes.

## 4. Microsoft Word Copilot for Document Drafting

### 4.1 Accessing Word Copilot

Microsoft Word Copilot is available to users with a **Microsoft 365** subscription (personal or business). As of 2026, Copilot is integrated into:
- Microsoft Word (desktop and web)
- Microsoft Outlook (email drafting)
- Microsoft PowerPoint (presentation generation)

To access in Word: open a new or existing document → click the **Copilot icon** (sparkle icon) in the ribbon → "Draft with Copilot" panel opens on the right.

### 4.2 Drafting a Document with Word Copilot

**Scenario:** Draft a quotation letter for IT training services.

1. Open a new Word document.
2. Click Copilot → "Draft with Copilot."
3. Enter the instruction: *"Draft a quotation letter for IT training services for 25 employees, 3 days, RM 450 per person, including SST at 8%, for a company in Petaling Jaya. Use formal Malaysian business English format."*
4. Word Copilot generates a draft directly into the document.
5. Review and edit: replace placeholder company names, verify the SST calculation (RM450 × 25 = RM11,250 × 1.08 = RM12,150), add actual company stamp reference.
6. Use Copilot's "Rewrite" feature to adjust tone if needed.

### 4.3 Google Docs — "Help me write" Feature

Google Docs AI is available with a **Google Workspace** account (business tier) or Google One AI Premium subscription.

To access: open Google Docs → click the **pencil/sparkle icon** in the left margin of a new paragraph → "Help me write" panel opens.

**Limitation compared to standalone ChatGPT or Claude:** Google Docs AI produces shorter outputs and may not maintain complex formatting. For multi-page documents, it is better to draft in ChatGPT/Claude and paste the result into Google Docs for final formatting.

## 5. Practical Application Summary

### 5.1 Choosing the Right Tool for Each Document Type

| Document Type | Recommended Tool | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| BM Surat Rasmi (government) | ChatGPT or Claude | Better BM output quality; flexible prompt control |
| English business letter | Word Copilot or Claude | Integrated into Word; outputs in document format |
| Internal memo | Word Copilot or ChatGPT | Quick, can specify memo format easily |
| Quotation letter | ChatGPT with calculation check | Can produce itemised tables; verify all figures manually |
| Formal email | Outlook Copilot or ChatGPT | Outlook Copilot integrates directly into email compose |
| Multilingual document | Claude or ChatGPT | Strongest multilingual performance |

### 5.2 Prompt Log — Why It Matters

A **prompt log (Log Arahan AI)** is a record of the AI instructions used to generate a document. Best practice is to save the prompt alongside the final document for:
- Audit trail — knowing how a document was produced
- Reproducibility — generating a similar document in future
- Quality accountability — showing that human review was conducted
- PDPA compliance — demonstrating that no personal data was inappropriately entered into AI tools

**Suggested Prompt Log Template:**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Date | DD/MM/YYYY |
| Tool Used | e.g., ChatGPT GPT-4o |
| Document Type | e.g., BM Surat Rasmi |
| Prompt Summary | First 50 words of the prompt used |
| Changes Made to AI Output | e.g., "Corrected reference number; adjusted salutation; removed hallucinated statistic in paragraph 2" |
| Reviewed By | Name and designation |
