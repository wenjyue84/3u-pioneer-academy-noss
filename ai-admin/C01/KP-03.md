## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees will be able to:
1. Design a phased AI implementation roadmap (Pelan Hala Tuju AI) for an office environment.
2. Identify and address common sources of employee resistance during AI adoption.
3. Develop a basic staff training plan tailored to different skill levels.
4. Calculate the Return on Investment (ROI) of an AI automation project using the time-savings formula.
5. Structure and present an AI business case to management in a clear, evidence-based format.

## 1. Pelan Hala Tuju Pelaksanaan AI / AI Implementation Roadmap

### 1.1 Why a Phased Approach?

Organisations that attempt to deploy multiple AI tools simultaneously across all departments typically face one or more of these problems:
- Staff overwhelmed with too many new tools at once
- IT and data integration issues compounding across tools
- No clear success metric, so management confidence erodes
- When one tool fails, confidence in the entire AI programme is damaged

A **phased roadmap (pelan berperingkat)** solves these problems by delivering value in stages, building confidence, and allowing the organisation to learn before scaling.

### 1.2 The Three-Phase AI Roadmap Structure

**Phase 1 — Foundation and Quick Wins (Months 1–2)**

Objectives:
- Complete workflow analysis and AI readiness gap assessment (KP-01)
- Address critical readiness gaps (digitise key data, set up Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace)
- Deploy 1–2 Quick Win automations from the Automation Opportunity Matrix

Typical Quick Win examples for a Malaysian SME:
- Automate leave application via Kakitangan.com (replaces paper forms and manual spreadsheet updates)
- Set up Power Automate to route incoming invoices from email to a shared SharePoint folder automatically
- Enable Microsoft Copilot draft in Outlook for staff who send high volumes of standard correspondence

**Success criteria for Phase 1:** At least one automation running reliably for 4 consecutive weeks; staff using it without trainer assistance.

---

**Phase 2 — Core Process Automation (Months 3–5)**

Objectives:
- Automate 3–5 higher-complexity workflows identified in Phase 1 analysis
- Integrate AI tools across departments (accounts, HR, admin)
- Conduct structured staff training for all affected roles
- Begin collecting ROI data (time saved per task per month)

Typical Phase 2 automations:
- AI-assisted invoice OCR processing (Dext or equivalent) integrated with AutoCount or SQL Accounting
- Automated monthly HR attendance report generation via Power Automate + Excel
- Document management system with AI-powered search (SharePoint + Copilot)

**Success criteria for Phase 2:** Three or more automations operational; time-saving data collected for at least 2 months; staff satisfaction survey conducted.

---

**Phase 3 — Scale and Optimise (Month 6 onward)**

Objectives:
- Scale successful automations to additional departments or workflow variants
- Review and optimise under-performing automations
- Conduct ROI review and present findings to management
- Plan next cycle of automation opportunities based on Phase 1–2 learnings

**Success criteria for Phase 3:** Documented ROI report presented to management; at least one automation retired or replaced based on performance data; next 12-month roadmap approved.

### 1.3 90-Day Roadmap Gantt Chart (Simplified)

| Week | Activity | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Complete workflow analysis; finalise automation opportunity list | Admin Manager / Project Lead |
| 3 | AI Readiness Gap Assessment; address critical gaps | IT support + HR |
| 4 | Tool selection and procurement approval | Management |
| 5–6 | Phase 1 Quick Win deployment (leave automation) | IT + HR |
| 7 | Staff training — Phase 1 tools | Trainer |
| 8 | Phase 1 review; data collection begins | Admin Manager |
| 9–12 | Phase 2 automation deployment (invoice + report) | IT + Accounts |
| 11–12 | Phase 2 staff training | Trainer |
| 13 | Phase 2 review; ROI data collection | Admin Manager |

## 2. Pengurusan Perubahan untuk Penerimaan AI / Change Management for AI Adoption

### 2.1 Why Change Management is Critical

The most common reason AI automation projects fail in SMEs is not technology — it is **people**. Research consistently shows that employee resistance (rintangan pekerja) is the primary barrier to successful technology adoption.

In a Malaysian workplace context, common fears include:

| Fear | What employees think | Effective response |
|---|---|---|
| **Job replacement** | "The AI will take my job" | Reframe: AI handles the tedious, repetitive parts; your role evolves to higher-value tasks |
| **Incompetence** | "I am not tech-savvy enough to use this" | Structured training; patient support; celebrate small wins |
| **Loss of control** | "I don't trust the machine to do this correctly" | Show the audit trail; demonstrate error detection; allow override |
| **Extra workload** | "Learning this system will create more work" | Honest timeline; start with one tool; show time savings early |
| **Management distrust** | "This is just a way to monitor us more" | Transparent communication from leadership |

### 2.2 Kotter's 8-Step Change Model Applied to AI Adoption

**Kotter's 8-Step Change Model** is a widely used framework for leading organisational change. Applied to a Malaysian SME AI adoption:

| Step | Action in AI Adoption Context |
|---|---|
| **1. Create urgency** | Show management and staff the time wasted on current manual processes (use the time-cost data from KP-01) |
| **2. Build a guiding coalition** | Identify an "AI Champion" (Juara AI) in each department — someone respected who is enthusiastic about the change |
| **3. Form a strategic vision** | Articulate clearly: "By December, we will reduce invoice processing time by 80%, freeing our accounts team to focus on collections" |
| **4. Enlist volunteers** | Start with willing early adopters; do not force resisters to adopt first — use peer influence |
| **5. Enable action** | Remove barriers: provide training, IT support, and time during working hours to learn the new tools |
| **6. Generate short-term wins** | Celebrate the first automation milestone publicly — even a simple "our leave system is now 100% digital" announcement |
| **7. Sustain acceleration** | After initial wins, expand to next phase; do not let momentum drop after the first success |
| **8. Institute change** | Update the Employee Handbook (Buku Panduan Pekerja) and SOPs to reflect the new automated processes |

### 2.3 Communication Plan for AI Rollout

Effective communication is the backbone of successful change management. Use this three-stage communication approach:

**Before the change (pre-launch):**
- Town hall or department meeting: explain what AI tools are being adopted, why, and the timeline
- Individual briefings for those whose daily tasks will change most significantly
- Written FAQ document addressing the most common fears

**During the change (launch to Month 2):**
- Weekly brief updates from the AI project lead — progress, wins, issues being resolved
- Open-door policy: staff can raise concerns without judgment
- Quick-reference guides (1-page cheat sheets) for each new tool

**After initial adoption (Month 3+):**
- Monthly "AI Update" in team meetings — sharing time savings achieved, celebrating wins
- Feedback form: what is working, what is frustrating, what could be better
- Recognition for staff who have embraced AI tools and helped colleagues

## 3. Pelan Latihan Kakitangan / Staff Training Plan

### 3.1 Training Needs Analysis

Not all staff require the same level of AI training. Classify staff into three tiers:

| Tier | Profile | Training Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Power Users** | Admin managers, project leads, advanced users | Tool configuration, workflow building, prompt engineering, ROI tracking |
| **Tier 2 — Regular Users** | Accounts clerks, HR staff, admin assistants | Using the tools in their daily workflow; basic prompt writing; safety practices |
| **Tier 3 — Light Users** | Directors, department heads | Overview of what the AI tools do; how to read AI-generated outputs; governance and approval |

### 3.2 Training Pathway Design

For each tier, design a structured training path:

**Tier 1 — Power Users Training Path (20 hours total)**

| Module | Duration | Content |
|---|---|---|
| AI Tool Foundations | 2 hours | What AI can and cannot do; risks and limitations; PDPA obligations |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot Advanced | 4 hours | Power prompting in Word, Excel, Outlook; connecting to organisational data |
| Power Automate Workflow Building | 6 hours | Building, testing, and monitoring cloud flows; connecting to SharePoint and Forms |
| RPA with Power Automate Desktop | 4 hours | Recording desktop flows; error handling; scheduling |
| ROI Measurement and Reporting | 2 hours | Time-cost calculation; building an ROI dashboard; presenting to management |
| Governance and PDPA Compliance | 2 hours | Acceptable use policy; data handling rules; incident response |

**Tier 2 — Regular Users Training Path (6 hours total)**

| Module | Duration | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction to AI Tools | 1 hour | What is AI; what it can do for your daily work; what NOT to put into AI tools |
| Using Copilot / Gemini in Daily Work | 2 hours | Hands-on: draft email, summarise document, analyse data |
| Using the Automated Workflows | 2 hours | How to use (not build) the automations set up by Tier 1; what to do if automation fails |
| Safety and Data Privacy | 1 hour | Never upload client IC numbers, salary details, or confidential data to public AI tools |

**Tier 3 — Leadership Overview (2 hours total)**

| Module | Duration | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Briefing | 1 hour | What AI is doing in your organisation; what decisions are still human; how to read AI output critically |
| Governance Responsibilities | 1 hour | Approving AI policy; PDPA accountability at director level; escalation when things go wrong |

## 4. Pengiraan ROI Automasi AI / AI Automation ROI Calculation

### 4.1 The ROI Formula

**Return on Investment (ROI / Pulangan atas Pelaburan)** measures whether the benefit of an investment exceeds its cost.

```
ROI (%) = [(Annual Benefit - Annual Cost) ÷ Annual Cost] × 100
```

For AI automation projects, the primary benefit is **labour time saved**, converted into monetary value using the effective hourly rate established in KP-01.

```
Annual Benefit (MYR) = Monthly Time Saved (hours) × Effective Hourly Rate (MYR/hour) × 12
```

Additional benefits that may be quantifiable:
- **Error reduction:** If manual data entry errors cost RM X per month in corrections, and the automation eliminates 90% of errors, include 90% × RM X × 12 as an additional annual benefit
- **Faster processing:** If faster invoice payment earns early-payment discounts, quantify this
- **Scalability:** If the automation allows the same headcount to handle 50% more volume, this is a benefit but more difficult to quantify directly

### 4.2 Annual Cost Components

| Cost Category | What to Include |
|---|---|
| **Software subscription** | Annual licence cost (Copilot add-on, Zapier, n8n hosting) |
| **Implementation** | Internal staff hours spent setting up and testing (at their effective hourly rate) |
| **Training** | Trainer cost + staff time during training (at each person's effective hourly rate) |
| **Ongoing maintenance** | Estimated monthly hours spent monitoring and updating automations × 12 |

### 4.3 Worked Example — Invoice Processing ROI

**Scenario:** A Malaysian trading SME (50 employees, finance team of 3) automates its invoice processing using Dext (OCR invoice capture) integrated with SQL Accounting.

**Step 1 — Calculate Annual Benefit**

From KP-01 time-cost analysis:
- Time saved per invoice: 10 minutes (from 12 minutes to 2 minutes)
- Monthly invoice volume: 80 invoices
- Accounts clerk effective hourly rate: RM13.46/hour

| Item | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly time saved | (10 min ÷ 60) × 80 invoices | 13.33 hours/month |
| Monthly saving | 13.33 × RM13.46 | RM179.42/month |
| Annual saving | RM179.42 × 12 | **RM2,153.04** |

**Step 2 — Calculate Annual Cost**

| Cost Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Dext subscription (approx.) | RM150/month × 12 = RM1,800/year |
| SQL Accounting integration setup (IT, 8 hours) | 8 × RM25/hour = RM200 (one-time) |
| Staff training (2 persons, 3 hours each) | 6 hours × RM13.46 = RM80.76 |
| **Total Annual Cost (Year 1)** | **RM2,080.76** |

**Step 3 — Calculate ROI**

```
ROI = [(RM2,153.04 - RM2,080.76) ÷ RM2,080.76] × 100
ROI = [RM72.28 ÷ RM2,080.76] × 100
ROI = 3.5% (Year 1)
```

> **Year 1 Note:** The ROI appears modest because setup and training are one-time costs included in Year 1. From Year 2 onward, annual cost drops to RM1,800 (subscription only), giving:

```
Year 2+ ROI = [(RM2,153.04 - RM1,800) ÷ RM1,800] × 100 = 19.6%
```

**Additionally**, the time freed (13.33 hours/month) can be redeployed to revenue-generating activities — this qualitative benefit should be noted even if not directly quantified.

### 4.4 Presenting ROI to Management — The Business Case Structure

When presenting AI automation findings to management, use this 5-section structure:

**Section 1: Executive Summary (1 paragraph)**
State the recommendation clearly: "We recommend implementing [Tool X] to automate [Process Y]. Based on our analysis, this will save [N hours/month] of staff time worth [RM amount/year], with a payback period of [N months]."

**Section 2: Current State Problem**
Show the flowchart of the existing process, the time-cost baseline, and the pain points (errors, delays, staff frustration).

**Section 3: Proposed Solution**
Explain the tool selected, why it was chosen over alternatives (reference the CITED framework from KP-02), and how it integrates with existing systems.

**Section 4: Financial Case**
Present the ROI table: Annual Benefit, Annual Cost, ROI %, and Payback Period. Keep this to one table — do not bury management in formulas.

```
Payback Period (months) = Annual Cost ÷ Monthly Benefit
Example: RM2,080.76 ÷ RM179.42 = 11.6 months
```

**Section 5: Implementation Plan**
The 90-day phased roadmap from Section 1 of this KP, with a request for management approval and budget sign-off.

## 5. Risiko dan Langkah Mitigasi / Risks and Mitigation

Every AI automation project carries risks. A professional presentation must acknowledge risks and propose mitigations:

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI tool subscription price increases | Medium | Low | Evaluate alternatives annually; negotiate annual contracts |
| Key staff member leaves — only person who knows the automation | High | High | Document all workflows; cross-train at least 2 staff on each automation |
| Integration breaks after software update | Medium | Medium | Set up monitoring alerts; subscribe to vendor release notes |
| Staff bypass the automation (old habits) | High | Medium | Measure compliance; address in management 1:1s; celebrate compliant teams |
| Data privacy incident — wrong data sent to AI | Low | High | Access controls; PDPA-compliant data handling policy; staff training |

## Soalan Ulangkaji / Review Questions

1. Describe the **three phases** of a typical AI implementation roadmap. What is the primary objective of each phase, and what is the risk of skipping Phase 1?
2. Using Kotter's 8-Step Change Model, explain how a Malaysian SME should handle an employee who is afraid of losing their job to AI. Which two steps of the model are most relevant to this situation?
3. A Malaysian logistics company automates its daily shipment status report. Before automation, an admin officer (salary RM2,500/month) spent 2 hours daily compiling the report (22 working days per month). After automation, this takes 10 minutes. The automation tool costs RM120/month, and setup took 12 hours of the IT manager's time (salary RM4,500/month). Calculate: (a) Annual labour benefit, (b) Year 1 total cost, (c) Year 1 ROI.
4. Why should an AI automation business case presented to management include a **risk register**, and what are the two highest-impact risks to include?
5. A company has completed an AI automation project. After 6 months, management asks: "Is this working?" Name **three** metrics the admin manager should present, and explain how each is measured.
