## 1. The True Cost of Meetings

### 1.1 Why Meeting Cost Matters

Meetings are often treated as free — time set aside without attaching a financial value to the hours spent. In reality, every meeting consumes paid work hours from every attendee. For an organisation to run efficiently, meetings must justify their cost.

**Meeting cost formula:**

> **Meeting Cost (RM) = Number of Attendees × Average Hourly Rate (RM) × Duration (hours)**

where **Average Hourly Rate** is calculated as:

> **Hourly Rate = Monthly Salary ÷ Total Working Hours per Month**

**Total Working Hours per Month** for a standard Malaysian 5-day working week (Employment Act 1955 — 8 hours/day, up to 48 hours/week):
- Approximately 22 working days × 8 hours = **176 hours/month**

> **NOTA:** Malaysia's Employment Act 1955 sets maximum working hours at 48 hours per week. The standard in most Malaysian offices is 8 hours per day, 5 days a week, yielding approximately 176 working hours per month. This figure is used as the denominator in hourly rate calculations.

### 1.2 Meeting Cost Calculation — Worked Example

A weekly operations meeting at a Malaysian SME:
- 6 attendees
- Average monthly salary: RM 3,500
- Meeting duration: 1 hour
- Frequency: weekly (approximately 4 per month)

**Step 1: Calculate hourly rate**
RM 3,500 ÷ 176 hours = **RM 19.89/hour** (approximately RM 20/hour)

**Step 2: Calculate cost per meeting**
6 attendees × RM 20/hour × 1 hour = **RM 120 per meeting**

**Step 3: Calculate monthly cost**
RM 120 × 4 meetings = **RM 480 per month**

**Step 4: Calculate annual cost**
RM 480 × 12 months = **RM 5,760 per year**

This single weekly meeting costs the company approximately **RM 5,760 per year** in staff time alone — before accounting for any lost productivity from context-switching after each meeting.

### 1.3 Interpreting Meeting Cost

| Annual Meeting Cost | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Under RM 2,000 | Low cost — meeting likely justified if it solves coordination problems |
| RM 2,000 – RM 6,000 | Moderate cost — periodically evaluate whether the meeting format is efficient |
| RM 6,000 – RM 15,000 | High cost — strong justification needed; consider reducing frequency or attendees |
| Over RM 15,000 | Very high cost — meeting should be redesigned or replaced with async updates |

## 2. Meeting ROI Threshold

### 2.1 What Is Meeting ROI?

**Meeting Return on Investment (ROI) (Pulangan Atas Pelaburan Mesyuarat)** compares the value the meeting produces against its cost. A meeting has positive ROI when the outcome it achieves is worth more than the time it costs.

**Meeting ROI formula:**

> **Meeting ROI (%) = [(Value of Outcome – Meeting Cost) ÷ Meeting Cost] × 100**

**Value of outcome** is estimated by asking: "What decision, alignment, or output did this meeting produce, and what would it cost to achieve that outcome another way?"

### 2.2 Qualitative ROI Assessment Framework

When the value of a meeting outcome is hard to quantify, use the following five-question framework to evaluate whether a recurring meeting should continue, change format, or be cancelled:

| Question | Positive Answer | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Does the meeting produce a decision or action? | Yes — decisions are made or tasks assigned | No — meeting is just information sharing |
| 2. Could the same outcome be achieved by email or async update? | No — live discussion is genuinely needed | Yes — meeting could be replaced |
| 3. Does every attendee contribute? | Yes — all attendees speak and have a role | No — some attendees are there only to listen |
| 4. Is the meeting consistently ending on time? | Yes | No — agenda is unclear or overlong |
| 5. Do action items have named owners and due dates? | Yes | No — meeting produces vague to-dos |

A meeting scoring "Concern" on 3 or more questions should be redesigned or cancelled.

### 2.3 Meeting Format Alternatives

Not every coordination need requires a meeting. Consider these alternatives:

| Traditional Meeting | AI-Powered Alternative | Time Saved |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly status update (1 hour) | Async Loom video + shared Notion status board | ~45–55 minutes |
| Daily stand-up (30 min) | Automated Slack/Teams daily question bot (async) | ~20–25 minutes |
| Briefing of one-way information | Recorded video + email summary | ~Full meeting duration |
| Document review meeting | Shared doc with AI-suggested comments; Slack thread | ~60–90 minutes |

## 3. Time Blocking (Pengehadan Masa)

### 3.1 What Is Time Blocking?

**Time blocking (pengehadan masa / blok masa)** is a time management strategy in which a person divides their workday into dedicated blocks, each assigned to a specific type of work. Rather than working from a to-do list and switching between tasks reactively, the time blocker plans all work in advance and protects each block from interruptions.

Time blocking is distinct from simply scheduling meetings — it schedules all types of work, including:
- Deep work (Kerja Mendalam) — focused cognitive tasks requiring concentration
- Administrative tasks (Kerja Pentadbiran) — email, filing, form processing
- Meetings and calls
- Personal recovery time (breaks, prayer time)

### 3.2 Time Blocking Categories for Administrative Staff

| Block Type | Duration | Content | Do Not Schedule During |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deep Work Block** | 90–120 minutes | Report writing, financial reconciliation, data analysis, content creation | Meetings, phone calls, email checking |
| **Admin Block** | 30–60 minutes | Email replies, filing, form completion, WhatsApp messages | — |
| **Meeting Block** | As scheduled | Meetings only | Overlap with deep work blocks |
| **Buffer Block** | 15–30 minutes | Catching up on urgent items, transitioning between tasks | — |
| **Personal Block** | As needed | Lunch, prayer (Zohor / Asar), breaks | All work activities |

### 3.3 Sample Time-Blocked Week for a Malaysian Administrative Professional

The following is a sample week assuming Monday–Friday working hours (8:00 AM – 5:00 PM), including Zohor prayer (approximately 1:15 PM) and Asar prayer (approximately 4:15 PM):

| Time | Monday | Tuesday | Wednesday | Thursday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:00–9:00 | Admin Block (email + WhatsApp) | Admin Block | Admin Block | Admin Block | Admin Block |
| 9:00–11:00 | **Deep Work Block** | **Deep Work Block** | **Deep Work Block** | **Deep Work Block** | **Deep Work Block** |
| 11:00–11:30 | Buffer | Buffer | Team Stand-up (30 min) | Buffer | Buffer |
| 11:30–1:00 | Meeting Block | Meeting Block | Meeting Block | Meeting Block | Jumaat Prayer (male staff) / Meeting Block (others) |
| 1:00–2:00 | Lunch + Zohor | Lunch + Zohor | Lunch + Zohor | Lunch + Zohor | Lunch + Zohor |
| 2:00–4:00 | **Deep Work Block** | Admin Block | **Deep Work Block** | Admin Block | **Deep Work Block** |
| 4:00–4:30 | Buffer + Asar | Buffer + Asar | Buffer + Asar | Buffer + Asar | Buffer + Asar |
| 4:30–5:00 | Week review / planning | Admin Block | Admin Block | Admin Block | Week planning for next week |

> **NOTA:** Prayer times in Malaysia vary by state, season, and latitude. The times above are indicative for the Klang Valley region. Staff should configure their actual prayer times using e-Solat (the official JAKIM e-Solat app) and block these precisely in their AI scheduling tool.

### 3.4 Implementing Time Blocking with AI Tools

| Tool | Time Blocking Feature |
|---|---|

## 4. Deep Work Principles

### 4.1 What Is Deep Work?

**Deep work (kerja mendalam)** is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task, producing high-quality output in less time than would be possible in a distracted state. The concept, developed by productivity researcher Cal Newport, has significant practical application for office administrators managing complex tasks such as financial reporting, compliance documentation, and data analysis.

**Shallow work (kerja cetek)**, by contrast, includes tasks that are easy to replicate and can be done while distracted — such as forwarding emails, attending status-update meetings, or filing standard forms.

### 4.2 Why Deep Work Matters in an AI-Assisted Office

AI tools are most effective at handling shallow work — repetitive, well-defined tasks. Human administrative professionals add the most value in deep work domains: interpreting data, drafting nuanced communications, exercising judgment in unusual situations, and managing relationships.

Protecting deep work time is therefore not just a productivity strategy — it is a **career strategy** in an AI-augmented workplace. The human's comparative advantage lies in the work that AI cannot replicate reliably.

### 4.3 Strategies for Protecting Deep Work Time

| Strategy | How to Apply in a Malaysian Office |
|---|---|
| **Monk mode scheduling** | Block the first 90 minutes of every working day for deep work before checking email or WhatsApp |
| **Meeting-free mornings** | Communicate to colleagues that mornings are for focused work; use Calendly availability settings to only allow meetings after 11 AM |
| **Notification batching** | Turn off all desktop and phone notifications during deep work blocks; check messages only at defined times (9 AM, 1 PM, 4 PM) |
| **Physical signal** | Use a visible do-not-disturb signal in open-plan offices (headphones, status light) |
| **Weekly planning session** | Every Friday afternoon, plan the following week's deep work blocks before anything else enters the calendar |

## 5. AI-Assisted Meeting Minutes and Action Item Tracking

### 5.1 The Meeting Documentation Problem

Malaysian offices frequently conduct meetings but do not consistently document outcomes. The consequences include:
- Decisions made but not recalled accurately by all parties
- Action items assigned verbally but not followed up
- The same issues raised repeatedly in consecutive meetings

AI tools address this by automating the capture and distribution of meeting outcomes.

### 5.2 AI Meeting Summary Tools

| Tool | Platform | How It Works | Key Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft Copilot for Teams** | Microsoft Teams | Transcribes the meeting in real time; generates a summary, key decisions, and action items list post-meeting | Summary email + action item list |
| **Google Meet AI Notes** | Google Workspace | Captures meeting transcript; AI extracts action items with owners | Shared Google Doc with meeting summary |
| **Otter.ai** | Web / mobile | Joins calls as a bot or transcribes audio; identifies speakers; flags action items | Searchable transcript + summary |
| **Fireflies.ai** | Web | Joins any video call; generates transcript, action items, and sentiment analysis | Shared meeting intelligence report |

### 5.3 Meeting Minutes Template (AI-Assisted Output Format)

A properly structured set of minutes produced with AI assistance should contain:

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Meeting name** | E.g., "Weekly Operations Review — 4 August 2026" |
| **Date, time, location** | 4 August 2026, 10:00 AM, Meeting Room B / Google Meet |
| **Attendees** | Names and departments |
| **Apologies** | Names of invited but absent parties |
| **Agenda items** | Numbered list of topics discussed |
| **Key decisions** | Clear, specific decisions made during the meeting |
| **Action items** | Task | Owner | Due Date |
| **Next meeting** | Date and agenda preview |

### 5.4 Action Item Tracking Post-Meeting

After AI generates the meeting summary, the action items should be transferred to the project management tool (Notion, Asana, Monday.com, or similar) to ensure:
- Each action item becomes a trackable task with a named owner and due date
- The owner receives an automated reminder as the due date approaches
- Progress on action items is reviewed at the start of the next meeting

**Workflow:**
1. Meeting ends → AI generates summary and action items list
2. Admin reviews AI output for accuracy within 30 minutes
3. Admin enters action items into the project management tool
4. System sends automated task notifications to owners
5. At the next meeting, the first agenda item is "Review open action items from last meeting"

1. A Malaysian company holds a weekly management meeting with 8 attendees. Each attendee earns an average of RM 5,000 per month. The meeting runs for 90 minutes. Calculate: (a) the hourly rate per person, (b) the cost per meeting, (c) the annual cost of this meeting.
