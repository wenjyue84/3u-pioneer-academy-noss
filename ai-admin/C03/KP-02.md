## 1. Employee Onboarding — Overview (Gambaran Keseluruhan Onboarding)

### 1.1 What is Employee Onboarding?

**Employee onboarding (onboarding pekerja / orientasi pekerja)** is the structured process of integrating a new hire into the organisation. It begins from the moment an offer is accepted and extends through the first weeks or months of employment. Effective onboarding has three goals:

1. **Administrative completion:** All statutory and contractual documents are collected, signed, and filed.
2. **Legal compliance:** The employee is registered with EPF, SOCSO, and EIS before their first day or within the statutory timeline.
3. **Workplace integration:** The employee understands their role, team, and workplace culture.

For Malaysian SMEs, the administrative component of onboarding is the most time-critical — failure to register a new employee with PERKESO (SOCSO/EIS) before their start date exposes the employer to legal risk.

### 1.2 Onboarding Document Checklist

The following documents are required for a complete employee onboarding in a Malaysian SME:

| # | Document | Purpose | Who Provides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Offer Letter (Surat Tawaran) | Formally offers employment terms | Employer |
| 2 | Employment Contract (Kontrak Pekerjaan) | Binds employer and employee to agreed terms | Employer |
| 3 | IC Copy (Salinan Kad Pengenalan) | Identity verification; statutory registration | Employee |
| 4 | Bank Account Details | Salary payment setup | Employee |
| 5 | EPF Form KWSP 3 | Employee EPF registration (if new member) | Employer submits |
| 6 | SOCSO Form 2 (Borang PERKESO 2) | SOCSO registration for new employee | Employer submits |
| 7 | Personal Particulars Form | Internal HR record (address, emergency contact, NOK) | Employee |
| 8 | Tax Form CP22 | Notification of new employee to LHDN | Employer submits within 30 days |
| 9 | Passport-size photograph | ID badge, HR file | Employee |
| 10 | Academic certificates (certified copy) | Credential verification | Employee |

> **IMPORTANT — LHDN CP22:** The employer must notify LHDN of every new employee by submitting Form CP22 within **30 days** of the employee commencing work. Failure to notify is an offence under the Income Tax Act 1967.

## 2. Automating Onboarding Documents with AI (Automasi Dokumen Onboarding dengan AI)

### 2.1 AI-Generated Offer Letters

**Tool:** ChatGPT, Claude, or Microsoft Word Copilot

An offer letter generated by AI must be reviewed and customised before sending. A reliable prompt structure:

```
Draft a professional job offer letter for the following hire:
- Company name: [Company Name] (registered under SSM)
- Company address: [Address]
- Candidate name: [Full name as per IC]
- Position: [Job title]
- Department: [Department]
- Start date: [DD/MM/YYYY]
- Monthly salary: RM[amount]
- Probation period: [e.g., 3 months]
- Working hours: [e.g., Monday–Friday, 9am–6pm]
- Annual leave entitlement: [e.g., 14 days] (consistent with Employment Act 1955)
- Location of work: [Office address]
- Offer expiry: [Date by which candidate must accept]

Language: Professional Bahasa Malaysia with English technical terms where standard.
Format: Formal business letter. Include signature blocks for HR Manager and Candidate.
Do NOT include any clause that contradicts the Employment Act 1955.
```

**Post-generation review checklist:**
- [ ] Verify leave entitlement matches Employment Act 1955 minimums (see Section 3)
- [ ] Confirm termination notice periods comply with the Act
- [ ] Remove any AI-added clauses on intellectual property ownership unless legally reviewed
- [ ] Ensure the salary stated matches the agreed amount (AI may add or alter figures)
- [ ] Have the final letter reviewed by the HR manager or company director before sending

### 2.2 AI-Generated Employment Contracts

For more complex employment contracts, AI can generate a first draft but **legal review is strongly recommended** before execution. The contract should include:

- Parties to the contract (employer and employee full legal names, registration numbers)
- Position, duties, and place of work
- Salary and payment date
- Working hours, rest days, and public holidays
- Leave entitlements
- Probation period and confirmation procedure
- Termination and resignation notice periods
- Confidentiality clause
- PDPA consent clause (employee's personal data processed by employer)

> **AI LIMITATION WARNING:** Never instruct AI to generate clauses on non-compete agreements, restraint of trade, or intellectual property ownership without legal counsel review. These clauses have specific enforceability requirements under Malaysian contract law.

### 2.3 Automating the Onboarding Checklist

A simple onboarding automation workflow using Google Forms + Google Sheets:

| Step | Tool | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google Form | New employee completes digital onboarding form (personal details, bank info, emergency contact) |
| 2 | Google Sheets | Form responses auto-populate a master HR register |
| 3 | Google Apps Script / n8n | Auto-generate welcome email and document request list |
| 4 | Google Drive | Auto-create a personal folder for each employee; route documents to it |
| 5 | Kakitangan.com | Admin creates employee profile and imports data |

AI (via ChatGPT) can generate the Google Apps Script code for Steps 3–4 when given the correct prompt, enabling administrators without coding experience to implement basic automation.

## 3. Leave Entitlement under Employment Act 1955 (Hak Cuti di bawah Akta Kerja 1955)

### 3.1 Annual Leave (Cuti Tahunan)

Under the **Employment Act 1955**, the minimum annual leave entitlement is:

| Length of Service | Minimum Annual Leave (Days) |
|---|---|
| Less than 2 years | 8 days |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 12 days |
| 5 years or more | 16 days |

> These are **statutory minimums**. Employers may offer more generous leave in the employment contract, but may not offer less.

### 3.2 Sick Leave and Hospitalisation Leave (Cuti Sakit)

| Length of Service | Sick Leave (without hospitalisation) | Hospitalisation Leave |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 2 years | 14 days | 60 days |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 18 days | 60 days |
| 5 years or more | 22 days | 60 days |

Hospitalisation leave is inclusive of sick leave. If an employee is hospitalised, the total sick leave and hospitalisation leave combined may not exceed 60 days.

### 3.3 Maternity Leave (Cuti Bersalin)

Following the Employment (Amendment) Act 2022 (effective 1 January 2023):
- Minimum maternity leave: **98 consecutive days** (increased from 60 days)
- Applies for each confinement (there is no longer a cap at the fifth confinement)

> **Employer obligation:** Maternity allowance (gaji semasa cuti bersalin) is paid by the employer, not by any government scheme, unless the employer is registered under SOCSO's Employment Insurance System for maternity benefits.

### 3.4 Paternity Leave (Cuti Bapa)

Under the Employment (Amendment) Act 2022:
- **7 consecutive days** paid paternity leave per confinement
- Applicable to **married** male employees
- Previously not provided under the Act

### 3.5 Public Holidays (Cuti Umum)

Employees are entitled to the following paid public holidays per year:

| Category | Days |
|---|---|
| National mandatory public holidays (Section 60D, Employment Act) | 11 gazetted days |
| State public holidays and local public holidays | Varies by state |

If work is required on a public holiday, the employee must be paid at a rate of **not less than triple the ordinary rate of pay** (triple pay) for the hours worked.

### 3.6 Overtime (Kerja Lebih Masa)

| Work Period | Overtime Rate (Minimum) |
|---|---|
| Ordinary day (weekday) | 1.5× hourly rate |
| Rest day | 2× daily rate for first half-day; full day rate for full day |
| Public holiday | 3× hourly rate |

Overtime hours must not exceed **104 hours per month** (as specified in regulations).

## 4. Kakitangan.com — Malaysian HR Platform (Platform HR Malaysia)

### 4.1 About Kakitangan.com

**Kakitangan.com** is a Malaysian-built cloud-based HR and payroll platform widely used by SMEs in Malaysia. It offers:

- Employee profile management
- Leave application and approval workflow
- Attendance tracking (clock-in/clock-out via mobile app)
- Payroll calculation with EPF, SOCSO, EIS, and PCB (Monthly Tax Deduction) calculations
- Payslip generation and distribution
- e-Leave calendar and team absence dashboard
- Integration with bank portals for salary disbursement

For Malaysian SMEs with 5–200 employees, Kakitangan.com provides an affordable, localised alternative to enterprise HR platforms.

### 4.2 Setting Up an Employee in Kakitangan.com

| Step | Action | Information Required |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create Employee Profile | Full name (as per IC), IC number, date of birth, gender |
| 2 | Employment Details | Job title, department, start date, employment type, salary |
| 3 | Bank Details | Bank name, account number (for salary payment) |
| 4 | Statutory Details | EPF number (if existing member), SOCSO number |
| 5 | Leave Policy | Assign appropriate leave entitlement group |
| 6 | Attendance Group | Assign to a work schedule (hours, rest days) |

### 4.3 Leave Approval Workflow in Kakitangan.com

1. Employee submits leave application via Kakitangan.com app (mobile or web)
2. Direct supervisor receives notification and reviews application
3. Supervisor approves or rejects; employee receives notification
4. Leave balance is automatically deducted from the employee's entitlement
5. Team leave calendar updates in real-time; HR admin has full visibility

### 4.4 Attendance Tracking Features

- **Geofencing:** Employees can only clock in/out when they are within a defined GPS radius of the office location
- **Facial recognition clock-in:** Optional; requires employee consent under PDPA 2010 (biometric data is sensitive personal data)
- **Attendance reports:** Auto-generated monthly attendance summary for payroll processing

> **PDPA consideration:** Facial recognition data and GPS location data constitute **sensitive personal data** under PDPA 2010. Employees must be informed and must consent to the collection of this data before such features are activated.

## 5. PDPA 2010 and Employee Personal Data (Data Peribadi Pekerja)

### 5.1 Employer as Data Processor

When an employer collects and stores employee personal data, the employer is a **data processor (pemproses data)** subject to PDPA 2010 (Act 709) obligations. Employee data includes:

- IC number, passport number
- Bank account details
- Medical records
- Salary and financial information
- Biometric data (fingerprint, facial recognition)
- Home address and emergency contact details

### 5.2 PDPA Obligations for Employee Data

| PDPA Principle | Application to Employee Data |
|---|---|
| **Consent (Persetujuan)** | Obtain signed consent in the employment contract or a separate notice for biometric data collection |
| **Notice (Notis)** | Inform employees in writing what personal data is collected and why |
| **Security (Keselamatan)** | Store HR records in password-protected systems; restrict access to HR personnel only |
| **Retention (Pengekalan)** | Employment records should be retained for at least 6 years after employment ends (for tax and legal purposes); do not retain longer than necessary |
| **Access (Akses)** | Employees have the right to request access to their own personal data held by the employer |

### 5.3 Data Retention for Former Employees

| Record Type | Recommended Retention Period | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll records | 7 years | Income Tax Act 1967 requirement |
| EPF / SOCSO records | 7 years | Statutory obligation |
| Employment contracts | Duration of employment + 6 years | Potential legal disputes |
| Medical records | Duration of employment + 6 years | Occupational Safety and Health Act |
| Resumes (unsuccessful) | 6 months maximum (unless talent pool consent given) | PDPA minimisation principle |
