## 1. The Recruitment Cycle and the Role of AI (Kitaran Pengambilan Pekerja dan Peranan AI)

### 1.1 Overview of the Recruitment Cycle

Recruitment (pengambilan pekerja) is the process by which an organisation identifies, attracts, evaluates, and selects candidates to fill vacant positions. A complete recruitment cycle typically involves:

| Stage | Activity | Traditional Approach | AI-Enhanced Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Job Analysis** | Define duties, skills, qualifications | HR officer drafts manually | AI generates draft from job title + department context |
| **2. Job Advertisement** | Publish vacancy | Manual copywriting | AI generates optimised copy in seconds |
| **3. Application Collection** | Receive and organise resumes | Email inbox; manual filing | ATS (Applicant Tracking System) auto-sorts |
| **4. Resume Screening** | Shortlist qualified candidates | Read each resume manually | AI scores and ranks against criteria |
| **5. Interview Preparation** | Design questions | Manually crafted | AI generates competency-based question banks |
| **6. Selection & Offer** | Choose and notify candidate | Manual decision | AI-assisted scoring summary supports decision |

For Malaysian SMEs that lack a dedicated HR department, AI tools dramatically compress the time from vacancy identification to shortlisting, allowing office administrators to manage recruitment as part of a broader administrative role.

### 1.2 Why AI in Recruitment Matters for Malaysian SMEs

Malaysia's workforce landscape presents specific challenges:

- **High volume, low differentiation:** Popular positions (admin clerk, sales executive) on Jobstreet may receive 200–500 applications within 48 hours.
- **Multilingual requirements:** Many SMEs require candidates proficient in BM, English, and Mandarin — making keyword screening complex.
- **Time cost:** Manual screening of 100 resumes at 3 minutes each = 5 hours per vacancy, before any interviewing.

AI tools address these challenges by performing first-pass screening at scale, flagging only the candidates who meet minimum criteria for human review.

## 2. Writing Job Advertisements with AI (Penulisan Iklan Jawatan dengan AI)

### 2.1 What Makes a Good Job Advertisement?

An effective job advertisement must:
- Clearly state the job title, location, and employment type (full-time / part-time / contract)
- Summarise key responsibilities (not an exhaustive list)
- State minimum qualifications (education, experience, skills)
- Include an attractive value proposition (company culture, benefits, growth opportunities)
- End with a clear call-to-action (how and where to apply)

Job advertisements that are vague ("looking for a hardworking individual") generate poor-quality applications and waste screening time.

### 2.2 Using AI to Draft Job Advertisements

**Tool:** ChatGPT, Claude, or Microsoft Copilot (via Word or Teams)

**Prompt structure for job ad generation:**

```
Write a professional job advertisement for the following position:
- Job title: [e.g., HR & Admin Executive]
- Company type: [e.g., Malaysian SME in logistics]
- Location: [e.g., Shah Alam, Selangor]
- Key responsibilities: [list 4–5 main duties]
- Requirements: [education level, years of experience, skills required]
- Working hours: [e.g., Monday–Friday, 9am–6pm]
- Salary range: [e.g., RM2,500–RM3,500]
- Language requirements: [e.g., fluent in BM and English; Mandarin an advantage]
- How to apply: [email / portal]
Tone: Professional but approachable. Length: 250–350 words.
```

**Key AI editing principles:**
- Always review AI output for factual accuracy before publishing.
- Remove or verify any salary benchmarks or benefit claims the AI adds that were not in your prompt.
- Ensure the requirements stated are legally compliant — avoid criteria that could constitute discrimination under Malaysian employment law (e.g., do not specify gender or ethnicity as a requirement unless it is a genuine occupational requirement).

### 2.3 Posting on Malaysian Job Portals

| Portal | Primary Audience | Employer Account | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jobstreet** (by SEEK) | General; all industries | Free listing + paid promotion | Volume applications; clerical, technical |
| **Hiredly** | Young professionals; tech-forward roles | Free + premium plans | Knowledge workers, fresh graduates |
| **LinkedIn Malaysia** | Professional, managerial, tech | Free post + LinkedIn Recruiter | Mid-senior roles; professional network |
| **MyFutureJobs** (PERKESO) | Malaysian citizens; social protection registrants | Free — government portal | Entry-level; priority for local hires |
| **Maukerja** | Blue-collar and semi-skilled | Free listing | Factory, warehouse, service roles |

> **Best Practice:** Post the same vacancy across at least two portals to maximise candidate pool. Hiredly's AI shortlisting feature automatically ranks applicants by skills match score.

### 2.4 LinkedIn AI Features for Recruitment

LinkedIn's Recruiter Lite platform (available on paid subscription) includes:
- **AI-generated job descriptions** based on similar roles in the same industry
- **Candidate match scores** that rank active candidates against job requirements
- **Smart reply suggestions** for InMail outreach to passive candidates

For Malaysian SMEs without a recruiter subscription, the free LinkedIn job post still benefits from LinkedIn's internal recommendation algorithm, which surfaces postings to members whose profiles match the job keywords.

## 3. Resume Screening with AI (Penapisan Resume dengan AI)

### 3.1 What is an Applicant Tracking System (ATS)?

An **Applicant Tracking System (ATS / Sistem Penjejakan Pemohon)** is software that collects, organises, and filters job applications. Enterprise ATS platforms include Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, and Taleo. For Malaysian SMEs, lightweight alternatives include:

| Tool | Tier | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Hiredly** | Portal with built-in ATS | Auto-ranking by skills match; BM/EN support |
| **Freshteam** (Freshworks) | Freemium SaaS | Resume parsing; interview scheduling |
| **Zoho Recruit** | Freemium SaaS | Pipeline management; email integration |
| **Google Workspace + Sheets** | Free / manual | Ad-hoc screening log for micro-SMEs |

### 3.2 AI Resume Screening Workflow

When no ATS is available, the following workflow applies AI for resume screening:

**Step 1 — Define the Scoring Rubric**

Before screening, create a scoring rubric with weighted criteria. Example for an Admin Executive role:

| Criterion | Weight | Scoring Description |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum education (SPM / Diploma / Degree) | 20% | 20 = meets or exceeds; 10 = one level below; 0 = does not meet |
| Relevant work experience (years) | 25% | 25 = 3+ years; 15 = 1–2 years; 5 = <1 year |
| Required software skills (MS Office, HR system) | 20% | 20 = all listed; 10 = partial; 0 = none |
| Language proficiency (BM + English) | 15% | 15 = both confirmed; 8 = one only; 0 = unclear |
| Industry background | 10% | 10 = same industry; 5 = adjacent; 0 = unrelated |
| Presentation quality of resume | 10% | 10 = professional, structured; 5 = adequate; 0 = poor |
| **TOTAL** | **100%** | |

**Step 2 — AI-Assisted Extraction**

Paste each resume text into ChatGPT with the prompt:

```
Read the following resume and extract this information in a table:
- Highest education level and field
- Years of total work experience
- Software skills listed
- Languages mentioned
- Industry/sector of past employers
- Overall resume presentation quality (Professional / Adequate / Poor)
Resume text: [paste resume]
```

**Step 3 — Score and Rank**

Apply the rubric to the AI-extracted information. The final score determines shortlisting priority:
- **80–100%:** Shortlist for interview
- **60–79%:** Reserve list (contact if shortlist pool is small)
- **Below 60%:** Not shortlisted

**Step 4 — Human Review**

Always conduct a final human review of AI-shortlisted candidates before issuing interview invitations. Do not rely solely on AI scores for the hiring decision.

### 3.3 Bias Awareness in AI Screening (Kesedaran Berat Sebelah dalam AI)

AI recruitment tools can replicate or amplify biases present in their training data. In the Malaysian context, administrators must be aware of:

| Bias Type | Description | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Name/ethnicity bias** | AI or ATS may inadvertently score resumes differently based on candidate names | Remove candidate names from screening rubric; evaluate on criteria only |
| **Gender bias** | If past hires were predominantly one gender, AI patterns may favour that gender | Ensure screening criteria are gender-neutral |
| **Language bias** | English-only prompts may disadvantage qualified candidates who wrote their resume in BM | Accept BM resumes; translate before AI extraction if needed |
| **Recency bias** | AI may over-weight recent employers even if total experience is equivalent | Explicitly define "years of experience" inclusively in the rubric |

> **IMPORTANT:** Malaysian employment law under the **Employment Act 1955** does not prohibit employers from setting genuine occupational requirements, but all selection criteria must be job-relevant and consistently applied.

## 4. Structured Interview Question Generation (Penjanaan Soalan Temuduga Berstruktur)

### 4.1 Types of Interview Questions

| Question Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Behavioural (STAR method)** | Past behaviour predicts future performance | "Tell me about a time you had to handle a difficult employee complaint." |
| **Situational** | Hypothetical scenario to assess judgment | "If you discovered an error in the monthly payroll after submission, what would you do?" |
| **Competency-based** | Assess specific skills | "Walk me through how you would set up an employee leave tracking system from scratch." |
| **Technical knowledge** | Verify subject knowledge | "What documents are required when a new employee is registered for EPF and SOCSO?" |

**STAR Method (Kaedah STAR):** A structured framework for answering or evaluating behavioural questions:
- **S** — Situation: What was the context?
- **T** — Task: What was the candidate's responsibility?
- **A** — Action: What specific actions did they take?
- **R** — Result: What was the outcome?

### 4.2 Using AI to Generate Interview Questions

**Prompt:**

```
I am interviewing a candidate for an HR & Admin Executive position at a Malaysian SME. 
Generate a structured interview question bank with:
- 4 behavioural questions (STAR method) focused on: organisation, problem-solving under pressure, 
  attention to detail, and handling confidential information
- 3 situational questions related to payroll discrepancies, difficult employees, and deadline management
- 3 technical questions on HR software, Employment Act 1955 leave entitlement, and document filing
Format as a numbered list with each question on a new line.
```

### 4.3 Interview Question Bank Review Checklist

Before using AI-generated interview questions, verify:
- [ ] No question asks about age, marital status, religion, ethnicity, or pregnancy — these may be discriminatory
- [ ] Questions are clearly tied to actual job requirements
- [ ] All interviewers use the same question set for all candidates (consistency)
- [ ] Scoring criteria for each question are defined before the interview begins

## 5. Employment Act 1955 — Recruitment Compliance (Pematuhan Pengambilan Pekerja)

### 5.1 Scope of the Employment Act 1955

The **Employment Act 1955 (Akta Kerja 1955)** governs the minimum terms and conditions of employment for employees in Malaysia. Following amendments effective **1 January 2023**, the Act's protections now extend to **all employees** regardless of monthly wage (the previous RM2,000 threshold has been removed for most provisions).

Key provisions relevant to the recruitment stage:

| Provision | Description |
|---|---|
| **Written contracts** | Employees must receive a written contract or terms of employment |
| **Probation period** | No statutory maximum; must be stated in the contract |
| **Non-discrimination** | All selection criteria must be applied consistently |
| **Child labour** | Minimum working age is 15 years; employment of children under 18 in hazardous work is prohibited |

### 5.2 Fixed-Term Contracts vs. Permanent Employment

Malaysian SMEs commonly engage staff on fixed-term contracts. Key distinctions:

| Feature | Fixed-Term Contract | Permanent Employment |
|---|---|---|
| Contract end | Specified date | No end date (until resignation or termination) |
| Renewal | May be renewed; repeated renewals may imply permanency | N/A |
| Termination notice | Per contract | Per Employment Act: 4 weeks for <2 years; 6 weeks for 2–5 years; 8 weeks for >5 years |
| Statutory benefits | Same as permanent (EPF, SOCSO, EIS, leave) | Same |

## 6. PDPA 2010 in Recruitment (Perlindungan Data Peribadi dalam Pengambilan Pekerja)

When collecting candidate personal data (IC number, address, phone number, educational certificates), employers act as **data processors** under the **Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709)**. Obligations include:

- **Consent:** Inform candidates in the application form that their data will be used for recruitment purposes only.
- **Security:** Store resume files in password-protected folders or a secure cloud drive; restrict access to authorised HR personnel.
- **Retention:** Resumes of unsuccessful candidates should not be retained indefinitely. A common practice is to destroy or anonymise data after 6 months, unless the candidate has consented to being kept in a talent pool.
- **Disclosure:** Candidate data must not be shared with third parties (e.g., recruitment agencies) without explicit consent.

> **Penalties for breach:** Under PDPA 2010 (Act 709) as amended by Act A1727 (in force 1 April 2025), conviction for unlawfully disclosing personal data carries a fine of up to **RM1,000,000** and/or imprisonment of up to **3 years**.
