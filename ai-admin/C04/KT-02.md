## Skema Jawapan / Marking Scheme

*(For trainer use only — not to be distributed to trainees)*

**Q1(a):**
- Gross Profit = RM 85,000 − RM 34,000 = RM 51,000 (2 marks)
- GPM = (51,000 ÷ 85,000) × 100 = 60% (2 marks)
- Net Profit = RM 51,000 − RM 28,500 − RM 1,200 = RM 21,300 (2 marks)
- NPM = (21,300 ÷ 85,000) × 100 = 25.06% (accept 25%) (2 marks)
- Revenue Growth Rate = ((85,000 − 72,000) ÷ 72,000) × 100 = 18.06% (accept 18.1%) (1 mark for method, 1 mark for answer = 2 marks but awarded 1 mark here as 9 total for (i)-(v))

*(Adjust: Qi = 2, Qii = 2, Qiii = 2, Qiv = 2, Qv = 1 mark for method + 1 for answer = but total shown as 9; accept reasonable rounding)*

**Q1(b):** Cannot say good or bad without industry context; 12% NPM may be excellent in retail but poor in professional services; should compare against same industry benchmark and own historical trend; management should track trend month-over-month. (3 marks)

**Q1(c):** Accounts Receivable Days (AR Days). 75 days against 30-day credit terms means customers are paying 45 days late on average; this creates cash flow pressure; management should follow up on overdue accounts. (3 marks)

**Q2(a):** Any five of: inconsistent date formats; blank rows within data; amounts stored as text not numbers; merged cells; missing values in key fields; duplicate entries; mixed currency without separation; narrative text in amount columns; SST amounts not separated from net amounts. (1 mark each)

**Q2(b):** Prompt must include: identification of the data source; specific request for revenue, expenses, net profit; instruction to use only uploaded data and not to estimate or invent figures. Award full 5 marks if all three elements present; deduct 1–2 marks for missing elements.

**Q2(c):** Step 1 — manually recheck the sum of the expense column row by row in the original CSV to confirm the manual figure; Step 2 — identify any row that ChatGPT may have included or excluded from its calculation (e.g., a header row mistaken as data, or a blank row); Step 3 — use the verified manual figure in the report and note the discrepancy in an appendix with explanation. (Full 5 marks for all three steps; 3–4 marks for two steps with clear explanation)

**Q3(a):** February — expenses (RM 74,000) exceeded revenue (RM 72,000). Significance: the business made a net loss in February; if this trend continued, cash flow would deteriorate; management should investigate the cause of the expense increase or revenue decrease. (4 marks)

**Q3(b):** The Y-axis starts at RM 60,000, not zero — this truncates the chart and makes small differences appear large; the revenue and expense bars look similar in height but the actual difference could be significant. Manager should check the actual RM difference, not rely on bar height. (4 marks)

**Q3(c):** Net Profit = RM 85,000 − RM 63,700 = RM 21,300; NPM = (21,300 ÷ 85,000) × 100 = 25.06%. (4 marks: 2 for correct Net Profit, 2 for correct NPM)

**Q3(d):** Pie chart — because it shows proportions of a whole (each expense category as a % of total expenses); suitable for up to 5–6 categories; easily interpreted by non-accountants. (3 marks)

**Q4(a):** Any five of the seven standard sections from KP-02: Cover/Header; Revenue Summary; Expense Summary; Profitability (Gross/Net Profit); Cash Position; Key KPIs; Observations. (2 marks each, 10 total)

**Q4(b):** Three-part structure: What happened / Why it happened / What action is needed. Example: "Net profit margin improved to 25% in March (what happened), driven by reduced operating costs following the end of the year-end marketing campaign (why), and management should consider maintaining reduced marketing spend if revenue remains stable (action needed)." (2 marks for structure, 3 marks for valid example using March 2026 data)

**Q5(a):** Award 2 marks per prompt: 1 for the prompt text being specific and actionable, 1 for the expected output being correctly stated. Total 12 marks. Acceptable prompts include those aligned with the five Copilot prompts in KP-02 (revenue/expense total, margin calculation, top expense category, chart, executive summary).

**Q5(b):** AI Usage Disclosure Note must include: which AI tool was used; statement that figures were manually verified; data source identification; date of preparation. (4 marks, 1 each)

**Q5(c):** Classification — Confidential (shared with directors and external party). Precaution 1 — any of: encrypt the file with password before sending; use company email not personal; use SharePoint/Google Drive with access control, not WhatsApp. Precaution 2 — confirm the bank officer's authority to receive the report; record the disclosure in a disclosure log. (4 marks)

**Q6(a):** Discrepancy = RM 280,000 − RM 264,500 = RM 15,500. (2 marks)

**Q6(b):** Any three of: ChatGPT may have included a header row as a data row; ChatGPT may have processed a formula result differently; one month's CSV may have had a data entry error causing a larger total; AI may have summed a wrong column; duplicate rows may have been in the dataset that AI counted twice; data type issues (some amounts as text, not numbers) may have caused different parsing. (2 marks each)

**Q6(c):** Faridah must not use an unverified AI figure — doing so is unprofessional and could misrepresent the company's financial position to management; it may constitute a false representation if the figure is materially incorrect. She should: identify the source of the discrepancy, use the manually verified figure, and note the AI output discrepancy in a footnote. (6 marks)

**Q6(d):** Must anonymise or remove any individual customer identifiers (customer names, invoice amounts linked to identifiable customers) before using as training material. If the report contains personal data (customers who are individuals), she needs to ensure the data is anonymised under the Security and Disclosure principles of PDPA 2010 before sharing more broadly. She should seek manager/data owner approval before sharing financial data for training purposes. (6 marks: 2 for identifying the PDPA issue, 2 for anonymisation action, 2 for approval process)
