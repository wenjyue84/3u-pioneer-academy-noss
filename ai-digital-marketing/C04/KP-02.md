## 1. Role of Audio in Digital Marketing (Peranan Audio dalam Pemasaran Digital)

### 1.1 Why Audio Matters

Audio is the invisible half of video marketing. Viewers consciously notice the visuals, but they *feel* the audio — a fact exploited by every major brand globally. Audio elements shape emotional response, build memory triggers, and communicate brand personality in ways that visuals alone cannot.

Key research findings relevant to Malaysian marketers:

- **85% of Facebook and TikTok videos** are watched without sound when auto-playing in feed — making captions essential — but when users **choose** to watch a video, audio quality is the primary satisfaction driver
- **Poor audio doubles** the likelihood of early video abandonment compared to poor visuals
- **Brand audio identity** (jingle, voiceover style, sonic logo) increases brand recall by up to 96% more than visual-only branding (research by IPSOS 2020)
- In Malaysia, **voice advertisements in Bahasa Malaysia** consistently outperform equivalent visual-only formats in emotional resonance for Malay audience segments

### 1.2 Audio Touchpoints in Digital Marketing

| Audio Element | Where It Appears | Marketing Function |
|---|---|---|
| Voiceover (Suara Latar) | Video ads, explainer videos, TikTok, YouTube | Narrates story; communicates key message; reflects brand personality |
| Background Music (Muzik Latar) | All video content; podcast intros | Sets emotional tone; maintains engagement; pacing |
| Sound Effects (Kesan Bunyi) | Product demos; UI sounds; TikTok content | Emphasises moments; adds production quality |
| Brand Jingle / Sonic Logo | Ad campaigns; video intros/outros | Creates instant brand recognition (e.g., McDonald's "I'm Lovin' It") |
| Podcast / Audio Content | Spotify, Google Podcasts, Apple Podcasts | Long-form brand authority; audience trust-building |

## 2. AI Text-to-Speech Technology (Teknologi Teks-ke-Ucapan AI)

### 2.1 How AI TTS Works

**Traditional TTS** (before AI): Concatenated pre-recorded phoneme segments. Output sounded robotic — mechanical timing, unnatural prosody, unable to convey emotion.

**Neural TTS (current AI standard):** Trained on thousands of hours of human speech recordings. The AI learns not just how phonemes sound but how human speakers naturally:

- Vary pitch (intonation contour)
- Adjust speed across different sentence types
- Emphasise specific words for meaning
- Insert natural pauses and breathing
- Convey emotional states (enthusiasm, calm, urgency)

**Process overview:**

1. Input text is processed by a **text analyser** (handles abbreviations, numbers, punctuation, language)
2. A **prosody model** predicts pitch, duration, and stress patterns
3. A **neural vocoder** (e.g., WaveNet, HiFi-GAN) synthesises the raw audio waveform
4. Output audio is post-processed for naturalness and noise reduction

### 2.2 Quality Comparison: Old TTS vs AI TTS

| Characteristic | Traditional TTS (pre-2020) | AI Neural TTS (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Naturalness | Robotic, flat intonation | Near-human; emotional nuance |
| Multilingual support | Limited; poor accent quality | Broad; Malay and Chinese now well-supported |
| Speed variation | Fixed mechanical pace | Natural variation; adapts to punctuation |
| Emotional range | None | Happy, calm, serious, excited, professional |
| Cloning capability | Not available | Can clone a specific voice from 3–60 seconds of sample audio |
| Use in advertising | Not suitable for customer-facing | Widely used in brand advertising, audiobooks, e-learning |

### 3.3 Language and Voice Selection for Malaysian Market

When selecting or configuring AI TTS for Malaysian audiences:

- **Bahasa Malaysia:** Select "Malay (Malaysia)" not "Indonesian" — different vocabulary, spelling, and pronunciation conventions
- **Mandarin Chinese:** Select "Chinese (Simplified)" with a neutral accent (Putonghua/Mandarin) rather than Cantonese or Taiwanese Mandarin
- **English:** Select a neutral English accent; Australian or British English is often perceived more positively than American English in Malaysian professional contexts
- **Voice gender and tone:** Research indicates that a **calm, warm female voice** performs best for healthcare and beauty products in Malaysian markets; **confident male voice** performs best for automotive and financial services

## 4. Voiceover Script Writing (Penulisan Skrip Suara Latar)

### 4.1 Pacing Rules

The pacing of a voiceover directly affects comprehension and audience engagement:

| Content Type | Recommended Pace | Practical Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| General marketing voiceover | 130–150 words per minute | Comfortable conversational pace |
| Urgent promotional (sale, limited time) | 150–170 words per minute | Slightly faster; creates excitement |
| Educational / tutorial | 110–130 words per minute | Slower; allows information absorption |
| Luxury brand / premium | 100–120 words per minute | Deliberate, confident, unhurried |
| Legal disclaimer / fine print | 200–240 words per minute | Fast, distinct — requires separate record |

**Practical word count targets:**

| Video Duration | Word Count (130 wpm) | Word Count (150 wpm) |
|---|---|---|
| 15 seconds | ~32 words | ~37 words |
| 30 seconds | ~65 words | ~75 words |
| 60 seconds | ~130 words | ~150 words |
| 90 seconds | ~195 words | ~225 words |

### 4.2 Tone Alignment with Brand Personality

| Brand Type | Voice Tone | Word Choice | Delivery Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friendly F&B / Retail | Warm, enthusiastic, conversational | Simple, everyday vocabulary | Upbeat; smiling voice |
| Professional Services | Authoritative, calm, reassuring | Precise, confident terminology | Measured; clear enunciation |
| Luxury / Premium | Elegant, unhurried, aspirational | Sophisticated; evocative language | Slow; deliberate pauses |
| Youth / Lifestyle | Energetic, relatable, colloquial | Casual; mix of EN/BM ("lahhh", "kan") | Fast; dynamic |
| Healthcare / Medical | Empathetic, professional, clear | Simple medical terms with explanation | Calm; reassuring |

### 4.3 Voiceover Script Structure: Hook, Message, CTA

Every effective marketing voiceover follows a three-part structure:

**Part 1 — Hook (Pengait) [First 3–5 seconds]**

The hook must immediately answer the listener's implicit question: "Why should I keep listening?" Effective hooks for Malaysian marketing:

- Problem statement: *"Susah nak cari skincare yang sesuai untuk kulit sensitif?"*
- Surprising statistic: *"Did you know 67% of Malaysian businesses lose customers due to slow website loading?"*
- Bold claim: *"This single change doubled our café's revenue in 30 days."*
- Direct address: *"Kalau you pernah rasa penat dengan rambut yang rosak — dengar ni."*

**Part 2 — Message / Body (Mesej Utama) [60–75% of duration]**

Core content: product/service features stated as **benefits**, not specifications. Use the bridge: *"[Feature] — which means [Benefit for you]."*

Example:
- Weak: *"Our product contains 10% niacinamide."*
- Strong: *"With 10% niacinamide — which means you'll see visibly brighter skin in just 14 days."*

**Part 3 — Call-to-Action (CTA) [Final 10–15% of duration]**

A single, specific, urgent action. Avoid multiple CTAs in one script.

| Effective CTA | Weak CTA |
|---|---|
| "Tap the link in bio to get 20% off — today only." | "Visit our website for more information." |
| "WhatsApp us now at 011-XXXX XXXX — reply 'START' to begin." | "Feel free to contact us." |
| "Click 'Shop Now' — free shipping for orders above RM 50." | "Check out our products." |

### 4.4 Malaysian Accent and Language Considerations

- Avoid scripts that are purely formal Bahasa Malaysia if the brand voice is casual — Malaysian consumers respond well to **colloquial BM mixed with English** (Manglish) for youth and lifestyle brands
- For formal or government-adjacent brands, use **standard Bahasa Malaysia (Bahasa Standard)**
- Chinese-language scripts should be in **Simplified Chinese (Mandarin)** unless the specific audience is predominantly Cantonese-speaking (older Chinese Malaysian segments in certain cities)
- Code-switching (mixing BM and EN) in the same sentence is natural and accepted in Malaysian marketing: *"Product ini dah clinically tested — so you boleh guna dengan yakin."*

## 5. Audio Branding Elements (Elemen Penjenamaan Audio)

### 5.1 The Five Audio Branding Elements

| Element | Malay Term | Description | Duration | Format | Licensing Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Brand Voiceover Style** | Gaya Suara Jenama | Consistent voice persona across all video content | Varies | MP3/WAV | Voiceover talent agreement or AI TTS commercial licence |
| **Sonic Logo / Audio Logo** | Logo Bunyi | 2–5 second audio signature; plays at start/end of all brand videos | 2–5 seconds | WAV | Original composition or licensed |
| **Brand Jingle** | Jingel Jenama | Short, memorable melody with brand name embedded | 15–30 seconds | MP3/WAV | Original composition; full copyright |
| **Background Music** | Muzik Latar | Royalty-free or licensed tracks that reflect brand personality | Video duration | MP3/WAV | Royalty-free licence (see Section 6) |
| **Sound Effects (SFX)** | Kesan Bunyi | Non-musical audio elements: clicks, transitions, emphasis sounds | 0.5–3 seconds | WAV | Royalty-free or original |

### 5.2 Building an Audio Brand Identity

Step-by-step process for establishing audio branding for a Malaysian SME:

1. **Define audio personality:** List 3–5 adjectives describing the brand sound (e.g., "warm, professional, trustworthy, Malaysian")
2. **Select voice persona:** Choose consistent voice gender, age perception, accent, and tone; use AI TTS or hire a voice talent
3. **Choose signature music style:** Select a recurring background music genre/tempo that matches brand personality (see Section 6)
4. **Commission or create sonic logo:** A simple 2–3 note sequence or brand name spoken with a distinctive musical chord
5. **Document in brand guide:** Record all audio specifications in the brand style guide alongside visual guidelines
6. **Apply consistently:** Use the same voice, music genre, and sonic logo across all video content regardless of platform

### 6.2 Malaysian Regulatory Requirements

**Music Authors' Copyright Protection Berhad (MACP)**

MACP is the Malaysian collecting society that administers performing rights for music in Malaysia. It is equivalent to ASCAP (USA) or PRS (UK).

| Scenario | MACP Requirement |
|---|---|
| Playing copyrighted music in a physical business premises (café, retail) | MACP licence required (annual fee based on premises size) |
| Playing copyrighted music in a marketing video published online | If using royalty-free music with correct licence — no MACP licence needed |
| Using a popular commercial song in a marketing video | Requires synchronisation (sync) licence from rights holder + MACP notification |
| Live performance or public broadcast of copyrighted music | MACP licence required |

**Broadcasting Act 1988 (Act 388)**

The Broadcasting Act 1988 governs content broadcast on Malaysian radio and television. For **online digital marketing video content** (social media, website), the act's direct provisions do not apply, but MCMC's content code does. Key principle: content that would be prohibited on broadcast media is also considered inappropriate for online marketing use.

**Practical Rule for Malaysian Marketers:** Use only royalty-free music from reputable platforms (Epidemic Sound, Artlist, YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay) for all marketing videos. Never use commercial songs (pop music, movie soundtracks) in marketing videos without obtaining a sync licence — social media platforms will automatically mute or remove such videos.

### 6.3 Music Genre and Brand Matching

| Brand Type | Recommended Music Genre/Mood | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Café / F&B lifestyle | Acoustic folk, lo-fi hip-hop, bossa nova, light jazz | Heavy metal, electronic EDM, news-style orchestral |
| Luxury property / premium brand | Orchestral strings, piano, ambient cinematic | Upbeat pop, comedy music, novelty sounds |
| Youth / fitness / sports | Upbeat electronic, hip-hop, energetic pop | Slow classical, sad ballads |
| Healthcare / clinic | Soft piano, ambient, light classical | Aggressive EDM, comedy |
| Islamic / halal products | Nasheed-inspired, acoustic without instruments containing prohibited elements | Music with sexual/suggestive lyrics or imagery |
| Technology / B2B | Corporate ambient, light electronic, cinematic tech | Novelty, comedy, or traditional folk |

## 7. Audio Mixing and Post-Production (Campuran Audio dan Pasca-Pengeluaran)

### 7.1 Volume Normalisation

**Loudness normalisation** ensures audio playback at a consistent perceived volume across different devices and platforms.

| Standard | Value | Platform Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **LUFS (Loudness Units Full Scale)** | –23 LUFS (broadcast standard) | European Broadcasting Union (EBU R128) |
| YouTube normalisation target | –14 LUFS | YouTube automatically normalises all uploads |
| Spotify / streaming | –14 LUFS | Applied at playback |
| TikTok / Instagram | –14 to –16 LUFS recommended | Not enforced; but consistent with platform mix |
| Facebook / Meta | –16 LUFS recommended | Not enforced |

**Practical guidance:** Mix voiceover content to peak at –14 LUFS for social media video content. Use tools like Adobe Audition, DaVinci Resolve Fairlight, or free tools like Audacity to measure and adjust LUFS levels.

### 7.2 Music Ducking (Pengurangan Muzik)

**Music ducking (pengurangan muzik)** is the technique of automatically lowering the volume of background music whenever the voiceover or dialogue is present, then returning music to normal volume during pauses and at the end.

**Volume levels:**

| Audio Layer | During Silence | During Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| Background music | 100% (e.g., –18 dB) | 20–30% (duck to –28 to –35 dB) |
| Voiceover | — | 100% (targeting –12 dB peak) |
| Sound effects | 100% when triggered | — |

**Music ducking in editing tools:**

- **CapCut:** Select background music layer → Enable "Smart Auto Ducking" → Adjust sensitivity
- **Adobe Premiere Pro:** Use Essential Sound panel → Clip type: Music → Enable "Auto Ducking"
- **DaVinci Resolve Fairlight:** Use automation lane; manually draw volume curve or use FairlightFX Dialogue Leveler

### 7.3 Export Formats for Audio and Video

| Format | Bitrate / Quality | Use Case | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| MP3 (128 kbps) | Low quality | Preview files; low-bandwidth delivery | Avoid for final marketing output |
| MP3 (320 kbps) | High quality | Background music delivery; audio tracks | Accepted by most social platforms |
| WAV (uncompressed) | Highest quality | Master files; archive; professional delivery | Large file size; use for source masters |
| AAC (256 kbps) | High quality; efficient | Apple ecosystem; podcast delivery | Preferred by Apple devices |
| MP4 (H.264) with AAC audio | Standard video | Social media video export | Universal compatibility |
| MP4 (H.265 / HEVC) with AAC | Smaller file; same quality | Efficient storage; 4K delivery | Less compatible with older devices |
