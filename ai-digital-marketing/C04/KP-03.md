## 1. The Hook-Body-CTA Structure (Struktur Pengait-Badan-Seruan Bertindak)

### 1.1 Why Structure Matters for Short-Form Video

Short-form video marketing lives and dies in the first three seconds. TikTok's internal data indicates that if a viewer does not engage within the first 3 seconds, there is over a 70% probability they will swipe past without watching the full video. A well-structured script addresses this by ensuring every second has a deliberate purpose.

The **Hook-Body-CTA** structure is the universal framework used by professional video scriptwriters and content marketers globally:

| Segment | Duration (60s video) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (Pengait)** | 0–5 seconds | Stop the scroll; create curiosity or emotion; earn the next 55 seconds |
| **Body (Badan)** | 5–50 seconds | Deliver value; build desire; demonstrate the product or idea |
| **CTA (Seruan Bertindak)** | 50–60 seconds | Direct one specific action; create urgency |

### 1.2 The Formula Applied

**Formula:**
```
[Hook: create a pattern interrupt] 
→ [Body: deliver on the hook's promise]
→ [CTA: capture the interest generated]
```

**Example — Halal Skincare Product (60 seconds):**

- **Hook (3s):** *"I had adult acne for 7 years. This cleared it in 14 days."* [Display before/after image]
- **Body (50s):** Show product; explain key ingredient (niacinamide 10%); show application; show day 1 vs day 14 results; include one customer testimonial quote as text overlay
- **CTA (7s):** *"Tap the link in bio. Get 15% off your first order — today only. Free shipping above RM 50."*

## 2. Types of Hooks (Jenis Pengait Perhatian)

Eight proven hook techniques with worked examples for Malaysian marketing contexts:

### 2.1 The Shocking Statistic Hook (Statistik Mengejutkan)

Opens with a surprising or counter-intuitive data point that challenges assumptions.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"67% of Malaysian small businesses have never replied to a single Google review — and it's costing them customers."* | Digital marketing services |
| *"Malaysians spend an average of 5.5 hours per day on social media — but most brands reach less than 2% of their own followers."* | Social media agency |
| *"A slow website loses 40% of visitors before the page even loads. Is yours one of them?"* | Web development |

### 2.2 The Relatable Problem Hook (Masalah Berkaitan)

Opens by naming a pain point the target audience experiences daily, creating immediate emotional recognition.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"Penat habiskan masa buat content tapi follower tak naik-naik?"* | Social media coaching |
| *"Every morning you look in the mirror and see the same dull, uneven skin — no matter what you try."* | Skincare product |
| *"Your café food is amazing. Your photos make it look like hospital food."* | F&B marketing services |

### 2.3 The Surprising Fact Hook (Fakta Mengejutkan)

States a non-obvious truth about the audience's situation or industry that reframes their understanding.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"The best time to post on TikTok is NOT prime time — it's actually 7 AM."* | Social media tips |
| *"Buying followers is not just useless — it actively hurts your reach algorithm."* | Digital marketing education |
| *"Most halal certification rejections in Malaysia are not about the product — they're about paperwork errors."* | Halal consultancy |

### 2.4 The Bold Claim Hook (Dakwaan Berani)

Makes a strong, specific promise of result — then the body of the video proves the claim is credible.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"I generated RM 47,000 in sales in one month with zero paid ads — using only TikTok."* | E-commerce coaching |
| *"This RM 19 tool replaced my RM 3,000/month graphic designer."* | AI design tool |
| *"Our clients double their website traffic in 90 days or we refund every sen."* | SEO agency |

### 2.5 The Direct Address Hook (Ucapan Terus)

Speaks directly and specifically to a narrow audience segment, creating immediate "this is for me" recognition.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"If you own a restaurant in Malaysia and you're not using Google Business Profile — watch this."* | Restaurant marketing |
| *"Attention mothers in Klang Valley looking for a trusted part-time income from home."* | MLM / direct selling |
| *"This video is specifically for Malaysian freelancers earning less than RM 5,000 a month."* | Freelance coaching |

### 2.6 The Visual Contrast Hook (Kontras Visual)

The first frame shows an unexpected visual juxtaposition that creates curiosity before any words are spoken.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| Split screen: cluttered messy desk (left) vs spotless minimalist workspace (right) — text: *"Before vs After: 30-day productivity system"* | Productivity coaching |
| Before/after skin transformation displayed for 2 seconds before any narration | Skincare product |
| Blank white website vs fully designed professional site — text: *"I built this in 4 hours using AI"* | Web design tool |

### 2.7 The Story Opening Hook (Pembukaan Cerita)

Drops the viewer into the middle of a compelling story with narrative tension — compelling them to watch to discover the resolution.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"Three months ago my business was RM 80,000 in debt. Last week we hit our first 6-figure month. Here's exactly what changed."* | Business coaching |
| *"I quit my RM 12,000/month corporate job to sell laksa online. Everyone said I was crazy. This is what happened."* | F&B entrepreneurship |
| *"My first TikTok video got 3 views. My 47th went viral with 2.3 million. The difference was one thing."* | Content creation |

### 2.8 The Question Hook (Soalan Mengundang)

Opens with a direct question that the target audience has been silently asking themselves.

| Example | Context |
|---|---|
| *"What if your social media could sell for you — even while you sleep?"* | Marketing automation |
| *"Korang tahu tak berapa ramai orang cari kedai anda dalam Google setiap hari?"* | Google Ads / SEO |
| *"Are you still doing graphic design the hard way when AI can do it in 10 seconds?"* | AI design tools |

## 3. Body Structure — The PSP Framework (Rangka Kerja PSP)

### 3.1 Problem-Solution-Proof (Masalah-Penyelesaian-Bukti)

The **PSP (Problem-Solution-Proof) framework** is the most effective body structure for short-form marketing video because it mirrors the natural decision-making process: a customer first needs to feel understood (Problem), then presented with an answer (Solution), then convinced the answer works (Proof).

**Structure:**

```
Problem → Agitate → Solution → Proof → Bridge to CTA
```

### 3.2 PSP Applied — Worked Example

**Product:** AI Social Media Management Tool for Malaysian SMEs
**Platform:** TikTok (60 seconds)

| Segment | Content | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | *"Running a business AND managing social media at the same time? You're probably staying up until midnight just to post content."* | 5–8s |
| **Agitate** | *"And the worst part? You put in all that work — and your posts still only reach 200 people."* | 5s |
| **Solution** | *"[Tool name] uses AI to write your captions, design your posts, and schedule them automatically — in under 10 minutes a day."* | 15s |
| **Proof** | Show screen recording of tool; flash customer testimonial: *"I went from 2 hours a day to 10 minutes — and my engagement doubled."* | 20s |
| **Bridge** | *"And it starts at RM 49 a month."* | 3s |
| **CTA** | *"Tap the link below. Start your 14-day free trial — no credit card needed."* | 5–7s |

### 3.3 Proof Types (Jenis Bukti)

Not all proof is equal. Use the highest-credibility proof available:

| Proof Type | Example | Credibility Level |
|---|---|---|
| Client testimonial (on-camera) | Real customer speaking about results | Highest |
| Before/after visual comparison | Product effect shown side by side | Very High |
| Third-party data / research | "According to Google, businesses with 10+ reviews earn 34% more clicks" | High |
| Screen recording of results | Analytics dashboard showing growth | High |
| Expert endorsement | Doctor, industry professional recommends | High (if credible figure) |
| User-generated content (UGC) | Customer's own video about the product | Medium–High (authentic) |
| Counter claim addressed | "You might think this is too good to be true — here's why it's not" | Medium |

## 4. Call-to-Action (CTA) Design (Reka Bentuk Seruan Bertindak)

### 4.1 Principles of an Effective CTA

An effective marketing CTA has three characteristics:

1. **Specific (Spesifik):** Tells the viewer exactly what to do — not "contact us" but "WhatsApp us at 011-XXXX XXXX"
2. **Urgent (Mendesak):** Creates a reason to act now rather than later — "today only," "first 20 customers," "limited stock"
3. **Single action (Satu Tindakan):** Presents only one action — never two CTAs in the same video

### 4.2 Platform-Appropriate CTAs

| Platform | CTA Format | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Text overlay + voiceover | Link in bio or TikTok Shop | "Tap the link in bio — use code TIKTOK20 for 20% off" |
| Instagram Reels | Text overlay + caption + Story link | Swipe up (verified) or link in bio | "Link in bio — or DM us the word 'INFO' for details" |
| YouTube Shorts | Pinned comment + description link | Description link | "Link in description — subscribe for weekly tips" |
| Facebook Reels | Caption link + CTA button | Page CTA button (Book Now, Shop Now) | "Click 'Shop Now' below — free delivery this weekend only" |
| WhatsApp Status | Overlay text | Reply to status or WhatsApp click-to-chat | "Reply 'YES' to this status — I'll send you the price list" |

### 4.3 Urgency Techniques for Malaysian Marketing

| Technique | Example | When to Use |
|---|---|---|
| Time limit | "Offer valid until midnight tonight" | Flash sales; limited promotions |
| Quantity limit | "Only 12 units left at this price" | Product launches; limited stock |
| Social proof urgency | "500 customers this week — join them" | Established product with proven demand |
| Exclusive access | "First 50 registrations only — after that, price doubles" | Coaching programs; events |
| Seasonal relevance | "Hari Raya is 3 weeks away — don't leave it too late" | Festive campaigns |

## 5. Storytelling Techniques for Video Marketing (Teknik Penceritaan untuk Pemasaran Video)

### 5.1 Why Storytelling Works

Stories are processed by the brain differently from factual statements. When humans hear a story, multiple brain regions activate simultaneously — language processing, sensory processing, and the emotional (limbic) system. This neurological response creates memory encoding and emotional connection that plain product information cannot achieve.

For Malaysian marketers, storytelling is doubly effective because Malaysian culture has strong oral storytelling traditions (cerita rakyat, pantun, gotong-royong community narratives) that make audiences receptive to narrative content.

### 5.2 Five Storytelling Techniques

**Technique 1 — Transformation Story (Kisah Transformasi)**

The brand or customer is shown before and after a transformation. Most powerful for wellness, education, financial, and beauty products.

Structure: Struggle → Discovery → Transformation → Result
Example: *"12 months ago Aiman couldn't afford to pay rent. After learning digital marketing, he now earns RM 15,000 a month from home."*

**Technique 2 — Before-After Comparison (Perbandingan Sebelum-Selepas)**

Visual or narrative comparison of a problem state versus a solved state. Most powerful for products with visible, demonstrable results.

Application: Show product effect visually (skin, cleanliness, food quality); use split-screen or time-lapse format.

**Technique 3 — Day-in-the-Life (Sehari dalam Kehidupan)**

Follow a character through their daily routine, naturally integrating the product or service. Creates aspirational identification rather than direct selling.

Example: TikTok video following a Malaysian working mother's morning routine, showing how the product saves time at each step. No hard sell — product is shown as a natural part of the ideal life.

**Technique 4 — Behind-the-Scenes (Di Sebalik Tabir)**

Show the process, craftsmanship, or team behind the product. Builds authenticity and trust — especially powerful in the current era of AI-generated content, where real human effort is valued.

Application: Show kitchen preparation for an F&B brand; show artisans making handcraft; show trainer preparing a class at a gym.

**Technique 5 — Community / Shared Identity (Komuniti / Identiti Bersama)**

Position the brand as part of a community or shared identity the audience belongs to or aspires to. Creates belonging rather than transaction.

Example: *"For all the Malaysian mamak business owners who built something from nothing — this is for you."*

### 5.3 Emotional Triggers in Malaysian Marketing

| Emotion | Trigger | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Pride (Kebanggaan) | Malaysian achievement; local success | "Made in Malaysia"; local entrepreneur success stories |
| Family connection (Ikatan keluarga) | Showing family bonds and togetherness | Festive campaigns; food products; home products |
| Community belonging (Semangat komuniti) | Gotong-royong; shared Malaysian experience | Brands with broad appeal; community causes |
| Aspirational hope (Harapan aspirasi) | Better future; achievement possible | Education; finance; career-focused products |
| FOMO (Takut ketinggalan) | Others already benefiting; limited access | Promotions; trending products; event registrations |
| Nostalgia (Nostalgia) | Childhood memories; "back to basics" | Traditional food brands; heritage products |

## 6. AI-Assisted Script Writing (Penulisan Skrip Dibantu AI)

### 6.1 Prompt Template for AI Script Generation

Use this structured prompt with any AI writing tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) to generate video scripts:

```
Write a [DURATION]-second short-form video script for [PLATFORM].

Product/Service: [PRODUCT NAME AND BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
Target Audience: [DEMOGRAPHICS + PSYCHOGRAPHICS]
Key Message: [ONE SENTENCE — THE MAIN POINT OF THE VIDEO]
Hook Type: [Choose from: shocking statistic / relatable problem / bold claim / question / story opening]
Body Framework: [PSP or specific benefit list]
CTA: [EXACT ACTION + ANY OFFER OR URGENCY]
Tone: [formal/casual/energetic/warm/Manglish/formal BM/English]
Language: [English/Bahasa Malaysia/Mandarin/Mixed EN-BM]

Format the script with:
- Hook (first 3 seconds)
- Body (with timestamps)
- CTA (final 5–7 seconds)
- [On-screen text] suggestions in brackets
- Notes on visuals or B-roll
```

### 6.2 AI Script Revision Checklist

After generating an AI script, review and edit for:

| Check | What to Look For | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | Does it create immediate curiosity or emotion? | Rewrite if the first line could be skipped without loss |
| Word count vs duration | Count words; at 150 wpm, 60s = 150 words max | Cut or trim to match duration |
| Passive vs active voice | "Results can be seen" → "You will see results" | Rewrite all passive voice to active |
| Benefit vs feature | "Contains 10% vitamin C" → "Brightens skin in 14 days" | Convert features to benefits throughout |
| CTA specificity | Is the action crystal clear and single? | Remove any secondary CTAs |
| Cultural appropriateness | Any content potentially offensive to Malaysian audiences? | Cross-check against Section 7 |
| Authenticity | Does it sound like a human or like an AI? | Humanise: add colloquialisms, shorter sentences, natural pauses |

### 6.3 Adapting One Script for Multiple Platforms

One core script can be adapted for different platforms efficiently:

| Platform | Adaptation Required | Key Change |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok (60s) | Full version | Original script; include "link in bio" CTA |
| Instagram Reels (30s) | Short version | Cut body by 50%; keep hook and CTA intact |
| YouTube Shorts (59s) | Near-full version | Add "subscribe" CTA at end |
| Facebook Reels (30s) | Short version | Same as Instagram Reels adaptation |
| WhatsApp Status (30s) | Short version | Visual-heavy; minimal voiceover; text overlay dominant |

## 7. Cultural Sensitivity in Malaysian Video Content

### 7.1 Racial and Religious Considerations

Malaysia's Federal Constitution protects the rights of all ethnic groups, and Article 3 declares Islam as the religion of the Federation. Video content for marketing must be designed to respect these sensitivities while also reaching Malaysia's diverse multicultural audience.

| Area | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Racial representation | Ensure multicultural representation in lifestyle content; avoid stereotyping any ethnic group |
| Religious imagery | Do not use religious symbols (salib, crescent, Om, Ganesha imagery) in commercial contexts without careful cultural consultation |
| Halal and dietary | Never show non-halal food with Malay Muslim subjects; always show appropriate food in appropriate cultural context |
| Modesty | For content featuring Malay Muslim women, respect typical dress norms unless the brand is specifically targeting non-Muslim audiences |
| Festive campaigns | Hari Raya content: crescent, ketupat, baju Melayu; CNY: lion dance, ang pow, lanterns; Deepavali: kolam, oil lamp, traditional attire — never mix symbols from different festivals |

### 7.2 MCMC Content Guidelines

The **Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC / Suruhanjaya Komunikasi dan Multimedia Malaysia)** regulates online content under the **Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (CMA 1998, Act 588)**.

Key provisions relevant to marketing video content:

| Provision | Section | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition of offensive content | Section 233 | Content must not be obscene, indecent, false, menacing, or offensive with intent to annoy |
| Prohibition of false content | Section 233 | Marketing claims must be truthful and substantiated |
| Prohibition of gambling content | Section 263 | Online platforms may not promote gambling services without specific licence |
| Prohibited alcohol advertising to Muslims | — | Alcohol advertising is permitted under strict conditions but must not target Muslim audiences |

### 7.3 Prohibited Content Categories

The following content categories are explicitly prohibited in Malaysian online marketing:

| Category | Legal Basis |
|---|---|
| Content ridiculing Islam or any religion | Sedition Act 1948; CMA 1998 Section 233 |
| Sexually explicit or obscene content | CMA 1998 Section 233; Penal Code |
| Content promoting racial hatred or disharmony | Sedition Act 1948 |
| False product claims (e.g., "cures cancer") | Consumer Protection Act 1999; Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956 |
| Unlicensed financial service promotion | Capital Markets and Services Act 2007 |
| Content targeting children with inappropriate advertising | MCMC content code |

> **Practical Note for Marketers:** Even if content is not explicitly illegal, brands should apply a higher standard: content that offends, excludes, or stereotypes any Malaysian community carries significant reputational risk and can result in social media backlash (cancel culture) that damages brand equity far more than any regulatory fine.
