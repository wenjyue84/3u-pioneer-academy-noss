### Section B

**B1** (8 marks — approximately 2–3 marks per section):

- **Hook (3 seconds)** — purpose: interrupt the scroll; make the viewer curious or emotionally triggered within the first 3 seconds before they can swipe away (2m). Example: "POV: You just discovered the CREAMIEST taro milk tea in Malaysia 🧋 — and it's only RM9.90." (1m)
- **Body (majority of video)** — purpose: deliver the core value proposition, product features, social proof, or demonstration (2m). Example: "Our new Taro Series uses real Taiwanese taro blended with fresh milk — available in 3 levels: OG, Brown Sugar, and Matcha Taro Swirl. Every cup is handmade in under 3 minutes." (1m)
- **CTA (final 5–10 seconds)** — purpose: tell the viewer exactly what to do next; remove ambiguity (2m). Example: "Grab yours today — link in bio, or walk into any of our 12 outlets nationwide. First 100 cups at RM6.90 only. Go now!" (1m) [Note: Accept any reasonable example that fulfils each structural purpose.]

**B2** (8 marks):
Neural TTS explanation (4 marks): Traditional TTS uses concatenative synthesis — splicing pre-recorded phoneme segments together, resulting in robotic, monotone output with unnatural prosody. Neural TTS uses deep learning (transformer models) trained on hours of human speech to predict and generate speech waveforms directly, producing natural prosody, intonation, and emotional nuance that closely resembles a real human voice (4m).
Four configurable parameters (4 marks — 1 each): (1) Voice selection (gender, accent, language — e.g. Malaysian English, Bahasa Malaysia); (2) Speaking speed/pace (words per minute — target 130–150 wpm for marketing); (3) Pitch and tone (higher pitch = energetic/youthful; lower pitch = authoritative/trusted); (4) Emphasis and pause markers (inserting pauses at key moments using SSML tags or tool-specific controls to improve listener retention).

**B3** (8 marks):
MACP licensing (4 marks): MACP (Music Authors' Copyright Protection Berhad) is the collective management organisation for musical composition and lyric rights in Malaysia. Any commercial use of a copyrighted musical work — including use as background music in a TikTok/social media video for a commercial client — requires a licence from MACP and potentially from PPCA/neighbouring rights holders for the sound recording (2m). Without a licence, using the song constitutes copyright infringement under the Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332), exposing the client to civil action and potential criminal penalty (2m).
CMA 1998 considerations (2m): Section 233 of the CMA 1998 prohibits using network facilities to transmit content that infringes third-party rights — a commercial post using an unlicensed song on a social media platform may constitute an offence under this section (2m).
Required steps (2m): Contact MACP (macp.com.my) to apply for a synchronisation licence specifying the song, duration of use, platform(s), and commercial purpose. Alternatively, replace the song with royalty-free music from TikTok's commercial sound library, Epidemic Sound, or an AI-generated music tool licensed for commercial use — and document the licence in the project file.

**B4** (8 marks — approximately 2–3 marks per tool):

| Tool | Primary Function | Key Marketing Feature | Limitation |
|------|------------------|-----------------------|------------|
| CapCut AI | Mobile and desktop video editing with AI features | AI auto-captions with speaker detection; TikTok template library; AI background removal for product demos | Limited professional colour grading; auto-captions may error on Malay/Chinese accent English |
| Runway Gen-2 | AI video generation and transformation (text-to-video, image-to-video) | Generate B-roll footage from text prompts; apply AI visual effects and style transfer to existing clips | Expensive credit system for long outputs; generated video quality inconsistent at 1080p |
| HeyGen | AI avatar video generation using a digital human presenter | Create a multilingual presenter video (EN/BM/ZH) from a script without filming a real person | Avatar lip-sync occasionally misaligns; platform requires disclosure that video is AI-generated |

**B5** (8 marks — 1 mark per hook type with example):
1. **Question hook** — "Do you know why your competitor's TikTok gets 10× more views?"
2. **Statistic hook** — "73% of Malaysians decide where to eat based on TikTok videos."

### Section C

**C1** (12 marks):

a) Five pre-production steps (5 marks — 1 each):
1. Define the campaign brief: confirm objective (awareness, conversion, or engagement), target audience (18–28 Malaysian youth), key message, and tone of voice.
2. Conduct competitor content research: analyse 5–10 top-performing taro/milk tea TikTok videos to identify hook formats, visual styles, and music trends.
3. Write and finalise the video script in Hook-Body-CTA structure — get client approval before any production begins.
4. Plan the shot list: decide between real filming, AI-generated footage, or hybrid; identify props, location (outlet), talent, and styling requirements.
5. Select and clear music: choose a track from TikTok's commercial sound library or a royalty-free source — confirm commercial licence before production begins.

b) Full script (7 marks):

**HOOK (3 seconds):** "Wait — this is what RM9.90 looks like? [extreme close-up of purple taro swirl pouring into clear cup] I think I'm obsessed."

**BODY (40 seconds):** "Okay so [Brand Name] just dropped their new Taro Series and honestly I wasn't ready. There's THREE flavours — OG Taro, Brown Sugar Taro, and Matcha Taro Swirl. They use real Taiwanese taro — you can actually taste it, it's not that fake purple powder stuff. I got the Brown Sugar one and the first sip gave me actual butterflies. It's creamy, it's thick, and the brown sugar pearls are perfectly chewy. They have 12 outlets across Malaysia — Klang Valley, Johor, Penang — so no excuses. Best part? They're doing an opening week promo — first 100 cups a day at RM6.90."

**CTA (10 seconds):** "Go to the nearest outlet NOW or tap the link in bio to find your nearest store. Tell them [social media handle] sent you. Don't wait — this promo ends Sunday!"

(Marks: hook captures attention with a visual reveal + question (2m); body includes product USP, social proof, availability, and pricing (3m); CTA includes clear action, urgency, and trackability (2m).)

**C2** (14 marks):

a) Four quality criteria (8 marks — 2 marks each):
1. **Naturalness and prosody** — does the voice sound human? Check by listening for robotic monotone, unnatural pauses, or mispronounced Bahasa Malaysia words. Test by playing the audio to a native BM speaker and asking for a naturalness rating. ElevenLabs/Murf: re-generate with emphasis markers if prosody is flat.
2. **Pacing (words per minute)** — count the words in the script and measure the voiceover duration; calculate WPM. Target 130–150 WPM for a clear, authoritative BM marketing voice; faster reads as rushed and loses comprehension; slower reads as unenthusiastic.
3. **Audio technical quality** — check for background noise, clipping (distortion at peaks), or inconsistent volume levels. Use a free tool (Audacity or Adobe Podcast) to view the waveform; peaks should not exceed 0 dBFS and average LUFS should match the platform target (-14 LUFS for YouTube, -16 LUFS for Facebook).
4. **Brand tone alignment** — does the voice match the client's brief? A Raya promotion should sound warm, celebratory, and familial — not cold and corporate. Cross-reference the voice sample against the tone-of-voice descriptor in the campaign brief before submission.

b) Three adaptations for Mandarin (Malaysian Chinese audience) (6 marks — 2 marks each):
1. **Localise vocabulary for Malaysian Chinese speech patterns** — direct Mandarin translation from BM often produces Standard Mandarin (Putonghua) that sounds foreign to Malaysian Chinese audiences who speak Cantonese-influenced Mandarin with local loanwords. Adapt the script to use familiar Malaysian Mandarin expressions (e.g., "促销" for promo is fine, but colloquial terms like "好康" for good deal are more resonant than formal equivalents).
2. **Select a Malaysian Chinese voice (not Taiwan/PRC accent)** — AI TTS tools like ElevenLabs and Murf offer multiple Mandarin voice variants. The Malaysian Chinese community is accustomed to a slightly Cantonese-inflected Mandarin accent; a pure Beijing Putonghua accent can sound distant or formal in a mass-market F&B ad. Select a voice that sounds locally familiar or use HeyGen's avatar with Malaysian Chinese presenter option.
3. **Adapt cultural references and celebration framing** — a Raya promotion script emphasises family gathering, festive meals, and togetherness. When adapting for Chinese Malaysian audiences (particularly if the content will run year-round or during CNY), replace Raya-specific imagery references in the script (ketupat, baju kurung) with universally Malaysian or Chinese-resonant cues (reunion dinner, mid-year sale). If the Raya framing is intentional (multicultural campaign), keep it but ensure the CTA references a shared benefit (great food, family time) rather than solely Hari Raya cultural specifics.

**C3** (14 marks):

a) Two violations + legislation (4 marks — 2 marks each):
1. **Music copyright infringement** — using a copyrighted Malay pop song without a synchronisation or public performance licence violates the Copyright Act 1987 (Act 332), Sections 13 and 36 (infringement of copyright in a musical work). The rights holder is entitled to seek an injunction and damages. (2m)
2. **Inappropriate content — community guidelines breach** — publishing a presenter in a bikini on Instagram Reels for a commercial skincare client violates Instagram's Community Standards on nudity and sexual content for commercial accounts. Under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, Section 211/233, transmitting indecent content over a communications network is also an offence — applicable when the content is published on a Malaysian commercial account targeting Malaysian audiences. (2m)

b) Immediate corrective actions (4 marks):
1. Remove the video from Instagram immediately — do not wait for Instagram to forcibly remove it, as repeated violations affect account standing.
2. Respond to the music rights holder's takedown notice: acknowledge receipt, confirm removal, and do not contest the notice if the infringement is clear. Avoid escalating to a formal counter-claim without legal advice.
3. Notify the client in writing the same day of both violations — explain what happened, what has been removed, and the corrective plan.
4. Re-edit the video: replace background music with a royalty-free or TikTok/Instagram-licensed track; re-shoot or re-edit the presenter footage to comply with Instagram's dress code standards for commercial content (covered shoulders and chest at minimum for a skincare promotion targeting a general Malaysian audience). Re-submit to client for approval before republishing.

c) Content compliance checklist — minimum six items (6 marks — 1 mark each):
1. **Music licence** — confirm all background music is from a royalty-free library, the platform's commercial sound library, or has a valid MACP synchronisation licence. Document the licence source in the project file.
2. **Talent consent** — obtain signed written consent from all individuals appearing in the video, covering: the platforms it will be published on, duration of use, and commercial purpose.
3. **Presenter/on-screen standards** — ensure on-screen presenters meet the client's modesty and brand standards and comply with platform community guidelines on dress and behaviour.
4. **Prohibited content check** — review against CMA 1998 Section 211 prohibited content list: no content that is obscene, indecent, false, menacing, or offensive on the basis of religion or ethnicity.
5. **Product claims verification** — confirm all verbal or on-screen product claims (e.g., "clinically proven", "whitening", "slimming") comply with the Medicines Advertisement Board (MAB) guidelines and Trade Descriptions Act 2011; avoid prohibited therapeutic claims for cosmetic products.
6. **Platform specifications** — verify aspect ratio, video duration, file size, and caption/hashtag compliance with the specific platform's current technical requirements before upload.
7. **AI-generated content disclosure** — if voiceover or presenter is AI-generated, check whether the platform requires disclosure (e.g., TikTok's AI-generated content label policy).
8. **Client final approval** — obtain written client sign-off on the final video before publishing. Keep a dated record of the approval email or message.

*Jawab SEMUA soalan. Setiap soalan bernilai 1 markah. / Answer ALL questions. Each question carries 1 mark.*

**Q21.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client asks you to generate a 30-second AI voiceover in Bahasa Malaysia for a Raya campaign using ElevenLabs. The generated voiceover mispronounces "ketupat" and sounds robotic on certain phrases. Describe the steps to improve the output before delivering it to the client. [CoCU C04.2.4 / C04.2.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Do not submit the voiceover without correction. First, identify all mispronounced or robotic-sounding words and timestamp them. For ElevenLabs: use the editor's pronunciation dictionary or phoneme override feature to correct "ketupat" (ke-TU-pat) using IPA notation or respelling. Break the script into shorter segments and regenerate problematic phrases separately — then splice them into the main audio using a free audio editor (Audacity or Adobe Podcast). Adjust the stability and clarity sliders in ElevenLabs: lower stability increases natural variation but may reduce consistency; increase clarity for cleaner enunciation of BM words. Check final output pacing against target 130–150 WPM by timing the playback. Play the corrected voiceover to a native Bahasa Malaysia speaker for a final naturalness check before client delivery.

**Q22.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee produces a 60-second TikTok video for a Malaysian food brand but the video receives only 200 views and a 15% watch-through rate. Analyse TWO (2) possible reasons related to the video's opening and suggest improvements. [CoCU C04.1.1 / C04.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Reason 1 — Weak hook: the video opens with a 5-second branded logo animation before any product or compelling visual appears. TikTok's algorithm distributes video based on early engagement signals — if viewers swipe away in the first 1–3 seconds, the algorithm stops distributing the video. Improvement: replace the logo animation with an immediate close-up product shot or a spoken hook line ("This is why everyone in KL is queuing for this") — reserve the logo for the end card. Reason 2 — No caption hook: the first line of the TikTok caption is generic ("New product launch!") rather than a curiosity-driving question or bold claim. TikTok displays the first 100 characters of the caption before the "more" truncation — this is valuable hook real estate. Improvement: rewrite the opening caption to "The bubble tea that's ACTUALLY worth the hype 👇 — here's why" to compel both the algorithm and the viewer to stay and engage.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client wants to use a popular K-Pop song as the background music for their Instagram Reel. They argue that "everyone uses K-Pop songs on Instagram and nothing happens." Advise the client professionally. [CoCU C04.2.6]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Acknowledge the client's observation but clarify the distinction: personal accounts using popular music are generally tolerated by platforms because of Meta's blanket licensing deals with major labels for personal (non-commercial) use. However, a business/brand account using the same song in a commercial promotional video is an explicitly commercial use — this falls outside the blanket personal-use licences. The K-Pop label's rights holders routinely monitor commercial accounts and submit takedown notices, particularly for content that drives commercial revenue. The potential consequences: the video is muted or removed by Instagram; the account receives a copyright strike that restricts future posting; the label can seek damages. Recommend alternatives: (1) use a TikTok/Instagram Commercial Music Library track that is already cleared for business accounts; (2) commission an AI music tool (Suno, Udio) to generate a track in a K-Pop style — no licence required; (3) purchase a commercial sync licence directly from the K-Pop label if the client insists on the specific song. Document the advice given to the client in writing.

**Q24.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee produces a video using HeyGen's AI avatar presenter and publishes it on the client's Facebook without disclosing that it is AI-generated. A follower comments "Is this a real person?" Describe the professional and platform-compliance response. [CoCU C04.2.4 / C04.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Respond to the comment publicly and transparently: "Great question! This video features an AI-generated presenter created using HeyGen AI. All information and claims in the video are real and verified by [brand name]." Transparency builds rather than undermines trust when handled promptly and openly. Review Meta's AI-generated content disclosure policy: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) requires disclosure of AI-generated content — particularly for realistic-looking AI avatars — using the "Made with AI" label available in the posting interface. Edit the post to add the label retroactively if it was not applied initially. Review the brand's content policy: advise the client to establish a standing rule that all AI-generated presenter videos are labelled "AI Presenter" or "AI-Assisted" in either the caption or as an on-screen overlay. Under Malaysia's MCMC Content Code and the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998, content that creates a false impression of identity may constitute misleading content — proactive disclosure is the legally prudent approach.

**Q25.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's YouTube video about their new supplement product claims it can "cure diabetes and reduce blood sugar by 40% in 7 days." Before publishing, describe the content compliance issue and the corrective action required. [CoCU C04.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* This content constitutes an illegal therapeutic claim. Under Malaysia's Control of Drugs and Cosmetics Regulations 1984 and the Medicines Advertisement Board (MAB) Guidelines, supplements are registered as food products — they are not permitted to make disease-cure or disease-prevention claims. "Cures diabetes" and "reduces blood sugar" are explicit therapeutic claims reserved for registered pharmaceutical drugs approved by the National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency (NPRA). Publishing this video exposes the client to enforcement action by the Ministry of Health, product recall, and fines up to RM3,000 under the Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956, and potentially up to RM50,000 under the Trade Descriptions Act 2011. Corrective action before publishing: remove all therapeutic claims entirely. Replace with permissible general wellness language: "formulated to support an active lifestyle" or "contains ingredients traditionally used in wellness practices" — without specifying any disease outcome. Recommend the client obtain a MAB-cleared advertising script review before any supplement video campaign is published. Document the corrective advice in writing.

### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

*Soalan senario lanjutan untuk penilaian kefahaman mendalam tahap 3. Jawapan contoh menunjukkan kedalaman teknikal yang diperlukan. / Extended scenario questions for in-depth Level 3 competency assessment. Sample answers demonstrate required technical depth.*

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the Hook-Body-CTA framework in detail. Why does a weak hook cause a video to fail regardless of content quality in the body? [CoCU C04.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The Hook-Body-CTA framework is the dominant short-form video script structure because it mirrors the psychology of attention in a high-competition, infinite-scroll environment. Hook (first 1–3 seconds): the sole purpose is retention — capturing enough curiosity, emotion, or pattern interrupt to stop the involuntary swipe reflex. The hook makes a promise to the viewer: "if you keep watching, you will get something valuable, entertaining, or surprising." Body (the middle): delivers on the hook's promise by providing the substance — product demonstration, storytelling, social proof, transformation, or education. The body builds desire and trust. CTA (final 5–10 seconds): converts passive interest into active action — follows up while emotional engagement is at its peak. Why a weak hook causes total failure: TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts all use real-time engagement signals (watch time, completion rate, shares) to determine distribution. The algorithm measures what percentage of viewers watched past the 3-second, 10-second, and 50% marks. A video with a weak hook that loses 80% of viewers in the first 2 seconds will receive a negative distribution signal — the algorithm will stop showing it to new audiences within the first 30–60 minutes of posting, before the body content has been seen by almost anyone. This means a technically excellent, deeply persuasive video body is simply never watched. In short: the hook is not the beginning of the video — it is the algorithm's gate that determines whether the video exists for practical purposes.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the complete workflow for producing a 60-second promotional video for a Malaysian SME using a hybrid approach: real footage + AI-generated B-roll + AI voiceover. [CoCU C04.1.2 / C04.2.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Step 1 — Script: write a Hook-Body-CTA script. Identify which moments require authentic real footage (hero product shots, real customer reactions, brand environment) and which can be supplemented with AI B-roll (abstract lifestyle shots, mood sequences, transition visuals). Step 2 — Real footage shoot: film the hook segment with real product (close-up pour shot, texture reveal) and any brand-specific footage that AI cannot authentically replicate. Use a smartphone on a stabiliser at 9:16 in good natural light. Step 3 — AI B-roll generation: use Runway Gen-2 or Pika Labs to generate supplementary visuals from text prompts: "upscale café interior, warm morning light, Malaysian customers enjoying coffee, no text" — generate 5–6 clips, select 2–3 that fit the script's body section. Step 4 — AI voiceover: write the full script narration. Generate in ElevenLabs using a warm, confident Malaysian English voice at 140 WPM. Correct any mispronunciations. Export as WAV. Step 5 — Edit in CapCut AI: import real footage and AI B-roll. Lay the voiceover audio track. Sync footage cuts to the voiceover rhythm. Add AI auto-captions (edit for accuracy). Apply brand colour filter/LUT. Add background music from TikTok Commercial Music Library at -12 LUFS (under voiceover level). Add text overlays for key messages and CTA. Step 6 — Export: 1080×1920 px, MP4, H.264, 9:16, under 500MB. Step 7 — Client review and publish after written approval.

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what LUFS means and why consistent audio loudness matters across different platforms and devices for a Malaysian digital marketing agency producing video content for multiple clients. [CoCU C04.2.6]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* LUFS stands for Loudness Units relative to Full Scale — it is the international standard (ITU-R BS.1770) for measuring the perceived loudness of audio over time, taking into account how human hearing weights different frequencies. Unlike peak dBFS (which only measures the highest amplitude spike), LUFS measures the integrated average loudness of a piece of audio as experienced by a listener. Why it matters for a marketing agency: different streaming and social platforms normalise all uploaded audio to their own target LUFS levels — YouTube normalises to approximately -14 LUFS, Facebook/Instagram to -16 LUFS, TikTok to approximately -14 to -16 LUFS. If a client's video voiceover is mastered at -6 LUFS (very loud), the platform will automatically turn it down to match their target — but this can cause the audio to sound compressed and distorted, or the relative balance between voiceover and music may shift. If mastered at -24 LUFS (very quiet), the platform normalisation brings it up, which amplifies background noise and reveals audio quality issues. For a Malaysian agency handling multiple clients across TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram simultaneously, standardising all audio masters to -14 to -16 LUFS as a delivery specification ensures: (1) consistent voiceover clarity across all devices (mobile phone speaker, earphones, laptop); (2) no jarring loudness jump when a brand's video auto-plays after a louder competitor video; (3) professional broadcast-quality deliverables that signal production competence to clients.

**Q29.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client in the supplement industry asks you to produce a YouTube video featuring an AI-generated doctor avatar who recommends their product. Describe all compliance and ethical issues with this request. [CoCU C04.2.4 / C04.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* This request raises multiple serious compliance and ethical concerns requiring full disclosure to the client before any production begins. Issue 1 — Impersonation of a medical professional: using an AI avatar designed to look like a doctor — even a fictional one — to endorse a supplement product creates a false impression of medical endorsement. Under the Malaysian Medical Act 1971, only registered medical practitioners may give medical advice. A fictional AI doctor providing health recommendations constitutes misleading medical advice. Issue 2 — Therapeutic claims: if the AI doctor avatar states or implies that the supplement treats, prevents, or cures any condition, this violates the Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956 and Medicines Advertisement Board (MAB) guidelines — as analysed in Q25 above. Issue 3 — AI disclosure obligation: Meta, YouTube, and TikTok all require disclosure of AI-generated content; using a realistic AI avatar without disclosure violates platform terms of service and, increasingly, local regulatory expectations under the MCMC framework. Issue 4 — Consumer deception: presenting an AI avatar as a credible medical authority is a form of deceptive marketing practice potentially actionable under the Consumer Protection Act 1999 and the Trade Descriptions Act 2011. Recommended action: decline to produce the video as described. Propose an alternative: a real, verified medical professional giving a recorded testimonial within MAB guidelines; or an AI-assisted animated explainer video that clearly presents as a graphic (not a person) with full AI disclosure and no therapeutic claims.

**Q30.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe how the PSP (Problem-Solution-Proof) framework differs from Hook-Body-CTA and give an example of when each is more appropriate for a Malaysian brand's video marketing. [CoCU C04.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Hook-Body-CTA is an attention-retention-conversion framework optimised for the scrolling behaviour of short-form platforms (TikTok, Reels). The Hook's job is purely to retain — it may be a visual surprise, a question, a bold claim, or a pattern interrupt that has nothing directly to do with a problem. It is effective for brand awareness, product launches, and trend-riding content where emotional engagement is the primary goal. The PSP (Problem-Solution-Proof) framework is a persuasion framework rooted in copywriting logic: (1) Problem — empathetically name a pain point the target audience recognises as their own; (2) Solution — position the product as the direct remedy to that specific problem; (3) Proof — validate the claim with evidence (testimonial, before-and-after, data, or expert endorsement). PSP is better for conversion-oriented content (sales videos, landing page videos, product demo ads) because it builds a logical case rather than relying solely on emotional spike. When to use each for a Malaysian brand: **Hook-Body-CTA** — a bubble tea brand launching a new seasonal flavour on TikTok targeting Gen Z; the goal is virality and trial, not rational persuasion. **PSP** — a Malaysian weight management supplement brand producing a 90-second Facebook ad targeting women aged 30–45; the goal is to convert a sceptical audience by acknowledging their frustration (belly fat after pregnancy), presenting the product as a targeted solution, and providing a before-and-after testimonial as proof. In practice, the two frameworks often combine: the Hook stops the scroll (Hook-Body-CTA's hook), then the body follows PSP logic, and the CTA closes.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what "audio branding" means and describe how a Malaysian SME café could develop a consistent audio identity across its TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube content without a large budget. [CoCU C04.2.6]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Audio branding is the deliberate, consistent use of sound elements to make a brand recognisable across all media touchpoints — the audio equivalent of a visual logo. Major elements of audio branding include: brand voice (the characteristic voice tone of the narrator/presenter), brand music (signature background track or jingle style), sonic logo (a short distinctive sound or musical motif played consistently, like Intel's five-note "bong"), and sound effects (consistent use of specific sounds in transitions or CTAs). For a Malaysian SME café with a limited budget: Step 1 — Define the audio personality: warm, cosy, artisanal, and Malaysian — not corporate, not loud. Step 2 — Select a signature background music style: use AI music tools (Suno or Udio) to generate a 60-second looping café-ambient jazz track with a light Malaysian musical influence (gentle gamelan or kompang notes layered under jazz piano) — free to generate, commercially licensable. Save this as the "brand music template" used consistently across all videos. Step 3 — Define the brand voice: the founder records a natural warm voiceover for all content (not AI, to maintain authenticity) at a consistent pacing of 140 WPM; use ElevenLabs to generate a backup digital clone of the founder's voice for content produced without a recording session. Step 4 — Create a sonic logo: use AI audio tools to generate a 2-second distinctive sound cue (e.g., coffee cup clink + two soft piano notes) — use this consistently as the last element before every video's end card. Step 5 — Maintain consistency: create a brief internal "audio brand guide" (one page) specifying the music track file name, voice settings, and sonic logo file — share with anyone producing content for the brand.

**Q32.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee's 60-second Instagram Reel has a 90% completion rate but zero conversions to the website link in bio. Diagnose the likely cause and recommend a fix. [CoCU C04.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A 90% completion rate indicates the video is excellent at retention — the hook and body are working well. Zero conversions despite high watch-through strongly suggests a CTA failure rather than a content failure. Possible diagnoses: (1) No CTA in the video: the video ends without explicitly telling the viewer what to do next — viewers enjoyed the content but had no prompt to act, so they moved on to the next video in the feed. (2) Weak CTA language: the CTA exists but uses passive language ("you can check our website if you want") rather than urgent, specific, action-driving language ("Tap the link in bio RIGHT NOW — 20% off ends tonight at midnight"). (3) Friction in the conversion path: the CTA says "link in bio" but the Instagram bio link either goes to the homepage (not the specific product/offer) or the link is not updated — viewers who do click face a mismatch and bounce immediately. (4) No urgency or reason to act now: the CTA is evergreen with no time-limited offer, so viewers feel they can return later — and do not. Recommended fixes: (1) Rewrite the final 5 seconds of the video with a specific, urgent, single-action CTA: "Tap the link in bio to get your 20% discount — this offer ends Sunday at midnight." Add an on-screen text overlay reinforcing the action. (2) Set up a Linktree or direct product link in bio that goes to exactly the product or offer page referenced in the video. (3) Add the CTA as the first line of the caption (not buried after the hashtags). (4) Add a time-limited offer to create urgency. Test the corrected CTA version against the original using Instagram's A/B content test feature.

**Q33.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe how a digital marketer should evaluate and select a voice for an AI TTS voiceover for a Malaysian brand targeting three different audience segments: young adults (18–25), parents (30–45), and senior citizens (55+). [CoCU C04.2.4 / C04.2.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Voice selection for AI TTS must match both the demographic of the listener and the emotional tone of the message — mismatched voice selection reduces engagement and can undermine brand credibility. Young adults (18–25): select a young, energetic, upbeat voice — slightly faster pace (150 WPM acceptable), conversational tone, with natural rising intonation that mirrors how Malaysian youth actually speak. Avoid overly formal pronunciation; a slight Malaysian English accent is relatable for this segment. ElevenLabs offers voice samples tagged "young" and "energetic" — audition at least 5 voices before selecting. Script language: use contemporary Malaysian English with light Malay/Chinese code-switching where appropriate ("this one memang best la"). Parents (30–45): select a confident, warm, trustworthy voice — medium pace (135–145 WPM), neutral Malaysian English accent, clear enunciation for complex product information (ingredients, health claims). Avoid voices that sound either too young (undermines credibility) or too old (creates distance). This segment responds to tone that feels like peer advice rather than a TV advertisement. Senior citizens (55+): select a slower, clearly articulated voice (120–130 WPM) with warm, respectful tone. Avoid jargon, slang, or rapid speech patterns. Bahasa Malaysia or Mandarin voiceovers may be more appropriate than English for this segment depending on the brand. Test on a small group of actual senior users — AI TTS mispronunciation of Malaysian words is more disorienting for older audiences who may already find digital content unfamiliar. For all segments: generate 3–5 voice samples with the actual script text (not generic demos), play them to a small focus group from the target demographic, and select based on audience feedback rather than the marketer's personal preference.

**Q34.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what "prohibited content" means under Section 211 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 and give THREE (3) specific examples of content types a digital marketer must never produce for a Malaysian client's social media channels. [CoCU C04.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Section 211 of the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (Act 588) prohibits any person from using network facilities or network services to provide content that is "obscene, indecent, false, menacing, or offensive in character with intent to annoy, abuse, threaten, or harass any person." Section 233 further prohibits the transmission of such content. The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission (MCMC) enforces these provisions — penalties include a fine up to RM50,000 and/or imprisonment up to one year for Section 211 offences, and up to RM50,000 fine for each day of a continuing Section 233 offence. Three specific examples a digital marketer must never produce: (1) **Content inciting racial or religious hatred** — for example, a social media video that portrays any Malaysian ethnic or religious community (Malay, Chinese, Indian, Orang Asal, Muslim, Christian, Hindu, Buddhist) in a degrading, mocking, or threatening manner. Malaysia's diversity makes this a particularly sensitive and legally dangerous area; Section 211 is regularly enforced against such content. (2) **Defamatory content against identifiable individuals or businesses** — a video falsely claiming a competitor's food product contains harmful ingredients or that a business owner committed fraud. Even if framed as satire, false statements of fact that damage a person's reputation constitute defamation under Malaysian common law AND may trigger CMA Section 211 enforcement. (3) **Indecent or sexually explicit content** — promotional videos featuring nudity, sexually suggestive imagery, or explicit content, regardless of the artistic intent. Malaysian regulators apply a conservative community standard — content that is widely accepted on Western platforms may still violate Section 211 in the Malaysian context.

**Q35.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A Malaysian digital marketing agency produces video content for clients across five industries: F&B, supplement, education, property, and fashion. Describe an industry-specific content compliance risk for EACH industry that the agency must manage before publishing any video. [CoCU C04.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Five industry-specific compliance risks: (1) **F&B** — Halal certification misrepresentation. A video showing a product with a halal logo or halal claim that does not match the product's current JAKIM halal certification status constitutes an offence under the Trade Descriptions (Definition of Halal) Order 2011. Risk: if the halal cert has lapsed or the product is under a different version, the video creates a false halal claim. Action: verify the current JAKIM halal certificate number and expiry date before including any halal claim or logo in a video. (2) **Supplement** — Therapeutic claims. As discussed throughout this assessment, supplement video content must not make disease-treatment or health-cure claims. Risk: even subtly worded claims ("helps manage your sugar levels" in the context of diabetes) may be interpreted as therapeutic claims by the Medicines Advertisement Board. Action: have all supplement video scripts reviewed against the MAB Supplement Guidelines before production. (3) **Education** — False qualification or accreditation claims. A private education provider's promotional video claiming "internationally recognised" qualifications that are not genuinely accredited by MQA (Malaysian Qualifications Agency) violates the Education Act 1996 and Consumer Protection Act 1999. Action: verify all accreditation claims with the official MQA register before including them in a video. (4) **Property** — Misrepresentation of development status or completion date. A property developer's sales video showing renders as if they are photographs of a completed development, or quoting a completion date that is materially incorrect, may constitute misrepresentation under the Housing Development (Control and Licensing) Act 1966 and the Trade Descriptions Act 2011. Action: all property video content must clearly label renders as "artist's impression" and completion dates as "estimated." (5) **Fashion** — Copyright infringement in garment design. A fashion brand's product video featuring garments that incorporate patterns, prints, or textile designs that are copied from another brand or designer without a licence may infringe the Copyright Act 1987 (artistic works) and registered design rights under the Industrial Designs Act 1996. Action: confirm that all garments and textile designs featured in the video are original or licensed before publication.
