### Section A (20 marks — 2 marks each)

### Section B

**B1** (8 marks — 2 marks each metric):
a) CTR: Percentage of people who clicked the ad after seeing it. Formula: (Clicks / Impressions) × 100. Indicates ad relevance and creative appeal — higher CTR means the ad resonates with the audience.
b) CPM: Cost per 1,000 impressions. Indicates audience reach efficiency — lower CPM means broader reach per ringgit; high CPM may signal a competitive or narrow audience.
c) CPC: Total spend divided by total clicks. Indicates the cost to drive one visitor to the destination. Lower CPC with high quality clicks is the goal.
d) ROAS: Revenue generated for every RM1 of ad spend. Formula: Revenue / Ad Spend. A ROAS of 3x means RM3 revenue per RM1 spent. Target varies by industry — e-commerce typically targets ≥3x.

**B2** (8 marks — 1 mark per setting, max 6; +2 marks for correct level identification):
Campaign level: (1) Campaign objective (Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App Promotion, Sales). (2) Campaign budget optimisation (CBO) on/off.
Ad Set level: (1) Budget and schedule (start/end date, daily or lifetime budget if not using CBO). (2) Audience targeting (custom audiences, lookalike, interest, demographic). Also: placement, bidding strategy.
Ad level: (1) Creative assets (image/video). (2) Ad copy (headline, primary text, description, CTA button).

**B3** (8 marks):
Variable (2m): Test only ONE variable at a time — e.g. two different ad creatives, two different headlines, or two different audiences. Testing multiple variables simultaneously makes it impossible to identify which change drove the difference.
Duration (2m): Run for minimum 7 days, ideally 14 days, to account for weekly variation in audience behaviour (weekday vs weekend performance differs significantly for Malaysian audiences).
Sample size (2m): Each variant needs sufficient impressions and conversions — minimum 100 conversions per variant for statistical reliability; for lower-traffic campaigns, use Meta's built-in A/B test tool which calculates required sample size automatically.
Interpretation (2m): Declare a winner when statistical significance reaches 95% confidence. The winning variant should be rolled out as the new control; document the learning (what worked and why) for the campaign knowledge base.

**B4** (8 marks — 2 marks each):
1. Anomaly Detection — GA4 automatically flags unusual spikes or drops in traffic and conversion data, alerting the marketer to investigate (ad issue, website error, viral traffic).
2. Predictive Audiences — GA4 predicts which users are likely to purchase or churn in the next 7 days, enabling retargeting of high-intent users via Google Ads integration.
3. Automated Insights — GA4 surfaces "insights cards" on the dashboard highlighting significant trends (e.g. "Mobile conversions increased 34% this week") without the marketer needing to manually query data.
4. Natural Language Queries — marketers can type plain-English questions into GA4's search bar ("What was my top traffic source last month?") and GA4 returns data visualisations without manual report building.

**B5** (8 marks):
a) ROAS = RM5,400 / RM1,200 = **4.5x** (2m — 1m formula, 1m correct answer)
b) CTR = (1,800 / 90,000) × 100 = **2%** (2m — 1m formula, 1m correct answer)
c) CPC = RM1,200 / 1,800 = **RM0.67** (2m — 1m formula, 1m correct answer)
d) Interpretation (2m): Campaign is performing well — ROAS of 4.5x exceeds the typical Malaysian e-commerce benchmark of 3x; CTR of 2% is above the 1–1.5% average for Malaysian Facebook feed ads; CPC of RM0.67 is cost-efficient. Recommended action: scale the budget by 20–30% while monitoring ROAS stability, or use the winning audience/creative in a new Advantage+ campaign for further AI optimisation.

### Section C

**C1** (12 marks):

a) Campaign setup steps (6 marks — 1 mark each, max 6): (1) Log into Meta Business Manager; verify Business Page and Ad Account are linked. (2) Open Meta Ads Manager → Create Campaign → select "Sales" objective. (3) At Ad Set level: set audience (location: Klang Valley; age: 18–45; interest: fashion, Raya shopping); set budget RM1,000 lifetime; set schedule (campaign dates). (4) Select placements — either Advantage+ Placements or manual Facebook Feed + Instagram Feed + Reels. (5) At Ad level: upload creative (Raya sale banner/video); write headline ("Raya Sale — Up to 50% Off"), primary text, and select CTA "Shop Now"; add destination URL. (6) Review and publish; monitor in Ads Manager for first 24 hours for delivery confirmation.

b) Metric calculations and evaluation (6 marks):
- CTR = (900 / 45,000) × 100 = **2%** (1m)
- CPM = (RM480 / 45,000) × 1,000 = **RM10.67** (1m)
- CPC = RM480 / 900 = **RM0.53** (1m)
- ROAS = RM2,160 / RM480 = **4.5x** (1m)
- Evaluation (2m): Campaign is successful. ROAS of 4.5x significantly exceeds the 3x benchmark; CTR of 2% is strong for fashion retail; CPC of RM0.53 is very cost-efficient for the Klang Valley market. Remaining RM520 budget should be deployed in the final days of the Raya sale period with the same creative, scaling the daily spend.

**C2** (14 marks):

a) Performance analysis process (6 marks — 1 mark each): (1) Access Meta Ads Manager → Campaigns tab → select the 30-day date range. (2) Break down results by Ad Set to compare performance between interest targeting and Advantage+ AI targeting. (3) Review key metrics for each Ad Set: impressions, reach, CTR, CPC, conversions, ROAS, cost per result. (4) Open GA4 → Advertising → Attribution to cross-validate Meta-attributed conversions against GA4 tracked goal completions. (5) Check GA4 Audience reports to understand the demographic and behavioural profile of converting users. (6) Compile findings into a performance summary: top-performing Ad Set, creative, audience segment, and budget efficiency ratio.

b) Ad Set analysis and recommendation (8 marks):
Better performer: Ad Set B — Advantage+ AI targeting (2m). ROAS 14.5x vs 10x; 31 enrolments vs 12 at lower cost per result. Revenue: 31 × RM1,500 = RM46,500 on RM640 spend; vs 12 × RM1,500 = RM18,000 on RM360 spend. Cost per enrolment: Ad Set A = RM30; Ad Set B = RM20.65 — Advantage+ acquired each student at 31% lower cost (2m). Why Advantage+ outperformed (2m): Advantage+ uses Meta's AI to dynamically discover the highest-converting audience beyond manually selected interest categories — it can identify patterns in user behaviour (video watch history, purchase behaviour, look-alike signals from the Pixel) that human-defined interest targeting misses, especially effective for a high-consideration RM1,500 purchase where the buying journey involves multiple ad touchpoints. Budget recommendation (2m): Allocate 80% of next campaign budget to Advantage+ (approximately RM800 of RM1,000 budget); retain 20% for a refreshed interest-targeting Ad Set as a control and to test new creative variants. Increase overall budget by 50% given proven ROAS well above break-even.

**C3** (14 marks):

a) TikTok Smart+ vs Meta Advantage+ comparison (6 marks — 1 mark per point, max 6):

Similarities (3m): (1) Both use AI to automatically optimise audience targeting, creative selection, and budget allocation toward the best-performing combinations. (2) Both require a Pixel/tracking tag installed on the business website to track conversions and feed optimisation data. (3) Both support lookalike audience expansion — the AI finds users similar to existing customers or converters.

Differences (3m): (1) Platform audience — Meta reaches an older, broader demographic (Facebook: 25–55 dominant in Malaysia; Instagram: 18–35); TikTok's Malaysian user base skews 16–30 with higher engagement in F&B content (food videos, FYP viral posts). (2) Creative format — Meta Advantage+ works across static images, carousels, and video; TikTok Smart+ is video-first and requires native-style vertical video (9:16) — highly produced ads perform poorly; authentic "lo-fi" F&B content (behind the kitchen, food prep ASMR) performs best. (3) Conversion window — Meta's attribution window is configurable (1-day, 7-day click); TikTok's default attribution is 7-day click + 1-day view, which can inflate reported conversions — this must be aligned when comparing cross-platform ROAS.

b) ROI analysis and CLV in Looker Studio (8 marks):
ROI calculation (2m): ROI = (Total Revenue − Total Ad Spend) / Total Ad Spend × 100. If combined Meta + TikTok spend = RM2,000 and total attributed revenue = RM9,000: ROI = (RM9,000 − RM2,000) / RM2,000 × 100 = **350%**.

CLV calculation (3m): Customer Lifetime Value estimates the total revenue a customer generates over their relationship with the business. Formula: CLV = Average Transaction Value × Purchase Frequency per Year × Average Customer Lifespan (years). For an F&B chain where average spend = RM35/visit, customers visit 2× per month (24×/year), and average customer lifespan = 3 years: CLV = RM35 × 24 × 3 = **RM2,520**. Knowing CLV allows the business to justify a higher cost per acquisition — if CLV is RM2,520, spending RM50 to acquire a new customer via ads is highly profitable even if the first transaction only generates RM35.

Looker Studio presentation (3m): Connect Meta Ads and GA4 as data sources in Looker Studio. Build a dashboard with: (1) Scorecard tiles for total spend, total revenue, ROAS, and ROI at the top. (2) Time-series line chart showing daily spend vs daily revenue for the 30-day period. (3) Bar chart comparing Meta vs TikTok ROAS side-by-side. (4) Table breaking down performance by outlet location (Klang Valley branches) using UTM parameters. Present to the business owner with the BLOT finding (which channel delivered higher ROI) at the top of the dashboard; reserve the detailed breakdown for the appendix.

**Q21.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A Malaysian skincare brand's Meta Ads campaign has a CTR of 0.4% and CPC of RM4.80 after 5 days. Malaysian beauty industry benchmark CTR is 1–1.5%. Diagnose the problem and recommend TWO (2) specific actions. [CoCU C06.2.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* CTR of 0.4% is significantly below the 1–1.5% Malaysian beauty benchmark, indicating poor creative-audience relevance — the ad is not compelling enough for the target audience to click. CPC of RM4.80 is high as a result (fewer clicks per impression means each click costs more). Two actions: (1) Refresh the ad creative — test a new video creative showing a before/after skincare transformation, which typically outperforms static product shots for Malaysian beauty audiences on Facebook; add a clear urgency-driven headline in Bahasa Malaysia (e.g. "Kulit Cerah dalam 14 Hari"). (2) Narrow or refresh the audience — if the current audience is broad (e.g. all women aged 18–45 in Malaysia), switch to Advantage+ Audience and let Meta's AI find the most responsive segment; alternatively, upload a Custom Audience of existing customers and create a 1% Lookalike Audience to reach high-intent prospects.

**Q22.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client asks why their Meta Ads Manager shows 45 conversions but GA4 shows only 28 conversions for the same campaign period. Explain the most likely reasons for this discrepancy. [CoCU C06.2.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* This discrepancy is normal and has three primary causes: (1) Attribution window difference — Meta's default attribution counts a conversion if the user clicked the ad in the last 7 days OR viewed it in the last 1 day before converting. GA4's default session-based attribution only credits the most recent traffic source — if the user came via organic search after seeing the Meta ad, GA4 credits organic search, not Meta Ads. (2) Cross-device tracking — a user may see the Meta ad on their mobile phone but convert on a desktop PC; Meta can match this via its logged-in user graph, but GA4 may count these as two separate sessions from different devices. (3) Cookie and adblocker interference — some users block the GA4 tracking script (Google Analytics) but not the Meta Pixel, causing Meta to record a conversion that GA4 never sees. Resolution: use GA4 as the "ground truth" for total website conversions and Meta Ads Manager as the channel-level directional indicator. Align attribution windows in both platforms to the same lookback period for fair comparison.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's TikTok Smart+ campaign has high views and low ROAS (1.2x). The Meta Advantage+ campaign for the same product achieves ROAS 3.8x. Should TikTok be cancelled? Advise with reasoning. [CoCU C06.1.1 / C06.3.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Do not cancel TikTok — instead, diagnose before deciding. A ROAS of 1.2x is below break-even for most businesses, but this must be evaluated in context: (1) TikTok's attribution window (7-day click + 1-day view) may be undercounting assisted conversions — users who discover the product on TikTok often convert via Google Search or direct website visit days later, which GA4 would credit to a different channel. Run a full-funnel attribution analysis in GA4 to see if TikTok appears in the conversion path. (2) TikTok's primary strength for Malaysian F&B and lifestyle brands is top-of-funnel awareness and brand discovery — it drives future demand that Meta Ads later converts. If the business's overall ROAS and new customer acquisition rate improved after adding TikTok, the channel is contributing even if its direct ROAS appears lower. (3) Immediate action: audit the TikTok creative — Smart+ optimises delivery but requires compelling native video content. Provide 3–5 authentic food/product video variants for Smart+ to test. If ROAS remains below 2x after 14 days with new creative, reduce TikTok budget and reallocate to Meta.

**Q24.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] You set up an A/B test comparing two Facebook ad headlines for a Malaysian online course: Variant A: "Belajar Digital Marketing — Daftar Sekarang" vs Variant B: "Dapat Sijil JPK dalam 3 Bulan — Tempat Terhad". After 4 days, Variant B has 22 conversions vs Variant A's 15. A colleague says "Variant B wins, pause A." Evaluate this advice. [CoCU C06.3.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The colleague's advice is premature and statistically unsound. Four days is insufficient to reach statistical significance for a comparison of 22 vs 15 conversions — this difference could easily be explained by random variation or day-of-week bias (e.g. if the test started on a Wednesday, Variant B may have been served more on a high-intent Thursday/Friday). The correct approach: continue the test until Meta Ads Manager's A/B test tool reports ≥95% statistical confidence, or until each variant accumulates at least 50–100 conversions. Additionally, the test should run for at least 7 days to capture a full weekly behaviour cycle (Malaysian course registrations often spike on Sunday evenings). Pausing Variant A prematurely wastes the testing investment and may lead to adopting a "winner" that does not actually outperform in a longer run. However, Variant B's headline premise (JPK certification + urgency) is directionally stronger for the Malaysian vocational training market and worth continuing to validate.

**Q25.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A Johor Bahru retail client has a monthly ad budget of RM3,000. How would you recommend splitting this across Meta Ads and TikTok Ads, and how would you measure combined ROI at month end? [CoCU C06.3.2 / C06.3.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Recommended split for a Johor Bahru retail brand with no prior TikTok data: RM2,200 (73%) Meta Ads + RM800 (27%) TikTok Ads. Rationale: Meta has proven reach and conversion infrastructure across all age groups; TikTok allocation is sufficient to gather 14–21 days of AI optimisation data for Smart+ without over-committing to an unproven channel for this business. Within Meta: RM1,500 Advantage+ Shopping Campaign (conversions), RM500 retargeting (Custom Audiences of website visitors), RM200 awareness (Reach objective for new audience expansion). Within TikTok: full RM800 to Smart+ with 3–5 native video creatives. Month-end ROI measurement: pull spend and attributed revenue from both Ads Managers; cross-validate conversion volume in GA4 using UTM parameters (utm_source=meta / utm_source=tiktok). Build a Looker Studio dashboard combining both sources. Calculate blended ROAS = Total Revenue / Total Spend. Calculate channel-level ROI. ### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

*Soalan senario lanjutan untuk penilaian kefahaman mendalam tahap 3. Jawapan contoh menunjukkan kedalaman teknikal yang diperlukan. / Extended scenario questions for in-depth Level 3 competency assessment. Sample answers demonstrate required technical depth.*

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the three levels of the Meta Ads campaign hierarchy in detail, including what happens to delivery if you set the wrong objective at the campaign level. [CoCU C06.1.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Campaign level sets the overarching objective — this is the single most critical structural decision because Meta's delivery algorithm optimises toward the campaign objective. Choosing "Traffic" when you want purchases means Meta will show your ad to people most likely to click, not most likely to buy — these are different user segments. The three levels: (1) Campaign — objective (Sales, Leads, Traffic, etc.), Campaign Budget Optimisation (CBO) on/off, A/B test setup. (2) Ad Set — audience definition (custom, lookalike, interest, location), budget/schedule (if not using CBO), placement (Advantage+ or manual), bidding strategy (lowest cost vs cost cap vs bid cap). (3) Ad — the actual creative unit: image or video, primary text, headline, description, call-to-action button, destination URL, and display URL. If the wrong objective is selected (e.g. Engagement instead of Sales), Meta delivers the ad to engagement-optimised users (people who like and comment) who have no purchase intent — ROAS collapses to near-zero. The campaign must be duplicated with the correct Sales objective; the original cannot be changed after spending has begun.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what MCMC guidelines prohibit in Malaysian digital advertising and give THREE (3) real-world examples of ad content that would violate these guidelines. [CoCU C06.1.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* MCMC (Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission) regulates digital content under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (CMA 1998) Section 211 and 233, which prohibit content that is obscene, indecent, false, menacing, or offensive in character with intent to annoy, abuse, threaten, or harass. For digital advertising specifically, prohibited content includes: (1) Gambling promotion — a Facebook Ad for an online casino or "sports betting tips" service violates CMA 1998 and additionally the Betting Act 1953 and Common Gaming Houses Act 1953. Malaysian advertisers whose ad accounts run such content risk permanent account suspension by Meta (which complies with Malaysian law for in-country delivery) and prosecution under Malaysian law. (2) Unverified health claims — a skincare ad claiming "Hilangkan kanser kulit dalam 30 hari" (eliminates skin cancer in 30 days) violates both MCMC content rules and the Control of Drugs and Cosmetics Regulations 1984 under the Sale of Drugs Act 1952. Such false health claims are also specifically targeted by KPDNHEP (Ministry of Domestic Trade). (3) Political disinformation — an ad that contains fabricated quotes attributed to Malaysian politicians or government agencies to influence public opinion violates CMA 1998 Section 233 and potentially the Sedition Act 1948 if the content touches on racial or religious sensitivity. Advertisers must pre-screen all ad copy against MCMC guidelines before launching, particularly for health, financial, and politically adjacent products.

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's Advantage+ campaign has been running for 6 weeks with consistently high ROAS (5x). They want to scale the budget from RM2,000/month to RM8,000/month immediately. Advise them on the correct scaling approach. [CoCU C06.1.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Immediate 4x budget increase is high-risk and likely to degrade ROAS significantly. When campaign budget increases by more than 20–30% at once, Meta's delivery system exits the "learned" optimisation state and re-enters the learning phase (requires ~50 conversions per ad set to stabilise) — during this period, CPR (cost per result) typically spikes 30–80% above the steady-state level. Recommended scaling approach: (1) Gradual increase — raise budget by 20–30% every 5–7 days, monitoring ROAS after each increment before increasing further. At this rate, RM2,000 → RM8,000 takes approximately 5–6 increments over 4–6 weeks. (2) Horizontal scaling — instead of scaling one campaign's budget, duplicate the campaign and add new audience segments (new Lookalike Audiences, new geographic targets — e.g. expanding from Kuala Lumpur to Penang and Johor Bahru) to absorb additional budget without disrupting the winning campaign's learned algorithm. (3) Creative expansion — at RM8,000/month scale, creative fatigue becomes a risk; brief 3–5 new creative variants and load them into the Advantage+ creative pool before scaling. (4) Bid cap consideration — at high budget scale, switch from "Lowest cost" to "Cost cap" bidding to maintain ROAS above the client's break-even threshold even as audience saturation increases.

**Q29.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the difference between last-click attribution and data-driven attribution in GA4, and why this matters for evaluating a Meta Ads + TikTok Ads + SEO campaign. [CoCU C06.2.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Last-click attribution gives 100% of conversion credit to the final touchpoint before conversion — the last channel the user interacted with before purchasing. In a multi-channel campaign, this systematically undervalues upper-funnel channels (TikTok awareness, organic social) that introduced the customer to the brand, and over-credits lower-funnel channels (Google Search branded terms, email) that merely captured demand that was already created. For a Malaysian consumer journey typical in e-commerce and education sectors: Day 1 — discovers product via TikTok Smart+ video → Day 3 — sees Meta retargeting ad and clicks → Day 5 — searches brand name on Google and converts via SEO listing. Under last-click: 100% credit to SEO. Under data-driven attribution: GA4's machine learning model analyses all conversion paths in the dataset and distributes credit proportionally based on each touchpoint's observed contribution to the conversion probability — TikTok, Meta, and SEO all receive partial credit reflecting their actual role. Why it matters for budget decisions: if the client uses last-click attribution and sees TikTok with near-zero attributed conversions, they may cancel TikTok — eliminating the awareness channel that was generating the demand Meta and SEO were converting. Switch to data-driven attribution in GA4 (Settings → Data Settings → Attribution Settings → Data-driven) to make evidence-based budget allocation decisions across all three channels.

**Q30.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe how to set up a Looker Studio dashboard to present a monthly campaign report to a non-technical Malaysian business owner. [CoCU C06.3.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Dashboard structure for a non-technical owner: Page 1 — Executive Summary (BLOT): Four scorecard tiles at the top showing Total Ad Spend (RM), Total Revenue Attributed (RM), Blended ROAS, and Total New Customers — these are the only four numbers the owner needs to evaluate whether the campaign is working. Below, a simple traffic light indicator (green/amber/red) for each metric vs the previous month target. Page 2 — Channel Breakdown: Bar chart comparing Meta vs TikTok ROAS side by side. Pie chart showing spend distribution by channel. Table with 5 rows: Channel | Spend | Revenue | ROAS | Recommendation. Page 3 — Trend Analysis: Line chart showing daily spend and daily revenue over the month — allows the owner to visually correlate campaign activity with sales peaks (e.g. did revenue spike when you increased budget?). Page 4 — Top Performing Ads: Screenshot thumbnails of the 3 best-performing ads with their CTR, CPC, and ROAS figures — non-technical owners respond strongly to seeing the actual creative alongside the numbers. Implementation: Connect Meta Ads as a data source via Looker Studio's native Meta Ads connector; connect GA4 via the native GA4 connector. All data refreshes automatically daily. Share the report link with the owner so they can self-serve anytime. Present the report in a 20-minute monthly meeting — spend 5 minutes on Page 1, 10 minutes on decisions, 5 minutes on next month plan.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client claims their Facebook campaign is failing because "Facebook ads don't work in Malaysia." Analyse this claim and identify FIVE (5) actual reasons a campaign might underperform. [CoCU C06.2.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The claim is a misattribution — Facebook Ads are the largest digital advertising platform in Malaysia with 24+ million active users (Datareportal 2025), used successfully by hundreds of thousands of Malaysian SMEs. The real issue is campaign execution, not the platform. Five reasons for underperformance: (1) Wrong campaign objective — using "Traffic" or "Engagement" objective when the goal is purchases; Meta optimises delivery for the wrong user behaviour. Fix: switch to "Sales" objective with Pixel conversion event. (2) Audience too broad or too narrow — targeting "Malaysia, Women 18–55" (too broad, wasted spend on irrelevant users) or "KL, Women 25–30, Yoga interest" with less than RM500 budget (too narrow, insufficient reach for Meta to optimise). Fix: use Advantage+ Audience for mid-budget campaigns. (3) Creative fatigue — using the same one or two ad creatives for more than 2–3 weeks causes frequency to rise (same person sees the same ad 6–8 times) and CTR to collapse. Fix: refresh with 3–5 new creative variants every 2 weeks. (4) Landing page mismatch — the ad promises one thing (e.g. "Free delivery") but the landing page doesn't mention it or is slow to load, causing high bounce rate and zero conversions despite good CTR. Fix: audit the full conversion funnel from ad click to purchase confirmation. (5) Insufficient learning phase budget — a campaign with RM50/day budget that needs 50 conversions to exit the learning phase will take 20+ days at RM1/conversion — during this period ROAS is unstable and appears to "not work." Fix: increase budget to allow Meta to exit learning phase within 7 days.

**Q32.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is, how to calculate it for a Malaysian café chain, and how it should influence the maximum acceptable Cost Per Acquisition (CPA). [CoCU C06.3.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* CLV (Customer Lifetime Value) is the total net revenue a business can expect from a single customer account throughout their entire relationship with the business. It transforms the advertising decision from "did this ad generate a sale today?" to "how much is a new loyal customer worth to us over 2–3 years?" For a Malaysian café chain: Average transaction value = RM22 (one coffee + pastry). Purchase frequency = 10 visits/month (regular customers). Customer lifespan = 2 years (average before the customer moves location or changes habits). CLV = RM22 × 10 × 24 = **RM5,280 per customer**. Gross margin = 60% (typical café margin after COGS). Adjusted CLV = RM5,280 × 60% = **RM3,168 gross profit per customer**. Maximum CPA: a business should not spend more than CLV × target margin on acquiring a customer. If the café targets 20% net margin after all costs: Maximum CPA = RM3,168 × 20% = **RM633**. This means spending RM100–200 to acquire a new loyal café customer via Meta Ads is highly profitable — even though the first transaction only generates RM22 in revenue. Without CLV, the café owner might look at CPA = RM80, compare to first-purchase revenue RM22, and conclude "ads are losing money." CLV reframes this: that RM80 investment returns RM3,168 over 2 years.

**Q33.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe how to use Meta Ads' Custom Audiences and Lookalike Audiences together to build a full-funnel Malaysian campaign targeting cold, warm, and hot audiences. [CoCU C06.1.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Full-funnel audience strategy using Custom + Lookalike Audiences: Cold audience (Top of Funnel — Awareness/Traffic objective): Create a 1–2% Lookalike Audience based on a Custom Audience of the top 25% of customers by purchase value (uploaded as a Customer List Custom Audience). This Lookalike finds Malaysian Facebook/Instagram users who most resemble your best customers. Budget: 40% of total spend. Ad format: video (15–30 sec) introducing the brand or product — optimise for ThruPlay (3-second video views). Warm audience (Middle of Funnel — Consideration/Traffic): Create a Custom Audience of "Website Visitors in the last 30 days" using Pixel data, AND "People who engaged with your Facebook/Instagram page in the last 60 days." These users have shown interest but haven't converted. Budget: 30% of total spend. Ad format: carousel or single image with product details and social proof (reviews, ratings). Hot audience (Bottom of Funnel — Conversion/Sales): Create a Custom Audience of "Website visitors who viewed product pages but did NOT complete purchase in the last 14 days" (retargeting with Pixel events). This is the highest-intent segment. Budget: 30% of total spend. Ad format: dynamic product ads (automatically shows the exact product the user viewed) with an urgency CTA ("Only 3 left!" / "Sale ends Sunday"). Exclusion: always exclude existing customers from cold and warm audiences, and exclude purchasers from hot audience retargeting (to avoid showing purchase ads to people who already bought).

**Q34.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's A/B test showed Variant B (new creative) outperformed Variant A (control) at 95% statistical confidence. What should happen next, and how is this learning documented? [CoCU C06.3.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Post-A/B test protocol: (1) Declare Variant B the winner — pause Variant A, retain Variant B as the new control creative in the active campaign. Do not delete Variant A — archive it with its performance data. (2) Document the learning — create a Campaign Learning Log entry with: Test ID, date range, hypothesis tested ("New lifestyle video creative will outperform static product image for this audience"), Variant A metrics (CTR, CPC, CPA, ROAS), Variant B metrics, percentage improvement, confidence level, and conclusion ("Lifestyle video creative outperformed static image by 34% lower CPA at 96% confidence — roll out to all ad sets"). Store this in the client's Campaign Playbook. (3) Update the creative brief — the winning creative insight (lifestyle video > static image for this audience) should be codified as a rule in the creative brief for future campaign production: "All ad creatives for this client should lead with authentic lifestyle video, not product-on-white-background photography." (4) Plan the next test — the scientific method is iterative. Now that creative format is validated, test the next variable: headline framing (price vs outcome vs urgency) or audience (Lookalike 1% vs 2% vs Advantage+). (5) Inform the client — present the A/B result in the monthly Looker Studio report, translated into business language: "Changing the ad from a product photo to a lifestyle video reduced our cost per new customer by 34%, saving RM1,100 this month on the same budget."

**Q35.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the concept of "ad fatigue" and describe the signals a Malaysian digital marketer should monitor in Meta Ads Manager to detect and address it. [CoCU C06.2.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Ad fatigue occurs when the same audience has been shown the same ad creative too many times — they begin ignoring, hiding, or developing negative associations with the ad. It is one of the most common causes of ROAS decline in campaigns that ran well initially but deteriorate over weeks. Key signals to monitor in Meta Ads Manager: (1) Frequency — this is the primary fatigue indicator. Frequency = Impressions / Reach. When frequency exceeds 3–4 for cold audiences (people who have never bought from the brand), fatigue is setting in; for retargeting audiences, up to 6–8 is acceptable given the higher purchase intent. Monitor in the "Delivery" columns. (2) CTR decline — if CTR was 1.8% in week 1 and drops to 0.9% in week 3 with no audience or budget change, the creative is fatiguing the audience. (3) Negative feedback rate — in "Columns → Customize Columns → Hide" metric. A rising "hid ad" rate (above 0.3%) signals the audience is actively rejecting the creative. (4) CPM increase — as engagement rates drop, Meta's algorithm reduces ad quality score, which increases the auction price (CPM rises). A rising CPM with flat or declining CTR confirms fatigue. Malaysian-specific note: during peak campaign periods (Raya, CNY, 11.11) when all advertisers compete, audience saturation happens faster — refresh creative every 7–10 days during peak seasons vs the normal 2–3 week refresh cycle. Remedy: upload 3–5 new creative variants into the Advantage+ creative portfolio; Meta will automatically shift delivery to the fresh creatives.
