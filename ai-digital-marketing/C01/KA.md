## SKEMA PEMARKAHAN / MARKING SCHEME

### Section A (20 marks — 2 marks each)

A1: B | A2: C | A3: B | A4: C | A5: B | A6: C | A7: B | A8: C | A9: C | A10: C

### Section B

**B1** (8 marks — 2 marks per channel: 1 for advantage, 1 for limitation):
Accept any four from: SEO (advantage: free organic reach; limitation: slow results, 3–6 months), SEM (advantage: immediate visibility; limitation: cost per click adds up quickly), Social Media Marketing (advantage: high reach among Malaysian youth; limitation: requires consistent content output), Email Marketing (advantage: high ROI for owned list; limitation: low open rates without segmentation), Content Marketing (advantage: builds long-term authority; limitation: requires sustained effort before results), WhatsApp Marketing (advantage: high open rate in Malaysian market; limitation: consent and PDPA compliance required).

**B2** (8 marks — 1 mark per element name + 1 mark per explanation; partial credit for explanation):
- C = Context: Provide background information about the situation, business, or industry so the AI understands the environment.
- R = Role: Tell the AI what expert role to adopt (e.g., "You are a senior digital marketing strategist").
- A = Action: Clearly state the specific task or deliverable the AI should produce (e.g., "Conduct a SWOT analysis", "Write a 30-day content calendar").
- F = Format: State the desired output structure (e.g., bullet list, table, 500-word report, JSON).
- T = Tone: Specify the language style and register for the output (e.g., "formal and professional", "friendly and conversational").

**B3** (8 marks — 2 marks per segmentation type: 1 per data point):
- Demographic: age, gender, income level, education, occupation, ethnicity. Malaysian-specific: B40/M40/T20 income classification, race/ethnicity for culturally relevant messaging.
- Psychographic: values, lifestyle, interests, attitudes, personality traits. Malaysian-specific: halal consciousness, language preference (BM/EN/ZH), social media usage patterns.
- Geographic: state, urban vs. rural, Peninsular vs. East Malaysia (Sabah/Sarawak), proximity to urban centres. Malaysian-specific: urban Klang Valley vs. rural East Malaysia audiences require different channel mix, content language, and pricing strategy.
- Behavioural: purchase frequency, brand loyalty, benefits sought, device usage, online shopping platform preference. Malaysian-specific: Shopee/Lazada usage, peak purchasing periods (Ramadan, Hari Raya, 11.11).

**B4** (8 marks — 2 marks each):
- a) Meta Business Suite Insights: tracks Facebook and Instagram page performance — reach, impressions, engagement rate, follower demographics, best posting times.
- b) GA4: tracks website traffic, user behaviour, conversion events, acquisition sources, and audience demographics; uses event-based data model replacing sessions.
- c) TikTok Analytics: measures video views, average watch time, follower growth, traffic sources, and content performance for short-form video campaigns.
- d) Google Search Console: shows organic search performance — impressions, clicks, average position, and which search queries drive traffic to the website.

**B5** (8 marks — 4 marks for process description, 2 marks each limitation):
Process: Input company background, target market, and Malaysia context into AI tool; prompt AI to generate SWOT analysis; use follow-up prompts to drill into each quadrant; cross-reference AI outputs with verified sources.
Limitations (2 marks each): (1) AI may produce generic SWOT items not specific to the Malaysian market or the brand's actual situation — consultant must validate with real market data; (2) AI knowledge cutoff means recent developments (new competitors, regulatory changes, market shifts post-training-date) may be missing — consultant must supplement with current industry research.

### Section C

**C1** (12 marks):

a) Five steps (6 marks — 1.2 marks each, accept equivalent):
1. Define research scope: prompt AI (ChatGPT/Claude) with role + context — "You are a market researcher. Summarise the online tutoring market in Malaysia in 2024-2025."
2. Competitor identification: prompt AI to list key online tutoring platforms in Malaysia; verify results against actual URLs.
3. Competitor content audit: use AI to analyse public competitor social media post types, posting frequency, and engagement patterns.
4. Keyword and topic research: use AI (Perplexity or ChatGPT with browsing) to identify search terms Malaysian parents use when looking for tutoring services.
5. Synthesis: prompt AI to compile findings into a competitive landscape summary table with columns for competitor, strengths, weaknesses, and opportunity gaps.

b) Buyer persona (6 marks — 2 marks per data type):
- Demographic: Female, age 35–48, M40 household income RM 5,000–RM 8,000/month, located in suburban Kuala Lumpur or Selangor, SPM-educated or diploma holder, employed in clerical or service sector. Informs: messaging language (BM primary), platform (Facebook), and price sensitivity (mid-range pricing).
- Psychographic: Values academic achievement; anxious about SPM results; trusts peer recommendations on WhatsApp parent groups; follows educational content on Facebook and YouTube; time-poor. Informs: content type (testimonials, results-based proof, short explainer videos), messaging tone (reassurance, not pressure).
- Behavioural: Searches Google for tutoring options after school exam results; compares 2–3 providers before deciding; prefers WhatsApp for enquiries; makes decisions within 2 weeks of first contact. Informs: channel mix (Google SEO + Facebook retargeting + WhatsApp CTA), content cadence during exam season.

**C2** (14 marks):

a) Content calendar process (7 marks):
Inputs to AI: brand name, product name and USP, target audience profile, campaign duration (30 days), platforms (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok), content pillars (educational, promotional, testimonial, entertainment), and posting frequency. Apply CRAFT: Role = "You are a social media content strategist for a Malaysian skincare brand"; Context = new product launch; Audience = Malaysian women aged 20–35; Format = "Output a 30-day content calendar in table format with columns: Date, Platform, Content Type, Caption Hook, Visual Direction, Hashtags"; Task = "Generate a 30-day launch campaign calendar". Structure: Week 1 — awareness content (before/after, ingredient education); Week 2 — engagement (polls, Q&A, influencer seeding); Week 3 — conversion (promo codes, limited time offer); Week 4 — retention (reviews, UGC reposting, loyalty content). Cross-platform: Instagram for lifestyle visuals, TikTok for short-form tutorials, Facebook for longer testimonials and community groups.

b) Four compliance requirements (7 marks — accept 4 from below, 1.75 marks each):
1. PDPA 2010 — Consent before collecting data: any giveaway or lead form must include explicit opt-in consent checkboxes; the brand may not add entrants to a marketing list without prior consent.
2. PDPA 2010 — Data minimisation: only collect data fields necessary for the campaign purpose; avoid collecting IC numbers or sensitive personal data for marketing purposes.
3. MCMC content guidelines — No false or misleading claims: skincare claims (e.g., "clears acne in 7 days") must be substantiated; exaggerated claims may attract MCMC enforcement action.
4. MCMC — No prohibited content: content must not contain indecent, offensive, or menacing material as defined under the Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 Section 211/233.
5. Advertising Standards Authority of Malaysia (ASAM) Code: testimonials and endorsements must reflect genuine user experience; paid partnerships must be disclosed with #ad or #sponsored.

**C3** (14 marks):

a) Five KPIs (7 marks — 1.4 marks each):
1. Engagement Rate = (Total Engagements ÷ Total Reach) × 100. Benchmark >3% on Instagram. Indicates how well content resonates with the audience.
2. Click-Through Rate (CTR) = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100. Industry benchmark for Google Ads: 2%. Indicates ad copy and targeting relevance.
3. Conversion Rate = (Conversions ÷ Total Visitors) × 100. Indicates the percentage of website visitors who completed a desired action (purchase, sign-up). Retail benchmark: 1–3%.
4. Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) = Revenue Generated ÷ Ad Spend. A ROAS of 4x means RM 4 earned per RM 1 spent. Indicates overall campaign profitability.
5. Cost Per Result (CPR) = Total Ad Spend ÷ Number of Results (clicks, leads, or purchases). Enables comparison of efficiency across channels and campaigns.

b) Diagnosis and revised strategy (7 marks):
Diagnosis with AI: Input Instagram post data (captions, formats, posting times, hashtags) into AI and prompt: "You are a social media analyst. Given these Instagram post metrics [data], identify the top 3 reasons engagement rate is below 3% for a Malaysian fashion brand." AI likely identifies: (i) content format mismatch — audience prefers Reels but brand posts static images; (ii) posting times not aligned with Malaysian peak hours (8–9 PM weekdays); (iii) captions lack a clear call-to-action or engagement hook. Revised strategy: shift to 70% Reels / 30% carousels; use AI to generate caption hooks with questions or polls; schedule posts at peak hours using Meta Business Suite; test 5 content variations over 2 weeks and use AI to analyse which performs best. For Google Ads (CTR 4.8% — performing well): maintain current keyword strategy; use AI to expand to similar audience segments and increase budget allocation to top-converting ad groups.

**Q21.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A marketing trainee uses ChatGPT to research competitor prices and claims the AI's output is fully accurate. The supervisor disagrees. Explain why the supervisor is correct, and describe what steps the trainee should take to verify AI-generated competitive data. [CoCU C01.2.3 / AC1.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* The supervisor is correct because AI language models do not have real-time access to competitor pricing and may produce plausible-sounding but outdated or fabricated data — a phenomenon known as AI hallucination. The trainee should: (1) Cross-reference all prices against the competitors' actual websites or e-commerce product listings (Shopee, Lazada, official site). (2) Check the AI's claimed data against recent industry reports or news articles. (3) Note the AI tool's knowledge cutoff date — any pricing data after that date will be absent. (4) Document the verification sources in the strategy document. Never use AI-generated competitor data as the sole source for commercial decisions.

**Q22.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A digital marketing consultant is tasked with building a buyer persona for a Malaysian health supplement brand targeting the T20 segment. Describe the data sources and AI tools the consultant would use, and explain how the persona would differ if targeting the B40 segment instead. [CoCU C01.3.1 / K1.7 / K1.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* For T20: Data sources include Meta Business Suite audience demographics for the brand's existing followers, Google Analytics GA4 audience reports, and publicly available Nielsen Malaysia consumer reports. AI tools: ChatGPT or Claude to synthesise persona attributes; Perplexity for current T20 lifestyle trend data. T20 persona: household income above RM 10,970/month, tertiary-educated, values premium quality and scientific credibility, browses LinkedIn and Instagram, purchases via brand website or premium pharmacies, responds to expert endorsements and clinical evidence. For B40 (household income below RM 4,849): persona shift — price-sensitivity is paramount; messaging must emphasise value-for-money and affordability; platform shifts from Instagram to Facebook and TikTok; purchase channel shifts to Shopee with free-shipping incentives; language preference more likely BM; social proof from community testimonials more persuasive than expert endorsements.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's Google Analytics 4 report shows a bounce rate (engagement rate inverse) of 85% on the landing page for a Google Ads campaign. The campaign's CTR is strong at 3.5%. What does this data suggest, and how would you use an AI tool to diagnose and resolve the issue? [CoCU C01.3.3 / K1.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A high non-engagement rate of 85% combined with a strong CTR of 3.5% indicates the ad is attracting clicks but the landing page fails to meet visitor expectations — a classic "message mismatch" problem. Users click expecting what the ad promised, but the landing page content, design, or offer does not match. To diagnose: paste the ad copy and landing page headline into Claude or ChatGPT with the prompt: "You are a conversion rate optimisation specialist. Identify why these two texts may cause visitor drop-off and suggest 3 specific improvements." AI diagnosis will likely flag: headline mismatch, slow page load, lack of clear CTA, or irrelevant imagery. Resolution steps: align landing page headline with ad copy; add a prominent CTA button above the fold; remove navigation that distracts users; test an improved version using Google Optimize or Meta A/B testing.

**Q24.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A digital marketing trainee is asked to use the CRAFT framework to write a prompt for AI-assisted content calendar generation for a halal food brand. Write the complete CRAFT-structured prompt they should use. [CoCU C01.2.4 / K1.5 / S1.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Complete CRAFT prompt: "**Context:** I am a digital marketing executive for Nasi Lemak Wangi, a Malaysian halal food brand based in Kuala Lumpur selling ready-to-eat frozen nasi lemak online and via Shopee. Our target customers are working adults aged 25–40, primarily Malay and Chinese Malaysians. **Role:** You are a senior social media content strategist with deep knowledge of the Malaysian halal food market and social media trends. **Action:** Generate a 30-day product awareness and sales conversion content calendar for October 2026, incorporating Deepavali festive season context where relevant. **Format:** Output a 30-day content calendar in a table with these columns: Day, Date, Platform (Instagram/Facebook/TikTok), Content Type (Reel/Post/Story/Ad), Caption Hook (first line only), Visual Direction (1 sentence), and Key Message. **Tone:** Professional yet friendly, suitable for a Malaysian halal food brand communicating with working adults on social media."

**Q25.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A brand manager receives an AI-generated SWOT analysis for a new beauty product launch in Malaysia. The Opportunities section lists "Malaysia's growing middle class" as a key opportunity. The manager is unsatisfied with this generic output. ### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the difference between a digital marketing strategy and a content calendar. Describe how AI tools support each, and what human judgement must still be applied to each output. [CoCU C01.2.4 / K1.4 / K1.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A digital marketing strategy is the overarching plan that defines objectives, target audience, channel mix, budget allocation, KPIs, and the overall approach to achieving business goals over a defined period (3–12 months). A content calendar is an operational execution tool — a scheduled timeline of specific posts, formats, captions, and publishing dates that implements the strategy on a week-by-week or day-by-day basis. AI supports strategy development by rapidly synthesising competitor research, generating SWOT analyses, and proposing channel recommendations based on audience inputs. AI supports content calendars by generating post ideas, caption hooks, hashtag lists, and scheduling tables at scale. Human judgement remains essential at both levels: for strategy, humans must validate AI assumptions against real business context, financial constraints, and local cultural nuances the AI may not accurately model; for content calendars, humans must review every AI-generated caption for brand voice consistency, factual accuracy, cultural sensitivity (e.g., avoiding insensitive references during religious observances), and regulatory compliance (PDPA, MCMC). Publishing raw AI output without human review is a professional and reputational risk.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe THREE (3) specific ways a digital marketer can use GA4 to build a more accurate buyer persona. [CoCU C01.3.3 / K1.8]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* (1) Audience Demographics Report: GA4's User Attributes report shows age range, gender, and geographic location (state-level in Malaysia) of actual website visitors. Cross-referencing this with the Conversions report reveals which demographic segments have the highest conversion rate — not just the most traffic. This grounds the persona in real purchaser data rather than assumptions. (2) Affinity and Market Segment Categories: GA4 uses Google's user interest modelling to classify website visitors into interest categories (e.g., "Beauty & Wellness enthusiasts", "Frequent Shoppers"). These categories reveal psychographic attributes — what the target audience is also interested in beyond the brand — enabling richer persona development without primary survey research. (3) User Journey and Path Exploration: GA4's Funnel Exploration and Path Exploration reports show the sequence of pages visitors navigate before converting. If 70% of converters visit the "testimonials" page before purchasing, the persona insight is that this audience is highly influenced by social proof — a behavioural data point that directly shapes content strategy (prioritise testimonial content formats).

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A digital marketing trainee asks: "Why do we need to segment audiences into B40/M40/T20 when Facebook already targets by interest?" Provide a detailed answer explaining the value of income-based segmentation in Malaysian digital marketing strategy. [CoCU C01.3.2 / K1.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Facebook interest targeting and income-based segmentation serve different strategic purposes and operate at different levels of the marketing funnel. Facebook interest targeting identifies users by their demonstrated online behaviour — pages liked, content engaged with, searches made. It is effective for top-of-funnel awareness but does not reliably indicate purchasing power or price sensitivity. Income segmentation (B40/M40/T20) provides a structural economic lens that determines product positioning, pricing strategy, promotional mechanics, and channel investment decisions. For example: a B40-targeted campaign for a food product would prioritise value-for-money messaging, Shopee vouchers, and Facebook/TikTok reach; the product price point would be below RM 15; payment options might include e-wallet promotions (Touch 'n Go, Boost). The same product repositioned for T20 would use premium packaging imagery, emphasise quality/origin story, use Instagram and website-direct sales, and price above RM 30 without voucher dependency. Facebook cannot make these strategic distinctions — it only executes reach. The marketer must apply B40/M40/T20 thinking at the strategy level before configuring any ad platform. In Malaysia's multicultural, multilingual market, income segmentation also correlates with language preference, platform usage, and purchase behaviour patterns that require distinct creative executions.

**Q29.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain what AI hallucination is, why it is particularly risky in digital marketing strategy work, and describe FOUR (4) practical steps a marketer should take to detect and mitigate it. [CoCU C01.2.3 / Attitude A2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* AI hallucination refers to the phenomenon where a large language model generates factually incorrect, fabricated, or misleading information presented with the same confident tone as accurate outputs. The model does not "know" it is wrong — it produces statistically plausible text based on training patterns, not verified facts. In digital marketing strategy, hallucination is particularly damaging because: a strategy document citing a non-existent regulation (e.g., a fabricated MCMC guideline), an invented competitor statistic, or an incorrect market size figure can lead to flawed budget allocations, legal non-compliance, or misinformed client presentations. Four practical mitigation steps: (1) Source verification — never accept any statistic, market data, or legal reference from an AI output without confirming it against the original source (MCMC official website, DOSM reports, Nielsen Malaysia, peer-reviewed publication). (2) Cross-model checking — if ChatGPT produces a claim, verify it with Perplexity or Gemini; if all models agree, the probability of accuracy is higher though not guaranteed. (3) Knowledge cutoff awareness — always note the AI tool's training cutoff and flag any market data that may have changed after that date; use AI with web-browsing capability (Perplexity, ChatGPT with search) for current data. (4) Internal expert review — all AI-generated strategy documents must be reviewed by a human professional with domain expertise before presentation to clients or senior management; the human must sign off on factual claims.

**Q30.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A Malaysian F&B brand wants to use digital marketing to expand from Kuala Lumpur to Sabah and Sarawak. Describe how a digital marketer would adapt the target audience strategy — including buyer persona, channel mix, and content language — for East Malaysian consumers using AI research tools. [CoCU C01.3.1 / K1.7 / K1.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* East Malaysian (Sabah and Sarawak) consumer profiles differ from Peninsular Malaysian norms in several strategic dimensions that require persona and channel adaptation. Research approach: prompt AI (Claude or Perplexity) with: "Describe digital consumption patterns, language preferences, top social media platforms, and F&B purchasing behaviours specific to urban consumers in Kota Kinabalu and Kuching in 2024-2025." Persona adaptation: (1) Ethnic diversity is higher — Kadazan-Dusun, Murut, Iban, Bidayuh communities alongside Malay, Chinese, and others; content should not assume Peninsular ethnicity norms. (2) Language mix: BM remains primary, but local dialects and English are commonly used; Mandarin is significant in Kuching's Chinese community. (3) Income distribution: higher B40 proportion in rural East Malaysia; pricing strategy must be adjusted. Channel adaptation: internet connectivity in interior areas is less reliable — high video content may underperform; text-based Facebook posts and image carousels load faster. Mobile-first is even more critical — desktop usage is lower. Shopee East Malaysia delivery costs affect e-commerce willingness. Content adaptation: use locally resonant food references (e.g., Sabah laksa, Sarawak kolo mee) to signal cultural awareness; avoid Peninsula-centric references; engage local micro-influencers in Kota Kinabalu and Kuching for authenticity. Compliance note: PDPA applies equally; MCMC guidelines apply nationwide.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the concept of "search intent" and describe how understanding the FOUR (4) types of search intent helps a digital marketer plan content that aligns with each stage of the marketing funnel. [CoCU C01.1.2 / K1.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Search intent is the underlying purpose behind a user's search query — what the user actually wants to achieve by searching. Google's algorithm prioritises content that best matches search intent, making intent alignment critical for SEO success. The four types and their funnel alignment: (1) Informational intent — user wants to learn something (e.g., "how to start a digital marketing business in Malaysia"). Top-of-funnel (awareness stage). Content type: long-form blog articles, explainer videos, infographics. Objective: attract organic traffic and build brand authority. (2) Navigational intent — user is looking for a specific website or brand (e.g., "Maxis Malaysia contact number"). Indicates brand awareness already exists. Content: ensure the brand's own pages rank for branded searches; maintain updated Google Business Profile. (3) Commercial investigation intent — user is comparing options before purchase (e.g., "best digital marketing course Malaysia 2026"). Mid-funnel (consideration stage). Content type: comparison articles, detailed course curriculum pages, student testimonials, case studies. (4) Transactional intent — user is ready to purchase (e.g., "buy digital marketing course online Malaysia"). Bottom-of-funnel (conversion stage). Content type: landing pages with clear CTA, pricing tables, limited-time offers, direct enrolment buttons. Digital marketers use AI tools to map existing content against these four intent types, identify gaps, and brief AI-assisted writers to produce content for underserved intent categories.

**Q32.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee submits a digital marketing strategy document that contains AI-generated content without any citations or source verification. The supervisor returns it for revision. Explain the professional and ethical obligations the trainee must fulfil, and describe the process for properly documenting AI assistance in a professional deliverable. [CoCU C01.2.1 / Attitude A1 / AC1.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Professional and ethical obligations: (1) Accuracy — the trainee has a professional duty to ensure all facts, statistics, and regulatory references in a strategy document are accurate and current. Unverified AI outputs may contain hallucinated data that, if presented to a client, constitutes professional negligence. (2) Transparency — Malaysian professional standards and growing industry norms require disclosure of significant AI assistance in commercial deliverables, particularly where the client has not agreed to AI-generated outputs. (3) Intellectual property — if the strategy is a commercial deliverable, the trainee's employer owns the output; presenting unverified AI content as original professional work may misrepresent the trainee's expertise. Proper documentation process: (1) Add a methodology note in the document: "Research and initial drafts were generated with AI assistance (ChatGPT / Claude) and verified against [list sources]." (2) For each statistic or market claim, add a footnote citing the original source that was used to verify the AI output — DOSM, Nielsen, MCMC annual reports, etc. (3) Maintain a working log of prompts used and verification checks performed — this demonstrates due diligence if the document is challenged. (4) Have the document reviewed by a senior professional before submission. (5) Remove or flag any AI claims that could not be verified with a credible independent source.

**Q33.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the complete process of using AI to conduct a competitor social media audit for a Malaysian retail brand. Include the specific steps, the types of data to collect, the AI prompts to use, and how the findings would inform the brand's content strategy. [CoCU C01.2.3 / K1.6 / S1.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Step 1 — Identify competitors: Prompt ChatGPT: "List the top 7 direct competitors for [Brand Name], a Malaysian online fashion retailer targeting women aged 20–35. Include their Instagram and TikTok handles if known." Verify handles manually. Step 2 — Manual data collection: For each competitor, record (using native platform analytics publicly visible): follower count, average post frequency, most common content formats (Reels vs static), engagement range (likes/comments on recent 10 posts), and posting times. Step 3 — Content audit with AI: Screenshot or describe competitor content patterns and prompt: "You are a social media strategist. Based on this data from 5 Malaysian fashion brands on Instagram, identify the top 3 content formats driving the highest engagement and explain why each resonates with Malaysian women aged 20–35." Step 4 — Gap analysis: Prompt: "Based on this competitor audit, identify 3 content opportunities that none of these brands are currently executing that could differentiate [Brand Name]." Step 5 — Strategy application: Use AI findings to brief the content team. If AI identifies that carousel posts showing outfit-styling guides outperform static product shots 3:1, the brand's content calendar should shift to 50%+ carousel/Reels formats with styling narratives. Document all AI prompts and manually verified data points in the strategy appendix.

**Q34.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A digital marketing student argues that "a good strategy can be fully generated by AI without a human strategist." Present a structured counter-argument, using specific examples from the Malaysian market context to demonstrate where human strategic judgement cannot be replaced by AI tools. [CoCU C01.2.1 / AC1.5 / Attitude A2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* While AI tools significantly accelerate research and content generation, a digital marketing strategy that is fully AI-generated without human judgement will systematically fail in at least five critical dimensions: (1) Local cultural and religious sensitivity — AI does not reliably understand the sensitivity required when marketing around Malaysian religious observances (e.g., Ramadan, Hari Raya, Wesak). A human strategist knows that certain promotional tactics acceptable in Western markets (e.g., using music in video ads during Ramadan) require careful adaptation; an AI may generate a strategy that would cause public backlash. (2) Client relationship and business context — AI has no access to the client's internal data, past campaign failures, team constraints, or budget politics. A human strategist synthesises these unstated factors into every recommendation. (3) Real-time market events — a political development, celebrity scandal, or viral social media moment in Malaysia can make a scheduled campaign tone-deaf overnight. Human judgement is required to hold or pivot campaigns in real time; AI cannot monitor and respond to live events without human instruction. (4) Ethical responsibility — the human strategist is legally and professionally accountable for the strategy deliverable. If an AI-generated strategy recommends a campaign that violates PDPA 2010 or MCMC guidelines, the human professional bears the professional and legal liability, not the AI tool. (5) Client trust and communication — presenting a strategy to a client requires negotiation, empathy, and the ability to respond to objections in the moment. AI cannot attend the client meeting, read the room, or adapt in real-time conversation.

**Q35.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain how engagement rate, CTR, and ROAS interact as a set of KPIs to give a complete picture of campaign performance. Use a specific example of a Malaysian e-commerce campaign to illustrate how each metric informs a different decision. [CoCU C01.3.3 / K1.8 / AC1.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* These three KPIs measure different layers of the marketing funnel and should be interpreted together: Engagement Rate measures content resonance (are people interested?), CTR measures persuasion efficiency (are people clicking?), and ROAS measures commercial return (is the campaign profitable?). Example: A Malaysian online bakery runs a Hari Raya campaign across Instagram Ads and Google Ads. Instagram Engagement Rate: 5.2% (above 3% benchmark) — content is resonating; festive visuals and captions are generating likes and shares. This informs a CONTENT decision: the creative is working; maintain this visual direction and expand reach. Instagram CTR: 0.8% (below 1% benchmark) — despite high engagement, few users are clicking through to the website. This informs a CTA decision: the ad lacks a compelling reason to click; test a stronger offer ("Free Delivery this Raya — Shop Now") or add a product price in the creative to attract serious buyers. Google Ads CTR: 3.8% (above 2% benchmark) — search ads are attracting clicks from users with high purchase intent. This informs a BUDGET decision: allocate more budget to Google Ads where intent is clearer. ROAS (overall campaign): 3.2x — for every RM 1 spent, RM 3.20 is returned. This is above the break-even threshold for most F&B businesses. This informs a SCALE decision: the campaign is profitable; increase total budget by 30% in the final week before Hari Raya. Interpreting any one metric in isolation risks misallocation: high engagement without CTR means content entertains but doesn't convert; high CTR without ROAS means traffic is cheap but the landing page or product is not converting.
