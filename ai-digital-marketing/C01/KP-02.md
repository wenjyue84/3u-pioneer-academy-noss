## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees will be able to:
1. Identify and categorise AI tools applicable to digital marketing strategy planning.
2. Apply the CRAFT prompt engineering framework to generate professional marketing outputs from AI tools.
3. Use AI tools to conduct SWOT analysis and competitive landscape research.
4. Evaluate AI-generated strategy outputs critically and verify them against real-world sources.
5. Apply ethical and data privacy principles when using AI tools in a professional marketing context.

---

## 1. Introduction to AI in Digital Marketing (Kecerdasan Buatan dalam Pemasaran Digital)

### 1.1 What is AI in the Context of Digital Marketing?

**Artificial Intelligence (AI / Kecerdasan Buatan)** in digital marketing refers to software systems that can perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence — such as generating written content, analysing data patterns, classifying audience segments, and conducting research — with speed and scale that exceeds human capability.

The rise of **Large Language Models (LLMs / Model Bahasa Besar)** — AI systems trained on vast quantities of text — has made conversational AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini accessible to individual marketers without technical expertise. A digital marketing professional in Malaysia can now conduct competitor research, draft a strategy document, or produce 20 content variants in the same time it previously took to write one.

### 1.2 AI as a Collaborative Tool

A critical mindset for professional AI use is understanding what AI can and cannot do:

| AI Excels At | Human Judgment Required |
|---|---|
| Generating first drafts rapidly | Verifying factual accuracy |
| Synthesising patterns from large text inputs | Applying local cultural knowledge |
| Producing multiple content variants | Strategic decision-making |
| Structuring information in tables and frameworks | Ethical review of output |
| Performing structured analysis (SWOT, persona) | Validating with real data sources |
| Translating between languages | Checking colloquial accuracy for Malaysian context |

> **NOTA PENTING / IMPORTANT NOTE:** AI tools are assistants — they amplify human capability but do not replace professional judgment. Always apply critical thinking to every AI-generated output before presenting it to clients or publishing it.

## 3. Prompt Engineering for Marketing — CRAFT Framework

### 3.1 What is Prompt Engineering? (Kejuruteraan Arahan)

**Prompt engineering (kejuruteraan arahan)** is the practice of designing input instructions (prompts) that guide an AI to produce high-quality, relevant, and accurate outputs. The skill of prompt engineering is as important as the choice of AI tool — a well-crafted prompt can produce professional-grade output from a free AI tool, while a vague prompt will produce mediocre output even from the most advanced model.

### 3.2 The CRAFT Framework

CRAFT is a structured approach to writing marketing prompts:

| Letter | Element | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | Context (Konteks) | Background information about the business, situation, or market | "I am marketing a halal skincare brand targeting Muslim women aged 22–35 in the Klang Valley. Our products are certified by JAKIM." |
| **R** | Role (Peranan) | The professional perspective the AI should adopt | "Act as an experienced digital marketing strategist with 10 years of experience in the Malaysian F&B and beauty industry." |
| **A** | Action (Tindakan) | The specific task you want the AI to perform | "Conduct a detailed SWOT analysis of our brand's current digital marketing position." |
| **F** | Format (Format) | How you want the output structured | "Present as a formatted table with 4 bullet points per quadrant. Follow with a 3-sentence strategic recommendation." |
| **T** | Tone (Nada) | The language style and register | "Use formal, professional English suitable for a strategy presentation to company directors." |

### 3.3 Worked CRAFT Examples for Marketing Tasks

**Example 1 — SWOT Analysis for a Malaysian F&B Brand:**

```
[C] I run a small home-based business selling traditional Nyonya kuih in Melaka. 
    I sell primarily via Instagram and WhatsApp. My competitors are Bake With Yen 
    and several local Nyonya kuih shops that have opened physical stores.

[R] Act as a senior digital marketing consultant specialising in Malaysian F&B SMEs.

[A] Conduct a comprehensive SWOT analysis for my business, with specific focus on 
    growing our digital marketing reach beyond Melaka to the Klang Valley.

[F] Present as a table with 4 bullet points per quadrant (Strengths, Weaknesses, 
    Opportunities, Threats). Follow the SWOT with a separate table of 4 strategic 
    recommendations (one per quadrant pairing: SO, ST, WO, WT).

[T] Professional English, accessible to a non-marketing business owner. 
    Avoid excessive jargon.
```

**Example 2 — 3-Month Digital Marketing Strategy:**

```
[C] I am launching a new after-school English enrichment centre in Subang Jaya, 
    Selangor targeting Primary 4–6 students. Parents make the purchasing decisions. 
    My budget is RM3,000/month for marketing. I have no existing social media presence.

[R] You are a digital marketing strategist specialising in Malaysian education businesses.

[A] Create a detailed 3-month digital marketing strategy. Include: (1) recommended 
    channels and why, (2) content themes per channel, (3) posting frequency, 
    (4) estimated budget allocation per channel, (5) KPIs for each month.

[F] Use a month-by-month table format for the tactical plan. Add a separate summary 
    table for channel budget allocation.

[T] Professional, clear, and actionable — suitable for presenting to a business owner 
    with no prior marketing background.
```

### 3.4 Iterative Prompting

Professional prompt engineering is rarely a single exchange. Use these iteration techniques:

| Technique | How to Use |
|---|---|
| **Refine** | "The tone is too formal. Rewrite in a friendlier, more conversational style." |
| **Expand** | "Expand Section 3 with two more specific examples relevant to Malaysian SMEs." |
| **Constrain** | "Reduce the output to a maximum of 200 words." |
| **Redirect** | "Focus only on Instagram and TikTok — remove LinkedIn and Twitter from the strategy." |
| **Verify** | "What are your sources for the statistics in this analysis?" |

## 4. AI for SWOT Analysis and Competitive Research

### 4.1 Understanding SWOT Analysis (Analisis SWOT)

**SWOT** is a strategic framework that maps an organisation's internal and external position:

| Quadrant | Type | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Strengths (Kekuatan)** | Internal, Positive | What the business does well; competitive advantages |
| **Weaknesses (Kelemahan)** | Internal, Negative | Limitations, gaps, areas for improvement |
| **Opportunities (Peluang)** | External, Positive | Market trends and conditions that can be exploited |
| **Threats (Ancaman)** | External, Negative | Competitors, regulations, or market shifts that pose risk |

### 4.2 Step-by-Step: AI-Assisted SWOT Analysis Procedure

- [ ] **Step 1:** Write a detailed business brief (product, location, target audience, current digital presence, competitors)
- [ ] **Step 2:** Input business brief into AI using the CRAFT framework with SWOT as the Action
- [ ] **Step 3:** Review AI output — add domain-specific knowledge the AI may not have
- [ ] **Step 4:** Cross-check Strengths against real data (sales figures, customer reviews)
- [ ] **Step 5:** Validate Opportunities against MCMC reports, Statista, or industry publications
- [ ] **Step 6:** Research Threats independently — ask AI: "What external threats might not have been captured here?"
- [ ] **Step 7:** Generate strategic recommendations using follow-up prompts (SO, ST, WO, WT strategies)
- [ ] **Step 8:** Document the validated SWOT in a professional format for the strategy report

### 4.3 AI-Assisted Competitive Analysis

| Step | Action | Recommended Tool |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | List 5 main competitors in your niche and location | ChatGPT / Perplexity (web search enabled) |
| 2 | Analyse each competitor's social media strategy | Perplexity + manual review of their pages |
| 3 | Check competitor website traffic and keywords | SimilarWeb, SEMrush (manual) |
| 4 | Identify gaps in competitor content | ChatGPT analysis of their content themes |
| 5 | Map competitors on a 2x2 positioning matrix | Claude / ChatGPT with your input |
| 6 | Summarise in a competitive benchmarking table | Claude for document quality |

> **NOTA PENTING / IMPORTANT NOTE:** AI chatbots without web browsing enabled may have outdated information about competitors. Always verify competitor data through direct observation of their social media pages, websites, and Google reviews before including it in a strategy document presented to clients.

## 5. AI Ethics and Responsible Use in Malaysia

### 5.1 PDPA Compliance When Using AI Tools

When using public AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) for client work, digital marketers must observe PDPA 2010 data protection principles:

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Entering identifiable customer data — such as customer names, phone numbers, email lists, or purchase histories — into public AI tools may constitute a breach of PDPA 2010. Personal data entered into AI tools may be used for model training or retained by the service provider.

**Safe practices:**
- [ ] Anonymise all data before entering into AI tools (replace names with "Customer A", phone numbers with "XXXX")
- [ ] Use aggregate data only in prompts (e.g., "30% of our customers are aged 25–34")
- [ ] For sensitive client campaigns, use enterprise AI tools with data privacy agreements (e.g., Microsoft Copilot Enterprise, ChatGPT Enterprise)
- [ ] Include an AI Use Policy clause in client service agreements

### 5.2 Accuracy and Anti-Hallucination Practices

**AI hallucination (halusinasi AI)** refers to AI generating confident-sounding but factually incorrect information — such as fabricated statistics, non-existent laws, or made-up citations.

| Hallucination Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fabricated statistics | Always verify with official sources (MCMC, DOSM, DataReportal) |
| Wrong legal references | Cross-check with MyLegal, Attorney General Chambers website |
| Non-existent competitor data | Verify directly on competitor websites and social media |
| Outdated information | Check date of AI training cutoff; use Perplexity for current data |

### 5.3 Transparency and Professional Disclosure

| Situation | Disclosure Requirement |
|---|---|
| Agency delivering AI-assisted strategy to client | Disclose AI use; present AI output as a starting point, not a finished product |
| Posting AI-generated content on brand social media | Follow platform guidelines; internal policy may require disclosure |
| Submitting a strategy report in a professional context | Acknowledge AI assistance; ensure human expert review and sign-off |
| Generating client testimonials with AI | Never fabricate testimonials — this violates Consumer Protection Act 1999 |
