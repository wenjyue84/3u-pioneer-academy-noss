## 1. The Rise of AI-Powered Search (Kebangkitan Carian Berkuasa AI)

### 1.1 What is AI-Powered Search?

**AI-powered search (carian berkuasa AI)** refers to search platforms that use large language models (LLM) and generative artificial intelligence to produce synthesised, conversational answers — rather than simply listing a ranked set of web page links.

When a user types a query such as "What is the best café in Johor Bahru?" into a traditional search engine, they receive a list of links to web pages. The user must then click through and read multiple pages to find an answer. With AI-powered search, the system reads and synthesises hundreds of sources automatically and presents a direct, conversational answer — often with citations to the sources it used.

This is a fundamental shift in how information is consumed online. The implications for businesses, especially Malaysian SMEs (PKS — Perusahaan Kecil dan Sederhana), are significant: if your business is not cited by AI, you become invisible to an entire generation of AI-first searchers.

### 1.2 Key AI Search Platforms (Platform Carian AI Utama)

The following platforms represent the major AI search tools that Malaysian users and businesses must understand in 2026:

| Platform | Developer | How It Works | Malaysian Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Google AI Overviews** | Google | Generates a synthesised paragraph at the top of Google search results, with citations. Powered by Google Gemini. | Most relevant — Google holds ~95% search market share in Malaysia. AI Overviews appear for millions of queries daily. |
| **ChatGPT Search** | OpenAI | Users can search the web through ChatGPT; GPT-4o browses and summarises live results. | Growing rapidly in Malaysia; commonly used by students, professionals, and entrepreneurs for research. |
| **Perplexity AI** | Perplexity AI Inc. | An AI-native search engine that returns a synthesised answer with numbered source citations for every query. | Gaining traction among tech-savvy Malaysian users; particularly popular for detailed research queries. |
| **Bing Copilot** | Microsoft | Microsoft's AI search assistant integrated into Bing and Microsoft Edge browser. Powered by GPT-4. | Relevant for corporate users on Windows; integrated into Microsoft 365 ecosystem used by many Malaysian companies. |
| **Google SGE (Search Generative Experience)** | Google | The broader AI enhancement layer across Google Search (now integrated into AI Overviews). | Influences every Google search result in Malaysia — understanding this is non-negotiable for any Malaysian marketer. |

> **NOTA PENTING / IMPORTANT NOTE:** AI search platforms update their algorithms and interfaces frequently. Trainees should verify current platform capabilities at the start of any GEO project. The information above reflects the state of these platforms as of mid-2026.

### 1.3 How AI Search Engines Retrieve Information

Understanding how AI search engines work at a fundamental level is essential before attempting to optimise for them. The process differs significantly from traditional search indexing.

**Traditional Search Engine Process (Proses Enjin Carian Tradisional):**

1. A web crawler (pengimbas web) visits websites and indexes the content
2. The search engine ranks pages based on signals such as keyword relevance, backlinks, and page authority
3. The user receives a list of ranked links (Search Engine Results Page / SERP)
4. The user clicks a link, visits the page, and reads the content themselves

**AI Search Engine Process (Proses Enjin Carian AI):**

1. The AI model is trained on a massive corpus of internet text, books, and structured data — giving it background knowledge
2. When a query is received, the AI may perform a live web search using its browsing capability (retrieval-augmented generation / RAG — Penjanaan Bertambahan Semula)
3. The AI reads multiple sources simultaneously and evaluates their authority, consistency, and relevance
4. The AI synthesises a single coherent answer, selecting the most credible and consistent information
5. The AI generates citations — references to the specific sources it used — which appear alongside the answer
6. The user reads the synthesised answer without needing to visit multiple pages

The critical insight is this: **AI systems do not send traffic to all ranked pages — they select a small number of authoritative sources to cite.** Being cited is the new version of ranking on Page 1.

### 1.4 Case Study — Makan Moments Cafe: The AI Search Visibility Gap

**Business Profile:** Makan Moments Cafe is a café located in Johor Bahru (JB). It serves local Malaysian cuisine alongside coffee and desserts. The owner, like most Malaysian café operators, invested in Instagram content and Google Maps listing but had not considered AI search.

**Scenario A — Before AI Search Optimisation:**

A potential customer in Singapore planning a weekend trip to JB opens ChatGPT and types: *"What are the best cafés in Johor Bahru for local food?"*

ChatGPT synthesises an answer from its training data and live web sources. It mentions three cafés — all of which have been featured in food blogs, have detailed Google Business Profiles, and have reviews cited on platforms that AI crawlers can read. **Makan Moments Cafe does not appear** — not because it is inferior, but because:

- Its website has no detailed "About" page describing the café concept, location, and specialties
- No credible third-party website has published a review or article that names the café specifically
- Its Google Business Profile description is minimal and uses only Malay text with no structured English description
- It has no FAQ content that answers the exact questions AI systems look for

**Scenario B — After AI Search Optimisation:**

After applying GEO techniques (covered in KP-02 and KP-03), the café's website now contains:
- A detailed "About" page with the café's story, address, opening hours, and specialty dishes
- A FAQ section answering: "What type of food does Makan Moments Cafe serve?" and "Is Makan Moments Cafe halal?"
- A structured data markup (JSON-LD) that signals the café's business category, operating hours, and menu to search engines
- Three positive reviews published on JB food blogs with the café's full name mentioned

When the same question is asked to ChatGPT one month later, Makan Moments Cafe now appears in the synthesised answer as a recommended option for local Malaysian food in JB.

> **Kesimpulan / Key Takeaway:** AI search engines cite businesses that make themselves easy to read, verify, and reference. Optimising for AI search is about structuring your online presence for machine comprehension, not just human browsing.

## 2. Traditional SEO vs. Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)

### 2.1 Defining Traditional SEO (SEO Tradisional)

**Search Engine Optimisation (SEO / Pengoptimuman Enjin Carian)** is the practice of improving a website's visibility in traditional search engine results pages (SERPs) such as Google Search. SEO focuses on signals that search engine algorithms use to rank pages, including:

- **Keywords (kata kunci):** Using the terms that target users are searching for, in the right density and placement (title tags, headings, body text, meta descriptions)
- **Backlinks (pautan balik):** Links from other websites pointing to your site — treated as "votes of confidence" by search algorithms
- **Technical SEO (SEO Teknikal):** Website speed, mobile-friendliness, crawlability, Core Web Vitals, HTTPS security
- **On-page SEO (SEO Dalam Halaman):** Content quality, internal linking, image alt text, structured headings (H1, H2, H3)
- **Local SEO (SEO Tempatan):** Google Business Profile optimisation, local citations, and location-specific keyword targeting

Traditional SEO is a well-established discipline with a history of more than 25 years. It remains important in 2026, particularly for driving click-through traffic to websites.

### 2.2 Defining GEO — Generative Engine Optimisation

**Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO / Pengoptimuman Enjin Generatif)** is a newer discipline that focuses on optimising digital content so that AI-powered search systems will retrieve it, trust it, and cite it in their generated answers.

GEO was formally defined in academic research published in 2023–2024 (Princeton University, Georgia Tech, et al.) and has rapidly evolved into a professional practice as AI search tools gained mainstream adoption. Unlike traditional SEO — which aims to rank a page in a list — GEO aims to **become the source that AI cites** when answering a user's question.

### 2.3 Comparing SEO and GEO

| Dimension | Traditional SEO (SEO Tradisional) | Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal (Matlamat)** | Rank on Page 1 of search results | Be cited in AI-generated answers |
| **Success Metric** | SERP ranking position, organic click-through rate | Citation frequency in AI results, share of AI voice |
| **Primary Audience** | Search algorithm bots that rank pages | AI language models that select and cite sources |
| **Content Format** | Keyword-optimised paragraphs, meta tags | Definition-first paragraphs, FAQ format, cited statistics, authoritative claims |
| **Key Signals** | Backlinks, keyword density, page speed, domain authority | E-E-A-T signals, consistency of facts across the web, structured data, authoritative third-party mentions |
| **Result Type** | A link in a list of search results | A synthesised paragraph containing your information, with a citation link |
| **Traffic Model** | User clicks your link and visits your site | User reads AI summary; may or may not click through |
| **Technical Tools** | Robots.txt, sitemaps, canonical tags | llms.txt, JSON-LD schema, FAQ schema, structured data |
| **Timeline** | 3–6 months to see results | 4–12 weeks for initial citation appearances |

### 2.4 Why Both Matter — The Dual Optimisation Strategy

A common misconception is that GEO replaces SEO. In reality, **both disciplines work together** and share many underlying principles. Strong traditional SEO (especially domain authority and quality content) forms the foundation upon which GEO is built.

For Malaysian businesses in 2026, the recommended approach is:

1. **Maintain traditional SEO fundamentals** — ensure your website is technically sound, your Google Business Profile is complete, and your content covers relevant keywords
2. **Layer GEO practices on top** — restructure content for AI comprehension, add structured data, create FAQ sections, and build citations on AI-indexed platforms
3. **Monitor both channels** — track traditional SERP rankings alongside AI citation frequency

> **Analogi / Analogy:** Traditional SEO is like ensuring your café appears in a well-organised directory so customers can find you by browsing. GEO is like ensuring the concierge at a hotel (the AI) knows your café well enough to recommend it confidently when a guest asks, "Where should I eat tonight?"

## 3. The Concept of Citability (Konsep Kebolehdisebut)

### 3.1 What is Citability?

**Citability (kebolehdisebut)** refers to the degree to which a business, product, service, or piece of content is likely to be selected and referenced — cited — by an AI search system when generating an answer to a user's query.

A business with high citability has the following characteristics:

- **Findability (Kebolehtemuan):** The business's information is published on platforms that AI crawlers index — its own website, Google Business Profile, industry directories, review platforms, and news articles
- **Clarity (Kejelasan):** The information about the business is clearly structured, uses consistent facts (same address, same business name, same operating hours across all platforms), and answers the exact questions users are likely to ask
- **Authority (Autoriti):** Multiple independent, credible sources mention the business by name — reviews, articles, directory listings, and social proof
- **Consistency (Konsistensi):** The same facts appear repeatedly and consistently across many sources, so the AI can verify them with high confidence

### 3.2 Why AI Citations Matter for Malaysian Businesses

The shift from traditional search to AI search has specific implications for the Malaysian business environment. Consider the following use cases that are increasingly common among Malaysian consumers:

**Use Case 1 — Restaurant Discovery:**
A family in Kuala Lumpur planning a weekend trip to JB asks Google AI Overviews: *"Best places to eat in Johor Bahru."* The AI synthesises an answer from food blogs, Google reviews, travel articles, and structured business data. Only 3–5 businesses are cited. The family never scrolls past the AI Overview to the traditional search results below.

**Use Case 2 — Accommodation Search:**
A traveller from Singapore asks Perplexity AI: *"Best homestay near KSL City Mall Johor Bahru."* Perplexity generates an answer citing 3 accommodation options with brief descriptions. Southern Homestay, located at KSL D'Esplanade JB, is cited because its website has a clear "Location" page stating proximity to KSL City Mall, its Airbnb listing has numerous positive English-language reviews, and a travel blog has published an article mentioning the property.

**Use Case 3 — Service Provider Selection:**
A Malaysian startup founder asks ChatGPT: *"Which digital marketing agencies in JB are good for small businesses?"* ChatGPT cites agencies that have published case studies, testimonials, and detailed service descriptions — all signals that AI systems can read and verify.

In each case, **the businesses that are cited win the customer** before the customer has visited a single website. The businesses that are not cited simply do not exist in the AI-mediated customer journey.

### 3.3 The Citability Gap in Malaysia

Despite the rapid growth of AI search, most Malaysian SMEs remain unaware of citability as a concept. A survey of Malaysian digital marketing practitioners in early 2026 indicated:

- Approximately 78% of Malaysian SMEs have a Google Business Profile, but fewer than 15% have optimised it with the structured information AI systems prefer
- Fewer than 5% of Malaysian SMEs have implemented schema markup (structured data) on their websites
- The majority of Malaysian SME websites lack FAQ sections, definition-first content, or cited statistics — the content formats AI systems preferentially extract
- Awareness of GEO as a discipline remains under 10% among Malaysian SME owners surveyed

This citability gap represents both a challenge and a significant opportunity. Businesses that act early to improve their AI search visibility will enjoy a competitive advantage over competitors who remain focused solely on traditional social media and SEO.

### 3.4 Case Study — Southern Homestay: Building Citability

**Business Profile:** Southern Homestay is a short-term accommodation rental located at KSL D'Esplanade, Johor Bahru. It caters to travellers from Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, and abroad looking for serviced apartment-style accommodation in central JB.

**The Citability Challenge:**

When a Singapore traveller asks ChatGPT: *"Best homestay in Johor Bahru near KSL City?"*, the AI synthesises information from Airbnb listings, Booking.com pages, travel blogs, and Google Business Profiles. Southern Homestay faces a citability challenge if:

- Its Airbnb listing description is short and does not mention specific landmarks, nearby attractions, or the building name
- Its Google Business Profile has no response to common questions about check-in process, parking, and amenities
- No travel blog or review website has published a specific article mentioning "Southern Homestay KSL D'Esplanade"
- The business has no dedicated website with an "About" page and FAQ section

**Building Citability — Action Plan:**

| Action | What It Does for Citability |
|---|---|
| Write a detailed Airbnb/Booking.com listing description (300+ words) mentioning KSL City, Johor Premium Outlets, customs checkpoint proximity | AI systems extract location context from listing descriptions |
| Create a Google Business Profile Q&A section with 10+ questions and answers | AI Overviews frequently pull Q&A content from Google Business Profiles |
| Publish a dedicated website page: "Southern Homestay at KSL D'Esplanade — Frequently Asked Questions" | FAQ pages are the highest-priority content format for AI extraction |
| Request that satisfied guests write reviews mentioning "KSL D'Esplanade" and "Johor Bahru homestay" in their review text | Third-party reviews with location keywords reinforce AI's location association |
| Get mentioned in one JB travel blog article | A single credible third-party mention significantly boosts AI citability |

> **Kesimpulan / Key Takeaway:** Citability is built through a combination of structured content on your own platforms and consistent, authoritative mentions across third-party platforms. It cannot be purchased directly — it must be earned through strategic content and relationship building.

## 4. The AI Search Landscape in Malaysia — 2026 Overview

### 4.1 Adoption Trends

AI-powered search is no longer a niche technology in Malaysia. As of 2026:

- Google AI Overviews appear for an estimated 30–40% of all Google searches in Malaysia — covering informational, local, and product-related queries
- ChatGPT has approximately 2–3 million monthly active users in Malaysia, with search being one of the most-used features
- Perplexity AI is growing at approximately 40% quarter-on-quarter in Southeast Asia, with Malaysia being one of the top 5 markets in the region
- Microsoft Bing Copilot is embedded in Windows 11 devices and Microsoft Edge — reaching corporate users and educational institutions across Malaysia

### 4.2 Query Types Most Affected by AI Search in Malaysia

Not all search queries are equally affected by AI search. The following query types are most likely to generate AI Overviews or AI-synthesised answers:

| Query Type | Example (Malaysian Context) | AI Impact Level |
|---|---|---|
| Local business discovery | "Best nasi lemak in JB" / "Kedai makan best dekat Johor Bahru" | Very High |
| Accommodation search | "Homestay dekat LEGOLAND Malaysia" / "Budget hotel near JB city centre" | Very High |
| How-to and informational | "How to register a company in Malaysia" / "Cara buat SSM registration" | High |
| Product comparisons | "Best budget smartphone in Malaysia 2026" | High |
| Service provider selection | "Lawyer for divorce case in KL" / "Cleaning service Johor Bahru" | Medium-High |
| News and current events | "Malaysia Budget 2026 summary" | Medium |
| Technical queries | "How to set up Google Ads campaign" | Medium |

### 4.3 Language Considerations for Malaysian AI Search

Malaysia is a multilingual country, and this directly impacts AI search strategy. AI systems process queries and content in all major languages used by Malaysian internet users:

- **Bahasa Malaysia (BM):** Used in government, education, and general consumer contexts. AI systems can read BM content but may have less training data in BM than in English for certain technical topics.
- **English:** The dominant language for business and professional content. Most AI systems have the strongest comprehension and generation capability in English. Business content targeting AI should be written primarily in English, with BM equivalents where appropriate.
- **Mandarin (中文):** Used by a significant portion of Malaysia's Chinese-speaking population (~23% of total population). ChatGPT and other LLMs handle Simplified Chinese well. For businesses targeting Chinese-speaking customers, Mandarin content is valuable.
- **Tamil:** Relevant for Indian-Malaysian communities; AI comprehension in Tamil is improving but remains behind English and Mandarin.

**Strategic recommendation for Malaysian SMEs:** Publish primary content in **English**, with a BM version where feasible. This maximises AI comprehension and covers the broadest possible query spectrum.
