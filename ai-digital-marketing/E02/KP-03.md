## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees will be able to:

1. Use manual prompting techniques across multiple AI search platforms to check whether a business is being cited and how it is described.
2. Set up a structured brand mention monitoring workflow using a combination of manual checks and third-party tools appropriate for Malaysian SME budgets.
3. Develop a consistent publishing strategy across AI-indexed platforms — including a business website, Google Business Profile, and industry directories — to build long-term AI search authority.
4. Define and measure GEO success metrics including share of AI results and citation frequency, and present these findings in a simple monthly report format.
5. Apply a continuous improvement cycle to a real Malaysian SME's GEO programme based on monitoring data.

## 1. Checking AI Mentions — Manual Prompting Techniques

### 1.1 Why Manual Prompting is the Starting Point

Before investing in paid monitoring tools, every digital marketer should master **manual prompting** — the practice of directly querying AI search platforms with relevant questions and systematically recording whether and how a business appears in the generated answers.

Manual prompting is valuable because:

- It is free and can be done immediately using existing AI platform accounts
- It provides first-hand insight into how AI systems currently describe your business — including any inaccuracies or missing information
- It reveals which competitors are being cited for the same queries — a form of competitive intelligence
- It tests the effectiveness of GEO improvements: if you restructured content last week, prompting today shows whether AI systems have updated their knowledge

### 1.2 Manual Prompting — The QRAT Framework

A structured approach to manual prompting prevents inconsistency and makes results comparable over time. Use the **QRAT Framework**:

| Letter | Stands for | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Q** | Query Set | A standardised list of 10–20 queries that represent how your target customers ask about your business category |
| **R** | Record | Systematically record the full AI-generated answer, the date, the platform, and whether your business is cited |
| **A** | Analyse | Compare results across platforms and over time to identify trends and gaps |
| **T** | Take Action | Translate findings into specific content or technical improvements |

### 1.3 Building a Query Set for Malaysian Businesses

The query set is the backbone of your monitoring programme. It should reflect the actual language that your target customers use when asking AI systems for recommendations.

**Query Set Example — Makan Moments Cafe, Johor Bahru:**

| Query # | Query Text | Platform to Test | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | "Best Malaysian café in Johor Bahru" | Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity | Discovery — broad category |
| Q2 | "Where to eat local food in JB near City Square" | ChatGPT, Perplexity | Discovery — location-specific |
| Q3 | "Halal café in Johor Bahru that serves nasi lemak" | Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT | Specific attribute filter |
| Q4 | "Makan Moments Cafe JB — what do they serve?" | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Bing Copilot | Direct brand query |
| Q5 | "Café in JB open for breakfast and dinner" | Google AI Overviews | Hours-specific query |
| Q6 | "Local food recommendations Johor Bahru 2026" | Perplexity, ChatGPT | Temporal + local intent |
| Q7 | "Best kaya toast in Johor Bahru" | ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews | Specific dish query |
| Q8 | "Budget-friendly cafés in Johor Bahru for families" | Perplexity, ChatGPT | Audience-specific query |

**Query Set Example — Southern Homestay, KSL D'Esplanade JB:**

| Query # | Query Text | Platform to Test | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | "Best homestay in Johor Bahru near KSL City Mall" | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews | Discovery — location-specific |
| Q2 | "Serviced apartment JB for Singapore tourists" | ChatGPT, Perplexity | Audience-specific query |
| Q3 | "KSL D'Esplanade homestay review" | Perplexity, Bing Copilot | Direct property query |
| Q4 | "Homestay JB that fits 6 people" | ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews | Capacity-specific query |

| Q6 | "Where to stay in JB for a family of 5 from Singapore" | ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews | Scenario-based query |

### 1.4 Conducting a Manual Prompting Session — Step-by-Step

Follow this procedure for each monitoring session:

**Step 1 — Prepare your recording sheet**
Create a simple spreadsheet with columns: Date, Platform, Query #, Query Text, AI Answer (copy full text), Business Cited? (Yes/No), Position in Answer (first, second, third, not cited), Competitor Names Cited, Notes.

**Step 2 — Use incognito / private browsing mode**
AI search results can be personalised based on your browsing history. Always use incognito mode to simulate the experience of a new user who has no prior relationship with your business.

**Step 3 — Test each query across all relevant platforms**
For each query in your set, test it on Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search, and Perplexity AI. Record the full answer text for each.

**Step 4 — Note competitor citations**
When your business is not cited, record which businesses are cited instead. These are your GEO competitors — the businesses whose content is currently more AI-citable than yours for that query.

**Step 5 — Document inaccuracies**
If an AI system cites your business but describes it incorrectly (wrong address, outdated hours, wrong cuisine type), this is a high-priority finding. Inaccuracies indicate that the AI is drawing on outdated or incorrect source data — requiring a content correction.

**Step 6 — Record the date and log the session**
GEO monitoring is longitudinal — its value comes from comparing results over time. A single snapshot is useful; monthly snapshots over six months reveal trends.

### 1.5 Reading an AI Answer — What to Look For

When reviewing the AI-generated answer for each query, examine the following elements:

| Element | What to Look For | Action if Problematic |
|---|---|---|
| **Citation appearance** | Is your business name mentioned? Is a link to your website provided? | If not cited: identify which content gap is causing the miss and address it |
| **Description accuracy** | Is the business described correctly (location, type, offerings, hours)? | If inaccurate: update source data on Google Business Profile, website, and listing platforms |
| **Citation position** | Is your business the first, second, or third result cited? | First position = most authoritative citation for that query; track position over time |
| **Language used** | What specific phrases does the AI use to describe your business? | These phrases reveal what content the AI extracted — ensure this language appears on your own platforms |
| **Competitor presence** | Which competitors appear when you do not? | Analyse their content to understand what signals they have that you are missing |

## 2. Third-Party Monitoring Tools

### 2.1 Overview of Available Tools

As GEO has grown as a discipline, a category of specialised tools has emerged to automate AI citation monitoring. These tools query AI platforms on your behalf, track mentions over time, and generate reports. The table below summarises the options most relevant for Malaysian digital marketing practitioners:

| Tool | Cost (Approx.) | Key Features | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Profound** | USD 49–199/month | Tracks brand mentions across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini; provides citation share reports | Agencies managing multiple clients |
| **Otterly.ai** | USD 29–99/month | AI mention tracking across major platforms; competitor comparison | SMEs wanting automated monitoring |
| **Brandwatch** | USD 800+/month | Enterprise-grade; includes AI mention tracking as part of broader social listening | Large corporations; not suitable for Malaysian SMEs |
| **AirPR / Onclusive** | Custom pricing | PR-focused; includes AI mention tracking | PR agencies |
| **Manual Prompting (Free)** | RM 0 | No automation; requires 1–2 hours/week | Malaysian SMEs with limited budget |
| **Google Alerts** | Free | Monitors web mentions of a brand name; indirect indicator of AI citation potential | All businesses as a baseline |

> **Panduan untuk PKS Malaysia / Guidance for Malaysian SMEs:** For most Malaysian SMEs in 2026, the cost of dedicated AI monitoring tools is not yet justified given the early stage of AI search adoption in Malaysia. A structured manual prompting programme (2 hours per month) combined with Google Alerts provides sufficient monitoring at zero cost. Paid tools become worthwhile when managing 5+ clients or when the business derives significant revenue from AI-driven customer acquisition.

### 2.2 Google Alerts — Setup and Use

**Google Alerts** is a free service that sends email notifications whenever Google indexes a new web page containing your specified search terms. While it does not directly monitor AI citation, it is a valuable proxy indicator: new web mentions of your business name are exactly the kind of third-party authority signals that AI systems use to build their knowledge of your business.

**Setting up Google Alerts for a Malaysian SME:**

1. Go to google.com/alerts (log in with a Google account)
2. Create alerts for the following term types:
   - Business name (exact match): `"Makan Moments Cafe"`
   - Business name + location: `"Makan Moments Cafe" "Johor Bahru"`
   - Business name + category: `"Southern Homestay" "JB"`
   - Competitor monitoring: `"[Competitor Name]" "Johor Bahru" café`
3. Set alert frequency to "As-it-happens" for brand name mentions; "Once a week" for broader category monitoring
4. Set the result type to "Everything" to capture blogs, news, forums, and directories
5. Set the region to Malaysia for locally relevant results

**Interpreting Google Alert data in the context of GEO:**

| Alert Type | GEO Implication |
|---|---|
| New blog post mentioning your business | Positive signal — a new third-party citation that AI systems may eventually index |
| Review platform update with your business name | Positive signal — review content is frequently cited by AI systems in local search |
| News article mentioning your business | Strong positive signal — news content from credible publishers carries high authority |
| No new alerts for 30+ days | Warning signal — your business is not generating new web mentions; consider outreach to bloggers or PR activity |

### 2.3 Perplexity AI's "Sources" Feature as a Monitoring Tool

An underutilised but powerful free monitoring technique is to use **Perplexity AI's source citations** as a diagnostic tool. Unlike ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews, Perplexity AI always shows numbered citations alongside its generated answers. This makes it uniquely transparent as a monitoring platform.

**Using Perplexity for GEO diagnostics:**

1. Query Perplexity with your target queries (e.g., "best homestay near KSL City JB")
2. Read the generated answer carefully — note whether your business appears
3. If competitors are cited: click on their numbered citation links to see exactly which pages Perplexity used as sources
4. Analyse those competitor pages: What content format are they using? Do they have FAQ sections? Is there a specific blog post or directory listing that is being cited?
5. Use these findings to replicate the effective content structures on your own platforms

This technique provides free, highly actionable competitive intelligence that would otherwise require an expensive monitoring tool.

## 3. Building Authority Through Consistent Publishing

### 3.1 The Authority Flywheel (Roda Terbang Autoriti)

AI search systems build their knowledge of a business through the accumulation of consistent, accurate mentions across many platforms over time. This is not a one-time exercise — it is an ongoing programme of **consistent publishing (penerbitan konsisten)** that progressively increases a business's AI search authority.

The relationship between publishing consistency and AI authority can be visualised as a flywheel:

```
[Publish quality content] → [Content is indexed by AI crawlers] → 
[AI cites your content] → [More users discover your business via AI] → 
[More engagement generates more reviews and mentions] → 
[Authority increases] → [AI cites you more frequently and prominently]
```

Each rotation of the flywheel builds on the previous one. The key is consistency — sporadic publishing produces sporadic results.

### 3.2 Platform Priority for Malaysian SMEs

Not all publishing platforms are equal in terms of their impact on AI citation authority. The following priority framework is based on the platforms that AI search systems most frequently crawl and cite for Malaysian local business queries:

**Tier 1 — Highest AI Citation Impact (Impak Tertinggi)**

| Platform | Why It Matters for AI | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Your own website** | The primary source AI systems use to verify your business identity and extract factual content | Publish detailed About page, FAQ page, Services/Menu page; implement schema markup; create llms.txt |
| **Google Business Profile (GBP)** | Google AI Overviews draws directly from GBP data; it is the most important local data source for Google's AI | Complete all fields: description (750 characters), services, products, hours, photos, Q&A section (add your own questions and answer them) |
| **Airbnb / Booking.com** (for accommodation) | AI systems crawl major OTA platforms for accommodation data | Write a detailed, keyword-rich property description (400+ words); ensure all amenities are checked; respond to all reviews |

**Tier 2 — High AI Citation Impact**

| Platform | Why It Matters for AI | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Business Page** | AI systems include Facebook content in their training data and live browsing; the About section is particularly important | Complete the About section with full address, hours, category, and description; publish at least 2 posts per week |
| **Industry directories** | Malaysian directories (e.g., SME Corp Malaysia, Tourism Johor, Halal Malaysia directory, Grab Maps) provide authoritative third-party mentions | List business on all relevant directories; ensure NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is identical to other platforms |
| **Review platforms** | Google Reviews, Tripadvisor, Agoda — AI systems extract review summaries and use review volume as an authority signal | Actively request reviews from satisfied customers; respond professionally to all reviews (positive and negative) |

**Tier 3 — Supporting AI Citation Impact**

| Platform | Why It Matters for AI | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram / TikTok** | AI systems have limited ability to read image/video content; captions and profile descriptions are indexed | Write descriptive captions that include business name, location, and relevant keywords; keep profile bio factual and complete |
| **YouTube** | Video descriptions are indexed by AI crawlers | Include business name, address, and category in video descriptions; create a channel About section |
| **WhatsApp Business** | Not directly crawled by AI | Use for direct customer service; WhatsApp reviews can sometimes appear on Google |

### 3.3 Google Business Profile — Full Optimisation Guide for Malaysian SMEs

The **Google Business Profile (GBP)** is the single most important platform for local AI search visibility in Malaysia, given Google's ~95% search market share. A fully optimised GBP significantly increases the probability of appearing in Google AI Overviews for local queries.

**Complete GBP optimisation checklist:**

| Section | What to Complete | GEO Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Business Name** | Use your exact legal/trading name; do not add keyword stuffing (e.g., "Makan Moments Cafe — Best Nasi Lemak JB" is against Google policy) | NAP consistency |
| **Category** | Select the most specific primary category (e.g., "Malaysian Restaurant" not just "Restaurant"); add up to 9 secondary categories | Enables category-specific AI queries |
| **Description** | Write the full 750-character description; use definition-first format; include cuisine type, location landmarks, and one key differentiator | AI Overview source material |
| **Services / Menu** | Add all menu items or services with descriptions and prices in RM | AI can answer "Does [business] serve [dish]?" |
| **Hours** | Complete all 7 days; update for public holidays using the "Special Hours" feature | AI can answer "Is [business] open on [day]?" |
| **Photos** | Add 20+ high-quality photos covering food/products, interior, exterior, team, menu board | Photo content is indexed; photo count is a quality signal |
| **Q&A Section** | Proactively add your own questions and answer them (you can ask and answer as the business owner) | FAQ content is directly extracted by Google AI Overviews |
| **Products/Services** | List each product or service with name, price, and description | Enables product-specific AI queries |
| **Website link** | Link to your main website (must be functioning and consistent with GBP name/address) | Cross-platform verification |
| **Respond to reviews** | Respond to every review within 7 days; use the customer's first name; thank them for specific feedback | Review engagement signals quality and trustworthiness |

**Case Study — Southern Homestay GBP Optimisation:**

Before optimisation, Southern Homestay's GBP description read: *"Cosy homestay in JB. Good location. Contact us for bookings."* (38 characters used of 750 available).

After optimisation: *"Southern Homestay at KSL D'Esplanade offers fully furnished 3-bedroom serviced apartment accommodation in central Johor Bahru. Accommodating up to 6 guests, the unit features free WiFi, full kitchen, washing machine, Smart TV, and complimentary covered parking. Located 500 metres from KSL City Mall and 15 minutes from the Johor-Singapore Causeway (Tambak Johor). Ideal for Singapore day-trippers, family holidays, and corporate short-term stays. Self-check-in available. Book via Airbnb, Booking.com, or direct WhatsApp."* (617 characters).

The optimised description contains: property name, building name, unit type, guest capacity, 7 specific amenities, 2 landmark distances, 3 target customer segments, check-in method, and 3 booking channels. Each of these is a data point that AI systems can extract and cite.

### 3.4 Publishing Cadence — A Practical Monthly Plan for Malaysian SMEs

Consistency is more important than volume. A realistic monthly publishing plan for a Malaysian SME managing GEO without a dedicated team:

| Week | Platform | Action | Time Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Website | Publish one FAQ-format blog post or update the FAQ page with 2–3 new questions | 2–3 hours |
| Week 1 | Google Business Profile | Add 3–5 new photos; respond to any new reviews | 30 minutes |
| Week 2 | Google Business Profile | Add one Google Post (news, offer, or event update) | 20 minutes |
| Week 2 | Facebook Business Page | Publish 2 posts (one informational, one promotional) | 1 hour |
| Week 3 | AI Monitoring | Conduct manual prompting session — test 10 queries across 3 platforms; update tracking spreadsheet | 1.5 hours |
| Week 3 | Airbnb/Booking.com | Review and update listing description; respond to any new guest reviews | 45 minutes |
| Week 4 | Website | Update one existing page with new information (e.g., update hours for upcoming public holiday, add new menu item) | 30 minutes |
| Week 4 | Directory check | Verify NAP consistency on 2–3 directories; submit to any new relevant directory | 1 hour |

**Total estimated time per month: 7–9 hours**

This is a realistic, sustainable cadence for a Malaysian SME owner or a marketing executive managing one business.

## 4. Measuring GEO Success

### 4.1 GEO Success Metrics (Metrik Kejayaan GEO)

Unlike traditional SEO — where success is measured by ranking positions and organic traffic — GEO requires a different set of metrics. The following are the primary indicators of GEO performance:

**Primary Metrics (Metrik Utama):**

| Metric | Definition | How to Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Citation Frequency (Kekerapan Petikan)** | The number of times your business is cited across AI platforms in a given month, measured across your standard query set | Manual prompting sessions; record "cited" vs "not cited" for each query × platform combination |
| **Share of AI Voice (Bahagian Suara AI)** | The percentage of relevant queries (from your query set) for which your business is cited, relative to total queries tested | (Number of queries where business is cited) ÷ (Total queries tested) × 100% |
| **Citation Position (Kedudukan Petikan)** | Whether your business appears as the first, second, or third citation in the AI's answer | Record position for each citation during manual prompting sessions |
| **Description Accuracy (Ketepatan Huraian)** | Whether the AI describes your business correctly (correct location, hours, offerings) | Manual review of AI-generated descriptions during prompting sessions |

**Secondary Metrics (Metrik Sokongan):**

| Metric | Definition | How to Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile Views** | The number of times your GBP listing was viewed — a proxy for local AI search visibility | GBP Insights dashboard (free) |
| **Website Organic Traffic from AI Referrals** | Traffic arriving at your website from AI platform links | Google Analytics 4 — Acquisition > Traffic Acquisition; look for referrals from perplexity.ai, chatgpt.com, bing.com |
| **Third-Party Mention Count** | The number of new web mentions of your business name from third-party sources per month | Google Alerts email count |
| **Review Volume and Rating** | Total Google reviews and average rating — both are authority signals for AI | GBP Insights; track monthly |

### 4.3 Interpreting GEO Data — The Three Progress Signals

When reviewing monthly GEO data, look for three types of progress signals:

**Signal 1 — Citation Rate Improvement**
If Share of AI Voice increases from one month to the next (e.g., 25% → 37.5%), GEO efforts are working. Identify which specific actions correlated with the improvement and continue doing them.

**Signal 2 — Description Quality Improvement**
If AI systems begin describing your business more accurately and in more detail (e.g., previously said "café in JB"; now says "halal Malaysian café serving nasi lemak in Johor Bahru, open daily 8 AM–10 PM"), this indicates that your structured content and schema markup have been indexed and processed.

**Signal 3 — Competitor Displacement**
If a competitor that was previously being cited instead of you begins appearing less frequently while your citation rate increases, this confirms that your relative authority is improving for those queries.

### 4.4 Setting Realistic GEO Targets for Malaysian SMEs

GEO is a medium-term strategy. The following benchmark targets are realistic for a Malaysian SME that begins a structured GEO programme from zero:

| Timeframe | Realistic Target | Key Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 (Baseline) | 0–15% Share of AI Voice | Establish baseline; complete content audit; no meaningful citations expected yet |
| Month 2–3 | 15–30% Share of AI Voice | FAQ page published; schema markup implemented; GBP fully optimised; first citations begin appearing |
| Month 4–6 | 30–50% Share of AI Voice | llms.txt implemented; 2–3 third-party mentions obtained; consistent publishing rhythm established |
| Month 6–12 | 50–70% Share of AI Voice | Multiple directory listings; regular blog/FAQ content updates; established as local authority for primary category |

> **Nota Penting:** These targets assume 7–9 hours per month of consistent GEO work across all recommended platforms. Results will vary based on competition level (a unique business in a less competitive niche will achieve results faster than a café competing in a market with 500 other JB cafés), content quality, and the rate at which AI platforms update their training data.

## 5. The GEO Continuous Improvement Cycle

### 5.1 The Plan-Do-Check-Act Framework Applied to GEO

GEO is not a project with a defined end date — it is an ongoing operational practice. The most effective approach applies the **Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA / Rancang-Buat-Semak-Tindak)** cycle to GEO management:

**PLAN (Rancang):**
- Define your query set (10–20 queries per business)
- Identify your target platforms (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity)
- Set monthly targets for Share of AI Voice and Citation Frequency
- Prioritise content gaps to address in the coming month

**DO (Buat):**
- Publish FAQ content, update GBP, implement schema markup
- Engage with bloggers and directories for third-party mentions
- Respond to customer reviews on all platforms
- Maintain consistent posting cadence on Google Business Profile and social media

**CHECK (Semak):**
- Conduct monthly manual prompting sessions
- Update the GEO tracking spreadsheet with new data
- Compare results against previous month and against targets
- Identify which actions produced measurable improvements

**ACT (Tindak):**
- Double down on strategies that improved citation rate
- Discontinue or modify strategies that showed no effect
- Investigate competitor citation sources for new ideas
- Adjust targets based on actual performance data

### 5.2 Case Study — Full GEO Programme: Southern Homestay at KSL D'Esplanade

The following case study illustrates a complete six-month GEO programme for Southern Homestay. This is a realistic scenario based on the GEO techniques covered across KP-01, KP-02, and KP-03.

**Month 1 — Baseline and Foundation:**
- Baseline manual prompting: tested 16 queries across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews
- Result: Business cited in 0 of 16 queries (0% Share of AI Voice)
- Findings: GBP description had only 38 characters; no website FAQ; Airbnb listing description was 80 words; no schema markup; no third-party web mentions found
- Actions taken: Updated GBP description to 617 characters; updated Airbnb listing to 420 words; began building a dedicated website

**Month 2 — Content and Technical Implementation:**
- Website launched with: About page (definition-first, 400 words), FAQ page (12 questions), Organisation schema, LodgingBusiness schema, llms.txt
- GBP Q&A section populated with 8 questions and answers
- First monitoring session: 3 of 16 queries cited the business (19% Share of AI Voice)
- Competitor analysis: identified that top-cited competitor had 3 travel blog mentions

**Month 3 — Authority Building:**
- Reached out to 2 JB travel bloggers; one published a review article mentioning "Southern Homestay KSL D'Esplanade"
- Submitted to Tourism Johor directory and AirBnB Experiences (to increase platform presence)
- Continued weekly GBP posts (every Wednesday)
- Monitoring session: 5 of 16 queries cited the business (31% Share of AI Voice)
- AI description accuracy improved: Perplexity now correctly stated "3-bedroom serviced apartment accommodating up to 6 guests"

**Month 4–6 — Consolidation and Growth:**
- Published 3 new FAQ blog posts targeting high-priority uncited queries
- Second blogger review published
- Monthly GBP photo updates (5 new photos per month)
- Month 6 monitoring: 9 of 16 queries cited the business (56% Share of AI Voice)
- Citation position improved: now appearing as first or second citation for 6 of the 9 cited queries
- Google Business Profile views increased from 320/month (Month 1) to 890/month (Month 6)

> **Kesimpulan / Key Takeaway:** Sustainable GEO improvement requires patience and consistency. Southern Homestay's 56% Share of AI Voice in Month 6 was achieved through a combination of technical implementation (schema, llms.txt), content creation (FAQ, About page), and authority building (blogger reviews, directory listings) — not any single tactic alone.
