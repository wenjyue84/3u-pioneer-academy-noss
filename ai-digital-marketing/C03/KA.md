### Section B

**B1** (8 marks — 2 marks per component):
1. **Subject** — the main element to be depicted. Example: "A bottle of organic skincare serum on a marble surface."
2. **Style modifier** — artistic treatment or mood. Example: "Clean, minimalist product photography, soft natural lighting."
3. **Context/setting** — environment or background. Example: "In a spa setting with tropical greenery, morning sunlight."
4. **Negative prompt** — elements to exclude. Example: "No text overlays, no cluttered backgrounds, no synthetic-looking skin."

**B2** (8 marks):
Four elements (4 marks — 1 each): (1) Logo usage rules (clear space, minimum size, approved versions); (2) Colour palette (primary, secondary, accent — with Hex/RGB codes); (3) Typography (primary and secondary typefaces, sizes, weights); (4) Imagery style (photography direction, illustration style, AI image tone).
Consistency rationale (4 marks): Brand recognition builds trust over repeated exposure — Malaysian SME audiences across Shopee, Instagram, and TikTok recognise the brand faster when visual elements are consistent (2m). Inconsistency signals unprofessionalism and reduces conversion rate from social media to e-commerce (2m).

**B3** (8 marks — 2–3 marks each point):
1. **Ownership uncertainty** — Malaysian Copyright Act 1987 does not explicitly assign ownership of AI-generated works; the marketer must check the platform's terms of service (Midjourney's licence grants commercial use to paid subscribers but the legal position under Malaysian law remains untested) (3m).
2. **Third-party IP in training data** — the generated image may incorporate styles or compositions from copyrighted artworks used in the model's training dataset; risk of inadvertent reproduction must be assessed (2m).
3. **Client disclosure** — the marketer should disclose to the client that the image is AI-generated so the client can make an informed decision about commercial risk and insurance coverage (3m).

**B4** (8 marks — approximately 2–3 marks per tool):

| Tool | Generation Method | Marketing Strength | Limitation |
|------|------------------|--------------------|------------|
| DALL-E 3 | Latent diffusion via ChatGPT / API | Excellent prompt adherence; text in images rendered accurately | Monthly usage cap on free tier; less artistic control than Midjourney |
| Adobe Firefly | Diffusion model trained on licensed Adobe Stock images | Commercially safe — images cleared for commercial use | Sometimes overly polished; less photorealistic for lifestyle shots |
| Lovart AI | Multimodal image generation with product upload support | Strong for product-centric Malaysian e-commerce; accepts product photo as input | Less widely known; smaller style library |

**B5** (8 marks — approximately 2–3 marks per principle):
- **Visual hierarchy** (3m): Organise elements by size, colour, and position so the viewer's eye travels from most to least important. For an Instagram promo banner: product image largest and centred, sale price bold and high-contrast, brand logo smallest at bottom corner.
- **Contrast** (2m): Use opposing tones (light text on dark background or vice versa) so key information is legible on mobile screens. Avoid low-contrast text on busy AI-generated backgrounds.
- **White space** (3m): Leave breathing room around key elements — do not fill every pixel. White space (or negative space using brand colour) reduces visual noise and makes the CTA stand out, increasing click-through rate.

### Section C

**C1** (12 marks):

a) Five steps (5 marks — 1 each):
1. Research the target audience and competitor visual benchmarks.
2. Define the brand name, tagline, and core values to anchor visual direction.
3. Select a colour palette (2–3 colours) with Hex codes that reflects the brand personality and is culturally appropriate for the Malaysian halal market (earthy tones, soft greens, or clean whites signal purity).
4. Choose primary and secondary fonts (clean, legible on mobile).
5. Create a simple Brand Kit in Canva with logo placeholder, colours, and fonts before generating any AI images.

b) Prompt + component identification (7 marks):
Example prompt: "A young Malaysian Malay woman in a modest cream baju kurung applying facial serum in a bright minimalist bathroom, natural morning sunlight, soft bokeh background with tropical plant, product bottle prominently displayed, clean editorial photography style, warm neutral tones, no jewellery, no text, photorealistic." (3m for completeness of prompt)
Components identified (4m): Subject (woman applying serum), Setting (minimalist bathroom, morning light), Style modifier (clean editorial photography, warm neutral tones), Negative prompt (no jewellery, no text).

**C2** (14 marks):

a) Platform sizes (4 marks — 1 each):

| Platform | Dimensions | Aspect Ratio |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Instagram Feed post | 1080 × 1080 px | 1:1 |
| Instagram Story | 1080 × 1920 px | 9:16 |
| Shopee product banner | 800 × 800 px (minimum) | 1:1 |
| Lazada hero banner | 1200 × 600 px | 2:1 |

b) Colour psychology (4 marks): Dark green in Malay cultural context represents Islam, nature, and trust — widely used in official branding and evokes sincerity (2m). Gold represents prestige, celebration, and wealth — strongly associated with Hari Raya gift-giving and festive generosity in Malaysian culture (1m). The combination is appropriate and resonant for a Raya campaign targeting the Malay-Muslim market (1m).

c) Canva workflow (6 marks — 1 each step):
1. Open Canva, select correct template size for each platform (use Custom Size if template not available).
2. Apply Brand Kit colours (#2E7D32 and #F9A825) and brand fonts to the template.
3. Use Canva Magic Studio or Text to Image to generate Raya-themed background visuals.
4. Place product images (baju kurung/baju melayu photography) as foreground elements with transparent or green background removal.
5. Add sale text, pricing, and CTA using high-contrast gold text on green background.
6. Export: Instagram/Story — PNG at 72 DPI for screen; Shopee/Lazada — JPG at 72 DPI, under 2MB per platform upload limit.

**C3** (14 marks):

a) What went wrong + two techniques (4 marks):
The trainee wrote a vague subject prompt without specifying cultural, religious, or modesty requirements — the model generated a generic Western lifestyle image (2m).
Techniques that should have been used: (1) **Explicit negative prompts** — "no alcohol, no sleeveless clothing, no non-halal food items, no cross-gender touching" (1m); (2) **Cultural style modifiers** — "Malaysian Muslim lifestyle, modest attire, hijab, halal setting" specified in the positive prompt (1m).

b) Revised prompt (4 marks):
Negative prompt: "no alcohol, no wine, no sleeveless tops, no revealing clothing, no non-halal symbols, no Western party setting."
Revised positive prompt: "A young Malaysian Malay woman wearing hijab and modest long-sleeved top, smiling while holding a bottle of halal herbal supplement drink, bright modern kitchen background, natural morning light, clean product photography style, warm tones, no text overlays, photorealistic." (2m for negative prompt, 2m for revised positive prompt)

c) Brand appropriateness evaluation checklist (6 marks — 1–1.5 marks each):
1. **Halal/cultural compliance check** — verify no haram elements (alcohol, pork symbols, immodest dress) are visible in the image.
2. **Racial representation review** — confirm the image accurately and respectfully represents the target audience demographic (Malay, Chinese, Indian, or multicultural as required by brief).
3. **Brand guideline conformity** — check logo placement, colour palette match, and font consistency against the brand style guide.
4. **Text legibility** — verify all text overlays are readable at mobile thumbnail size (Instagram grid: 110 × 110 px).
5. **Legal/IP check** — confirm no identifiable real persons, trademarked logos, or third-party IP appear in the AI image.
6. **Client brief alignment** — cross-reference the final image against the original campaign brief objectives and key message before submission.

**Q23.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee exports a social media banner from Canva as a JPG at 300 DPI for an Instagram post. Identify TWO (2) issues with this decision and state the correct export settings. [CoCU C03.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Issue 1: 300 DPI is the print standard — for digital screen display, Instagram renders all images at 72 DPI regardless of the file's embedded DPI metadata. Exporting at 300 DPI creates a larger file size with no visible quality benefit on screen and risks exceeding Instagram's 8MB file upload limit. Issue 2: JPG does not support transparency — if the design contains a transparent background or logo overlay layer, JPG will flatten it with a white fill, destroying the design intent. Correct export settings: PNG format (preserves transparency), 72 DPI, 1080 × 1080 px for feed post or 1080 × 1920 px for Story. If file size is a concern, use JPG at 72 DPI only for opaque, fully-coloured backgrounds with no transparency required.

### Bahagian C / Section C — Soalan Senario Lanjutan / Extended Scenario Questions (0 markah / marks)

*Soalan senario lanjutan untuk penilaian kefahaman mendalam tahap 3. Jawapan contoh menunjukkan kedalaman teknikal yang diperlukan. / Extended scenario questions for in-depth Level 3 competency assessment. Sample answers demonstrate required technical depth.*

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain how a latent diffusion model generates an image from a text prompt. Why does prompt specificity affect output quality? [CoCU C03.1.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* A latent diffusion model operates in a compressed latent space rather than pixel space. When a text prompt is submitted, the model's text encoder (e.g., CLIP) converts the words into numerical embeddings representing semantic meaning. The model then starts with random noise in latent space and iteratively denoises it over multiple steps, guided by the text embeddings toward an image that statistically matches the prompt's described content. Each denoising step refines the image — moving it closer to what the training data associated with similar text. Prompt specificity affects output quality because vague prompts (e.g., "a drink") leave the model with a high-entropy distribution across many possible correct images, resulting in generic or unexpected outputs. Specific prompts (e.g., "a glass of teh tarik with condensed milk swirl, traditional Malaysian kopitiam table, warm evening light, commercial food photography") constrain the latent space path toward a narrow, well-defined target, producing a coherent and usable marketing image. Subject, style, environment, and negative constraints each reduce entropy at different layers of the denoising process.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's social media visuals across Instagram, TikTok, Shopee, and Lazada use four different shades of green and two different logo versions. Describe how you would audit and correct this brand consistency problem. [CoCU C03.2.4 / C03.2.5]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Conduct a brand consistency audit: screenshot all live assets across all four platforms and place them side-by-side in a single Canva or PowerPoint document. Use an eyedropper tool to extract the exact Hex code of each green shade used — compare against the official brand colour in the style guide. Identify which platforms are non-compliant. Then audit logo versions: check whether the correct primary logo (with or without tagline, correct proportions) is used consistently. Produce a single-page Brand Visual Standards sheet specifying: (1) the single official primary green Hex code; (2) approved logo versions (primary, secondary, icon-only) with minimum size rules; (3) platform template files locked to the correct colours. Update all platform profile images, highlights covers, and template files to use the corrected assets. Train the content team on the Canva Brand Kit feature to enforce the standards automatically on future designs. Document the correction in the project changelog.

**Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe the full workflow for creating a five-post Hari Raya social media series using AI visual tools, from brief intake to client delivery. [CoCU C03.2.5 / C03.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Step 1 — Brief intake: confirm the campaign theme, product, target audience, key message, and platform. Collect all existing brand assets (logo, official Hex codes, approved fonts). Step 2 — Mood board: generate 6–8 reference images using AI tools (Midjourney or Adobe Firefly) to propose visual direction (colour treatment, style, photography vs illustration). Present to client and get written sign-off. Step 3 — Prompt engineering: write five distinct subject prompts sharing a common style modifier template: "Hari Raya celebration, Malaysian Muslim family, pastel ketupat motif, warm golden hour light, clean minimal background, commercial photography." Add consistent negative prompts across all five. Step 4 — Generation and curation: generate 3–5 variations per post. Select the best for each slot, ensuring visual variety across the five posts (different compositions, not just different subjects). Step 5 — Canva production: import selected AI-generated background images into Canva. Apply Brand Kit. Add text overlays, CTA, and product imagery. Resize each design to all required platform dimensions (1080×1080, 1080×1920). Step 6 — Internal review: check cultural appropriateness, brand compliance, text legibility at thumbnail size. Step 7 — Client delivery: export all files as PNG (72 DPI), ZIP and deliver with a platform-labelled naming convention (e.g., Raya2026_IG-Feed_Post1.png). Include brief usage notes.

**Q29.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client demands that you use AI to replicate the visual style of a competitor's highly successful campaign exactly. How do you handle this situation professionally and legally? [CoCU C03.1.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Decline the request as stated. Explain to the client that replicating a competitor's campaign style constitutes potential copyright infringement under the Copyright Act 1987 (artistic works including layout, colour treatment, and composition may be protected) and could amount to passing off under common law if consumers are likely to be confused. The reputational and legal risk to the client's business outweighs any short-term marketing advantage. Instead, propose a competitive analysis approach: study why the competitor's campaign was effective — identify the underlying principles (colour psychology, hook-based structure, emotional triggers) without copying the specific creative execution. Use these insights to brief an original creative direction that achieves the same strategic goal. Present the client with a mood board of original AI-generated concepts that are inspired by the competitive landscape but clearly distinct in execution. Document the client's original request and your professional advice in writing for the project file.

**Q30.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain how colour psychology differs across Malaysian ethnic communities (Malay, Chinese, Indian) and how a digital marketer should account for this in a multicultural campaign. [CoCU C03.2.4]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Malaysian multicultural colour psychology varies significantly by community and occasion. For the Malay community: green is the colour of Islam and national identity — appropriate for everyday and Raya campaigns; white signifies cleanliness and modesty; avoid black in celebratory contexts (associated with mourning). For the Chinese community: red and gold signal fortune, luck, and celebration — essential for CNY; white and black are mourning colours, never used in CNY or wedding campaigns; pink is accepted in modern luxury branding. For the Indian Tamil community: vibrant jewel tones (orange, yellow, red, purple) signal festivity and are central to Deepavali and wedding campaigns; white is associated with mourning; gold is auspicious. For a multicultural campaign (e.g., a national brand running a single Merdeka Day campaign): use the shared neutral Malaysian palette — red, blue, and yellow from the Jalur Gemilang are culturally safe across all communities; avoid any single-community festival palette that may exclude others. A sophisticated approach involves A/B testing: generate parallel creative sets with community-specific colour treatments and deploy via targeted digital advertising to the appropriate demographic segments, rather than forcing a single visual to serve all communities equally.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] What are the file format differences between PNG, JPG, and WebP? Recommend the correct format for three different digital marketing use cases and justify each. [CoCU C03.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* PNG: lossless compression; supports transparency (alpha channel); larger file size than JPG; best for graphics with sharp edges, logos, and overlays. JPG: lossy compression; no transparency; smaller file size; best for photographic content with continuous tone gradients. WebP: Google-developed format; supports both lossy and lossless compression plus transparency; 25–35% smaller than PNG/JPG at comparable quality; increasingly supported by modern browsers and social platforms. Three use cases: (1) Brand logo overlay on a promotional banner → PNG: transparency is required to place the logo cleanly over any background without a white box; file size is acceptable for a single logo element. (2) Lifestyle product photograph for a Facebook advertisement → JPG: photographic content with no transparency requirement; JPG's lossy compression keeps file size under Facebook's 8MB ad image limit without perceptible quality loss at 1200×628 px. (3) Hero image on a landing page → WebP: page load speed directly affects Google SEO ranking; WebP's smaller file size reduces page weight and improves Core Web Vitals (Largest Contentful Paint score), giving the website a ranking advantage with no visible quality trade-off on modern mobile browsers.

**Q32.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client's Instagram engagement rate dropped 40% after switching from photography to AI-generated images. Analyse possible causes and recommend a hybrid visual strategy. [CoCU C03.1.2 / C03.3.7]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Possible causes of the engagement drop: (1) Uncanny valley effect — AI-generated human faces and hands often appear subtly wrong (extra fingers, unnatural skin texture, asymmetrical features), which audiences subconsciously distrust, reducing emotional connection and save/share behaviour. (2) Loss of brand authenticity — Malaysian social media audiences, particularly for food and lifestyle brands, strongly prefer "real" content: genuine product photos, real customer faces, and authentic settings build trust that AI images cannot replicate. (3) Visual repetitiveness — AI tools default to certain aesthetic styles (overly smooth, hyper-saturated, perfect lighting) that can look identical across different brands, reducing distinctiveness. (4) Algorithm signal — Instagram's algorithm may demote content that receives lower early engagement, creating a compounding effect. Hybrid visual strategy: use AI-generated images for background elements, decorative patterns, and abstract campaign visuals; use real photography for product hero shots, before-and-after demonstrations, and human testimonials. Combine both in a single post: AI-generated background + real product photo overlay. A/B test the hybrid format against pure AI to measure engagement recovery. Invest AI tool savings into one professional photoshoot per quarter to maintain a library of authentic anchor images.

**Q33.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Describe how you would use Canva's AI features to produce a complete brand identity package for a new Malaysian SME. What are the limitations of using AI alone for branding? [CoCU C03.2.6]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Canva AI workflow for SME brand identity: Step 1 — Use Canva's Magic Design or Brand Hub to input the business name and industry; Canva generates colour palette suggestions and font pairings based on the industry aesthetic. Step 2 — Use Canva Text to Image to generate logo concept explorations (note: not for final logo — see limitation below). Step 3 — Use Magic Write to generate tagline options. Step 4 — Assemble all approved elements into the Canva Brand Kit: upload finalised logo, set Hex colours, set fonts. Step 5 — Apply Brand Kit to produce the full package: business card, letterhead, Instagram profile template, Facebook cover, and email signature — all auto-applying brand colours and fonts. Limitations of AI-only branding: (1) Logo uniqueness — AI-generated logos often resemble existing logos in the training data, creating trademark conflict risk; a professional logo requires trademark screening that AI cannot perform. (2) Strategic depth — AI does not understand the business's competitive positioning, customer psychology, or long-term brand story; a brand identity without strategic intent produces generic results. (3) Legal ownership uncertainty — trademark registration in Malaysia (via MyIPO) requires a demonstrably unique mark; AI-generated designs of uncertain originality may face rejection. Recommendation: use Canva AI for layout production and template generation; engage a human designer for the primary logo and core brand mark to ensure legal registrability.

**Q34.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the difference between CMYK and RGB colour modes. A trainee applies the wrong colour mode to a client's print brochure. What is the likely outcome and how should it be corrected? [CoCU C03.3.9]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is an additive colour model used for light-emitting screens — colours are created by mixing light, with white being the combination of all colours and black being the absence of light. RGB has a wider colour gamut than print can reproduce. CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key/Black) is a subtractive colour model used for print — ink absorbs light, and colours are created by combining four ink layers. The CMYK gamut is smaller than RGB, meaning some vivid screen colours cannot be exactly reproduced in print. If a trainee designs a brochure in RGB mode and sends it to a commercial printer: the printer's RIP software will convert the RGB file to CMYK automatically, but this conversion causes colour shift — vibrant social media blues may print as dull navy, bright oranges may become muddy brown, and neon accents lose their intensity entirely. The client's brand colours may print incorrectly. Correction procedure: in Canva or Adobe Express, export the file at 300 DPI (print standard) and specify CMYK output if the tool supports it (Canva's paid tier offers CMYK PDF export). Alternatively, convert the RGB design in Adobe Acrobat or a professional printer's prepress workflow. Always request a physical proof print from the printer before the full print run, especially for brand-critical colours. For critical brand colours, specify the Pantone equivalent in the design brief so the printer can match by eye.

**Q35.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A trainee posts an AI-generated image on a client's Shopee listing and the client receives a takedown notice claiming the image resembles a competitor's registered product visual. Describe the steps to resolve this situation. [CoCU C03.1.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Immediate action: remove the contested image from the Shopee listing immediately to halt ongoing infringement and prevent escalating penalties. Do not wait for legal confirmation before removing. Notify the client in writing the same day, explaining the situation clearly and without attribution of blame. Investigation: review the original AI generation prompt and tool used; examine the competitor's registered visual carefully to assess the degree of similarity — determine whether the resemblance is coincidental (common style elements) or substantive (specific composition, colour, and subject combination). If substantive similarity exists, the takedown notice is likely valid. Documentation: preserve all evidence — original prompt, AI tool session, generated image, client approval email, and the competitor's IP registration number cited in the notice. Response to takedown: consult the client's legal adviser or a Malaysian IP attorney regarding the Copyright Act 1987 and whether a formal response to the notice is required. If the notice is from Shopee's IP protection programme, follow Shopee's Counter Notice procedure only if genuinely confident the image does not infringe. Replacement image: generate a new, clearly distinct product image using a substantially different prompt direction; conduct a reverse image search (Google Lens) on the new output before publishing to verify no similarity to known competitor visuals. Process improvement: implement a pre-publish AI image clearance checklist for all future client content.
