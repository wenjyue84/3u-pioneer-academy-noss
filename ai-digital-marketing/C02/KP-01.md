## Objektif Pembelajaran / Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this Information Sheet, trainees will be able to:
1. Explain how Large Language Models (LLMs) generate written content and describe their principal limitations.
2. Categorise digital marketing content by type, purpose, platform, and AI suitability.
3. Apply the DEEP workflow (Draft, Edit, Evaluate, Publish) to produce AI-assisted content that meets professional quality standards.
4. Identify and prevent AI hallucinations through a structured fact-checking procedure.
5. Apply Malaysian copyright law and MCMC content guidelines to AI-generated content for commercial use.

---

## 1. What is AI Content Generation? (Penjanaan Kandungan AI)

### 1.1 The Content Creation Challenge

Consistent, high-quality content creation is one of the most demanding requirements of modern digital marketing. A typical Malaysian SME running an active digital presence may need:

- 20–30 social media posts per month across 2–3 platforms
- 2–4 blog articles per month for SEO
- Weekly email newsletters
- Product descriptions, WhatsApp broadcast messages, and ad copy on demand

Producing this volume at professional quality using purely manual effort is time-intensive and costly. **AI content generation (penjanaan kandungan AI)** tools — powered by Large Language Models — dramatically accelerate the drafting phase, enabling a skilled digital marketer to produce, review, and publish quality content at scale.

### 1.2 What AI Content Generation Is (and Is Not)

| It Is | It Is Not |
|---|---|
| A fast-draft generation tool | A replacement for human creativity and judgment |
| Capable of producing multiple variants rapidly | A source of verified facts or current data |
| Useful for structure, flow, and language | A substitute for local cultural knowledge |
| Excellent for overcoming writer's block | A tool that understands your specific brand |
| A productivity multiplier for skilled marketers | A tool that can be used without editorial oversight |

## 2. How Large Language Models Work (Cara LLM Berfungsi)

### 2.1 Tokenisation (Pempecahan Token)

When you enter text into an AI writing tool, the system first breaks your input into **tokens (token)** — units of text that typically represent a word or part of a word. For example, the word "marketing" is one token; "pemasaran digital" is two tokens.

The AI processes your prompt as a sequence of tokens and generates a response by predicting which tokens should follow, one at a time, based on statistical patterns learned during training.

> **Key insight:** An LLM does not "understand" language the way a human does. It predicts statistically likely sequences of words. This is why it can produce fluent, professional-sounding text that is nevertheless factually incorrect.

### 2.2 Training (Latihan)

A Large Language Model is trained on a massive corpus of text — estimated at trillions of words — drawn from books, websites, academic papers, social media, and other sources. During training, the model adjusts billions of internal parameters (weights) to minimise the difference between its predicted output and the actual text in the training data.

**Implications for marketers:**
- The model knows patterns from its training data — it does not have access to real-time information unless given a web search tool
- The model may have limited training data in Bahasa Malaysia or Malaysian-specific contexts, making outputs in BM less reliable than English
- Training has a **knowledge cutoff date** — events or product releases after this date are unknown to the model

### 2.3 Inference (Inferens)

When you submit a prompt, the model runs **inference** — using its trained parameters to generate a response token by token. Each token selection is probabilistic; the model picks the most likely next token based on the full context of the prompt and all previously generated tokens. This is why:

- Asking the same prompt twice often yields different answers
- Longer, more detailed prompts produce more accurate and relevant responses
- Specifying format requirements (tables, bullet points, numbered steps) in the prompt produces better-structured output

### 2.4 Key Characteristics and Limitations Summary

| Characteristic | Implication for Marketers |
|---|---|
| Trained on historical data (knowledge cutoff) | Statistics and current events may be outdated — verify before publishing |
| Probabilistic output — not deterministic | Same prompt may yield different results on different attempts |
| No real-world understanding | Cannot verify its own facts — only patterns from training |
| Highly responsive to prompt structure | Invest time in prompt quality; use CRAFT framework |
| Multilingual but quality varies | English output is generally highest quality; BM and Mandarin require additional editing |
| No memory between sessions (by default) | Must re-provide context in each new conversation |

## 3. Types of Digital Marketing Content

### 3.1 Content by Purpose

Digital marketing content is created for four primary purposes. Understanding the purpose determines the appropriate structure, tone, and platform:

| Purpose | Malay Term | Goal | Examples | AI Suitability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | Maklumat | Educate audience; build authority | Blog posts, how-to guides, FAQs, explainer videos | High — AI drafts well; human adds local accuracy |
| **Promotional** | Promosi | Drive awareness of offer; create urgency | Product launch posts, discount announcements, ad copy | High — AI generates variants fast; human ensures accuracy |
| **Engagement** | Penglibatan | Spark interaction; grow community | Polls, questions, relatable memes, challenges | Medium — human cultural knowledge required |
| **Conversion** | Penukaran | Drive a specific action (purchase, signup, enquiry) | Landing pages, email CTAs, WhatsApp scripts, retargeting ads | High — AI follows persuasion formulas |

### 3.2 Content by Format and Platform

| Format | Typical Length | Primary Platform(s) | AI Can Generate | Human Must Add |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blog article | 600–2,000 words | Website / Google | Full draft | Fact-check, local examples, brand voice |
| Social media caption | 50–300 characters | Instagram, Facebook, TikTok | Multiple variants | Emojis, local slang, relevant hashtags |
| Email newsletter | 200–500 words | Email (Mailchimp, Brevo) | Full draft | Personalisation, current offer, correct links |
| Product description | 100–300 words | Shopee, Lazada, website | Multiple variants | Accurate specs, verified certifications |
| WhatsApp broadcast | 50–150 words | WhatsApp Business | Draft | Brand tone, opt-out instruction, urgency |
| TikTok/Reels script | 150–250 words (60 sec) | TikTok, Instagram Reels | Full draft | Hook refinement, timing notes, on-screen text |
| Ad copy (headline + body) | 25–125 characters | Meta Ads, Google Ads | Multiple variants | Compliance check; no false claims |
| Video description | 100–300 words | YouTube | Full draft | Keywords, chapter timestamps, CTAs |

### 3.3 Content Pillar Strategy

A **content pillar (tiang kandungan)** is a core theme around which multiple pieces of content are created. Using 3–5 content pillars ensures consistency and strategic focus:

| Pillar | Example (F&B Business) | Example (Training Centre) |
|---|---|---|
| Education | "How to choose a healthy breakfast" | "5 study techniques for SPM success" |
| Behind-the-scenes | Kitchen prep; ingredient sourcing | Teacher introductions; classroom tours |
| Customer stories | Client testimonials; transformation posts | Student success stories; exam results |
| Promotions | Weekly specials; Raya offers | New intake announcements; early bird discounts |
| Entertainment/Relatable | Funny food moments; staff banter | Exam stress humour; school life content |

## 4. The AI Content Production Workflow: DEEP

### 4.1 Overview of the DEEP Workflow

The **DEEP workflow** is a structured four-stage process for producing AI-assisted content that meets professional marketing standards:

```
D → Draft      Generate AI draft using structured prompt
E → Edit       Human editing for quality, brand voice, and local relevance
E → Evaluate   Fact-check, compliance review, and plagiarism scan
P → Publish    Format, schedule, and post across platforms
```

### 4.2 Stage 1: Draft (Draf)

The drafting stage uses the AI tool to produce a first draft based on a well-constructed CRAFT prompt.

**Draft stage checklist:**
- [ ] Define the content purpose (informational / promotional / engagement / conversion)
- [ ] Identify the target platform and required format/length
- [ ] Write a CRAFT-structured prompt including context, role, action, format, and tone
- [ ] Generate 2–3 variants from the AI and select the best foundation
- [ ] Save the raw AI output before editing (for record-keeping)

**Time required:** 5–10 minutes

### 4.3 Stage 2: Edit (Sunting)

Human editing transforms the AI draft from a generic template into brand-specific, audience-appropriate content.

**Edit stage checklist:**
- [ ] Replace generic phrases with brand-specific language and examples
- [ ] Add Malaysia-specific context: local place names, prices in RM, cultural references
- [ ] Adjust tone and vocabulary for the target platform and audience
- [ ] Break up long sentences (aim for average sentence length under 20 words)
- [ ] Ensure the opening hook captures attention within the first 1–2 lines
- [ ] Confirm the call to action (seruan bertindak) is clear and specific

**Time required:** 10–20 minutes

### 4.4 Stage 3: Evaluate (Nilai)

The evaluation stage ensures the content is accurate, compliant, and original before publication.

**Evaluate stage checklist:**
- [ ] Verify all statistics, percentages, and named claims against credible sources
- [ ] Check all regulatory claims (halal status, product certifications, health claims)
- [ ] Review for cultural sensitivity: halal/dietary references, ethnic/religious imagery
- [ ] Run through plagiarism checker if required (Grammarly, Copyscape, Originality.ai)
- [ ] Confirm the content does not violate MCMC content guidelines (CMA 1998)
- [ ] Check for AI hallucinations (see Section 5)

**Time required:** 10–15 minutes

### 4.5 Stage 4: Publish (Terbit)

The publication stage involves formatting, scheduling, and posting.

**Publish stage checklist:**
- [ ] Format for the platform: correct image size, caption length, hashtag count
- [ ] Add visual assets (image, video, graphic) — verify image is licensed or original
- [ ] Add internal links (for blog content) or relevant hashtags (for social media)
- [ ] Schedule at optimal posting time based on audience analytics
- [ ] Set a reminder to check performance metrics 24–48 hours after posting

**Time required:** 5–10 minutes

## 5. Detecting and Preventing AI Hallucinations (Pengesahan Fakta)

### 5.1 What is an AI Hallucination?

**AI hallucination (halusinasi AI)** is when an AI model generates text that is confidently stated but factually incorrect. This occurs because LLMs predict statistically likely words — they do not retrieve verified facts from a database. Common types of hallucination include:

| Hallucination Type | Example | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Fabricated statistics** | "According to MCMC 2025, 78% of Malaysians use TikTok daily." (number does not exist) | HIGH — damages credibility if published |
| **Non-existent citations** | AI cites a journal article or report that was never published | HIGH — academic/professional credibility damage |
| **Wrong legal references** | AI states an incorrect section number or year for a Malaysian law | CRITICAL — legal liability risk |
| **Outdated information** | AI references a product, company, or policy that has changed | MEDIUM — factual inaccuracy |
| **Misattributed quotes** | AI attributes a quote to the wrong person or invents a quote entirely | HIGH — reputational damage |

### 5.2 Anti-Hallucination Procedure (Prosedur Anti-Halusinasi)

Apply this procedure to every AI-generated piece of content before publishing:

- [ ] **Identify all factual claims:** Highlight every statistic, percentage, legal reference, product claim, and named citation
- [ ] **Verify statistics:** Cross-check with official sources (MCMC, DOSM, DataReportal, Statista)
- [ ] **Verify legal references:** Check the Attorney General's Chambers website (www.agc.gov.my) or MyLegal
- [ ] **Verify citations:** Search for the cited report, article, or book — confirm it exists
- [ ] **Check dates:** Confirm all year references are current and the information has not been superseded
- [ ] **Challenge suspicious claims:** If a statistic seems too precise, too dramatic, or too convenient — investigate it
- [ ] **Do not use unverifiable claims:** If a claim cannot be verified within reasonable effort, remove it or replace with a verified equivalent

> **AMARAN / WARNING:** Publishing AI-generated content containing fabricated statistics or false claims may violate the Consumer Protection Act 1999 (Act 599) and MCMC content guidelines under CMA 1998. In regulated industries (health, finance, legal), false claims may also trigger regulatory action by KPDNHEP, Bank Negara Malaysia, or the Medical Practitioners Act.

## 6. Ethical and Legal Considerations for AI Content in Malaysia

### 6.1 Copyright and AI-Generated Content (Hak Cipta)

The **Copyright Act 1987 (Akta Hak Cipta 1987)** governs intellectual property in Malaysia. Its application to AI-generated content involves several important considerations:

| Question | Current Position in Malaysia |
|---|---|
| Does AI-generated content qualify for copyright protection? | Uncertain — Malaysian law has not been updated for AI; human authorship is generally required for copyright |
| Who owns AI-generated content? | The person who directed the AI (via prompt) has the strongest claim to ownership |
| Can AI content infringe existing copyright? | Yes — if AI reproduces substantial portions of copyrighted works; risk exists even if unintentional |
| Is it safe to use AI images commercially? | Only if using tools with commercially licensed outputs (Adobe Firefly, Canva licensed images) — not all AI image tools grant commercial rights |

> **NOTA PENTING / IMPORTANT NOTE:** Malaysian copyright law (Act 1987, as amended 1997 and 2012) does not yet specifically address AI-generated works. As of 2026, businesses should adopt a conservative approach: treat AI outputs as requiring human creative input before asserting copyright, and ensure any AI tool used commercially provides clear licensing terms for generated content.

### 6.2 MCMC Content Guidelines

All digital content published in Malaysia is subject to the **Communications and Multimedia Act 1998 (Act 588)**. AI-generated content is not exempt — the publisher remains legally responsible.

| Content Category | MCMC Position | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| False or misleading information | Prohibited under Section 211 and 233 CMA 1998 | Verify all factual claims before publishing |
| Content inciting racial or religious tension | Prohibited — high enforcement risk | Review all AI content for sensitivity to Malaysia's multiracial society |
| Health claims (supplement, medical) | Must be substantiated; regulated by MOH | Remove unsubstantiated health claims from AI drafts |
| Financial promotions | Regulated by Securities Commission | AI financial content must be reviewed by licensed advisors |
| Advertising sponsored content | Must be clearly disclosed | Label all paid partnerships with #Iklan / #Berbayar / #Sponsored |

### 6.3 Plagiarism and Originality (Plagiat dan Keaslian)

AI tools generate text by synthesising patterns from training data, which includes published works. While direct plagiarism is not the primary risk (AI paraphrases rather than copies), originality remains a professional concern:

**Tools for checking content originality:**
- **Grammarly** — writing quality + originality score
- **Copyscape** — web plagiarism detection for blog content
- **Originality.ai** — AI content detection + plagiarism check
- **GPTZero** — AI content detection (useful for client transparency)

## Soalan Ulangkaji / Review Questions

1. Explain in plain language how a Large Language Model generates text. Why can it produce a statistic that sounds precise and credible but is completely fabricated?
2. A trainee produces an Instagram caption using ChatGPT, reads it once, and posts it immediately without any changes. Using the DEEP workflow, identify which stages were skipped and explain what could go wrong.
3. Categorise the following content types by their primary purpose (informational / promotional / engagement / conversion) and identify the most appropriate AI tool for drafting each: (a) a Shopee product description for a halal body lotion; (b) a Facebook post asking followers to vote on their favourite menu item; (c) a 700-word blog article about "how to choose the right meal prep service in Malaysia"; (d) a WhatsApp broadcast offering a 30% discount to existing customers.
4. A digital marketer in Malaysia uses Midjourney to generate images for a client's advertising campaign and posts them without checking the licensing terms. What are two legal risks this creates under Malaysian law?
5. An AI tool generates a blog article claiming: "A 2024 Universiti Malaya study found that 91% of Malaysian SMEs increased revenue after adopting digital marketing." You cannot find this study online. What should you do, and what are the consequences of publishing this claim without verification?
