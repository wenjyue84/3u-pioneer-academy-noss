### Section B

**B1** (8 marks — 1.5 marks per step explanation + 0.1 marks each example, rounded; award holistically at 2 marks per HEARD step for strong answer):

H — Hear: Listen fully to the complaint without interrupting or dismissing. Example: Read the full negative review about cold delivery carefully before drafting a response; do not respond defensively within the first 30 seconds of seeing it.

E — Empathise: Acknowledge the customer's feelings and validate their experience. Example: "Kami faham betapa mengecewakan apabila makanan tiba sejuk selepas anda menantikan dengan sabar." (We understand how disappointing it is when food arrives cold after waiting.)

A — Apologise: Offer a sincere apology for the experience regardless of fault. Example: "Kami memohon maaf atas pengalaman yang tidak memuaskan ini." (We sincerely apologise for this unsatisfactory experience.)

R — Resolve: Offer a concrete solution or next step. Example: "Sila hubungi kami di [nombor/emel] supaya kami dapat menawarkan penghantaran semula atau baucer penggantian." (Please contact us so we can arrange a replacement delivery or compensation voucher.)

D — Diagnose: Investigate the root cause to prevent recurrence. Example: After the public response, internally check the delivery packaging, driver route time, and packaging insulation standard; if a systemic issue is found, update the SOP.

**B2** (8 marks — 2 marks per type for strengths/limitation, 1 mark each use case):

Rule-based chatbot (4m): Strengths — (1) Reliable and predictable: always gives the exact pre-approved answer, essential for businesses with legal/compliance requirements (property disclosures, pricing accuracy). (2) Fast and low-cost to build: can be set up in ManyChat within hours using button-based menus, no AI training required. Limitation — cannot understand free-text queries outside the button menu; if a user types "What time do you open on Sunday?" instead of clicking the "Opening Hours" button, the bot fails. Use case: a clinic appointment booking bot where patients select from fixed options (new patient / follow-up / specific doctor).

NLP chatbot (4m): Strengths — (1) Understands natural language input — can process "Is there parking nearby?" as a location/facilities query without the user selecting a specific button. (2) Handles a wider range of enquiry variations, reducing handoff rate for complex businesses. Limitation — requires training data and periodic tuning; can hallucinate incorrect answers if the knowledge base is incomplete or outdated. Use case: a property developer's enquiry bot where potential buyers ask highly varied questions about unit types, pricing, financing, location, and timeline.

**B3** (8 marks — 2 marks per step including PDPA consideration):

1. Register with a WhatsApp Business Solution Provider (BSP) — e.g. Wati, Respond.io, or 360dialog. PDPA: ensure the BSP is contractually bound as a data processor under PDPA 2010; request their Data Processing Agreement.

2. Design conversation flows and message templates — WhatsApp requires all outbound proactive messages to use pre-approved templates. PDPA: message templates must include an opt-out instruction ("Reply STOP to unsubscribe") and must not request sensitive personal data (IC number, bank details) via unencrypted chat.

3. Build and test the chatbot logic — connect the BSP platform to the chatbot builder; test all flows with real Malaysian phone numbers before going live. PDPA: collect only data necessary for the stated purpose (name + enquiry type, not full address unless needed for delivery); apply data minimisation principle.

4. Go live and monitor containment rate — launch publicly; monitor chatbot performance daily for the first 2 weeks. PDPA: store all conversation logs securely (encrypted at rest); implement a data retention policy — delete conversation data after the retention period stated in the Privacy Notice (e.g. 12 months).

**B4** (8 marks — 2 marks each violation):

Consumer Protection Act 1999 (CPA) Section 10 prohibits false and misleading representations about goods or services. For reviews:

1. Paying a third-party to post fake 5-star reviews — constitutes a false representation about the quality or popularity of the business in violation of CPA Section 10.

2. Incentivising reviews with a reward conditional on posting 5 stars — "Post a 5-star review to get 10% off your next order" is a conditional incentive that coerces positive feedback, creating a misleading impression of authentic customer satisfaction.

3. Business owner or staff posting reviews on personal accounts without disclosure — undisclosed affiliated reviews constitute deceptive commercial practice; consumers would falsely believe the reviews are from independent customers.

4. Posting negative reviews about competitors — fabricating or exaggerating false negative reviews about a competing business violates CPA Section 10 (false representation about competitor's goods/services) and may additionally constitute defamation under the Defamation Act 1957.

**B5** (8 marks — 2 marks each):

a) Sentiment analysis: Use AI tools such as MonkeyLearn, Google Natural Language API, or ChatGPT batch processing to classify each of the 50 reviews as Positive / Neutral / Negative and extract the emotional keywords driving each sentiment. This allows the business to see at a glance that 38 reviews are positive (food quality), 7 neutral (mixed), and 5 negative (service speed) without reading each review manually.

b) AI response drafting: Use ChatGPT or Claude with a prompt specifying the review language and business tone: "Draft a professional, warm response in [BM/EN/ZH] to this [positive/negative] review for a Penang tourism business, thanking the reviewer and addressing any concern raised." Review, personalise, and post — this reduces response drafting time from 5 minutes per review to under 1 minute.

c) Theme identification: Ask ChatGPT to analyse all 50 reviews and identify the top 5 recurring themes: "Read these 50 reviews and list the 5 most frequently mentioned topics, both positive and negative." Common output: food quality (mentioned 32 times), service speed (18 times), parking (12 times), cleanliness (9 times), pricing (7 times). This surfaces actionable insights management can act on.

d) Management reporting: Use a simple monthly review report template: total reviews received, average star rating, sentiment breakdown (%), top 3 positive themes, top 3 improvement areas, response rate and average response time. AI can generate the first draft of this report from the raw data; the marketer reviews and presents it in the monthly management meeting.

### Section C

**C1** (12 marks):

a) Ethical review acquisition strategy (6 marks — 1 mark each tactic, max 6):

1. QR code review stand at the counter — design a Canva QR card linking directly to the Google review page; place on tables, at the payment counter, and with takeaway packaging. No incentive attached — just a polite "Share your experience."

2. WhatsApp follow-up message (Day 1 after visit) — for dine-in customers who opt into WhatsApp, send a personalised follow-up: "Hi [Name], thank you for visiting us yesterday! We hope you enjoyed your meal. If you have a moment, we'd love your feedback on Google: [link]." Use ManyChat or Wati to automate this trigger 24 hours post-visit.

3. Staff verbal request at checkout — train all staff to say: "If you enjoyed your experience today, a quick Google review would mean a lot to us!" This is the highest-converting review acquisition method for Malaysian F&B; authentic in-person requests outperform digital-only prompts.

4. AI-generated multilingual review request — for customers who communicate in BM or ZH (identified from their WhatsApp language), use AI to draft the follow-up message in their preferred language, increasing response rate from non-English-speaking customers.

5. Google review link in email receipt/footer — if the café has an email receipt system (via Square, StoreHub, or Ecount), embed the Google review link in every receipt footer: "Loved your visit? Leave us a review."

6. Respond visibly to every review — when potential reviewers see that the café responds thoughtfully to all reviews (including negative ones), they are more likely to leave one themselves. Visible engagement signals that reviews are read and valued.

b) Negative review response strategy (6 marks):

For both negative reviews, apply the HEARD framework publicly (2m): Respond within 24 hours; acknowledge the experience; apologise; offer to resolve offline (provide email/phone); sign off with the manager's name for human accountability.

For the false review alleging stale bread (2m): In addition to the HEARD response (which should factually but professionally state "We maintain strict daily freshness standards for all baked goods and we take this feedback seriously — please contact us directly so we can investigate"), report the review to Google if it violates Google's review policies: (1) factually false content ("stale bread" if fabricated); (2) conflict of interest (if the reviewer is a competitor). Use Google Business Profile → "Flag as inappropriate" with a specific reason. Note: Google does not remove reviews simply because the business disagrees; it only removes reviews that violate its policies. Document the internal investigation (delivery record, staff log, kitchen SOP compliance) in case of escalation.

Do not (2m): Do not respond aggressively, call the reviewer a liar publicly, or offer incentives to change the review — all of which can damage reputation further and may violate CPA 1999.

**C2** (14 marks):

a) Community management audit (6 marks — 1 mark each step):

1. Audit current posting frequency and content mix — check the last 3 months of posts; calculate average posts per week; categorise content by type (product, lifestyle, promo, UGC, engagement).

2. Analyse engagement rate per post type — use Instagram Insights or a third-party tool (Metricool, Later) to identify which content types historically drove the highest engagement; compare to the current declining period.

3. Review DM inbox — analyse the volume, type, and unanswered percentage of incoming DMs; identify if enquiry volume has grown faster than response capacity.

4. Check posting time alignment — use Instagram Insights → Audience → Most Active Times to confirm posts are published when the Malaysian follower base (typically active 8–10 PM MYT) is online.

5. Competitor benchmarking — review 3 competitor accounts of similar size; compare their engagement rate, posting frequency, content types, and comment interaction style to identify gaps.

6. Audience sentiment check — read the last 50 comments; identify whether negative or neutral comments are left unanswered (unanswered comments depress engagement rate as the algorithm deprioritises low-response posts).

b) AI-assisted community management system (8 marks):

DM automation with ManyChat (3m): Set up a ManyChat Instagram DM automation: (1) Keyword trigger — if a DM contains keywords like "price," "harga," "how much," "available," or "sizing," the bot instantly replies with a structured message: product size guide image + pricing table + "For further help, type HUMAN to speak with our team." (2) Welcome flow — any first-time DM receives an automated greeting within 30 seconds: "Hi! Thanks for reaching out to [Brand]. What can we help you with today? [Pricing / Availability / Order Status / Other]" — button menu. (3) Human handoff — if a customer types "HUMAN," selects "Other," or the bot cannot match the query after 2 attempts: auto-notify the human community manager via ManyChat's mobile app notification; tag the conversation as "Needs Human"; set a 5-minute SLA for the human to respond.

Engagement rate improvement (3m): (1) Comment reply automation — use Meta Business Suite's automated comment reply for high-volume posts: any comment containing a question mark automatically receives a reply like "Great question! DM us for details." This signals to the algorithm that the post is highly engaged and extends reach. (2) Content calendar shift — based on audit findings, shift to a 3-pillar content mix proven to drive Malaysian fashion accessories engagement: 40% product styling (flat-lay + model shots), 40% customer UGC and testimonials (request permission, repost), 20% interactive content (polls, "Which would you wear?" stories). (3) Engagement pods schedule — publish 3 posts per week at 8:30 PM MYT (peak Malaysian audience time identified from Insights); within the first 30 minutes, have the account owner respond to every comment — this 30-minute engagement window is the highest-leverage action to signal post quality to the Instagram algorithm.

Human handoff protocol (2m): Any conversation involving: (1) a complaint, (2) a request for a refund or return, (3) a custom order enquiry above RM500, or (4) a legal threat or media inquiry — must be immediately escalated to a named human agent. The ManyChat flow tags these as "Priority" and sends an in-app alert. Response SLA for priority handoffs: 15 minutes during business hours (9 AM–10 PM MYT), 8 AM next morning for overnight escalations. Document all escalated conversations in a shared CRM log (Google Sheets or HubSpot) for quality review.

**C3** (14 marks):

a) Platform recommendation and conversation flow (7 marks):

Platform: Wati (3m — 1m recommendation, 2m justification). Wati is the recommended platform because: it is specifically built for WhatsApp Business API (the primary enquiry channel for Malaysian property buyers); supports both website widget and WhatsApp chatbot from a single platform; has a Malaysian-context team and Ringgit-denominated pricing; integrates with CRM tools (HubSpot, Google Sheets) for lead management; and supports broadcast messaging for property launch announcements to opted-in prospects.

Conversation flow — 5 nodes (4m — 0.8m per node):

Node 1 — Welcome & Language Selection: "Welcome to [Developer Name] Property Enquiries. Please select your preferred language: [English] [Bahasa Malaysia] [中文]"

Node 2 — Enquiry Type Menu: "What can we help you with today? [New Project Info] [Unit Availability & Pricing] [Book a Site Visit] [Loan & Financing] [Speak to an Agent]"

Node 3A — Project Info: Bot delivers a pre-built project brochure PDF + key facts (location, unit types, price range, expected completion) → "Would you like to book a site visit? [Yes — Book Now] [No — Other Enquiry]"

Node 3B — Unit Availability: Bot asks for preferred unit type and budget range → queries a connected Google Sheet price list → returns available units matching criteria → "To reserve a unit or get a full pricing sheet, please speak to our sales team." [Human handoff trigger 1]

Node 4 — Site Visit Booking: Bot collects name, phone, preferred date → confirms available slots via calendar integration → sends booking confirmation via WhatsApp.

Node 5 — Fallback / Human Handoff: If the user's free-text message is not understood after 2 attempts: "Let me connect you to one of our property consultants who can assist you better." [Human handoff trigger 2 — all unrecognised queries]

b) PDPA compliance and chatbot metrics (7 marks):

PDPA compliance (4m):

Data collection consent (1m): At Node 1, before collecting any personal data (name, phone), the bot must display a consent notice: "By continuing, you agree to [Developer Name]'s Privacy Policy and consent to us contacting you regarding property enquiries. [View Privacy Policy] [I Agree]" — the conversation must not proceed to data collection without the user tapping "I Agree."

Data storage (1m): All conversation data and lead information collected via Wati must be stored on servers compliant with PDPA data security requirements; if data is transferred outside Malaysia (e.g. to a US-based CRM), the developer must ensure adequate data protection measures equivalent to PDPA standards are in place and disclose this in the Privacy Policy.

Opt-out right (1m): Every outbound broadcast message must include an opt-out instruction: "Reply STOP to unsubscribe from property updates." Upon receiving "STOP," the system must immediately cease all outbound communications to that number and remove them from broadcast lists — this must be automated, not manual, to ensure compliance.

Data retention (1m): The Privacy Notice must state how long lead data is retained (e.g. "We retain enquiry data for 24 months from the date of last contact"). After the retention period, data must be securely deleted from Wati and the CRM.

Chatbot metrics (3m):

Containment rate (1.5m): Percentage of conversations fully resolved by the bot without human handoff. Formula: (Conversations resolved by bot / Total conversations) × 100. For a property enquiry chatbot, a reasonable target is 60–70% containment — property purchases are high-consideration decisions where many prospects ultimately want to speak to a human agent before committing. A containment rate below 40% indicates the bot's FAQ coverage is insufficient; above 85% may indicate the handoff triggers are too restrictive and genuine leads are not reaching the sales team.

Handoff rate (1.5m): Percentage of conversations transferred to a human agent. Formula: (Conversations handed off / Total conversations) × 100. For a property chatbot, target handoff rate is 30–40% — this represents genuinely interested prospects who have progressed beyond basic information needs and are ready for a sales conversation. A handoff rate above 60% indicates the bot is failing to answer basic questions (poor FAQ coverage); a handoff rate below 10% may indicate the bot is not surfacing the right moments to connect high-intent leads with the sales team.

**Q26.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain the difference between rule-based and NLP chatbots in detail, including the technical mechanism behind each and when each is more appropriate for a Malaysian SME. [CoCU C07.3.1]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Rule-based chatbots operate on explicit decision tree logic: the developer pre-defines every possible conversation path as a set of "if this input → then this response" rules. User interaction is typically via button menus or exact keyword matches — the bot cannot deviate from its decision tree. Technical mechanism: the chatbot engine parses the incoming message for exact keyword matches (e.g. "booking" triggers the booking flow) or button selection (the user taps "Check Availability"). No machine learning is involved; the system is deterministic and 100% predictable. Appropriate for: businesses with highly standardised enquiry types (clinic appointment booking, restaurant reservation, simple FAQ delivery); businesses with no AI budget who need a bot running within 48 hours; regulated industries where every response must be pre-approved (finance, healthcare). NLP (Natural Language Processing) chatbots use machine learning language models (e.g. Dialogflow CX's BERT-based intent classification, or GPT-4-powered chatbots) to understand the semantic meaning of free-text input regardless of exact phrasing. A user can ask "Do you have any rooms for this weekend?" or "Can I book a stay for Saturday night?" — both are classified as the same "Room Availability" intent. Technical mechanism: the user's message is vectorised and compared against a trained intent classification model; the closest matching intent triggers the appropriate response template. Training data (50–100 examples per intent) must be provided and periodically updated. Appropriate for: property developers, educational institutions, tourism businesses where enquiry language is highly varied; any business receiving more than 100 DMs per day where button menus frustrate users with complex questions.

**Q27.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A Malaysian F&B chain (3 outlets in KL) wants to build a review strategy that generates 50+ new Google reviews per month ethically across all three outlets. Design the full strategy. [CoCU C07.1.2]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* Full ethical review acquisition strategy for 3 KL outlets: Infrastructure setup (Month 1): (1) Create separate Google Business Profile listings for each outlet with accurate NAP, photos, and categories. Each outlet needs its own review link. (2) Design a Canva QR review card for each outlet (with the outlet-specific Google review shortlink) — print on table tents, takeaway bags, and receipt footers. (3) Set up ManyChat or Wati WhatsApp automation with a post-visit review request flow: trigger = customer WhatsApp opt-in at checkout; timing = 3 hours after estimated visit. Acquisition cadence (per outlet): QR card requests generate approximately 5–10 reviews/month per 300 daily customers (2–3% conversion rate). WhatsApp follow-up generates approximately 8–12% response rate from opted-in customers — for 100 opted-in customers per month per outlet, this yields 8–12 reviews/month. Staff verbal requests at checkout generate 5–8 reviews/month. Total per outlet: approximately 18–30 reviews/month. Across 3 outlets: 54–90 reviews/month — exceeds the 50 target. Language personalisation: train staff to send the review link in the customer's preferred language (EN/BM/ZH). ManyChat detects the language of the customer's last WhatsApp message and sends the review request in the same language using an AI-drafted template. **Q28.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] A client receives a highly detailed, articulate 5-star review that appears to have been written by AI. Should they be concerned? What are the ethical and legal implications? [CoCU C07.1.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* This warrants careful investigation. An AI-written review is not automatically unethical — a real customer may have used ChatGPT to help articulate their genuine experience, which is a grey area not explicitly prohibited by current Malaysian law or Google's policies (as of 2026). However, if the AI-written review was: (1) Posted by the business itself — this constitutes a fake review (Consumer Protection Act 1999 violation, false representation); (2) Posted by a paid third party on behalf of a real customer who did not actually author it — also fake review territory; (3) Posted by a real customer who asked AI to polish their genuine experience — ethically ambiguous but legally defensible, as the underlying experience is authentic. Practical concern: Google's detection systems are increasingly flagging AI-generated review text using their own classifiers; a review removed by Google's spam filter will count against the business even if it was legitimate. Best practice: advise clients never to solicit AI-written reviews and to always ask customers to write in their own words, however simple. A 2-sentence genuine review ("Food was delicious, will come again!") carries more long-term value than a suspiciously eloquent 5-star paragraph. From a legal risk standpoint: if KPDNHEP (Ministry of Domestic Trade) investigates a business for fake reviews and finds AI-generated text commissioned by the business, this would constitute evidence under CPA 1999 regardless of whether a human name was attached to it.

**Q31.** [SENARIO / SCENARIO] Explain how PDPA 2010 applies specifically to a WhatsApp chatbot that collects leads for a Malaysian property developer. What are the five PDPA principles most relevant to this use case? [CoCU C07.3.3]

*Jawapan Contoh / Sample Answer:* PDPA 2010 governs the processing of personal data by commercial entities in Malaysia. For a property developer's WhatsApp lead chatbot collecting name, phone number, enquiry type, and budget: (1) General Principle — personal data must only be processed for a specified, explicit, and legitimate purpose stated to the data subject at the time of collection. The chatbot's consent notice must specify: "We collect your name, phone, and enquiry details to assist with property enquiries and to contact you regarding our projects." The chatbot must present a consent notice and require the user to affirmatively agree (tap "I Agree") before proceeding. Pre-ticked boxes or continued scrolling do not constitute valid consent. (3) Disclosure Principle — personal data must not be disclosed to third parties without the data subject's consent or a lawful basis. If the developer shares leads with partner banks (for home loan referrals), this must be disclosed at consent time. (4) Access Principle — the data subject has the right to request access to their personal data held by the developer and to request correction of inaccurate data. The chatbot's Privacy Policy must include a contact address for data access requests (typically the Data Protection Officer's email). (5) Retention Principle — personal data must not be retained longer than necessary for the stated purpose. A lead that expressed interest but did not proceed to purchase should be deleted after a defined retention period (e.g. 24 months). Implement automated data deletion workflows in the CRM to enforce retention periods consistently.
