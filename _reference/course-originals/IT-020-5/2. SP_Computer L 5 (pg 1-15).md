**NATIONAL OCCUPATIONAL SKILLS STANDARD (NOSS) FOR;**

**COMPUTER SYSTEM MANAGEMENT**

**LEVEL 5**

**INTRODUCTION**The ever-changing world fuelled largely by the continuing advancement in computer technology requires organizations to be well-equipped to remain current and relevant. Businesses from multi-national corporations to home-based enterprises have to adopt current technologies to be competitive. Government agencies and institutions too, need to stay “at the top of the game” in order to provide fast, reliable and accurate services to the people. 

This climate creates job opportunities in computer systems management personnel to support the industry growth.  Computer system management job area personnel normally known by IT Manager plays a vital role in the implementation of technology within organisation to plan, execute, coordinate, and facilitate the computer-related activities. They help determine both technical and business goals in consultation with top management, and develop guidelines and procedures to achieve business goals. This requires a strong understanding of both technology and business practices

The IT Managers are also required to analyse project requirements, estimate budgets, and develop schedules of the projects. They coordinate such projects from development through implementation, working with internal and external clients, vendors, consultants, and computer specialists. These managers are increasingly involved in projects that upgrade the computer system infrastructure and information security of organisations.

As computer systems become more closely connected with day-to-day operations of businesses, IT Managers are also expected to be aware of business practices. They must possess strong interpersonal, communication, and leadership skills as they are required to interact with staff members and other people outside their organisations. Further, they should possess teamwork skills to work on group projects and other collaborative efforts. In addition, they must also have an understanding of how a business functions, how it earns revenue, and how technology relates to the core competencies of the business.

Employment of IT Managers is expected to grow in this decade. Through Economic Transformation Program (ETP) initiated by the government and rapid development of information technology in the workplace will continue to drive demand for more managers. To remain competitive, firms will continue to install sophisticated computer system and networks, making the outlook for IT Managers remains strong

IT Managers may advance to progressively higher leadership positions in the information technology department. Some may become managers in non-technical areas such as marketing, human resources, or sales because in high technology firms, an understanding of technical issues is helpful in those areas

**JOB COMPETENCIES**IT Manager is competent in performing the following core competencies:

Computer Network Maintenance AdministrationComputer System asset ManagementComputer System security ManagementDisaster Recovery ManagementComputer System project ManagementComputer System Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Development and Implementation ManagementOptionally, the IT Manager is competent in performing Server Scripting as an elective competency. 

**WORKING CONDITION**IT Managers generally spend most of their time in offices. Most of them work at least 40 hours a week and some may have to work evenings and weekends to meet deadlines or solve unexpected problems. Some of them may experience pressure in meeting technical goals with shorts deadlines or tight budgets. As networks continue to expand and more work is done remotely, IT Managers have to communicate with and oversee offsite employees using Internet. 

IT Managers need to keep up to date with the ever-changing specific software and operating systems and hardware related technology. They must also work well with people and should be able communicate effectively.

**EMPLOYMENT PROSPECT**The increasing use of technology in the workplace is projected to lead and faster than average growth in this occupation. As so much business is carried out over computer networks, security will continue to be an important issue for businesses and other organisations. The emergence of security as a key concern for businesses should lead to strong growth for IT Managers.

IT Managers are employed by a range of organisations including government departments and agencies, institutions of higher learning, private corporations, banks, private companies that provide computer, database and network services to clients, telecommunication companies and many other private organisations. A growing number of computer system management advanced diploma holders are self-employed, working independently as contractors or consultants.
